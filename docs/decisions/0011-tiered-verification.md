# ADR-0011: Tiered verification

- Status: accepted, 2026-10-20
- Reviewed: decided by the project owner on 2026-10-20, including the explicit approval this policy reduction needs; the after-fix rule amended on 2026-10-21 with the owner's approval and clarified the same day as part of the owner-approved slice 1 review fixes; the after-fix rule and the slice review amended on 2026-10-22 by the owner's decision after the slice 2 review; wording pending line-by-line owner review; the slice tier amended on 2026-11-16 with the slice 10 sub-slice reviews and the re-verification of after-fix rows, decided by the owner with the slice 10 plan (D1, D11, K17), wording pending owner review
- Deciders: project owner
- Related: [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#development-verification), [ovc-verify](../../.claude/skills/ovc-verify/SKILL.md), [roadmap](../roadmap.md), [ADR-0009](0009-owner-approved-exceptions-and-visibility.md), [ADR-0018](0018-targeted-checks-and-merge-gate.md) (the checks of each tier are the change checks, and the full run moves to the merge gate; amended 2026-10-27, pending owner review)

## Context

Every nontrivial change used to need a fresh independent reviewer for each affected component and each applicable profile (correctness, security, browser, performance), both on the plan and before readiness, and again after every fix. A documentation-only change to the visual language took five review rounds and about a dozen reviewer runs. Most findings were minor wording issues, and the cost would grow with every code change in the coming slices. The owner wants routine work reviewed lightly and the full matrix kept for moments when a whole roadmap slice is complete (since replaced by one reviewer per layer, see the 2026-10-22 amendment).

## Decision

Verification has three tiers. The coordinating agent picks the tier and decides whether the change meets the security trigger, states both in the plan, rechecks them against the final diff, and picks the higher tier and assumes the trigger is met when in doubt.

- **Trivial** (a typo, a broken link, an obvious one-line bug): the checks from the development guide, no independent review. A change that meets the security trigger is never trivial.
- **Standard** (every other change inside a slice): the checks, plus one fresh independent reviewer who covers the complete diff with the short [standard checklist](../../.claude/skills/ovc-verify/references/standard.md), applying every relevant profile in one context.
  - A change that meets the **security trigger** also gets a separate fresh reviewer with the full [security profile](../../.claude/skills/ovc-verify/references/security.md). The trigger, defined once in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#development-verification), covers any change, including a specification-only one, that touches a trust boundary or maps to a threat in the [threat model](../threat-model.md); agent configuration and verification policy always meet it.
  - After fixes, the checks rerun. At most one fresh reviewer, scoped to the fixes, runs, and only when a blocker or major finding was fixed or a contract changed; it applies the standard checklist to the fixes, and the full security profile as well when such a finding concerned security. If the recheck before readiness finds that the final diff meets the security trigger and no security-profile reviewer has reviewed the change, one runs over the complete diff even when the scoped re-review has already run; when both would run, it replaces the scoped re-review and also applies the standard checklist to the fixes. Minor findings of the re-review are fixed when cheap or recorded as known limitations in the slice's known-limitations file (`knowledge/reviews/slice-<n>-known-limitations.md`, with the finding, its severity, the change and why it was accepted), and start no further review round; a blocker or major finding of the re-review is fixed, the checks rerun, and the change does not merge until the owner answers a multiple-choice question on whether another reviewer runs (recommending a security-profile reviewer when the finding concerned security). The readiness report says, for each fixed security finding, whether its fix touches a trust boundary. Other fixes to minor findings need only the checks. Severity is the reviewer's; the coordinator never downgrades it. (Amended 2026-10-21: re-reviewing every minor security fix produced an endless chain of new minor findings. Amended 2026-10-22: the rule that fixes meeting the security trigger always got a fresh reviewer still produced four or five review rounds on single changes after the slice 2 review, mostly over minor gaps in the secret guard hook, which guards against accidental disclosure only; one re-review at most now applies to every change.)
- **Slice** (when every planned change of a roadmap slice has merged, before the slice is closed): over the cumulative diff since the previous slice closed, one fresh reviewer per layer the slice changed: the backend (correctness and performance), the frontend (correctness, browser and performance), security across the whole slice, and development tooling with agent configuration; each reviewer applies every relevant profile to the components of its layer, follows their contract, data-flow and resource-lifecycle consequences, and may subdivide its scope with fresh contexts; the security reviewer runs whenever any change in the slice met the security trigger, subdivides into fresh contexts when the slice touches more than one family of trust boundaries (for example authentication, uploads, agent configuration), and reports each affected threat as examined, not affected or unverified; each reviewer also re-verifies the entries of its layer in the slice's known-limitations file, given to it as items to check rather than as verdicts, and the slice review is resolved only when each entry is fixed, accepted by the owner or carried into a named later slice; performance measurements run one reviewer at a time on an otherwise idle machine. Findings are fixed in follow-up changes under the after-fix rule of the standard tier. Each follow-up change is a standard-tier change with its own reviews, names the slice review finding it resolves, and its reviewer confirms that finding is closed. Each reviewer also re-verifies each `after-fix` row of its layer in the known-limitations file against its recorded evidence ([ADR-0018](0018-targeted-checks-and-merge-gate.md), pending owner review). Each sub-slice of slice 10 (10.1 to 10.5 of the [slice 10 plan](../../knowledge/plans/slice-10.md)) counts as a roadmap slice for the slice tier: its slice review's scope is its own list of changes in that plan, including any inserted consolidation, its plan revision and the follow-ups of the previous sub-slice's review that merged after that review started, stated in the review record with each change's commit, instead of the diff since the previous slice closed; the record maps every commit on `main` since the previous sub-slice's closing commit (for 10.1, since `c218028`) to exactly one sub-slice's list, a commit in no list joins the scope of the earliest sub-slice whose slice review has not started (a commit that merges after 10.5's review started is listed in 10.5's record as carried and goes to the next slice's review, which covers it in addition to its own diff), and 10.5's record shows that the lists, with the commits it lists as carried, cover every commit from `c218028` to its own closing commit; the security reviewer of 10.5's slice review also covers, per trust-boundary family, the cumulative diff since `c218028`; the known limitations stay in one file, `knowledge/reviews/slice-10-known-limitations.md`, with a sub-slice column, the development tooling reviewer re-verifies every `rerun` and `after-fix` row in it while each layer reviewer also checks the rows of its own layer, and each entry is resolved as fixed, accepted by the owner or carried into a named later sub-slice or slice (decided by the owner on 2026-11-16 with the slice 10 plan's D1 and K17, pending owner review). (Amended 2026-10-22: the slice 2 review used eleven component and profile reviewers plus a second wave of performance reviewers; four layer reviewers keep an independent look per layer at about a third of the reviewer runs.)

The plan for a whole slice gets one standard reviewer before work starts; a slice or task plan that meets the security trigger also gets a security-profile reviewer before approval; other task plans are approved by the owner without a planning review. The commit that closes a slice is recorded under that slice in the roadmap and is the base of the next slice review. For slice 10, the closing commit of each sub-slice is recorded under slice 10, and each sub-slice review's scope is its list of changes as stated above, which overrides, for slice 10 only, the roadmap's rule of reviewing everything merged since the previous slice closed (decided by the owner on 2026-11-16, pending owner review).

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the full matrix for every change:** thorough, but its cost dominated small changes and mostly produced minor findings.
- **Self-review only for standard changes:** cheapest, but loses the independent look that catches what the implementing context is blind to.
- **Checks only:** documentation and code checks cannot judge contract consistency, threat coverage or design quality.
- **Full matrix whenever a new package or architectural layer appears:** harder to decide consistently than the slice boundary, which the roadmap already defines.
- **One reviewer per component and profile at the slice review:** the original rule; thorough, at about three times the reviewer runs.
- **No reviewer at all after fixes:** cheapest, but a fixed blocker or major finding deserves one independent look.

## Consequences

- Routine changes need one reviewer run (two when the security trigger is met) instead of one per component and profile, and at most one more after fixes.
- A change can merge with minor findings recorded as known limitations in the slice's known-limitations file; the slice review re-verifies each entry, and the slice closes only when each is fixed, accepted by the owner or carried into a named later slice.
- A fix for a minor finding, including a minor security finding, merges without its own independent review; the slice review is the next independent look.
- Problems that span several changes in a slice may surface only at the slice review; slices stay small enough for that review to be practical. Once OVC is deployed, a slice is not deployed before its slice review is resolved.
- Performance measurements run at slice reviews; standard reviewers only flag performance risks.
- `AGENTS.md`, the ovc-verify skill, the development guide, the roadmap and the verification policy record describe the tiers.
