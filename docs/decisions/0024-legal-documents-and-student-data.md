# ADR-0024: Legal documents and the retention of student data

- Status: accepted, 2026-11-23; its retention rules gain the export's 3-day retention, added 2026-11-23 by change U1b of the interface rework plan (pending owner review)
- Reviewed: decided by the project owner on 2026-11-22 and 2026-11-23 with [proposal 0006](../proposals/0006-legal-documents-and-student-data.md); wording pending owner review
- Deciders: project owner
- Amends: [ADR-0008](0008-hosting-and-data-residency.md): the privacy policy, the consent text and the user agreement live in the repository as public pages, while registration with the data-protection authority stays an owner obligation; retention bounds are added and the data still without a bound is listed below as open against ADR-0008's retention consequence
- Related: [ADR-0005](0005-tutor-centric-domain.md) and [ADR-0014](0014-students-join-through-their-tutor.md), refined and not superseded; [ADR-0015](0015-web-push.md), [proposal 0003](../proposals/0003-students-aged-14-and-over.md), FR-1.1, FR-1.7, FR-2.7, NFR-1.3, [threat model](../threat-model.md), [legal gap analysis](../../knowledge/analysis/legal-gaps-2026-11-22.md), [outline of the legal documents](../../knowledge/analysis/legal-documents-outline-2026-11-23.md)

## Context

OVC has no privacy policy (the `/privacy` page is a placeholder), no consent text behind the consent checkbox of FR-1.1 and no user agreement, and some student data outlives its purpose: an unaccepted card keeps a prospective student's email without a bound; withdrawn, merged and removed cards become discarded rows that keep a student's name, email or notes, invisible to the tutor, until the tutor's purge; after a student's purge the tutor's records keep the student's name and notes with no route to delete them. [Proposal 0006](../proposals/0006-legal-documents-and-student-data.md) set out the owner's choices; the owner adopted them on 2026-11-23 as the working basis for the specifications and code, ahead of a lawyer's review. The legal interpretations behind them are agent analysis marked `[verify]` in the [legal gap analysis](../../knowledge/analysis/legal-gaps-2026-11-22.md).

## Decision

1. **Operator:** the operator of personal data in OVC is the owner's individual entrepreneur, the same party for the user agreement, the regulator notification, processor contracts and later payments.
2. **Only operator:** OVC is the only operator of all personal data in the service, including what tutors enter about their students; a tutor is a user, not an operator, and OVC is not a tutor's processor.
3. **Legal bases:** consent (item 1 of part 1 of article 6 of 152-FZ) and the user agreement (item 5); the name and email a tutor enters before a student signs up rest on the legitimate interests of the operator and the tutor (item 7), supported by the tutor's warranty in the user agreement that the student agreed to be invited.
4. **Three documents:** a privacy policy published at `/privacy`; a consent text separate from it, confirmed by the existing consent checkbox; a user agreement, a public offer of the free service, accepted by pressing the sign-up button with one line under it linking to the agreement, never by the consent checkbox; the same on the invitation page. A lawyer reviews the three texts before the first deployment, and they are not published before that review.
5. **Legal wording in documents:** explanations, warranties and disclosures live in the documents; the interface carries only what the law requires there, such as the consent checkbox and the acceptance line.
6. **Invitation notice:** the invitation email carries one fixed catalogue line naming the operator and linking to the policy, as the notice of article 18 part 3 to a person whose data came from a tutor. The operator's name and contact are constants or operator settings, never user input; the link is built from `OVC_PUBLIC_URL` with no per-recipient query or token. The email's only links are the invitation link and this policy link. A send dropped silently (by the per-recipient cap, or for an address whose account is blocked or deleted) carries no notice, and the invitation page links the policy for every card, unconditionally, wider than proposal 0006's "for a card without an email" (T-3, T-27), so that the response of `GET /invitations/{token}` gains no field that reveals the card's email or whether it has one; the invitation page keeps `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.
7. **Private notes:** stay; the user agreement tells tutors that notes are personal data of the student and may be disclosed to the student on a lawful request.
8. **Retention:**
   - an unaccepted card's email is erased after 90 days;
   - withdrawing or merging a card erases its display name, email and notes at once;
   - removing a provisional or reclaim-pending link, by any path that applies the removal effects of the accounts specification (the tutor's removal, the student stopping studying, a dropped or expired reclaim, the 7-day cleanup, a student account's deletion or purge), erases the display name that the discarded row keeps, in the same transaction; the notes and email move to the new invited card as before;
   - when a purged student held a provisional or reclaim-pending link, the replacement invited card stays a live card the tutor sees, with the notes and email moved onto it; its email falls under the 90-day rule counted from the removal;
   - a student's purge, over every relationship row of the student including discarded ones, replaces the card's display name with a fixed label and erases its notes; lesson and issue history stays under the label; the student's direct chat keeps the tutor's own messages, which the tutor may delete. The documents say "replaces the name with a fixed label", never "anonymises": chat, issue content and dates may still identify the student.
   - an export of a user's data (FR-18) is kept at most 3 days after it was built, and deleted earlier with its storage object when data it holds becomes unreadable in OVC, as the [files specification](../../knowledge/specs/files.md#exports) lists the effects; the user agreement says that a user is responsible for an export once downloaded, `[verify]` for the lawyer (added 2026-11-23 by change U1b of the [interface rework plan](../../knowledge/plans/ui-rework.md), pending owner review).

### Constraints on the implementation

- The 90-day erasure revokes the card's open invitation in the same transaction and records an erasure marker; regenerating and sending refuse a card with the erasure marker until the tutor enters an email, checked under the card lock, because an email-less card otherwise legitimately holds an unbound link (T-7, T-23). The specification decides whether the period runs from the card's creation or from the last time the tutor entered the email, and how replacement cards count beyond the rule above.
- The erasure job locks each card in the acceptance lock order and re-checks it, skipping a card that is no longer `invited` and never erasing an email the tutor entered concurrently.
- Wherever a label reads an erased display name (`invitation.accepted` rows after a merge or a removal, audience labels of schedules and chats), it shows the surviving or replacement card's display name or a fixed label code, never the account name (T-44); the specification relaxes the required length of `display_name` accordingly.
- If the specification moves a merged card's notes to the surviving card first, the move stays within the same tutor's scope and is never visible to the student.
- Negative tests at the invitation boundary: after erasure the old link answers the same `404` on the invitation page and refuses sign-up and signed-in acceptance whatever the email; regenerating is refused until an email is entered; an invitation email queued before erasure is obsolete at dispatch; a purge test through `Tick(now)` covers a student who held a provisional link; the invitation email holds exactly two links, the policy link being `OVC_PUBLIC_URL` with the policy path and no query, fragment or per-recipient value; after a merge, a removal and a purge, every tutor-facing label (`invitation.accepted` rows, schedule and chat audience labels) shows the surviving or replacement card's display name or the fixed label, never the account name.

### What does not change

The 30-day purge after deletion, the 7-day cleanup of unconfirmed accounts and the invitation's email binding stay. Erased values remain in database backups for up to 31 days and in the mail provider's message logs, which the provider must keep for at most 30 days, a selection condition not yet confirmed; the conferencing host keeps its logs for 14 days; recordings, once they exist, keep a purged student's likeness and displayed name until their own expiry, at most 365 days. Throttle counters keep only keyed hashes and outbox payloads only identifiers, so neither keeps an erased email. Student accounts the operator has blocked are never purged automatically (T-22), so their records are not relabelled until the operator acts. Minors stay with proposal 0003 and web push with ADR-0015. No rows exist before the first deployment, so no backfill is needed.

## Alternatives considered

- **The tutor as an operator, with OVC as the tutor's processor:** every tutor, often self-employed, would have to notify the regulator and keep their own documents.
- **The tutor's warranty alone as the basis for invitation data:** a weaker footing than legitimate interests balanced against the student's rights.
- **Erasing withdrawn and merged cards by the 90-day job:** keeps data the tutor can no longer see for months without purpose.
- **A button for tutors to delete a purged student's records:** leaves the student's name in place until a tutor acts.
- **Waiting for the lawyer before deciding:** blocks the specification and code work the first deployment needs; a lawyer's change becomes a later record superseding this one in part.

## Consequences

- The specification and code change follows as its own planned change, deviating from proposal 0006's plan of one change with the record: the accounts specification (invitations, relationships, removal, reclaim, deletion), the data model (relationship columns and the erasure marker, the purge table, the paragraph on the tutor's own data), notifications (the invitation email's links, labels and links), realtime and video and schedule (labels), homework (issues under the label), the API's student card routes (regeneration and sending refused for a card with the erasure marker), the web design's public pages and addresses and the student card (regeneration refused for a card with the erasure marker), FR-1.1, FR-1.7 and the threat model's T-7, T-23, T-31, T-34 and T-44. Until it lands, the sections named under Where it is applied and the threat-model rows carry a pointer to this record, and where they disagree this record wins; an implementer who meets the old text stops and follows this record with its constraints.
- The first deployment depends on that change and on the lawyer's review of the three texts ([roadmap: before the pilot](../roadmap.md#before-the-pilot)).
- Data still without a bound, open against ADR-0008's retention consequence: an invited card's display name and notes until the tutor withdraws it; an ended card's display name, email and notes, which cannot be withdrawn, until the student's or the tutor's purge; the replacement card a purged student's provisional link leaves; blocked accounts; the reverse proxy's access logs, the `ovc-admin` audit trail, the application's diagnostic logs and the lesson presentation on the conferencing server, decided in the pilot deployment plan.
- A lawyer's later answer that changes a decision is recorded as a new decision record that supersedes this one in the parts it changes.
