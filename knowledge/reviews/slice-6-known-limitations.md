# Slice 6 known limitations

Findings recorded during slice 6. Minor findings may be accepted instead of starting another review round ([ADR-0011](../../docs/decisions/0011-tiered-verification.md)). The slice review re-verifies each entry; each is fixed, accepted by the owner or carried into a named later slice.

| Finding | Severity | Change | Why it was accepted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student deletion and final student purge acquire channels per relationship before later groups, lessons and final membership removal, conflicting with another student's globally ordered digest in shared groups | major (reopened as S6-BE-04) | Chat channels and membership | The former minor classification and user/profile serialization rationale are invalid: another student's digest holds neither the deleted student's user row nor its tutors' profiles. The [student deletion lock-order change](slice-6-student-chat-lock-order.md) stabilizes and locks the complete channel set globally before lesson work. Implementation checks and independent closure evidence are tracked there; this entry is not accepted as a residual limitation. |
| Download authorization reads the message reference and the attachment's scope channel, not the message itself: `GET /attachments/{id}` and `GET /files/{id}` rely on binding attaching an upload only to a message of its own scope channel and on deleting or erasing a message removing its references in the same transaction | minor (security) | Chat upload pipeline and downloads | The message table lands with sending and binding, after the download routes; until then no binding exists, so no reference can break either invariant. Carried to the change that adds messages, which must join the message (its `channel_id` equal to the attachment's scope and not deleted) in `GetDownloadableChatAttachment` and `GetServableChatAttachment` and add a test that a reference to a message of another channel is refused. **Resolved** by the chat messages change: both queries join the message in the attachment's scope channel and refuse a deleted or erased one, and `TestADownloadNeedsALiveMessageOfItsOwnChannel` refuses a reference to a message of another channel and to a deleted message on both routes. |
| Two chat uploads completing at the same time can each pass the completion quota check, because each excludes only itself and counts the other at its reserved bytes; the plan's assertion that two racing completions are bounded at binding is not yet tested | minor | Chat upload pipeline and downloads | The completion check is an early refusal by design, and binding under the charge locks is the exact bound, which lands with sending and binding; the racing-completions assertion is carried to that change. **Resolved** by the chat messages change: the subtest "two racing completions are bounded at binding" of `TestPendingUploadsCountTowardTheQuota` completes two uploads at once and races their bindings under the tutor's profile lock, and exactly one binds while the other answers `507 quota_exceeded`. |
| The immediate purge of an unconfirmed account (its owner deleting it) runs no message batches: its messages keep their bodies and their `sender_id` until the erasure job removes them, on a once-per-minute schedule with bounded passes and no fixed physical-retention deadline, while the 30-day purge and the 7-day cleanup erase them in batches before the account's row goes | minor | Chat messages | An unconfirmed account can write only in its provisional direct channel, which the same transaction discards; from that commit no one reaches the channel, and the erasure job deletes every message of it. Erasing up to a week of messages inside the deletion's single transaction would make it unbounded. The owner approved retaining immediate access revocation and deferred batched erasure on 2026-11-01. Backlog, lock contention and storage failures can extend retention; eventual progress requires those failures and blocking transactions to end. The erasure execution deadline covers candidate selection, pool acquisition and transaction locks; physical file deletion remains retryable storage work, and dead jobs require recovery. This replaces the earlier one-minute retention claim; implementation evidence is in [the erasure follow-up](slice-6-erasure-deadline.md). |
| A send flags every active member other than the sender in `digest_pending`, because persisted presence does not exist yet, and it also flags unconfirmed provisional accounts | minor | Chat messages | Until the presence change every member counts as offline, which matches the rule that an account without a presence row is offline, and nothing reads the flags before the digest change. The presence change must flag only members whose `online_until` has passed, and the digest change must skip unconfirmed addresses, so no digest reaches a possibly squatted mailbox ([T-44](../../docs/threat-model.md)). **Partly resolved** by the original presence and typing change (cross-instance correctness remains pending completion evidence): `FlagDigestPending` flags only the active members that have no `user_presence` row whose `online_until` is still in the future, which `TestAClockAdvanceKeepsConnectedAccountsOnline` proves for a connected and an offline member; skipping unconfirmed addresses stays with the digest change. |
| The student purge erases the account's messages, also in live group channels, without message frames | minor | Chat messages | The erased messages show as "Message removed" on the members' next fetch or refetch; a frame per erased message would multiply the purge's notifications by its batch size, and the bodies are already unreachable through REST once each batch commits. |
| A repeated delete of an already deleted message answers `204` and still uses one of the 30 write slots a minute | minor | Chat messages | The repeat is a write request that is answered, not refused, so the rule that refused requests count nothing does not cover it; it changes nothing, and a client repeats a delete only on a retry. |
| Presence `FOR KEY SHARE` user locks can wait for short purge and operator transactions holding `FOR UPDATE` | minor | Chat presence and typing | The owner approved these waits without changing operator or purge locks. User locks precede summary and lease locks; presence takes no relationship or channel locks and uses a five-second admission-and-operation deadline. The replacement `TestPresenceWaitsOnlyForConflictingUserRowLocks` must prove allowed waits and passage through `FOR SHARE` and `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`. **Resolved** by the approved contract and integration evidence: `TestPresenceWaitsOnlyForConflictingUserRowLocks` proves the allowed conflict and passage through the non-conflicting lock modes; `TestPresenceOperationsRespectAdmissionAndShutdownDeadlines` proves bounded admission and shutdown. Operator and purge locks remain unchanged. |

## Approved upload cleanup uncertainty

On 2026-11-01 the owner approved [ADR-0019](../../docs/decisions/0019-durable-upload-cleanup.md): unresolved remote Complete or persistent failures can retain technical intents indefinitely, and finite automatic cleanup does not guarantee physical removal. The 4,096-intent cap can refuse new chat uploads; explicit operator receipts have no total count cap, with indexed lookup and bounded pages. This is the approved design residual, not evidence that S6-BE-03 has been fixed. Implementation readiness and independent verification are recorded in [the upload cleanup change](slice-6-upload-cleanup.md).

### Historical browser diagnostic provenance

The owner approved one changed-source production acceptance attempt on 2026-11-03 while accepting
limited historical cause uncertainty, as bounded in the
[production diagnostic follow-up plan](../plans/slice-6-production-diagnostic-followups.md).

two-tab/proactive extra events and a pilot readiness-fetch access-control event did not recur in the

an evidence limit for progressing to the named checks, not a waiver for new warnings/errors, a claim
that each old producer was proven, or readiness approval. Any new unexplained event still blocks
readiness. Current implementation/check evidence is retained in the
