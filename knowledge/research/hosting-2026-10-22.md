# Hosting in Russia: providers, prices and a recommended setup

Checked 2026-10-22. Prices, limits and certifications are volatile; recheck them in each provider's calculator before ordering. This file is agent research: it informs the owner's choice and decides nothing ([ADR-0008](../../docs/decisions/0008-hosting-and-data-residency.md) owns the constraint that every component handling personal data is hosted in Russia).

## Method and conventions

- Sources are official provider pages and documentation unless marked **third-party**. Facts that could not be confirmed on an official page are marked **unconfirmed**.
- Russian VAT is 22% in 2026: the [Selectel price page](https://selectel.ru/prices/) says all prices include 22% VAT ("Цена всех услуг указана с учетом НДС 22%"). "Incl. VAT" and "excl. VAT" follow each provider's own statement; a figure divided or multiplied by 1.22 is marked as derived.
- A month is 720 hours unless the provider says otherwise (Selectel bills 730 hours).
- Yandex Cloud's Russian pages fill price tables with JavaScript that could not be read. Ruble figures for Yandex Cloud are therefore derived from the USD rows (Russia region, excl. VAT, mostly effective 2026-04-30) in the Markdown documentation at `docs.yandex.cloud/int/en/<service>/pricing.md` as RUB incl. VAT = USD × 122. That factor reproduces every ruble figure the Russian pages state directly (for example Ice Lake vCPU 1.24 ₽/h, RAM 0.33 ₽/GB/h, Object Storage 2.376 ₽/GB-month, public IP 0.26352 ₽/h), but each derived figure is still **derived**.
- Several Selectel and Timeweb Cloud price tables also render only in the browser; their exact configuration prices are **unconfirmed**.

## What OVC needs

From the [non-functional requirements](../../docs/requirements/non-functional.md), the [stack table](../architecture/detailed.md#stack-per-component), the [files](../specs/files.md), [video](../specs/realtime-and-video.md#recordings) and [notifications](../specs/notifications.md#email) specifications and the [BigBlueButton research](bigbluebutton-2026-10-20.md):

- **Application:** the Go API, the worker and a TLS reverse proxy serving the built frontend. The NFR-2 reference deployment is one API and one worker with 2 vCPU and 4 GB RAM each, and PostgreSQL with 2 vCPU and 4 GB RAM on SSD; NFR-3.1 expects that to carry 500 tutors, 5,000 students and 2,000 connected sockets. Matching the reference deployment keeps performance at the original plan.
- **PostgreSQL 18** (`LISTEN/NOTIFY`, `SKIP LOCKED`), point-in-time recovery, and backups kept 30 days. ADR-0008 states "database backups are kept 30 days" under bounded retention; this file reads that as the retention to configure, so a provider must allow 30 days.
- **S3-compatible storage** with SigV4 presigned `GET`, the `response-content-disposition` override, multipart upload with 8 MiB parts, a lifecycle rule that aborts incomplete multipart uploads after one day, private buckets, and an `https` endpoint. NFR-3.2 sets the default quotas as "5 GB of files per tutor, and 50 GB of recordings once recordings land after the MVP".
- **No recordings in the MVP.** The owner moved lesson recordings (FR-5.4 to FR-5.6) after the MVP on 2026-10-22 ([roadmap](../../docs/roadmap.md#after-the-mvp)). MVP rooms are created with `record=false`, and the conferencing host keeps neither recordings nor raw meeting events ([video specification](../specs/realtime-and-video.md#video)). MVP storage is therefore sized for files only; recording figures appear separately as "after the MVP".
- **BigBlueButton** on a dedicated Ubuntu host on a subdomain of the OVC domain, keeping its logs 14 days (ADR-0008).
- **SMTP** with SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the OVC domain, bounce reporting, message logs kept at most 30 days, and a sustained send rate that meets NFR-4.5 (a reminder within 2 minutes while 1,000 other emails are queued).
- No foreign service receives personal data, including monitoring and error tracking.

### Sizing

| Deployment | Proposal | Reason |

### PostgreSQL on a VM instead

A 2 vCPU / 4 GB VM with 40 GB SSD costs roughly 2,700–3,500 ₽ on the clouds above, against 3,300–6,000 ₽ managed. The saving is small, and the operator then owns WAL archiving to object storage (for example with WAL-G or pgBackRest), restore drills, minor-version upgrades and failover. For a single-operator project at the larger sizes the managed service is the better trade; for the small pilot of two tutors the owner chose PostgreSQL on the application VM ([ADR-0013](../../docs/decisions/0013-pilot-deployment.md)). Selectel's fixed 7-day backup window does not reach the 30 days to configure; Timeweb lacks PITR.

**Backup storage at Yandex Cloud** stays within the free allowance (backups up to the cluster's total storage size) at both sizes, by estimate: the pilot database is expected to stay under a few GB, so 30 days of the daily automatic backups Yandex documents (the first and every seventh backup full, the rest incremental, about five full copies over 30 days) fit in the 20 GB of storage; at 200 tutors the two hosts hold 100 GB in total. If backups outgrow it, each extra 10 GB costs ≈22 ₽ a month (derived).

Yandex Cloud documents every feature checked except the `nosniff` header. The [files specification](../specs/files.md#downloads) requires signed storage responses to carry `Content-Type` from detection and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, a T-35 control in the [threat model](../../docs/threat-model.md); no provider page states that its `GET` responses carry `nosniff`. S3-compatible services generally cannot add arbitrary response headers, so whichever provider is chosen, a probe like the one in [local S3 research](local-s3-2026-10-21.md#probe) must check the headers on a presigned `GET` before adoption. **Open question:** if the provider does not send `nosniff`, the files specification needs a decision on how it is delivered, for example a proxy in front of downloads that adds the header. Cloud.ru additionally lacks confirmation of `response-content-disposition` and of the content-type behaviour. Selectel's storage fails the lifecycle requirement.

### Sizing

Quotas are ceilings, so the upper bound is quota × tutors. **In the MVP only the file quota applies:** 5 GB per tutor gives **100 GB for 20 tutors** and **1 TB for 200 tutors**. Egress comes from downloads of library files, chat attachments and answers; assuming about 150 MB downloaded per student a month (an estimate), it is about 30 GB for the pilot and 0.3 TB at 200 tutors. Requests cost a few roubles at both sizes.

**After the MVP**, recordings add 50 GB per tutor: 1 TB for the pilot and 10 TB at 200 tutors, filling over their retention (at most 365 days). Playback then dominates egress; assuming each student watches about two hours of recordings a month at roughly 0.5 GB per hour (an estimate: the rendered format is an item of the [recordings probe after the MVP](bigbluebutton-2026-10-20.md#open-probe-items-for-recordings-after-the-mvp)), that adds about 0.2 TB for the pilot and 2 TB at 200 tutors.

**Dedicated CPU is needed.** The official page asks for it, and audio mixing and WebRTC forwarding suffer from CPU steal. On most clouds a vCPU is one hardware thread, so "8 cores" may mean 16 vCPU; VK Cloud states that one vCPU is one physical core of the virtualization server, which reads as a thread-versus-core claim but not a no-overcommit guarantee (**unconfirmed**). A dedicated server gives real cores without that ambiguity.

### Traffic for about 10 concurrent lessons of 2–5 participants

BigBlueButton forwards every webcam to every other participant without mixing. Planning figures: about 250 kbit/s per received webcam, 400–600 kbit/s at higher quality, and 40–60 kbit/s per audio stream ([a hosting vendor's requirements page](https://support.converia.de/help/en-us/40-bigbluebutton-bbb/173-technical-requirements-for-the-use-of-bigbluebutton-bbb), third-party); the [official FAQ](https://bigbluebutton.com/docs/help/faq/what-are-the-minimum-requirements-for-bigbluebutton) asks clients for 1 Mbit/s up and 2 Mbit/s down and 2 Mbit/s up for screen sharing. The figures below are derived from those planning numbers and must be replaced by a measurement in the slice 7 probe.

- Server egress per lesson with every camera on: n × ((n − 1) × camera + audio), so 0.6–1.3 Mbit/s for 2 participants and 5–12 Mbit/s for 5.
- 10 concurrent lessons: about 6–120 Mbit/s at peak, within the 250 Mbit/s minimum.
- Per lesson-hour: 0.3–0.6 GB for 2 participants, 2.4–5.5 GB for 5.
- Pilot volume, assuming 20 tutors × 15 lesson-hours a week ≈ 1,300 lesson-hours a month at an average of 3 participants: about 1–2 TB a month, up to about 7 TB if every lesson had 5 participants with high-quality cameras.
- 200 tutors, the same assumptions: about 10–22 TB a month, up to about 70 TB; at an evening peak of about 50 concurrent lessons, 30–600 Mbit/s, which needs two hosts.

At Yandex Cloud's ≈1.42 ₽/GB, 1–2 TB costs about 1,300–2,800 ₽ a month and 20 TB about 28,000 ₽. Providers with unmetered traffic avoid this line entirely, which makes them the natural home for BigBlueButton.

- **Data in Russia:** Unisender Go, DashaMail and NotiSend say their servers are in Russia; Postbox runs in a Yandex Cloud Russian region; Mailganer **unconfirmed**. SendPulse was excluded for lack of an official statement on data location.
- **Message log retention at most 30 days** (notifications specification): not stated by any provider except DashaMail's 30-day API window; ask before signing.
- **NFR-4.5:** Unisender Go's per-connection rate (about 50 a minute per connection) lets the worker's 10 email lanes drain 1,000 emails in a few minutes while high-priority reminders go first. Postbox's default 1 a second would need a quota increase.

Budget about 200 ₽ once and up to 3,000 ₽ a year; choose the registrar by its renewal price, not the first-year price.

- **DNS hosting:** Selectel DNS is free with unlimited zones once the account holds a balance ([DNS](https://selectel.ru/services/additional/dns/)); Yandex Cloud DNS ≈43 ₽ per zone a month plus ≈37.94 ₽ per million public queries ([pricing](https://docs.yandex.cloud/int/en/dns/pricing.md), derived); Cloud.ru ≈33 ₽ per zone a month ([tariff](https://cloud.ru/documents/tariffs/evolution/evolution-dns)). Registrar DNS is usually free (**unconfirmed**). The zone holds the SPF, DKIM and DMARC records and the BigBlueButton subdomain.

## 7. Backups, monitoring and personal-data law

### Backups

- **Database:** the managed service's automatic backups and PITR (section 2), with retention set to 30 days where the provider allows it, matching ADR-0008. In the small pilot ([ADR-0013](../../docs/decisions/0013-pilot-deployment.md)), WAL-G on the application VM writes daily base backups and the WAL archive to object storage instead, keeps a 30-day recovery window and deletes anything older, so no backup is more than 31 days old.
- **Object storage:** each provider keeps several copies inside its region; a second copy of files is not required by any OVC document. Versioning would keep deleted objects and break the purge rules, so it stays off.
- **VMs:** stateless (containers built from static binaries and configuration); rebuilding is cheaper than backing them up. Yandex Cloud Backup would cost ≈281 ₽ per VM plus ≈4.97 ₽/GB ([pricing](https://docs.yandex.cloud/int/en/backup/pricing.md), derived) if wanted.
- **BigBlueButton:** no backup; in the MVP it keeps no recordings or meeting events, and after the MVP it holds recordings only until OVC copies them. Its logs are kept 14 days (ADR-0008).

### Monitoring and logs

Foreign SaaS monitoring and error tracking are excluded by ADR-0008. Options in Russia:

- Yandex Cloud Monitoring: platform metrics free; custom metrics ≈0.32 ₽ per million values ([pricing](https://docs.yandex.cloud/int/en/monitoring/pricing.md), derived). Cloud Logging: ≈25.55 ₽/GB written after 5 GB free a month ([pricing](https://docs.yandex.cloud/int/en/logging/pricing.md), derived); the page announces its shutdown in the second quarter of 2027 in favour of a successor service, a migration risk.
- Selectel Logs: currently free with 30-day retention, to become paid later ([logs](https://docs.selectel.ru/logs/about-logs/)); availability monitoring 3 checks free, then 52 ₽ a check ([monitoring](https://selectel.ru/services/additional/monitoring/)).
- VK Cloud monitoring free; logging free during beta ([monitoring](https://cloud.vk.ru/monitoring/), [logging](https://cloud.vk.ru/logging/), **unconfirmed** whether still free).
- Cloud.ru Cloud Logging free with 3-day default retention ([product](https://cloud.ru/products/cloud-logging)).
- Self-hosted Prometheus, Grafana and Loki on a small VM (about 2,700 ₽ for 2 vCPU / 4 GB) keeps everything under the owner's control and is provider-neutral.

For the pilot, the provider's free metrics plus its log service and an external availability check are enough; the [observability specification](../specs/observability.md) governs what is logged.

**Owner steps that are legal acts, not purchases** (a summary for orientation, not legal advice; confirm with a lawyer):

- File the operator notification with Roskomnadzor before processing starts (152-FZ article 22), online through the regulator's portal; late or missing notification is fined under article 13.11 of the Code of Administrative Offences (КоАП) (amounts in 2026 **unconfirmed**).
- Publish the privacy policy and collect consent at sign-up (articles 9 and 18.1); the product already supports consent and deletion (FR-1.7).
- Appoint a person responsible for personal data processing (article 22.1).
- Determine the required protection level under Government Decree 1119 from the data categories, the number of subjects and the threat type, and pick a provider segment certified at least to that level. Ordinary data of fewer than 100,000 subjects usually lands at protection level 3 or 4 (УЗ-3, УЗ-4); lesson recordings with faces and voices, which arrive after the MVP, are biometric data only when used to establish identity (article 11), which OVC does not do. Both points are interpretations to confirm.
- Prepare to report personal data leaks to Roskomnadzor within 24 hours and with results within 72 hours (article 21 part 3.1).
- Keep the data in Russia, which this setup does; no cross-border transfer notification is needed while no foreign service receives personal data.

The application tier, database and storage sit with one provider in one region, so the API, worker, PostgreSQL and S3 talk over the provider's internal network (free and low-latency, preserving the NFR-2 reference assumptions). ## Main risks

- **Small-pilot shortcuts** ([ADR-0013](../../docs/decisions/0013-pilot-deployment.md)): one VM holds the application and PostgreSQL without failover; the operator owns WAL-G backups, their 30-day deletion and restore drills; the backup credentials, the WAL-G encryption key, the Postbox credentials and the Data Streams reader credential are new secrets; backups share the cloud account with the database; the BigBlueButton host runs at the development minimum until the slice 7 probe confirms it.
- **BigBlueButton bandwidth and CPU.** Traffic and CPU per lesson are estimates until the slice 7 probe measures them. On a metered cloud, 20 TB of lesson traffic costs about 28,000 ₽ a month; keep BigBlueButton on unmetered traffic and confirm the vCPU-to-core mapping, or use a dedicated server.
- **Egress pricing.** MVP storage egress is small (file downloads only); it grows with recording playback after the MVP. Cloud.ru's 10 TB free egress is a promotion that ends 2026-12-31 unless extended, and Cloud.ru VM traffic is only temporarily free.
- **Unconfirmed list prices.** Selectel and Timeweb configuration prices and the Selectel dedicated server price need a calculator check; the Yandex figures are derived from USD rows.
- **Lock-in.** S3, PostgreSQL and SMTP are standard protocols, so moving providers means copying data, not rewriting code. The provider-specific parts are the managed database's backups (restore needs a logical dump to move) and any provider log or monitoring service; Yandex Cloud Logging's announced shutdown in 2027 is an example.
- **Storage features.** No provider documents `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` on `GET`, a T-35 control the files specification requires; probe the chosen provider before adoption, and if the header is missing, decide how the files specification delivers it (for example a download proxy). Selectel S3 also lacks lifecycle rules and may not honour `response-content-disposition`; Cloud.ru and VK Cloud need a probe of every feature in section 3.
- **Conferencing data residency.** Whether the BigBlueButton client loads external STUN servers or other foreign hosts is a [slice 7 probe item](bigbluebutton-2026-10-20.md#open-probe-items-for-slice-7); a foreign STUN server would see participants' IP addresses, so the host may need its own STUN and TURN configuration.
- **Email quotas and deliverability.** New accounts start with low daily caps (Unisender Go 500 a day on the trial, Postbox 200 a day); raise them before inviting users, and warm up the sending domain.
- **Backup window.** Selectel's managed PostgreSQL keeps backups only 7 days, short of the 30 days this file reads ADR-0008 as requiring; Yandex Cloud and Cloud.ru allow 30.
