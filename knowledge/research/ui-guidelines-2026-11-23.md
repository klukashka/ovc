## 1. Principles

### 1.1 Action hierarchy

- **One primary action per view.** GOV.UK says to use its default (primary) button for the page's main call to action and to avoid several, because competing main actions make the next step unclear ([GOV.UK Button](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)). USWDS says to give the most important action a distinct look and to avoid too many buttons on one page ([USWDS Button](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/button/)). Android also allows only one action at a time at the highest level of importance, the FAB ([Android layout and navigation patterns](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/layout-and-nav-patterns)). The von Restorff effect says the one item that differs is the one remembered ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/), **third-party**, a summary site by Jon Yablonski).
- **Secondary and rare actions.** Android puts secondary actions in the top bar or next to the content they act on, and actions that are not needed often in an overflow menu ([Android patterns](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/layout-and-nav-patterns)). NN/g found that actions hidden in contextual menus get used less, and that menu triggers need a visible, prominent icon such as a kebab ([NN/g contextual menus](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/contextual-menus-guidelines/)).
- **Where the primary action goes on a page or form.** GOV.UK aligns the primary button to the left edge of the form, and its question pages put "Continue" on the left "so users don't miss it" ([GOV.UK Button](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/), [GOV.UK Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)). USWDS button groups sit on the left on large screens and stack vertically on phones, first source button on top ([USWDS Button group](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/button-group/)). Design systems such as Atlassian's align the primary button with the left edge of the fields (**third-party**, [Atlassian forms](https://atlassian.design/patterns/forms)).
- **Where the primary action goes in a dialog.** Apple puts the most likely button and the default button on the trailing side of a row, with Cancel on the leading side ([HIG Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts)). Material Components for Android end-aligns dialog buttons and adds them in the order neutral, negative, positive, so the confirming action ends up last ([material-components-android Dialog.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/Dialog.md)). USWDS examples put the primary action first as a button and the secondary action second as unstyled text ([USWDS Modal](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/modal/)). Section 2 covers the disagreement.
- **Destructive actions.** Keep consequential actions away from frequent, harmless ones, and use space and more than one visual cue (colour, icon, position) to tell them apart ([NN/g proximity of consequential options](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/proximity-consequential-options/), 2021). GOV.UK keeps its warning button for serious actions that are hard to undo. It recommends a normal button to start the action and the warning button only for the final confirmation, and never colour alone to signal the consequence ([GOV.UK Button](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)). Apple uses the destructive style only when people did not deliberately choose the destructive action ([HIG Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts)).
- **Labels.** Name buttons by their result ("Delete file", "Keep file"), not "OK" or "Yes/No" ([NN/g OK–Cancel](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ok-cancel-or-cancel-ok/), 2008; [NN/g confirmation dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/), reviewed 2026). Start with a verb and use sentence case ([USWDS Button](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/button/), [GOV.UK Button](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)).
- **Disabled buttons.** GOV.UK advises against them because of poor contrast and confusion ([GOV.UK Button](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)). USWDS says `aria-disabled="true"` keeps a button in the tab order, the application must block activation, and the interface should "explain why an action is unavailable and how to make it available" ([USWDS Button](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/button/)).
- **Cancel in forms.** Use it sparingly. People leave with Back, and a second button at the bottom of a form makes the next step harder to see ([NN/g Reset and Cancel](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/reset-and-cancel-buttons/), 2000).

### 1.2 Information hierarchy and scanning

- When nothing on the page guides the eye, people scan in an F shape and miss content on the right and lower down. Counter it with front-loaded headings, bullets and grouping ([NN/g F-pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/), reviewed 2026-08-19).
- Hierarchy comes from contrast, scale and grouping. NN/g suggests at most 3 type sizes, at most 2 large elements, at most 3 contrast variations, borders and backgrounds used sparingly, and a "squint test" ([NN/g visual hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/), 2021).
- Items close together read as a group (proximity), and so do items inside one boundary (common region) ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/), **third-party**).
- Recognition rather than recall, minimalist design, and consistency and standards are three of the ten heuristics ([NN/g heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), reviewed 2024-01-30).

### 1.3 Navigation

- **Desktop:** do not hide navigation. Show the top-level links across the top or down the left side. In NN/g's study, hidden navigation was used in 27 % of desktop cases against 48–50 % for visible or combined navigation, and desktop tasks took at least 39 % longer ([NN/g hidden navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/), 2016).
- **Phones:** show up to 4 top-level links. With more than 4, hide some, accept the cost, and add in-page links to the hidden destinations. Combined navigation (some links visible, the rest in a menu) was used in 86 % of mobile cases, hidden navigation in 57 % ([NN/g hidden navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). A tab bar suits a small number of options and becomes cramped above 5 ([NN/g mobile navigation patterns](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-navigation-patterns/), 2015).
- **Bottom bar size:** 3 to 5 destinations on the same level. On large screens it becomes a rail; do not use a bottom bar there ([Android patterns](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/layout-and-nav-patterns)). Apple suggests a default of five or fewer tabs, labels with single words where possible, badges only for critical information, and no actions in the bar ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)).
- **The More item:** Apple says "The More tab makes it harder for people to reach and notice content on tabs that are hidden", so put the important sections in the visible tabs ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)).
- **Keep the bar visible** across sections. Hiding it makes people "forget which area of the app they're in". The only exception Apple names is a modal view ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). Do not disable or hide tab items. When a section is empty, say why ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)).
- **Jakob's law:** people expect a product to work like the others they already use ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/), **third-party**). Android makes the same argument for common navigation pairings ([Android patterns](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/layout-and-nav-patterns)).

### 1.4 Forms

- **Labels:** keep visible labels and put hints outside the field. Placeholders must not replace labels ([NN/g placeholders](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/), reviewed 2018). Mark optional fields "(optional)", not with asterisks, and keep hints to one short sentence ([GOV.UK Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)).
- **One column:** multi-column forms led to skipped fields and errors. Exceptions are closely related fields on one line (date parts, a name in parts, city and postal code), and only inside an otherwise single-column form ([Baymard multi-column forms](https://baymard.com/blog/avoid-multi-column-forms), 2023-10-31). GOV.UK uses one thing per page and a two-thirds-width form column ([GOV.UK Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)).
- **Inline validation:** validate when the person leaves the field, not while they type. Remove the error as soon as the input becomes valid, and confirm success on complex fields ([Baymard inline validation](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation), 2024-01-09). Show the message next to the field, in colour and with an icon, and do not use tooltips for errors ([NN/g form errors](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/errors-forms-design-guidelines/), reviewed 2024-12-12).
- **Error summary:** after a submit, show a summary at the top of `main` (below the back link, above the `h1`), headed "There is a problem", and move focus to it. List every error as a link to its field, in the same words as the inline message, and prefix the page title with "Error:" ([GOV.UK Error summary](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-summary/)). The summary supplements inline messages and never replaces them ([NN/g form errors](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/errors-forms-design-guidelines/)).
- **Error wording:** say what went wrong and how to fix it. Do not clear what the person typed. Problems the person cannot fix (permission, eligibility) are not field errors ([GOV.UK Error message](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/)).

### 1.5 Lists and tables on phones

- Keep only as many columns as stay legible without zooming. Lock the row-header column if the table scrolls sideways, keep headers sticky on tables longer than one screen, and let people filter before they compare ([NN/g mobile tables](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tables/), 2017).
- Under WCAG 1.4.10 a data table as a whole may scroll in two dimensions, but its cells, its heading, its search and its pagination must reflow at 320 CSS px ([WCAG 2.2 Understanding Reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)).

### 1.6 Empty, loading and error states

- **Empty:** never leave a container blank. Say what is going on, teach what the area is for, and give a direct link or button that fills it. Never show "no records" while data is still loading ([NN/g empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/), 2021).
- **Loading:** show nothing under 1 s. From 1 to 10 s, use a skeleton for a whole page or a spinner for one module. Above 10 s, use a progress bar with an estimate. Use progress bars for uploads ([NN/g skeleton screens](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/), reviewed 2026-09-02). Responses within about 400 ms keep people productive (the Doherty threshold, [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/), **third-party**).
- **Errors:** show form errors in context, not in a modal ([NN/g modal vs nonmodal](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/), 2017). Toasts suit passive information but are "a poor fit for errors": one participant missed an error toast and waited five minutes ([NN/g indicators, validations, notifications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications/), 2024).

### 1.7 Dialogs, pages and sheets

- **Modal dialogs** are for critical warnings, for information needed to continue a task the person started, and for short focused steps. They are not for errors that can be fixed in context, and not for complex decisions that need information the dialog hides ([NN/g modal vs nonmodal](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/)). USWDS calls modals "a last resort" and sends multi-step flows and long forms to their own pages, because long content pushes the buttons out of view on phones ([USWDS Modal](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/modal/)). Dialogs are "purposefully interruptive, so they should be used sparingly", and full-screen dialogs are for tasks with a series of steps ([material-components-android Dialog.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/Dialog.md)).
- **Sheets** are for short, scoped tasks or quick controls, not for navigation or for content that is always needed. Give them a visible Close button, support Back, and never stack them ([NN/g bottom sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/), 2023; [HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)). NN/g calls the idea that sheets improve thumb reach "largely incorrect" ([NN/g bottom sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)). In Apple's sheets, Cancel is leading and Done trailing in the top toolbar, and Done is always paired with Cancel or Back ([HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)).
- **Pages** are stable destinations. Do not turn page-to-page flows into sheets or dialogs ([NN/g bottom sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)). GOV.UK puts a Back link at the top of every question page ([GOV.UK Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)).

### 1.8 Touch targets and spacing

- **WCAG 2.5.8 (AA):** at least 24 × 24 CSS px, or enough spacing that a 24 px circle around each target touches no other target. Inline links in text are exempt. WCAG 2.5.5 (Enhanced) asks for 44 × 44 CSS px and is recommended for important controls ([WCAG 2.2 Understanding Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)).
- **Platforms:** Apple's default control size on iOS is 44 × 44 pt and its minimum 28 × 28 pt. Apple asks for about 12 pt of padding around controls with a visible boundary and about 24 pt around controls without one ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)). Android recommends at least 48 × 48 dp for touch and allows smaller targets for mouse and trackpad ([Android accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)).
- **Focus not obscured (WCAG 2.4.11, AA):** sticky headers, footers and banners must never fully cover the focused element. CSS `scroll-padding` is a sufficient technique ([WCAG 2.2 Understanding Focus Not Obscured](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)).
- **Fitts's law:** larger and closer targets are faster to reach ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/), **third-party**).

### 1.9 Typography

- Body text reads best at 50–75 characters per line, and no more than 80, matching WCAG 1.4.8 ([Baymard line length](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability), 2022).
- Apple's default text size on iOS is 17 pt, with a minimum of 11 pt. Apple wants text that can grow to 200 % and warns that thin weights are harder to read ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)).
- NN/g suggests at most 3 type sizes for a clear hierarchy ([NN/g visual hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)).

### 1.10 Feedback and confirmations

- **Visibility of system status** is the first heuristic ([NN/g heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)).
- **Confirm only serious, hard-to-undo actions.** Routine confirmations train people to click through. Restate what will happen and name the item, label buttons by their outcome, and avoid a default "Yes". Prefer undo where possible ([NN/g confirmation dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)). Never make Cancel the default button. If the alert should be read, make no button the default ([HIG Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts)).
- **Notifications:** match them to urgency. An action-required notification is intrusive. A passive one (a badge or toast) is easy to miss ([NN/g indicators, validations, notifications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications/)).
- **Double submits:** guard on the server and show progress on slow connections ([GOV.UK Button](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)).

### 1.11 Consistency

- Consistency and standards (heuristic 4, [NN/g heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)). Inconsistency with what people expect costs more than a slightly better layout saves ([NN/g OK–Cancel](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ok-cancel-or-cancel-ok/)). Apple calls a tab bar whose items come and go "unstable and unpredictable" ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)).

## 3. Gap map for OVC

Legend: **follows** = the current design matches the principle. **gap** = it departs from it or says nothing about it. Each gap is a candidate for the rework, not a finding of a review.

### 3.1 Frame and navigation (`navigation.md` § Frames, L10–35)

- **Follows:** a visible left sidebar on desktop with labels and counts (L10–16, [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). A bottom bar of 4 links plus More, with labels that never truncate, an `aria-current` item and counts as badges (L29–30). `scroll-padding-bottom` keeps focus clear of the bar (L33, WCAG 2.4.11 C43). The sheet is a native modal with a "Close" button, Esc and a tap on the scrim (L31, [NN/g sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)). Unavailable items are hidden, never greyed (L32).
- **Gap G1, the More sheet mixes destinations and account actions:** it holds sections, Notifications, Settings and Sign out (L31). Apple's guidance is to keep important sections out of More ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). For a tutor, Students and Groups are core objects and sit behind More, while Schedule and Today both stay visible. Check with real use which four a tutor needs most. Students is reachable from Today and lessons, which is the in-page compensation NN/g asks for. Make sure every tutor page links to it.
- **Gap G2, the bottom bar is hidden in a conversation** (L33, `pages.md` L426). This is acceptable under §2, but the Back control is then the only way out. Keep its 44 px target and its visible label or name (already specified).
- **Gap G3, the bar's contents change for unconfirmed students** (L32). Apple advises against items that come and go ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). OVC hides them because the API refuses them, which matches AGENTS.md. The design could explain the missing sections on Home ("Confirm your email to see Schedule and Homework") instead of letting items appear without notice.
- **Gap G4, the Notifications button differs by width:** on desktop the sidebar item opens a panel, in the sidebar overlay it is a link, and on phones the top bar opens the panel while the sheet row links to the page (L13, `pages.md` L460, L470). These are three behaviours for one item. Consider one rule per frame and state it once.

### 3.2 Page header and action hierarchy (`pages.md` § Shared behavior, Desktop layout, L620–625; `tokens.md` L10, L115, L121–123)

- **Follows:** one accent button per view (`tokens.md` L10). The header holds the main action on the right and a menu for rare actions (L621). Dangerous actions sit in a menu with a confirmation (L625, [NN/g proximity](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/proximity-consequential-options/)). Destructive confirmations open on Cancel (L637), which avoids a default "Yes" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)).
- **Gap G5, no written rule for the order or look of dialog buttons.** `tokens.md` § Components defines primary, quiet and text buttons, but no danger button and no order. The code has `dangerButtonClass` (`frontend/src/components/ui/Form.tsx` L15) and puts primary or danger first (`LessonDialogs.tsx` L516–529). This is a spec–code gap. Write down the order (§2) and the danger style, with a word on the button that names the consequence and no reliance on colour alone (GOV.UK).
- **Gap G6, primary actions sit far from their content on wide screens.** The header puts the main action at the far right of a 1120 px content area (`tokens.md` L42), while lists and forms start on the left. That is a long reach for the pointer and outside the F-scan ([NN/g F-pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/), Fitts). It is a common convention, so keep it for page-level actions. For actions inside a section (for example "Add students" or "Open review queue"), place them next to the section heading or under the list.
- **Gap G7, Today can show several accent buttons at once:** a Join for each lesson inside its window (L89), plus Accept on each join request (L77) if Accept is styled as primary (this draft did not check). Make sure only the next lesson's Join is filled and the rest are quiet.
- **Gap G8, actions are placed differently on phones.** On the lesson page the actions row comes before the back link (L196). On the builder and the review screen the heading comes before the back link (L336, L381). GOV.UK puts Back at the top, above the heading ([GOV.UK Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)). The owner kept the code order on 2026-11-22. The rework could revisit it, because the back link is the most consistent orientation cue on detail pages.

### 3.3 Forms and validation (`states.md` § Feedback messages, L473–481; Sign up L24–30; New lesson dialog L123–131; Issue dialog L338–357)

- **Follows:** validation appears after the first blur or submit, below the field, in `danger` with an icon (`states.md`, [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/errors-forms-design-guidelines/)). Field refusals move focus to the field (L351). Inputs are 16 px so iOS does not zoom (`tokens.md` L28). Rare fields are collapsed ("Add description", L128).
- **Gap G9, no linked error summary.** Sign up shows the generic alert "Check the highlighted fields" (L26). There is no list of problems linked to fields, no "Error:" title prefix and no rule that the summary repeats the inline wording ([GOV.UK Error summary](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-summary/)). Add one shared pattern for forms with more than about three fields.
- **Gap G10, nothing says when an error goes away.** No rule removes an error as soon as the field becomes valid ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)).
- **Gap G11, how optional fields and hints are marked is not specified** ("(optional)" rather than an asterisk, hints outside the field, never placeholder-only labels; [GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/)).
- **Gap G12, a disabled resend button.** "Check your email" disables resend for 60 seconds (L27). Follow USWDS: keep it focusable with `aria-disabled` (the shared busy rule already does this, L583) and say when it becomes available ("Send again in 42 s").

### 3.4 Dialogs, pages and sheets (`pages.md` L632, L637; Builder L332; Issue dialog L338)

- **Follows:** one sheet only, never stacked. Dialogs trap focus and return it (L637).
- **Gap G13, long tasks in dialogs.** The New lesson dialog (audience search, title, date, time, duration, weekly repeat, description, materials) and the Issue dialog (template, audience, lesson, deadline, visibility) are long, multi-part forms. NN/g, USWDS and Material all point long or multi-step tasks to a page or a full-screen view ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/), [USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/modal/), [Dialog.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/Dialog.md)). On phones they already fill the screen (L117, L357). The rework could make them routes on every width, which would also give Back a meaning.
- **Gap G14, full-screen dialogs on phones have no close control at the top** ("phones add no close control", L632). Their actions sit at the end, below a long form. NN/g wants a visible Close on sheets, and USWDS warns that scrolling pushes buttons out of view. Consider a top row with the title and Close (leading), like Apple's sheet toolbar ([HIG Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)). Keep the commit button at the end of the form.

### 3.5 Lists and tables (`tokens.md` L116, L120; `pages.md` L631)

- **Follows:** semantic tables with a row header, row actions in the last cell, no links nesting buttons (`tokens.md` L116). On phones, the row header plus at most one column, with hidden values under the name (L631). This matches [NN/g mobile tables](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tables/) and keeps cells reflowing (WCAG 1.4.10). Filter rows wrap rather than scroll (L633).
- **Gap G15, row actions behind a kebab only.** Library rows keep every action in the last-cell menu (L291). NN/g says hidden menus reduce use. Keep the most frequent row action visible (for example "Open" or "Issue assignment" on a ready template) and the rest in the menu.
- **Gap G16, a message menu by long press on touch** (L418). NN/g says gestures are not discoverable and need a visible alternative ([NN/g contextual menus](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/contextual-menus-guidelines/)). Add a visible "More actions" button on each own message on touch screens, or after the person taps a message.

### 3.6 Empty, loading and error states (`pages.md` L581–606; `tokens.md` L133)

- **Follows:** skeletons appear only after 300 ms (L582, [NN/g skeletons](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)). Uploads show progress (L294, L420). Empty states have a heading, a sentence and an action (`tokens.md` L133), and most pages define one (Today L73, Students L212, Library L301). A failed refetch keeps the data and offers Retry (L599).
- **Gap G17, error toasts.** "failed actions show an error toast with Retry" (L601). `states.md` makes error toasts persist until dismissed, which reduces but does not remove NN/g's concern ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications/)). Many pages already show refusals inline. Make inline the default and keep toasts for results only.
- **Gap G18, empty states without a next step:** the review queue ("Nothing to check") and the student homework tabs have no action (L591, L594). NN/g asks for a learning cue or pathway. For example, "Nothing to check. Submitted work appears here." with a link to issued work, and for a student "Nothing to do" with a link to the schedule or the last checked work.

### 3.7 Targets, spacing and typography (`tokens.md` L20–44)

- **Follows:** 44 px targets below 1024 px or with a coarse pointer, and at least 24 px everywhere (L41; WCAG 2.5.8 and 2.5.5). A 4 px spacing scale (L38). Every text colour pair meets AA (L88).
- **Gap G19, the reading width is too wide for body text.** The reading width is 720 px with 15 px Golos Text (L27, L42). By estimate (not measured) that is about 90–100 characters per line, above the 50–75 (at most 80) of [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability). Cap running text (descriptions, feedback, bios, assignment instructions) at about 65–70 `ch`, not 720 px.
- **Gap G20, many type sizes.** There are 8 sizes from 12 to 36 px (L22–32), against NN/g's suggestion of about 3 for hierarchy. Most are small steps (12/13/14/15/16). Consider merging `text-label`/`text-caption` and `text-small`/`text-body` on desktop in the rework.
- **Gap G21, small desktop body text.** Body text is 15 px on computers and 12 px for captions. Apple's iOS default is 17 pt, though OVC uses 16 px on phones. This is not a violation, but check readability for tutors over 50 (an assumption about the users, not data).

### 3.8 Feedback and confirmations (`states.md` L473–481; `pages.md` L79, L371, L533)

- **Follows:** confirmations only for destructive actions, naming the consequence ("Students no longer see it… This cannot be undone.", L371), with outcome labels ("Withdraw", "End", "Stop studying"). A decline-and-block has no dialog because it can be undone in Settings (L79), which matches NN/g's preference for undo. Submit confirmations list the unanswered questions (L533). Notices that report a result take focus (L641).
- **Gap G22, no undo for other reversible actions.** Removing a group member, archiving a group and moving a library item could offer "Undo" in the result notice instead of only a notice ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)).

### 3.9 Consistency

- **Gap G23, three patterns for one thing.** Tabs are sometimes ARIA tabs and sometimes links (`tokens.md` L124). Filters are a segmented control on student pages and a select on tutor pages (L125, L204). Back links sit in different places (G8). State one rule per concept in `tokens.md` and apply it across both roles.

## 4. Reviewer checklist for each page in the rework

1. Is there exactly one filled primary button in the view, and is it the action most people come for?
2. Are rare actions in one overflow menu, and is the most frequent row or section action visible rather than hidden?
3. Is every destructive action away from frequent ones, labelled by its outcome, confirmed only when it cannot be undone, with the dialog opening on Cancel?
4. Do the dialog buttons follow the one documented order, and on phones do they stack with the primary on top?
5. Do the heading and the first lines of every section start with the words that matter (F-scan), and do grouping and spacing show what belongs together?
6. On phones, can the page be reached from the bottom bar or from a visible in-page link, without opening More?
7. Do forms use one column (except for related field groups), visible labels, "(optional)" marking and hints outside the field?
8. Does validation run on blur, clear as soon as the input is valid, and does a submit with errors show a linked summary that takes focus?
9. Does each list keep the row header and at most one more column at 360 px, with no sideways scrolling of the page?
10. Does every list or panel have an empty state with a sentence and a next step, and a load failure inside the page with Retry?
11. Are errors shown inline (not only in a toast), with what went wrong and how to fix it, and is the typed input kept?
12. Is each long or multi-step task a page or full-screen view with a clear way back, rather than a dialog?
13. Is every target at least 44 × 44 px under a coarse pointer and at least 24 × 24 px everywhere, with space between neighbours?
14. Is a focused element never fully hidden under the top bar, bottom bar, banners or toasts (WCAG 2.4.11)?
15. Does running text stay within about 65–75 characters per line, and does the page use the shared type tokens only?
16. Does the page work at 320 px width and at 200 % text size without loss of content or two-dimensional scrolling (WCAG 1.4.10, 1.4.4)?
17. Do both roles use the same pattern for the same concept (tabs, filters, back links, notices)?
