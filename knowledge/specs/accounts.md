# Accounts, relationships and groups specification

Behavior for FR-1, FR-2, FR-3 and FR-16. Decisions: [ADR-0005](../../docs/decisions/0005-tutor-centric-domain.md), [ADR-0014](../../docs/decisions/0014-students-join-through-their-tutor.md). Entities are in the [data model](data-model.md); routes and error codes are in the [API](api.md#accounts).

## Roles and accounts

- An account has one role, `tutor` or `student`, fixed at creation. The operator is not an account role and has no HTTP surface; operator actions exist only in the local `ovc-admin` command.
- **Account states:**
  - `unverified`: email not confirmed;
  - `active`;
  - `blocked` by the operator, remembering the state it was blocked from;
  - `deleted`: pending purge.
- **Routes an `unverified` account may use** are marked `unverified` in the [API](api.md#accounts):
  - sign-in, refresh, sign out and sign out everywhere;
  - confirm email and resend confirmation; password reset and change;
  - `GET /users/me`, `PATCH /users/me` (name, avatar, language, timezone, theme), email preferences, `GET /home`, notifications and marking them read, the WebSocket (`GET /ws`), avatar uploads;
  - account deletion;
  - for tutors also: tutor profile and settings, reading the tutor code, the library, and library and prompt-image uploads;
  - for students also: My tutors (`GET /tutors`) and ending a relationship there, entering a tutor code and the list of sent requests, and accepting a first invitation while signed in when the account holds no provisional relationship; from the slices that add them, the one-to-one lessons (from slice 5, `GET /calendar` and `GET /lessons/{id}`, which write nothing), the private chat and the student's own work of a provisional relationship, never a group's. From slice 6 the chat routes (`GET /channels`, `GET /channels/unread`, `GET /channels/{id}`, its messages, sending, editing and deleting messages, the read marker) and `GET /attachments/{id}` are `user, unverified ok`, and the only channel in an unconfirmed student's scope is the provisional link's direct channel; chat uploads are for confirmed accounts only, so an unconfirmed account sends text only (the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review).

  Every other route answers `403 email_not_verified`.
- **Cleanup:** an account that stays unverified for 7 days is removed at once by the cleanup job, applying the [purge rules](data-model.md#purge) immediately; its provisional link gets the [removal effects](#provisional-links-and-reclaim), silently, so its tutor gets a new card, and its held requests are deleted with it. Its email is free again.
- **A student with no active relationship** sees "Enter your tutor's code" on Home, or "Request sent. Waiting for the tutor." while a request waits.
- **On every authenticated request**, and on a WebSocket at `auth` and then for its inbound frames at most once per second per socket ([authentication and revocation](realtime-and-video.md#authentication-and-revocation)), the server checks that:
  - the access token's session (`sid`) is not revoked;
  - the account is `active`, or `unverified` for the routes above.

  This is one indexed lookup per request.
- **Closing live connections:** every path that revokes a session or ends an account signals a `recheck` for the account inside its own transaction, through database triggers on the session and user rows ([revocation triggers](data-model.md#revocation-triggers)), so a path added later signals too. The paths today are sign-out, sign out everywhere, refresh-token reuse, the same-browser confirmation (the other sessions), a reclaim (every session, the squatter's included), a password reset, a password change (the other sessions), account deletion, an operator block through `ovc-admin`, the 7-day cleanup of an unconfirmed account and the purge. The affected WebSocket connections close with code `4401` within 5 seconds while the listener is alive, also when the revocation commits while the socket is still authenticating; a listener that is lost closes every socket of its instance with `1012`. A socket also closes with `4401` 30 seconds after its access token expires unless the client sent a fresh one.
- **Narrowing, not closing, on lost scope:** ending a relationship, removing a provisional or reclaim-pending student, dropping a reclaim link, and the endings of a deletion close no socket. Their transactions decide the recipients of every frame from relationship rows held under locks that conflict with ending, removal and dropping, so nothing about the relationship reaches a party who has lost it, and both parties get `entity.changed` when what they see changes ([authorization of frames](realtime-and-video.md#authorization-of-frames)).

## Registration and email verification

- **Sign-up form:**
  - name, email, password (10 to 128 characters), language, and a consent checkbox for personal-data processing; the consent version and time are recorded;
  - the browser's IANA timezone is sent automatically and stored as the account timezone;
  - a student sign-up outside an invitation carries a tutor code, which is required.
- **Student sign-up** happens only through a tutor ([ADR-0014](../../docs/decisions/0014-students-join-through-their-tutor.md)): on the invitation page ([invitations](#invitations)), or with a tutor code. `role: student` without `tutor_code` is refused with `422 validation_failed` (`body.tutor_code`: `required`), and an unknown, regenerated or inactive code with `422 tutor_code_invalid`, which creates nothing; `role: tutor` with a `tutor_code` is refused with `body.tutor_code`: `not_allowed`. The checks run in this order: the per-IP request slot, the code (a missing code is reported here, before any other field), then validation and password hashing. For a valid code, a new and a taken email both hash the password and get the same `202`; a new account gets a [held join request](#tutor-code-and-join-requests) to the code's tutor, and a taken email changes nothing beyond the duplicate notice. The consent version is the constant `ConsentVersion` in `internal/domain`, changed in code when the privacy policy changes. A browser timezone that is not a known IANA name in canonical form (letters, digits, `_`, `+` and `-`, at most four `/`-separated segments, at most 64 bytes; no `.` segments; not `Local`, `Factory`, `localtime` or `posixrules`, and not under the `posix/` or `right/` trees) is stored as `UTC`, which the person can correct in settings. The server resolves names against the host's zone database, so on a case-insensitive development filesystem a wrongly cased name such as `europe/moscow` may still be accepted; production hosts are case-sensitive. Passwords are compared byte for byte, without Unicode normalisation, and their length is counted in characters.
- **Response:** `POST /auth/register` always answers `202 Accepted` with no session, so a new and an already-used email look identical. The client shows "Check your email"; the person can sign in at once, as an unverified account, or wait for the confirmation link.
- **Duplicate email:**
  - registering with an email that already has an account, including a `deleted` account before purge, does not reveal that fact;
  - the response is the same as a successful sign-up;
  - the existing account's owner receives at most one "someone tried to sign up with your email" message per day.
- **Confirmation tokens:**
  - the worker mints a 32-byte random token when it sends the confirmation email, and commits its hash before sending;
  - valid for 24 hours, single use;
  - each send invalidates earlier unused tokens of the same purpose.
- **Sending limits:**
  - confirmation and reset emails together: at most one per 60 seconds, five per hour and ten per day per account;
  - sign-up, password-reset and resend requests: at most 10 per hour per IP address, counted together; the eleventh answers `429` with `Retry-After`;
  - the per-account limits are counted per normalised email when a request is queued, for an unknown email as for a known one, so both do the same counting work: a sign-up counts as the first confirmation, and a sign-up over a limit (for example right after a reset request for the same email) still creates the account and answers `202`, only without queuing the confirmation, which the person can resend later; on the public routes (forgot, and resend with an email) a request over a per-account limit is silently dropped and answers `202` like any other, and only the signed-in resend answers `429`;
  - resend and forgot send nothing for an unknown email, resend sends nothing to a confirmed account, and forgot sends nothing to a blocked or deleted account.
- **Confirming an email** (`POST /auth/verify-email`):
  - from a browser signed in to that account, it activates the account, revokes every other session, makes its provisional relationship `active` and releases its held join requests;
  - from any other browser, the page asks the person to set a new password. Confirming then sets that password, activates the account, revokes every session, signs this browser in, and is a [reclaim](#provisional-links-and-reclaim). Whoever created a squatting account therefore loses it, because the mailbox owner chooses the password and decides about every link and request it made.
- **Tokens are bound to the account state:** confirming accepts only an `unverified` account and resetting only an `unverified` or `active` one; every other state, like an unknown, expired, used or superseded token, answers the same `422 token_invalid`. A token is consumed by one conditional update while the account row is locked, so the account cannot change state in between. Confirmation, reset and the password change of an active account discard every unused token of both purposes and every queued request that has not used a token, so an email queued before the change is dropped at dispatch. A password change of an unverified account keeps them: whoever signed up with someone else's email knows the password, and must not be able to cancel the mailbox owner's links. The worker mints a token in one transaction that locks the account row and checks again that the account is in a state the token applies to, that the request still exists, has not used a token and is the newest of its purpose.
- **Completing a password reset** also confirms an unverified email, because it proves access to the mailbox; a reset of an `unverified` account is a [reclaim](#provisional-links-and-reclaim). A reset of an `active` account changes no relationship or request, because such an account has no provisional link or held request.
- **The outcome:** confirmation and reset return one `outcome`, chosen by precedence: `choice_required` when any item (a link or a request) waits for the owner's choice after a reclaim; otherwise `linked` when a provisional relationship became `active`; otherwise `request_sent` when a held request was released, visible or suppressed alike; otherwise `code_invalid` when a held request expired at release because its tutor is no longer `active`; otherwise `null`. A reset answers only `choice_required` or `null`.
- **Sign-up through an invitation** is described under [invitations](#invitations).

## Sessions and passwords

- **Access token:** short-lived (15 minutes), carrying `sid`. The frontend keeps it in memory only, never in Web Storage or cookies.
- **Refresh token:**
  - rotating, valid for 30 days;
  - held in a cookie named `__Host-ovc_refresh` with `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Strict` and `Path=/`;
  - reuse of a rotated refresh token revokes the session;
  - its value is `<session>.<generation>.<random>.<mac>`: the session identifier (16 bytes), the decimal generation (from 1, no sign or leading zero), 32 random bytes and an untruncated HMAC-SHA-256 over the session identifier and generation with a key derived by HKDF from `OVC_JWT_SECRET` (info `refresh-generation`), each binary part in unpadded base64url and parsed strictly;
  - the MAC is verified before the session is looked up, so a forged cookie costs no query and changes nothing; a cookie with a valid MAC and an older generation than the stored one is reuse and revokes the session; a newer generation, or the current generation with another random part, is refused without revoking; a refresh whose rotation loses a race is re-checked against the stored row by the same rules, so the losing request counts as reuse, which the browser's per-tab lock keeps from happening;
  - the session expires 30 days after sign-in; rotation never extends it;
  - a session row is deleted by the hourly `sessions.cleanup` job once it has been expired or revoked for more than a day, after which its cookie is refused as unknown, as a revoked one is; email requests and their tokens are deleted 7 days after the request, and a token never outlives its request.
- **Refresh route checks:**
  - it accepts a request only when it carries exactly one `Origin` header and that header equals the configured OVC origin exactly; a same-site subdomain such as the conferencing host does not qualify. Sign-in, the confirmation route and the WebSocket upgrade apply the same comparison, one plain `net/http` function over every `Origin` value, so each of them also refuses a request with two `Origin` headers, one of them correct;
  - a request without a refresh cookie gets `204` with no token, which the client treats as "signed out" rather than as an error;
  - it is the only route authenticated by a cookie;
  - the conferencing origin is never in the CORS allowlist.
- **Sign out** revokes the current session; **sign out everywhere** revokes all of the user's sessions.
- **Throttling:** more than 5 failed sign-ins for one email within 15 minutes, or more than 20 from one IP address (an IPv4-mapped IPv6 address counts as its IPv4 address and an IPv6 address by its /64), returns `429` with `Retry-After`. The response never says whether the email exists. Throttle counters are rows in PostgreSQL keyed by purpose and subject, with fixed windows; each attempt reserves its per-IP slot and then, only within that limit, its per-email slot, atomically and before any password is hashed; an attempt refused by either limit returns what it reserved at once, and a successful sign-in returns both, so the counters hold failed attempts (a correct password for a blocked account counts as one) and attempts in flight. The email in a subject is trimmed and lower-cased with Unicode case mapping. A rehash after sign-in replaces the stored hash only if it has not changed meanwhile, so it never undoes a concurrent reset or change; when another sign-in replaced it first, the password is verified again against the stored hash. The session starts in a transaction that locks the user row and requires the verified hash and an active or unverified state to be unchanged, so a sign-in that races a reset or a block creates no session; a block found at that point answers `account_blocked`. This guarantee needs the reset, block and deletion flows to change the user row and revoke its sessions in one transaction that holds the same row lock. When hashing is busy or fails, or the request is cancelled while a password is being verified, both reserved slots are returned. The client address is the TCP peer, or, when the peer is inside `OVC_TRUSTED_PROXY_CIDRS`, the right-most `X-Forwarded-For` entry outside those ranges; an unparsable entry falls back to the peer.
- **Sign-in answers:** an unknown email, a wrong password and a deleted account all answer `401 invalid_credentials`, and an unknown email still costs one password verification against a placeholder hash; a blocked account answers `403 account_blocked` only after the correct password. A password hashed with older parameters is rehashed on a successful sign-in. Password hashing runs at most `GOMAXPROCS` hashes at once and answers `503 service_unavailable` after waiting 5 seconds for a slot.
- **Password reset:**
  - requesting a reset always returns the same response, within the sending limits above;
  - the worker mints the emailed token (32 bytes, hashed and committed before sending, valid for 1 hour, single use);
  - a successful reset revokes all sessions.
- **Password change** requires the current password and revokes every other session. Wrong current passwords are counted per account in their own counter with the same numbers as the sign-in failure limit (5 per fixed 15-minute window), separate from the sign-in counters and shared by every current-password check (password change and [account deletion](#account-deletion)), and an attempt refused by it returns its slot; the sixth attempt in a window, on either route, answers `429` with `Retry-After`, and a correct password returns its slot.
- **Email change** is not in the MVP. A feature that adds it must move signed-in acceptance's binding check (the card email against the account's email) under the account's row lock, because signed-in acceptance reads the account email before it takes that lock.

## Tutor–student relationships

Students join through their tutor ([ADR-0014](../../docs/decisions/0014-students-join-through-their-tutor.md)).

- **States:**

  | State | Meaning |
  |---|---|
  | `invited` | A student card exists; no student account is linked |
  | `provisional` | Linked to an account whose email is not confirmed ([provisional links](#provisional-links-and-reclaim)) |
  | `reclaim_pending` | The mailbox owner reclaimed the account and has not yet kept or dropped this link ([reclaim](#provisional-links-and-reclaim)) |
  | `active` | Linked and working together |
  | `ended` | Ended by the tutor, by the student, or by a deletion (`ended_by`: `tutor`, `student`, `deletion`); a new invitation or request can reactivate it |
  | `discarded` | Withdrawn, merged into another card of the same student, removed while provisional or reclaim-pending, discarded by the tutor's deletion, or kept only as history after the student's purge (`discard_reason`: `withdrawn`, `merged`, `removed`, `tutor_deleted`, `student_purged`) |

- **Transitions:**

  | From | To | Cause |
  |---|---|---|
  | — | `invited` | Card created; the new card that a removal or a dropped link gives the tutor |
  | — | `active` | Join request accepted when the tutor has no relationship with the account and no `invited` card with its confirmed email: a new card whose display name is a copy of the student's account name at accept time, without an email |
  | `invited` | `provisional` | Invitation accepted by a new account, or by a signed-in account whose email is not confirmed and which holds no provisional relationship |
  | `invited` | `active` | Invitation accepted by a signed-in confirmed account; join request accepted with the card's email |
  | `invited` | `discarded` | Withdrawn; merged; tutor deleted |
  | `provisional` | `active` | Email confirmed from the account's own browser |
  | `provisional` | `reclaim_pending` | Reclaim by the mailbox owner |
  | `provisional` | `discarded` (`removed`) with a new `invited` card | The tutor removes it; the student stops studying; the 7-day cleanup; the student account is deleted (and so purged at once) |
  | `provisional` | `discarded` (`tutor_deleted`), no new card | The tutor deletes their account |
  | `reclaim_pending` | `active` | The owner keeps it while the tutor is `active`; or the owner accepts, signed in, an invitation of the same tutor, which counts as a keep and consumes that invitation |
  | `reclaim_pending` | `discarded` (`removed`) with a new `invited` card | The owner drops it; the owner keeps it while the tutor is not `active` (treated as a drop); `reclaim.expire` 7 days after the reclaim; the tutor removes it (`POST /students/{id}/remove` accepts `reclaim_pending` as well as `provisional`, serialised on the student's user row); the student account is deleted or purged |
  | `reclaim_pending` | `discarded` (`tutor_deleted`), no new card | The tutor deletes their account; that tutor's held requests of the account expire |
  | `active` | `ended` | The tutor ends; the student ends; the tutor or the student deletes their account |
  | `ended` | `active` | New invitation accepted by the same student account; join request from the same account accepted |
  | `ended` | `discarded` (`student_purged`) | Purge of the student (the row is kept for the tutor's history, with its `ended_at` and `ended_by`); purge of the tutor deletes the row |

  A purge of the student (the 7-day cleanup and the immediate purge of a deleted unconfirmed account included) gives the tutor the new card also while the tutor is blocked; its link answers like any invalid one until the tutor is `active` again. Deleting a tutor discards their provisional and reclaim-pending links first, so a deleted tutor never gets such a card.

- **Linking:** a relationship belongs to exactly one tutor and, once linked, exactly one student account forever. There is at most one non-discarded relationship per tutor and student account, at most one non-discarded card per tutor and email, compared case-insensitively, and at most one `provisional` relationship per student account, which is why an unconfirmed account cannot hold a second tutor's link. Linking a card without an email never copies the account's email onto the card, so a removed or dropped link leaves no address on the new card. Whenever a student links to a tutor by any path (an invitation, an accepted join request, a reactivation, the owner's keep after a reclaim), every held or pending join request from that student to that tutor that is not past its `expires_at`, and every such request an unblock closed whose `expires_at` has not passed, becomes `accepted` (an overdue open request is left for the expiry job, and the student already sees it `expired`), and the card's open invitation, if any other than the one being used, is revoked (`linked`) and its ciphertext cleared.
- **The student card** is the tutor's view of the relationship:
  - display name, set by the tutor and private to them; for a card that accepting a join request creates, it starts as a tutor-owned copy of the student's account name taken at accept time, which the tutor can edit and which never follows later changes of the account name;
  - optional email;
  - state, and the linked account (name, email, avatar and whether its email is confirmed), shown neither while the card is `invited` nor while it is `reclaim_pending`, so the tutor never sees a reclaimed account before its owner keeps the link, nor while the linked account is `deleted`: from the deletion until the purge the tutor sees no account name, email or avatar on the card and keeps only the card's own display name, email and notes, and an operator's restore shows the account again;
  - the open invitation link with its expiry and status (open, expired, or revoked after repeated wrong emails);
  - groups;
  - lesson history (slice 5);
  - issues and scores (slice 9);
  - private notes, never returned to the student; notes hold at most 10,000 characters and no control characters other than line feed and tab, and anything else answers `422 validation_failed` (`invalid`) at `body.notes` (owner's decision of 2026-10-28, pending owner review).
- **Names the student sees:** the student sees their own account name everywhere. Other participants (group members, the conferencing room) also see the student's account name, never the tutor's private display name.
- **Tutor colour:** each relationship has a colour that labels this tutor's items in the student's views. It is chosen from the fixed palette at linking time: the first palette key that none of the student's other non-discarded relationships uses, otherwise the least used one, in palette order.
- **Isolation:** a tutor lists, reads and acts only on their own relationships, and a student only on their own. Any route that takes a student, relationship, request or group identifier resolves it within the caller's scope first and returns `not_found` otherwise, with the same body as for a random identifier; this resolution runs before any role-specific or state refusal (`409`), so a state refusal never confirms that an out-of-scope identifier exists. A `discarded` card is outside scope, except that repeating the withdrawal of a card this tutor withdrew answers `204`.
- **Listing:** the tutor's list of students is ordered by creation time, newest first, with keyset pagination on creation time and identifier. The `state=active` filter includes `provisional` and `reclaim_pending` cards. The search `q` is a case-insensitive substring of the display name or the card email, with `%`, `_` and `\` taken literally.
- **Duplicate cards:** creating a card with an email that one of the tutor's non-discarded cards already has is refused with `student_exists`, and the response names the existing card.
- **Card limits:** a tutor creates at most 50 cards and changes card emails at most 50 times per day (`429 rate_limited`). The card that a removal or a dropped link creates does not count.
- **Withdrawing a card:** the tutor can withdraw an `invited` card; it becomes `discarded` (`withdrawn`) and its open invitation is revoked. Repeating the withdrawal answers `204`; withdrawing a card that is `provisional`, `reclaim_pending`, `active` or `ended` is refused with `relationship_not_active`; any other `discarded` card is outside scope and answers `not_found`.
- **Changing the email of an `invited` card** revokes its open invitation and issues a fresh link in the same transaction. The email of a card in any other state cannot be changed (`not_editable`).
- **Ending:** ending an `ended` relationship returns its current state (idempotent); ending an `invited`, `provisional` or `reclaim_pending` card is refused with `relationship_not_active` (a provisional student is removed instead); a `discarded` card is outside scope and answers `not_found`.
- **Scheduling and issuing** require an `active` relationship, so invited cards never get lessons or work; from slice 5 one-to-one lessons may also be scheduled for a `provisional` relationship, which passes the [join rule](schedule.md#join-rule) for one-to-one lessons only, and from slice 9 work may also be issued to it directly, never through a group.

## Invitations

- **Creating:** a confirmed tutor creates a student card with a display name and an optional email. The card starts as `invited` and gets its invitation link at once.
- **One open invitation per card**, the same link whether the tutor copies it or OVC emails it:
  - the token is 32 random bytes in base64url, valid for 14 days and single use;
  - it is stored as a SHA-256 hash for lookup and as AES-256-GCM ciphertext under `OVC_FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (a 12-byte random nonce; as additional data the label `ovc.invitation-token.v1` followed by the invitation identifier, as the [data model](data-model.md) specifies), so the card can show the link again and the worker can email it;
  - the ciphertext is cleared when the invitation is used or revoked, and the hourly `invitations.expire` job clears it once the invitation expires;
  - the key is not rotated in the MVP: a new key makes open links unreadable, and the tutor regenerates them.
- **Regenerating** an `invited` or `ended` card revokes its open invitation and issues a new one; a tutor regenerates at most 50 links per UTC day (`429 rate_limited`).
- **Sending by email** (`POST /students/{id}/invitation/send`) emails the card's open link to the card's email, and refuses a card without an email. An open link that has expired is revoked (`expired_on_send`) and a fresh one is sent; a card with no open link (an `ended` card after the ending revoked it, or a link revoked after repeated wrong emails) gets a fresh one, which counts against the regeneration limit and, once that is used up, is refused with the same `429 rate_limited` as a regeneration; refreshing an expired link does not count against it. Each queued send counts on the invitation, and the count is part of the email's idempotency key, so an earlier send of the same link still waiting at dispatch becomes obsolete. The checks run in this order: scope, state, the card email, the tutor's budget, then the regeneration limit when a fresh link is needed; a refused send changes nothing.
- **Sending limits:**
  - a tutor in their first 7 days after sign-up may send 10 invitation emails per UTC day, and 50 per UTC day after that; only this budget, and the regeneration limit when a send needs a fresh link, answer `429`, never anything about the recipient;
  - at most 3 invitation emails per recipient address per UTC day across all tutors; over that cap, and for an address whose account is `blocked` or `deleted`, the route answers `202` like any other send and the email is dropped; a dropped send still counts toward the tutor's budget, still refreshes an expired link, and still issues a fresh link for a card with no open link, counted as a regeneration, so the card and the counters look exactly as after a queued send; the card never shows delivery status.
- **The invitation email** is written in the language of the account that holds the card's email, otherwise in the tutor's language. The tutor's name appears only in the body, as escaped plain text, never as a link or in the subject.
- **An active tutor only:** a link of a tutor whose account is not `active` (unverified, blocked or deleted) is invalid.
- **An `ended` card** can be given a new invitation. It is **bound to the card's linked student account**: only that account, signed in, can accept it; any other account, and every sign-up on its page, is refused with `invitation_email_mismatch`.
- **The invitation page** (`GET /invitations/{token}`):
  - shows the tutor's public profile (name, avatar, subjects) and never the invitee's email;
  - an unknown, expired, used, revoked or malformed token, and the link of a tutor who is not `active`, all get one identical `404 not_found`;
  - every response that carries an invitation link or accepts one is sent with `Cache-Control: no-store`;
  - follows the [token page rules](../design/web/navigation.md#token-pages); the token stays in the page's memory and never travels in `next` or any other address.
- **Accepting with a new account** (`POST /invitations/{token}/accept` without a bearer token), in this order:
  1. the per-IP request slot, shared with sign-up, reset and resend;
  2. the token is usable and its tutor `active`, otherwise `409 invitation_invalid`;
  3. validation of the sign-up fields;
  4. the email binding: a card email must equal the sign-up email, normalised as for sign-up, and the link of an `ended` card is always a mismatch here; a mismatch is counted and refused with `409 invitation_email_mismatch` before any password is hashed;
  5. the password is hashed, whether or not the email is taken;
  6. for a free email, one transaction creates the account `unverified`, consumes the invitation, makes the relationship `provisional`, chooses the colour and queues the confirmation; for a taken email, nothing changes, the invitation stays usable and the existing owner gets the duplicate sign-up notice. When the link stops being usable inside that transaction for a free email (its tutor stopped being `active`, or the link was consumed, revoked or regenerated while the sign-up waited), the transaction is rolled back, so no account is created.

  All these cases answer the same `202 {}` with no session. The page then signs in with the password just chosen; when that fails it shows "Check your email, or sign in to accept", never a wrong-password error.
- **Accepting as a signed-in account** (`POST /invitations/{token}/accept` with a bearer token and the body `{}`; sign-up fields sent with a bearer are refused with `422 validation_failed`, `not_allowed`), in this order:
  1. a tutor account is refused with `409 role_mismatch`, and an account whose email is not confirmed and which already holds a provisional relationship with `403 email_not_verified`, both before any counter;
  2. the token is usable and its tutor `active`, otherwise `409 invitation_invalid`;
  3. the binding: the card email equals the account's email, or the `ended` card's bound account is the caller; a mismatch is counted and refused with `409 invitation_email_mismatch`;
  4. linking, below. An account whose email is not confirmed links `provisional`; a confirmed account links `active`.

  The transaction locks the account's row and re-checks the session and the account state the request was authorised for ([locks and the session re-check](#locks-and-the-session-re-check)), and an unconfirmed account's provisional relationship again under that lock; then it locks the tutor's profile, then the card and the account's other relationship with that tutor, in identifier order. The answer is `200 {tutor: MyTutor}` for the relationship that is kept. Signed-in acceptance reserves no per-IP slot, because it hashes nothing and every wrong email is counted on the link. When the account's `reclaim_pending` link with that tutor is kept this way and nothing else waits for the owner's choice, the same transaction clears the account's reclaim deadline.
- **The mismatch limit:** every `invitation_email_mismatch` is counted on the invitation in one atomic update, and the fifth revokes the link; the card then shows "Link revoked after repeated wrong emails", and later attempts get the ordinary invalid answer. A link holder can therefore revoke a leaked link on purpose.
- **Consumption is atomic:** a conditional update that succeeds only while the invitation is unused, unrevoked and unexpired, and that clears its ciphertext; a token links only while its card is `invited` or `ended`. Concurrent acceptances of one link, or a concurrent regeneration, lose with `invitation_invalid`, except a new-account sign-up that already passed the check of step 2, which loses inside its transaction with the ordinary `202 {}` and no account; concurrent acceptances by one account are serialised by that account's row lock, so neither uniqueness rule of linking ever surfaces as a server error.
- **Linking:**
  - links the account to the card, sets the relationship `provisional` or `active` as above, chooses the colour, and consumes the invitation used;
  - if the student already has a non-discarded relationship with this tutor through another card, that existing relationship is kept (reactivated if `ended`, and made `active` if `reclaim_pending`, which counts as the owner's keep), its own open invitation is revoked (`linked`), and the invited card becomes `discarded` (`merged`);
  - every held or pending join request from the same student to the same tutor that is not past its `expires_at`, and every such request an unblock closed whose `expires_at` has not passed, becomes `accepted`;
  - the tutor gets the in-app notification `invitation.accepted` in the linking transaction, also when the link is provisional, labelled with the card's own display name and never with the account name a possibly squatting account chose; no second notification follows when the email is confirmed ([in-app producers](notifications.md#in-app-producers-in-slice-4)).

## Provisional links and reclaim

- **A provisional link** reaches only the one-to-one relationship: it is listed on the student's My tutors, it cannot be added to a group (`409 relationship_not_active`), and no notification email about it is sent to the unconfirmed address. From slice 5 it reaches the relationship's one-to-one lessons, and from slice 6 its private chat, with text messages only, never a group's; the direct channel is created in the linking transaction. The card shows "Email not confirmed", and so do the tutor's channel list and conversation header for that channel.
- **Removal** (`POST /students/{id}/remove`) is allowed only while the card is `provisional` or `reclaim_pending`, and answers `409 relationship_not_provisional` otherwise. In one transaction, which locks the student's user row, then the tutor's profile (answering `401 unauthenticated` when the tutor is no longer `active`), then the relationship, and re-checks its state:
  - the relationship becomes `discarded` (`removed`), its open invitation is revoked, and its notes and email are cleared;
  - a new `invited` card with the same display name, email and notes and a fresh link is created and returned as `new_card`; it does not count toward the card limit;
  - nothing is sent: removal is silent, with no email and no in-app notification, and the removed account gets only the `entity.changed` frame that makes My tutors refetch; the removed account no longer lists the tutor, the discarded row is invisible to the tutor, and it is never reactivated, even when the same account later accepts the new link;
  - from slice 5 the link's future one-to-one lessons are cancelled (`cancel_cause` `provisional_removed`) and its series end, without an email or an in-app row, and the account's roster rows of every lesson of that link are deleted, whatever the lesson's status, so the removed account no longer sees those lessons ([schedule](schedule.md#lessons-and-series)); the transaction locks the link's series and lessons after the relationship;
  - from slice 6 the link's direct channel is discarded: the transaction locks it `FOR UPDATE` after the relationship, deletes both member rows, sets `discarded_at` and `erase_pending_since`, marks the channel's `pending` chat uploads `deleted`, and sends both parties one last `entity.changed channel`; the deferred erasure job then erases every message of the channel, both parties' included, releasing their quota charges ([channels](realtime-and-video.md#channels), the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review). The discarding transaction releases no charge itself, so it takes no tutor profile lock beyond those it already holds.

  Repeating a removal names a discarded card and answers `not_found`; removal is not among the retry-safe routes. The same removal effects apply when the student stops studying on a provisional link, when the 7-day cleanup removes the account, when a deleted unconfirmed account is purged at once, and when a reclaim's link is dropped. A removal racing a confirmation or a reclaim locks the same user row and checks the state, so exactly one of them wins.
- **Confirmation by the account's own browser** (a browser signed in to the account) makes its provisional relationship `active` and releases its held join requests.
- **Reclaim** is a confirmation that sets a new password from a browser not signed in to the account, or a password reset of an `unverified` account. Its transaction, which already holds the account's row, confirms the email, moves the provisional relationship to `reclaim_pending`, marks every held join request as waiting for the owner's choice, and sets the account's reclaim deadline to 7 days later; from slice 6 it also locks the link's direct channel `FOR UPDATE`, suspends the account's member row (`suspended_since`) and sets the channel's `provisional_through_seq` to its `last_seq`, while the tutor's member row stays and the channel stays writable for the tutor ([channels](realtime-and-video.md#channels)). Nothing becomes permanent, and no request becomes visible to a tutor, without an explicit keep. The outcome is `choice_required`.
- **While `reclaim_pending`** the link grants the account nothing: `GET /tutors` leaves it out, `POST /tutors/{id}/end` answers `not_found`, no group can take it, the suspended member row reaches nothing of the direct channel and is shown to no one, and the card shows no account and the state "Waiting for the student". `GET /users/me` reports `reclaim_pending: true` while any link or request waits for the choice, derived from those rows.
- **The choice:**
  - `GET /users/me/reclaim` lists each waiting link and request with its tutor's name, avatar and subjects, and the deadline; the choice page also shows the account's current name and avatar with a link to edit them before anything is kept;
  - `POST /users/me/reclaim` names every waiting item exactly once, as kept or dropped. An identifier that is not one of the caller's waiting items answers `not_found` (another account's identifier, and one that is not a well-formed identifier, answer like a random one), checked before `422 validation_failed` for an item that is missing or named twice; on either the client fetches the list again. A waiting link left out is reported as `body.keep_links` `required`, a waiting request left out as `body.keep_requests` `required`, and an item named a second time (in the same list or in its keep and drop lists) as `invalid` on the list that names it again. When nothing waits (a repeat, or after the deadline, or after the tutor removed the link), it answers `200` with `outcome: null` and changes nothing, whatever the body names;
  - keeping a link makes it `active` while its tutor is `active`, revokes any other open link of the card (`linked`) and accepts the account's waiting requests to that tutor, and is treated as a drop otherwise; dropping a link applies the removal effects, so its tutor gets a new card and its direct channel is erased whole; from slice 6 keeping a link also locks its direct channel, clears the suspension, sets the restored member row's read marker to just before the first provisional-period message the account did not send, so those messages count as unread, and its digest marker to the channel's latest message, and the account's own provisional-period messages show the fixed cue "Sent before the account was reclaimed" to both sides (the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review, [channels](realtime-and-video.md#channels)); keeping a request releases it as at confirmation, and a kept request already past its 14 days expires instead, like one whose tutor is not `active`; dropping a request expires it silently. The owner's decision on each request is applied before a kept link accepts the account's waiting requests to its tutor, so a dropped request to the tutor of a kept link stays expired, and a kept one is released and then accepted with the link;
  - the answer's `outcome` follows the confirmation precedence over the kept items: `linked` when a kept link became `active`, otherwise `request_sent` when a kept request was released, otherwise `code_invalid` when a kept request expired because its tutor is not `active` or its 14 days had passed, otherwise `null`;
  - the transaction locks the account's row and re-checks the session, then reads the waiting items, locks the profiles of their tutors in identifier order, then the waiting relationships; it applies the link decisions (with their invitation steps), then locks the waiting requests and applies the request decisions, so it follows the general lock order; `reclaim.expire` and the account's deletion drop items in the same order;
  - the account's deadline is cleared once nothing waits.
- **Signed-in acceptance** of an invitation of the same tutor while the link is `reclaim_pending` counts as a keep and consumes that invitation.
- **Code entry during a pending choice:** entering the code of a tutor whose request waits for the choice counts as a keep of that request; entering the code of a tutor whose link is `reclaim_pending` answers `409 already_linked` and changes nothing.
- **Deadline:** the hourly `reclaim.expire` job takes accounts whose deadline has passed in batches of 100, oldest deadline first, keyset-paged on the deadline and the account identifier through a partial index on the deadline, and at most 10 batches (1,000 accounts) per run; for each, in its own transaction, it locks the account's row, re-checks the deadline and drops every item still waiting, whatever the account's state (links with the removal effects, requests expired), then clears the deadline; it also clears a passed deadline with nothing left to choose. An account that fails is reported and skipped, and the run continues with the next one, also into the next batch, so failing accounts never hold back the others beyond the slots they take in a run; the run is then recorded as failed. A deleted account never reaches it, because deletion already dropped its items.
- **Deletion while a choice is pending:** deleting the account (it is confirmed, so it is only marked `deleted`) drops every waiting item in the same transaction, links with the removal effects and requests expired, and clears the deadline. Deleting a tutor's account discards that tutor's `reclaim_pending` links (`tutor_deleted`, no new card, open links revoked) and expires that tutor's requests that wait for an owner's choice, locking only the tutor's own user row and profile; the student's deadline stays until the account's own next decision or `reclaim.expire` clears it, and `GET /users/me` no longer reports a choice because nothing waits. The other effects of a tutor's deletion are listed under [account deletion](#account-deletion).
- **The deadline is written only by the account's own transactions:** for example the reclaim, the owner's choice, a signed-in acceptance that keeps the last waiting link, a code entry that keeps the last waiting request, the account's deletion, and the job. The list is not exhaustive, because whether a choice is due is derived from the waiting rows and `reclaim.expire` clears a deadline lazily once nothing waits. Tutor paths (removal, tutor deletion) never write the deadline; tutor deletion never locks a student's user row at all.
- **Restoring or unblocking** an account into `unverified` restarts its 7-day clock, including for an account that holds a provisional link; restoring only ever returns an account to `active`, so only unblocking is checked against linked accounts.

## Tutor code and join requests

- **The code:**
  - each tutor has one code of 8 characters from `23456789ABCDEFGHJKMNPQRSTUVWXYZ` (no 0, 1, I, L or O), shown as `XXXX-XXXX` and matched case-insensitively with spaces and hyphens ignored;
  - minted at tutor sign-up, retried on a collision;
  - shared openly by design, shown in Teaching settings and in the join-request panel on Today;
  - can be regenerated at most 10 times per tutor per UTC calendar day (`429 rate_limited`; a refused attempt is not counted), and the old code stops working immediately;
  - inactive while the tutor is unverified (`GET /tutor/code` reports `active: false`), and invalid while the tutor's account is not `active`.
- **Entering a code** (`POST /join-requests`, also open to a student whose email is not confirmed; at sign-up through `POST /auth/register`):
  - a tutor account entering a code is refused with `role_mismatch`;
  - the entry then reserves its slot of the per-student limit below, so refused and invalid entries count as well;
  - an unknown, regenerated or inactive code is refused with `tutor_code_invalid`;
  - the transaction then locks the student's user row and re-checks the session ([locks and the session re-check](#locks-and-the-session-re-check)), locks the tutor's profile and re-checks that the tutor is `active` and that the entered code, normalised, is still the code read under that lock, so a regeneration that committed while the entry waited makes the old code answer `tutor_code_invalid`;
  - a student who already has a `provisional`, `reclaim_pending` or `active` relationship with that tutor is refused with `already_linked`;
  - a student who already has a held or pending request to that tutor gets the existing request;
  - otherwise a request is created; created, existing, held and suppressed requests all get the same `200` answer with the student's view of the request.
- **Held requests:** a request from an account whose email is not confirmed is `held`: the tutor does not see it, it does not count toward the tutor's limit, and its entry never answers `tutor_unavailable`. A regenerated code does not affect a request already held.
- **Visible and suppressed requests:** a request from a confirmed account is a visible `pending` request when the tutor has fewer than 50 visible pending requests, and is refused with `429 tutor_unavailable` when the limit is full. The limit is checked before the block, so a blocked student's entry is refused with the same `429` while the limit is full. Otherwise, when the tutor has blocked the student, the request is suppressed instead: the answer is identical to a created request, the student's request list shows it as sent until it expires, and nothing reaches the tutor. `tutor_unavailable` carries `Retry-After: 3600`, because the hourly expiry job is the earliest regular point at which a slot frees.
- **Release:** at confirmation from the account's own browser, and when the owner keeps a request after a reclaim, a held request becomes visible `pending` when the tutor is `active` and has fewer than 50 visible pending requests; it is suppressed when the tutor has blocked the student or the limit is full; it expires when the tutor is not `active`.
- **Un-suppression:** a request suppressed for the limit becomes visible, oldest first, in the transaction that frees a slot (an accept, a decline, an expiry, a student's deletion, an invitation acceptance, signed in or through a merge, or the owner's keep of a link after a reclaim, that turns a visible pending request into `accepted`); a suppressed request already past its 14 days is left for the expiry job, and a request of a student account the operator has blocked is skipped and stays suppressed, with no notification row and no frame for the tutor; after the operator's unblock it becomes visible only in a later transaction that frees a slot, oldest first as any other. Unblocking closes the requests suppressed by the block without telling the student: internally they are `expired` with `decided_at` set to their own future `expires_at`, they stay outside the tutor's scope, and the student's list shows them `pending` until that `expires_at` and then `expired` with that time as `decided_at`, as it shows an ordinary request. A code entry to that tutor before the `expires_at` reopens the closed request in place, with the same identifier, `created_at` and `expires_at`, as `pending` and visible, or suppressed for the limit when the tutor's limit is full (never `429`), and answers it exactly as a re-entry of an ordinary open request answers that request. Residual: the reopened request reaches the tutor with a `created_at` from before the unblock and less than 14 days left, and a reopen does a little more work than an ordinary re-entry, so it can answer slightly later. A student's deletion closes such a request at the deletion time, as it expires an open one.
- **Limits:** code entries are throttled to 10 per student per hour, held entries included (`429 rate_limited`); a sign-up with a code uses the per-IP request counter; each tutor sees at most 50 pending requests, except after an operator's unblock as described under the tutor's scope.
- **The tutor's scope:** a tutor reaches the visible pending requests sent to them that are not past their `expires_at`, and their decided or expired requests; a pending request past its `expires_at` (before the job has expired it), a held or suppressed request, a request closed by an unblock before its `expires_at`, and any request of a student account the operator has blocked (while it stays blocked) answer `not_found` to accept and decline, like a random identifier, and are left out of the tutor's list. The limit of 50 counts visible pending requests not past their `expires_at` of student accounts the operator has not blocked, so a request the tutor cannot see takes no slot. An operator's block runs no un-suppression, so the slot that a blocked account's visible request stops taking goes to the next code entry or release rather than to the oldest request waiting for the limit, which waits until a later transaction frees a slot. After the operator's unblock, a visible request of that account that has not expired is visible again and counts again, so the count can then stand above 50 until decisions and expiries bring it back; while it does, the limit is full: code entries are refused with `429 tutor_unavailable`, released requests wait suppressed for the limit, and un-suppression frees nothing.
- **Accept** (idempotent when already accepted, answering the card linked to the student) locks the student's user row, the tutor's profile, the relationships, then revokes invitations, and only then updates the request, following the lock order:
  - reactivates the tutor's existing relationship with that student account if one exists (`ended`);
  - otherwise links a non-discarded `invited` card whose email equals the student's confirmed email, if one exists;
  - otherwise creates the relationship with a new card whose display name is a tutor-owned copy of the student's account name at accept time, editable by the tutor, and whose email stays empty;
  - revokes the open invitation of the linked or reactivated card (`linked`) and clears its ciphertext, so that link can no longer be used;
  - and answers with the card. Only a `pending` request can be decided; a decided or expired one is refused with `not_editable`.
- **Notifications:** a request that becomes visible to the tutor (created visible, released, un-suppressed or reopened after an unblock) gives the tutor an in-app `join_request.created` row; an accept gives the student `join_request.accepted`, and a decline, with or without a block, gives the student one identical `join_request.declined` row; a held or suppressed request gives the tutor nothing, and a block, a suppression or an unblock gives the student nothing distinguishable, neither a row nor a frame ([in-app producers](notifications.md#in-app-producers-in-slice-4), [authorization of frames](realtime-and-video.md#authorization-of-frames)).
- **Decline** (idempotent; a repeat with the block requested adds the block): the student learns only that the request was not accepted; an accepted or expired request is refused with `not_editable`. "Decline and block" also stops future requests from that student: they are suppressed and shown to the student as sent until they expire. The tutor manages blocked students in Teaching settings and can unblock them.
- **Expiry:** held and pending requests expire 14 days after they were created, on the injected clock, through the hourly `join_requests.expire` job (batches of 200, at most 5 per run, oldest expiry first, keyset-paged on the expiry time and the identifier, each request in its own transaction that locks the student's user row, then the tutor's profile, then the request, and un-suppresses the next request when a visible one expired; the expiry is recorded with `decided_at` equal to `expires_at`). A request that fails is reported as `join_requests.expire_failed` and skipped, the run continues past it, also into the next batch, and is recorded as failed; a run that stops at its bound remembers where it stopped, and the next run resumes there and starts again from the oldest once it reaches the end, so failing requests never keep later ones from expiring (the position is kept in the worker's memory and restarts from the oldest after a worker restart). The student's list already shows an open request past its `expires_at` as `expired` with `decided_at` equal to `expires_at`, whenever the job runs. A code entry that finds its open request already past `expires_at` expires it in the same transaction and creates a new one. A held request that expires on release, at the owner's choice or at the account's deletion records `decided_at` as the earlier of its `expires_at` and that moment, so an overdue request keeps its own expiry time. A declined or expired request does not block a new one, unless the student is blocked.
- **Unblock** (`DELETE /tutor/blocked-requesters/{studentId}`) locks the student's user row, then the tutor's profile, removes the block and closes the student's requests suppressed by it as described under un-suppression; a student the tutor has not blocked answers `not_found`.
- **The tutor's list** (`GET /join-requests`) shows the visible pending requests, oldest first, with the student's account name, confirmed email and avatar; **the blocked students** (`GET /tutor/blocked-requesters`) are listed most recently blocked first, each with the student's account name and avatar; a student whose account is `deleted` stays listed until the student's purge, without the account name or avatar, so the tutor can still unblock the row but no longer sees the account name or avatar.
- **The student's list** (`GET /join-requests/sent`), newest first, shows each request's state as `pending` (held, visible and suppressed alike), `accepted`, `not_accepted` (declined) or `expired`, with its times only, never the tutor's decision details.

## Ending a relationship

Either party ends a relationship, after confirming in a dialog: the tutor from the student card (`POST /students/{id}/end`), the student from My tutors with "Stop studying" (`POST /tutors/{relationshipId}/end`). The effects are the same. In one transaction:

- the relationship becomes `ended` with `ended_at` and `ended_by` (`tutor` or `student`), and the student is removed from all of the tutor's groups; the card holds no open invitation, because every path that makes a card `active` revokes its other links (`linked`) and an `active` card takes no new link, so the ending leaves none;
- **locks:** the tutor's ending locks the tutor's profile, then the relationship `FOR UPDATE`; the student's ending first locks the account's row and re-checks the session ([locks and the session re-check](#locks-and-the-session-re-check)), then locks the relationship `FOR UPDATE`. Both delete the relationship's group memberships only after that lock, so a group change that holds the relationship with its shared lock finishes first and the ending then removes the membership it added;
- **lessons** (slice 5):
  - one-to-one series of the relationship end with an end date of today in the series timezone, a count being converted into that end date;
  - future one-to-one lessons are cancelled with `cancel_cause` `relationship_ended` and `cancelled_at` equal to the relationship's `ended_at`, as the ending statement stored it; their rosters are frozen from the audience as it was before the ending, so they keep the student and stay visible to them;
  - the transaction locks the relationship's one-to-one series and lessons after its existing locks;
  - the student leaves the audience of future group lessons through membership alone: no group lesson row changes and none is locked, and the student gets no frame for a lesson they no longer see;
  - a lesson already under way at the ending, a `scheduled` one-to-one lesson of the relationship or a `scheduled` group lesson of a group the student belonged to whose start is at or before `ended_at`, keeps its status and gets a roster row for the student in the ending's transaction, so the status job later ends it with the student on its roster and it stays in the student's history ([roster](schedule.md#lessons-and-series), owner's decision of 2026-10-28, pending owner review);
  - from slice 7 the student can no longer join any of the tutor's lessons, including live ones ([join rule](schedule.md#join-rule));
- **work** (slice 9):
  - issued work the student has not submitted (`not_started`, `draft`, `returned`) becomes `closed`;
  - `submitted` work stays checkable by the tutor but can no longer be returned;
  - checked work stays readable by both;
- **chat** (slice 6): the private chat becomes read-only for both: the transaction locks the direct channel `FOR UPDATE` after the relationship and sets its `read_only_since` to `ended_at`; both member rows stay, so both keep reading it, and authors and the tutor may still delete messages; the student's group memberships end with their channel member rows, and their `pending` uploads to those group channels are marked `deleted`; members get `entity.changed channel`;
- **recordings:** recordings of lessons whose roster includes the student stay readable by the student until they expire;
- **notifications:** one `relationship.ended` email to the other party, the student when the tutor ends and the tutor when the student ends, through the outbox, with the key `(relationship.ended, relationship, ended_at)`; it is never sent to an unconfirmed address; from slice 5 it lists the lessons the ending cancelled, at most 20 and then the number left out, found at dispatch from their cause and `cancelled_at` ([aggregated emails](notifications.md#events)). The other party also gets an in-app `relationship.ended` row with the same key, and both parties get `entity.changed` for the relationship ([in-app producers](notifications.md#in-app-producers-in-slice-4)). The email to the tutor names the student by the tutor's own card display name, escaped, never by the current account name; for a card created from a join request that is the tutor-owned copy taken at accept time, and links to the student card; the email to the student names the tutor by the tutor's account name and links to My tutors.

Ending an `ended` relationship again returns it unchanged and queues no second email, whichever party ended it. A student's "Stop studying" on a `provisional` link is a silent [removal](#provisional-links-and-reclaim) answering `204`, so a provisional link never becomes `ended`; on any other of the student's own identifiers that is not `active` or `ended` (invited, discarded or `reclaim_pending`) it answers `not_found`.

**Reactivation:** a new invitation accepted by the same account, or an accepted join request from it, reactivates the same relationship with its history; either party can reactivate it only through these paths. It makes the private chat writable again (the same channel, its `read_only_since` cleared under its lock, with its history). It restores no group memberships, no series, no cancelled lessons and no closed work.

## Groups

- **A group** belongs to one tutor and has:
  - a name (1–60 characters);
  - a colour;
  - members who are students with `active` relationships to that tutor (at most 30); a `provisional` or `reclaim_pending` student can never be a member.
- **Limits:** a tutor has at most 200 groups, archived ones included; the 201st is refused with `422 validation_failed`, counted under the tutor's profile lock, so the group list stays unpaginated and bounded.
- **Members:** a member outside the tutor's relationships answers `not_found`, and one whose relationship is not `active` is refused with `409 relationship_not_active`, naming the relationships.
- **Locks:** creating a group locks the tutor's profile first, which serialises concurrent creations for the limit, then the named relationships in identifier order with a shared lock that holds them in their state until the group and its members are written; changing a group's members locks the named relationships the same way and then the group's row. A change that ends or removes one of those relationships therefore waits for the group change, or the group change sees the new state and refuses it.
- **The group chat** (slice 6) is created in the transaction that creates the group, with the tutor's and the initial members' member rows; every membership change below updates the member rows in its own transaction after locking the channel `FOR UPDATE`.
- **Adding a member** gives them:
  - the group chat, including its whole history, also the messages written while a re-added member was away, with their read and digest markers at the channel's latest message, so nothing counts as unread and no digest lists the past (the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review);
  - future group lessons, through the [visibility rule](schedule.md#visibility);
  - from slice 7 the right to join a group lesson that is already live, which also adds them to that lesson's roster.

  It does not give them work issued to the group earlier ([issuing](homework.md#issuing)). Adding a member to an archived group is refused with `audience_not_active`; renaming, recolouring and removing members stay possible while it is archived.
- **Removing a member:**
  - removes them from future group lessons and the group chat, through membership alone: no lesson row changes, and they get no frame for a lesson they no longer see; from slice 7 they can no longer join a live group lesson; from slice 6 their channel member row is deleted, their `pending` uploads to the group channel are marked `deleted` with their deletion queued, and they get one last `entity.changed channel`, losing the history;
  - they keep their issued work, which they may still submit while their relationship with the tutor is active.
- **Archiving** (idempotent):
  - ends the group's series and cancels its future lessons with `cancel_cause` `group_archived` and `cancelled_at` equal to the group's `archived_at`, freezing their rosters from the members as they were before the archiving;
  - makes its chat read-only (`read_only_since` set to `archived_at` under the channel lock, which the archiving takes after the group);
  - refuses new issues to the group (`audience_not_active`);
  - from slice 5, members get one aggregated `group.archived` email and in-app row listing the lessons cancelled from their rosters ([aggregated emails](notifications.md#events)), only when the archiving cancelled at least one lesson; every lesson it cancels freezes the same members, so either every member gets them or nobody does, and an archiving after an unarchiving that finds no future lesson sends nothing (pending owner review);
  - archiving and unarchiving are not under a rate limit of their own: an archiving notifies only when it cancels a lesson, unarchiving restores none, so each further notified archiving needs a lesson created after the previous one: a notified archiving costs one lesson write, like a move, counted toward the tutor's 120 lesson writes per hour, and falls under the same residual of the shared daily cap ([T-20](../../docs/threat-model.md#threats-and-controls)) ([scheduling limits](schedule.md#scheduling-limits), pending owner review);
  - **locks** (from slice 5): the tutor's profile, then the members' relationships `FOR SHARE` (read first without locks), then the group `FOR UPDATE`, then the members are read again: when they differ from the locked set, the archiving retries once with the new set and then answers `409 conflict`, changing nothing; then the group's series and lessons. Slice 3's order of the profile and then the group gains the relationships between the two.
- **Unarchiving** (idempotent) restores issuing and chat, but no series and no cancelled lessons.

## Account deletion

- **Starting deletion:** it requires the current password and an explicit confirmation. A wrong password answers `422` and is counted in the same per-account counter as password change, shared by every current-password check (5 per fixed 15-minute window, [sessions and passwords](#sessions-and-passwords)); beyond it the attempt answers `429` with `Retry-After`, returns its slot and changes nothing, and a correct password returns its slot. The account becomes `deleted` at once: sessions are revoked, sockets are closed and sign-in is refused. A purge job runs 30 days later.
- **From deletion until purge**, the account's messages show their sender as "Deleted user" (`label_kind: deleted_user`) and keep their bodies; the purge erases a student's message bodies (shown as "Message removed") and deletes a tutor's channels with their messages ([purge](data-model.md#purge)).
- **Student deletion:**
  - active relationships end (`ended_by` `deletion`), with the effects above, lessons included, without sending `relationship.ended`;
  - provisional and reclaim-pending links get the removal effects, lessons included, so each tutor gets a new card;
  - held and pending join requests expire;
  - the reclaim deadline is cleared.

  The deletion locks the account's row, then the profiles of every tutor named by its relationships or channel memberships and its waiting or open requests, in identifier order, then every relationship of the account, discarded ones included, in identifier order; it drops the waiting items first, then expires the open requests and closes the requests an unblock closed (`decided_at` set to the deletion time), makes the freed slots visible to waiting requests, and finally ends the active relationships, deleting their group memberships.
- **Tutor deletion:**
  - active relationships end (`ended_by` `deletion`), invited cards become `discarded`, and provisional and reclaim-pending links become `discarded` (`tutor_deleted`) without a new card;
  - every open invitation of the tutor is revoked (`tutor_deleted`), including the regenerated links of ended cards;
  - held and pending join requests expire;
  - groups are archived, series end and future lessons are cancelled: from slice 5 with `cancel_cause` `tutor_deleted` and `cancelled_at` equal to the tutor's `deleted_at`, their rosters frozen from the audience as it was before the relationships and groups changed; a `scheduled` lesson whose start is at or before `deleted_at`, already under way, keeps its status and gets the roster rows of that audience in the same transaction, so the status job later ends it with its audience (owner's decision of 2026-10-28, pending owner review); the future one-to-one lessons of a provisional or reclaim-pending link it discards are cancelled with `provisional_removed`, silently (no email and no in-app row), and the account's roster rows of every lesson of that link are deleted, as for a removal;
  - from slice 6 the ended relationships' direct channels and the archived groups' channels become read-only through the same statements, and the channels of the provisional and reclaim-pending links it discards lose their member rows and are queued for erasure; the deletion locks those channels `FOR UPDATE` after the groups and releases no quota charge itself;
  - every student whose relationship was `active` at deletion and whose email is confirmed receives one `tutor.deleted` email, which from slice 5 lists the cancelled lessons whose roster includes them (at most 20 and then the number left out), instead of separate ended, cancellation or archiving emails; students of discarded provisional links get no email;
  - the deletion locks only the tutor's own user row and profile, never a student's user row; from slice 5 it locks the tutor's series and lessons after its existing locks, and freezing rosters takes no lock on a student's user row, because roster rows have no foreign key to it;
  - for 30 days, students keep read-only access to their own submissions, results and recordings from that tutor.

  As implemented in slice 3, inside the deletion's transaction and after the tutor's row is marked `deleted`: the tutor's profile is locked, then every non-discarded relationship of the tutor in identifier order; then, each as one statement, `invited`, `provisional` and `reclaim_pending` cards become `discarded` (`tutor_deleted`), `active` relationships become `ended` (`deletion`), every open invitation of the tutor's cards is revoked (`tutor_deleted`) with its ciphertext cleared, every `held` or `pending` request to the tutor and every request an unblock closed becomes `expired` with `decided_at` the earlier of its `expires_at` and the deletion time, every group membership of the tutor's groups is deleted, and every unarchived group is archived, in that order (relationships, invitations, requests, groups). One `tutor.deleted` email is queued per student of a relationship this deletion ended whose email is confirmed at that moment (the dispatch drops it again for an unconfirmed recipient). Blocked students stay listed until the purge. Restoring the account changes none of this: relationships stay `ended`, cards `discarded` and groups archived, and the queued emails become obsolete.
- **An unverified account** is purged at once when its owner deletes it, so its email is free again immediately.
- **In slice 2** deletion and purge covered the account's own data (sessions, email requests and tokens, the tutor profile, uploads and avatar references, the user row); slice 3 adds the effects on relationships, cards, invitations, join requests and groups, and the lesson and message effects arrive with the slices that add those tables. The hourly `accounts.purge` job purges accounts deleted 30 days ago and the hourly `accounts.cleanup_unverified` job removes accounts unverified for 7 days, in batches of 100, each account in its own transaction; an account whose purge fails is logged as `account.purge_failed` and skipped for that run, so it never holds up the others.
- **Purge** is defined per table in [the purge rules](data-model.md#purge). The purge job re-checks the account's state under a row lock, so a restore never races it.
  - Every purge path (the 30-day purge, the 7-day cleanup and the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account its owner deletes) deletes the notification rows whose recipient, tutor or student is the account inside its existing batches, and the user row's deletion removes the email preferences; every producer of a notification row that names an account holds a lock the purge also takes, so no row names the account once the purge commits ([channels](notifications.md#channels)).
  - Purging a student erases their submissions, answers, uploaded files and message bodies (shown as "message removed"), including the files attached to those messages; from slice 6 the messages are erased in batches of 500 inside the purge's budget, each batch locking the charged tutors' profiles and then their channels, and the final transaction locks the account's channels before it deletes the account's member and digest rows ([purge](data-model.md#purge)). Every relationship row of the student, discarded ones included, loses its `student_id` and its card email; the non-discarded ones become `discarded` (`student_purged`), keeping `ended_at` and `ended_by`, and stay in the tutor's history under the card's display name. OVC keeps no copy of the student's name, except that the display name of a card created by accepting a join request began as a copy of the account name at accept time; it is the tutor's own data and is deleted with the tutor's purge.
  - Purging a tutor deletes the tutor's library, lessons, recordings, issues, chats, files, student cards, notes, the emails stored on cards, invitations, join requests, blocked students and groups, in batches of 500 rows per transaction, each re-checking under the user row lock that the account is still `deleted`. As implemented in slice 3, each batch deletes up to 500 rows of the first non-empty kind in the order lesson rosters, lessons and series (from slice 5; series newest first), then from slice 6 the attachment references and quota charges of the tutor's channels, messages, channel members and channels, then group members, groups, invitations, join requests, blocked students, relationships, choosing and locking them in key order; the transaction that finds none left deletes the uploads, the profile and the user. One run of the purge job (or of the 7-day cleanup) spends at most 100 such batches across all accounts, shared round-robin from slice 5: each round gives every account still due one batch, oldest deletion first, until the budget is spent or no account is due; when they are spent the run ends without an error, records `account.purge_paused`, and the next hourly run continues the same account where it stopped. `account.purged` and `account.purge_paused` record the number of batch transactions that run spent on the account.
  - An `active` relationship that still exists when a student is purged (a deletion always ends them first) is ended (`deletion`) before it is discarded, so the history keeps its ending fields.
  - Recordings in which a purged student took part are kept until their own expiry (at most 365 days).
- **Backups:** database backups are retained for 30 days, so purged data leaves backups within 30 days after purge ([ADR-0008](../../docs/decisions/0008-hosting-and-data-residency.md)).
- **After purge**, deletion is irreversible. Before purge, only the operator can restore an account; restoring does not reactivate ended relationships.

## Locks and the session re-check

- **Lock order:** a transaction that needs user rows locks all of them up front, in one statement that locks the rows in identifier order (`FOR NO KEY UPDATE`), re-checks their states, and never locks another user row later. It then locks every tutor profile row it needs (for the pending-request limit, code regeneration, un-suppression and the group limit) up front, in identifier order, and never another profile later; release at confirmation, student deletion and un-suppression can touch several tutors. Then come relationships in identifier order, then invitations, then join requests, then groups, then (from slice 6) channels in identifier order, then messages in identifier order, then (from slice 5) lesson series in identifier order, then lessons in identifier order; in full: user rows, tutor profiles, relationships, invitations, join requests, groups, channels, messages, series, lessons. Every path that names students as an audience, a roster or frame recipients holds their relationships with at least `FOR SHARE` before any group, series or lesson, as the [schedule's lock rules](schedule.md#locks-and-jobs) apply per path; chat producers name their recipients from the channel's members read under the channel lock instead (below). Every path that frees or fills a pending-request slot follows this order, including the jobs: code entry, decline, accept, unblock, `join_requests.expire`, student deletion, release at confirmation, un-suppression and invitation acceptance (signed in or through a merge, which can accept a visible pending request) lock the student's user row and then the tutor profiles before touching relationships and requests; `reclaim.expire` locks the user row first, as the owner's choice does, so the two serialise. Tutor deletion is the exception that locks no student's user row: it locks only the tutor's own user row and profile, then relationships, invitations, requests and groups, and from slice 5 its series and lessons. Its discard and revoke steps are statements issued after those locks are held, so they also see the cards and invitations that a concurrent owner's drop, `reclaim.expire` or removal committed while it waited; no invited card or open invitation of the deleted tutor remains. Tutor removal locks the student's user row, then the tutor's profile, then the relationship. So no two transactions wait on each other's rows in a cycle.
- **Channels** (slice 6): every path that changes a channel's membership or writability (linking, confirmation, the reclaim and the owner's choice, removal, stopping studying, ending, reactivation, group creation, adding and removing members, archiving and unarchiving, student and tutor deletion, the 7-day cleanup, the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account, `reclaim.expire` and the purge) locks the channel `FOR UPDATE` after its existing relationship and group locks. Every chat producer locks the channel `FOR SHARE` (an edit, a delete without attachments, a read-marker move, a typing check) or `FOR UPDATE` (a send, and every release of a charge), after the user row of a student or the profile of a tutor, and reads the channel's active members under that lock; those members are the frame recipients. This replaces, for chat, the rule that a frame recipient's relationship is held `FOR SHARE`: every path that removes a recipient also locks the channel, so one lock per message gives the same guarantee. Presence recipients are the one stated exception, read without locks ([authorization of frames](realtime-and-video.md#authorization-of-frames)).
- **Student deletion and final purge channel phase** (slice 6): after the user, all affected tutor profiles, all relationships (discarded ones included), invitations and join requests, lock the groups named by the student's group memberships or channel memberships in identifier order. Under these locks, ensure the direct and group channels needed by the remaining mirror operations, then lock the union of those channels and every channel containing a member row for the student in one statement, globally ordered by channel identifier. Suspended members, ended and discarded links remain in that lock set; do not use the active-member projection to select it. Complete this phase before any message, lesson series or lesson work. The user and profile locks prevent concurrent linking and group membership changes from growing the set; later mirrors only revisit channels already ensured and locked, and final membership removal acquires no new channel lock. Replacement invited cards have no channel. A digest for another student sharing groups takes no deleted-student user lock or tutor profile lock, so per-tutor channel ordering is insufficient.
- **Charge locks** (slice 6): every path that creates or removes a quota charge locks the charged tutors' profiles up front, after the user rows it locks and before any relationship, in identifier order, reading which tutors are charged first without locks and re-checking under the locks (a changed set retries once, then answers `409 conflict` for a request or defers the item to the next run for a job); then the channel `FOR UPDATE`, then the message. `file_usage_bytes` is a column of the tutor profile, so a later profile lock would invert the order against tutor writes. The paths and their locks:
  - **binding** (a send with attachments): the sender's user row when a student, the channel's tutor's profile, the channel;
  - **an author's delete:** the author's user row when a student, the channel's tutor's profile when the message has attachments, the channel, the message;
  - **a tutor's delete:** the tutor's profile, already first, the channel, the message;
  - **the deferred erasure job:** the channel's tutor's profile, the channel, its messages;
  - **a student's purge batches:** the student's user row, already locked by the purge, then the profiles of the tutors charged for the batch's attachments, then their channels;
  - **a tutor's purge batches:** the tutor's user row and profile, as before;
  - `uploads.expire` releases no charge, because pending uploads are not charged.

  The discard paths (removal, stopping studying on a provisional link, a dropped reclaim link, `reclaim.expire`, the 7-day cleanup, the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account, tutor deletion) and a student's ending release no charge in their own transactions, because the erasure is deferred; they add only the channel lock after the locks they already take. Read-marker moves take no profile lock.
- **Upload cleanup intents** (slice 6): after existing business locks, acquire all affected attachment rows in identifier order, then their intents in the same order, then the capacity row and outbox bookkeeping. Never acquire another attachment/account/channel after an intent. Purge requests cleanup before deleting uploading attachments; the journal survives without account/channel identifiers. Recovery and operator retries also lock attachment before intent. No S3 call runs inside these transactions ([durable cleanup](files.md#durable-chat-upload-cleanup)).
- **Presence** (slice 6): lease extensions, releases and expiry cleanup lock existing user rows `FOR KEY SHARE` in identifier order, then initialize and lock account summaries in identifier order, then change per-instance leases. They never take relationship or channel locks and skip already purged accounts. The user locks pass `FOR SHARE` and `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`; waiting for short operator and purge transactions holding `FOR UPDATE` is explicitly approved, without weakening their locks. The account deletion cascades both `user_presence` and `user_presence_instances`; the summary serializes operations on the same account across APIs ([data model](data-model.md#presence-and-digest-bookkeeping)).
- **Frame recipients, signals and notification rows:** a transaction that names a student as a frame recipient reads the relationship row with at least `FOR SHARE`, or, for chat, the channel as above; the paths that name students already lock the relationship in the order above, so the rule adds no new ordering. Signals (`pg_notify`) and notification inserts take no row locks of their own, because notification rows have no foreign keys; they are issued after the transaction's existing locks, at the end of linking, confirmation, the reclaim choice, removal, ending, request creation, release, un-suppression, accept, decline, expiry, unblock, deletion and purge, without changing their lock order. PostgreSQL's notification lock is taken at commit, after every row lock, so it cannot close a cycle with them. The revocation triggers run inside the statements that revoke sessions or change account state, on rows those statements already lock.
- **The tutor's account state under the profile lock:** every path that changes whether a tutor is `active` for linking takes the tutor's profile lock in the same transaction, after the tutor's user row: the tutor's deletion and the operator's block, unblock and restore of a tutor (`ovc-admin users block`, `unblock` and `restore`). Every path that links a student to a tutor or creates a request to one reads the tutor's account state only after it holds that profile lock: invitation sign-up, signed-in acceptance, code entry and sign-up with a code. So does every tutor write that creates, changes or ends a card, a link, a group, a join request decision, a block or the code: creating a card, changing a card (an email change issues a fresh link), withdrawing a card, regenerating a link, sending a link, removing a provisional or reclaim-pending student, ending a relationship, creating, changing, archiving and unarchiving a group, from slice 5 creating, editing and cancelling lessons, from slice 6 every chat write except a read-marker move (sending, editing and deleting messages, and a chat upload's creation and completion), regenerating the code, accepting and declining a join request (with or without the block), and unblocking a student take the profile lock first (before the card or the group row; removal, a decision and an unblock lock the student's user row before it) and answer `401 unauthenticated`, changing nothing, when the tutor is no longer `active`, so a request authenticated before the tutor's deletion or block cannot act after it, also not once an operator restores the account. A path that reads the card, the request or the blocked student before it takes the profile lock answers that read's own refusal (`not_found` or a state conflict) instead when the deletion or block committed before the read; either way it changes nothing. A block or deletion that commits first is therefore seen, and one that commits later waits for the link, so no link or request reaches a tutor after the tutor stopped being `active`. A sign-up with a code whose tutor stopped being `active`, or whose code was regenerated, while it waited creates the account without a request and answers the ordinary `202`, exactly as for a taken email, so the race reveals nothing about the address; an invitation sign-up whose link stopped being usable while it waited creates nothing and answers the same `202`. Linking paths never lock the tutor's user row, and the block and the deletion never lock a student's row, so this adds no cycle. A student's purge locks the profiles of every tutor named by its relationships or channel memberships after its own row and before all its relationships, so a concurrent tutor deletion also discards the new card that the removal gives.
- **Session re-check under the lock:** a request is authenticated once at its start, while confirmation, reset and reclaim revoke sessions inside their own transaction, so a request authenticated before a reclaim could wait on the user row and act after it. One rule therefore applies to every write open to an account whose email is not confirmed, and to every transaction that acts for a signed-in student: all of `PATCH /users/me`, an avatar upload's creation and settling, `POST /auth/logout-all`, the signed-in `POST /auth/verify-email/resend`, `PUT /users/me/email-preferences` and `POST /notifications/read` once they land in slice 4, an unverified tutor's writes to the tutor profile and settings, the library, and library and prompt-image uploads as they land, signed-in acceptance, code entry, a student's ending of a relationship, confirmation from the account's own browser, the reclaim choice, from slice 6 every chat write by a student (sending, editing and deleting messages, moving the read marker, and a chat upload's creation and completion), and any later write the same audience opens. Password change and account deletion are already guarded, because they compare the verified password hash under the same row lock; `POST /auth/logout` revokes only the caller's own session, which a reclaim has already revoked, so it needs no re-check. Each locks the user row first, then reads the session in a separate statement, so it sees a revocation committed while it waited, and re-checks that the session is not revoked and that the account is still in the state the request was authorised for. Otherwise it answers `401 unauthenticated` and changes nothing; an upload whose session was revoked while its object was being stored is settled as discarded (its row is marked deleted and the object's deletion is queued), so it can never be bound. A squatter's request, edit or upload authenticated before a reclaim therefore cannot land after it; a code entry authenticated before the reclaim answers `401` and creates no request.

## Administration command

`ovc-admin` runs on a production host against the production database with operator credentials. It has no HTTP surface. Commands exit non-zero with a message for an unknown email, a wrong role or an impossible state change. The subcommands:

- `ovc-admin users list [--role tutor|student] [--email <substring>] [--state <state>]` prints identifier, role, state, email and creation time.
- `ovc-admin users block <email>` sets the account `blocked`, remembers its previous state and revokes its sessions; the state change signals the revocation through its trigger, so the account's sockets close. A `deleted` account cannot be blocked. Blocking a tutor also takes the tutor's profile lock ([the tutor's account state under the profile lock](#locks-and-the-session-re-check)).
- `ovc-admin users unblock <email>` returns the account to the state it was blocked from. Unblocking a tutor also takes the tutor's profile lock.
- `ovc-admin users restore <email>` returns a `deleted` account before purge to `active`, and restoring a tutor also takes the tutor's profile lock; an unverified account is never `deleted`, because deleting it purges it at once. Once the 30-day retention has passed the purge is due, and the restore is refused as an impossible state change even if the purge has not run yet or has deleted only part of a tutor's rows.
- `ovc-admin tutors set-quota <email> --files <bytes> --recordings <bytes>` changes a tutor's quotas; it refuses student accounts.

- `ovc-admin uploads inspect <attachment-id>` inspects safe cleanup state and paginated operator receipt history, at most 100 receipts per keyset page. Output excludes storage keys, MPU IDs, names and credentials.
- `ovc-admin uploads retry-cleanup <attachment-id> --attempt <uuid>` explicitly requests one bounded cleanup cycle. Reuse the UUID after an uncertain CLI outcome; its retained receipt returns the original identity/status without resetting attempts. Existing pending/in-progress work refuses a new UUID and identifies that cycle. A removed intent is already resolved/not found and is never recreated. No force-forget or provider-fence override exists. Receipt history is not globally capped and remains until safe intent removal ([ADR-0019](../../docs/decisions/0019-durable-upload-cleanup.md)).

Every command writes an audit diagnostic event with the operator's system user name.

As implemented in slice 2:

- **Settings:** the command needs only `OVC_DATABASE_URL` and the logging settings, refuses to run while migrations are pending, and writes its diagnostics to standard error; results go to standard output.
- **Output:** `users list` prints one tab-separated line per account (identifier, role, state, email, creation time in UTC), at most 1000, oldest first; the `--email` filter matches a case-insensitive substring with `%`, `_` and `\` taken literally. The account and quota mutation commands print `done <identifier>`. Upload inspection prints safe intent state, current outbox/generation and receipt rows plus a continuation cursor; retry prints the cycle identity/status, also when refusing overlapping work.
- **Exit codes:** `0` on success, `1` for a refusal (`refused: no account has this email`, `the account's state does not allow this change`, `the account does not have the role this command needs`, `a quota must be between 0 and 2^50 bytes`) or a failure, and `2` with the usage text for an unknown command or malformed arguments. Messages never repeat the email.
- **Block** also discards the account's unused email tokens and queued email requests; from slice 4 the trigger on the account's state signals the revocation that closes its live connections.
- **Unblock** into `unverified` restarts the 7-day clock, so the cleanup job does not remove the account at once. Blocked accounts are never removed by the cleanup or purge jobs.
- **Failures before the command runs** (an unknown system user, an unreachable database, pending migrations) are audited as `admin.command` with outcome `failed`, at INFO like every other outcome, and a refusal by the test-control database checks, or a database URL the driver cannot parse, prints its reason without any part of the URL. A settings refusal is printed as `configuration: <problem>` and is not audited, because logging is configured from those settings. Under `OVC_CLOCK=test` the command uses the shared test clock, like the API and the worker.
- **Email lookup** is case-insensitive and trims spaces; every change locks the user row.
