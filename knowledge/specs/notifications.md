# Notifications specification

Behavior for FR-13 and the worker that delivers it. Decisions: [ADR-0006](../../docs/decisions/0006-background-worker-and-email.md), and [ADR-0014](../../docs/decisions/0014-students-join-through-their-tutor.md) for the relationship emails.

## Events

- A lesson is "moved" when its start or duration changes; other edits notify no one.
- **Audience of homework events** (slice 9): the student recipient of `issue.*` and `submission.*` events is the student who owns the submission, even after leaving the group, and only while `ReadableStudentSubmission`, the one readability predicate of [homework](homework.md#student-readability), lets that student read the submission at that moment: no row, frame or email is produced for a student who cannot, so a student whose link is `reclaim_pending` or discarded gets nothing. Events that remove access (a withdrawal, a marking for erasure) decide their student recipients by that predicate as it held just before the change, under the change's own locks, so the student still gets `issue.withdrawn`. The tutor recipient of `submission.submitted` is the issue's tutor. Fully automatic work becomes checked on submission and sends `submission.checked` with review revision 0. A later publication on checked work that keeps it `checked` sends `submission.review_updated` only when its comments, its corrected file identifiers or its feedback differ from the previous published revision's; a points-only publication sends the student no row, frame or email.
- **Aggregated emails:**
  - when a relationship ends, the cancelled lessons are listed in the `relationship.ended` email, and no separate `lesson.cancelled` emails go out;
  - when a group is archived, in the `group.archived` email, likewise; the email and its in-app row go only to the members on the roster of at least one lesson the archiving cancelled, and every lesson it cancels freezes the same members, so an archiving that cancels nothing, such as a repeated archiving after an unarchiving, notifies nobody. The email says the group's future lessons are cancelled and lists them under the same heading as the relationship emails ("These lessons were cancelled:") (pending owner review);
  - when a tutor deletes their account, in the `tutor.deleted` email, and no other email goes out for that deletion;
  - a provisional link's discarded lessons are cancelled silently, with no email and no in-app row ([lessons and series](schedule.md#lessons-and-series)).
- **Finding the listed lessons at dispatch:** payloads stay identifiers only, and the worker finds each aggregated email's lessons from the cancellation cause, the subject and the instant ([schedule](schedule.md#lessons-and-series)):
  - `relationship.ended` lists the lessons with `cancel_cause = 'relationship_ended'` of that relationship whose `cancelled_at` equals its `ended_at`, read through the relationship's `(relationship_id, starts_at)` index from that instant on, because every lesson it cancelled started after it;
  - `group.archived` lists the lessons with `cancel_cause = 'group_archived'` of that group whose `cancelled_at` equals its `archived_at` and whose roster includes the recipient;
  - `tutor.deleted` lists the lessons with `cancel_cause = 'tutor_deleted'` of that tutor whose `cancelled_at` equals its `deleted_at` and whose roster includes the recipient;
  - the two roster listings read the recipient's roster rows whose `lesson_starts_at` is after the effect's instant through the roster's `(student_id, lesson_starts_at)` index and look each lesson up by its key, one query per kind of listing, so a tutor deletion that cancelled thousands of lessons costs each email the recipient's own later roster rows, never a probe of every lesson the deletion cancelled (pending owner review);
  - `lesson.series_changed` carries the operation identifier and the identifiers of the affected lessons (at most 100 per series operation) and lists those whose roster or current audience includes the recipient at dispatch;
  - `cancelled_at` is written in the same transaction from the value the ending, archiving or deletion statement stored, read back with `RETURNING` at the column's microsecond precision, never from a second clock read, so the equality holds; a relationship reactivated and ended again gets a new `ended_at` and lists only its own cancellations; for a tutor deletion the instant is the deletion's own instant, which the same transaction stored as `deleted_at` through the same driver encoding, so the two are equal at microsecond precision without a read back (lesson effects of tutoring change, pending owner review);
  - each email lists at most 20 lessons in start order and then "and {count} more".
- **Lesson emails** carry the lesson's title, its start in the recipient's profile timezone and language, and its audience label from the [label table](schedule.md#labels-per-role), and link to the lesson page for the description; they never carry the description. The `lesson.series_changed` email lists each listed lesson as its title and start, with "cancelled" after a cancelled one, and links to the first of them; `lesson.moved` and `lesson.cancelled` are sent with normal priority (lesson edits and cancellation change, pending owner review).
- Each event names its tutor, so the student's interface and emails carry the tutor label and colour.
- **Slice 3** sends the `invitation.created`, `relationship.ended` and `tutor.deleted` emails; `relationship.ended` and `tutor.deleted` carry no lesson list until slice 5, and `group.archived` waits for slice 5. **Slice 4** adds the notification table and the in-app rows of the slice 3 events ([in-app producers](#in-app-producers-in-slice-4)). **Slice 5** adds the lesson events, the `group.archived` email and row, the lesson lists of the aggregated emails ([in-app producers](#in-app-producers-in-slice-5)) and the first optional emails, and with them the unsubscribe headers and link, minting tokens at render time and the dispatch-time preference check ([optional emails](#email)). **Slice 9** adds the six homework events with their rows ([in-app producers](#in-app-producers-in-slice-9)), emails and daily caps.
- **Unconfirmed recipients:** no notification email reaches an address whose account is `unverified`: at dispatch every email other than the account emails (confirmation, reset, duplicate sign-up notice) and invitation emails is dropped for such a recipient, including `relationship.ended` and `tutor.deleted`, and removing a provisional link sends nothing.

## Channels

- **In-app:**
  - a notification row per recipient stores identifiers only, never names, titles, message bodies or answers: the recipient, the tutor, the student the event concerns, the event, its subject (the relationship, the join request, from slice 5 the lesson or the group, from slice 9 the submission) and the relationship for the tutor colour, with creation time and read time ([data model](data-model.md#notification)); labels are resolved when the rows are read ([labels and links](#labels-and-links)), so no row keeps a copy of a name that a purge or a later edit should remove;
  - rows are written by the producer (a service or a scheduler job) in its own transaction, with the recipients decided at that moment from the rows it holds locked; email preferences never suppress them;
  - each row has an idempotency key, the same as its email's where the event has one ([keys](#outbox-and-worker)), and is inserted with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`;
  - `notification.new` is signalled through the [broadcaster](realtime-and-video.md#broadcaster) in the same transaction, only when a row was actually inserted, and is delivered once that transaction commits, so rows created by the worker reach connected users too and repeated job runs never duplicate them;
  - **purge:** once the purge of an account commits (the 30-day purge, the 7-day cleanup of an unconfirmed account, or the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account its owner deletes), no notification row names that account as recipient, tutor or student. The rows have no foreign keys; the purge deletes them through indexes inside its batches, and every producer of a row that names an account holds a lock that the purge of that account also takes (the account's user row, its tutor profile, or the relationship or join request row the purge discards or deletes) and checks the state again under it, so a producer waiting behind a purge finds its subject gone and inserts nothing; the final purge transaction deletes the rows naming the account after the user row, whose deletion cascades to the account's join requests, so a producer holding one of those rows (an un-suppression) has either committed before that statement reads the table or finds no row to change;
  - **erasure** (slice 9): when `homework.cleanup` erases an issue marked for erasure, the same batch deletes every in-app row whose subject is one of its submissions (the `issue.*` and `submission.*` events, for the student and the tutor), so a later holder of the account never sees rows of the earlier holder's work ([scheduler](#outbox-and-worker));
  - **retention:** the hourly `notifications.cleanup` job deletes rows older than 90 days through the `created_at` index, in batches of 1,000, at most 10 batches per run, oldest first, and the next run resumes where it stopped.
- **Email:** rendered and sent by the worker through SMTP ([email](#email)).
- **Web push** (from slice 10, off until its gate opens): decided in [ADR-0015](../../docs/decisions/0015-web-push.md), including the send-time recipient check, and specified with the slice 10 plan.

### In-app producers in slice 4

Each row below is written in the producer's transaction with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, and `notification.new` is signalled only when the row was inserted:

| Event | Recipient | Produced when | Key |
|---|---|---|---|
| `invitation.accepted` | the tutor | an invitation links its card: a new account (also while the link is provisional, when the card changes to "Email not confirmed"), a signed-in acceptance, a merge, a reactivation; no second row when the email is later confirmed | `(invitation.accepted, invitation, tutor)` |
| `join_request.created` | the tutor | a request becomes visible to the tutor: created visible, released at confirmation or by the owner's keep after a reclaim, un-suppressed, or reopened in place after an unblock | `(join_request.created, request, tutor)` |
| `join_request.accepted` | the student | the tutor accepts | `(join_request.accepted, request, student)` |
| `join_request.declined` | the student | the tutor declines, with or without a block, as one identical row | `(join_request.declined, request, student)` |
| `relationship.ended` | the other party | either party ends the relationship; nobody when a deletion ends it | `(relationship.ended, relationship, ended_at)`, the key of its email |

- A held or suppressed request produces nothing for the tutor, and a block, a suppression or an unblock produces nothing for the student ([T-33](../../docs/threat-model.md#threats-and-controls)).
- Tutor deletion and student deletion insert no notification rows.

### In-app producers in slice 5

The notification `event` check widens with the five events below. Each row's key is its email's key from the [key table](#outbox-and-worker), and its recipients are decided in the producer's transaction under the [schedule's locks](schedule.md#locks-and-jobs):

| Event | Recipient | `subject_id` | `student_id` | Produced when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lesson.reminder_1h` | each student of the current audience, and the tutor | the lesson | the student recipient; for the tutor's row the student of a one-to-one lesson, `null` for a group lesson | the reminder job queues the 1-hour reminder |
| `lesson.moved` | each student of the current audience | the lesson | the student recipient | one lesson's start or duration changes |
| `lesson.cancelled` | each student of the frozen roster | the lesson | the student recipient | the tutor cancels one lesson |
| `lesson.series_changed` | each student of the roster or current audience of an affected lesson, once per operation | the occurrence the tutor acted on | the student recipient | an edit or cancellation of "this and following" |
| `group.archived` | each member at archiving, when the archiving cancelled at least one lesson | the group | the student recipient | the tutor archives the group and the archiving cancels lessons (pending owner review) |

- `relationship_id` names the recipient's relationship with the tutor, so students see the tutor colour.
- The cancellations of an ending, an archiving or a tutor deletion produce no `lesson.cancelled` row; they are part of the `relationship.ended` and `group.archived` rows (a tutor deletion inserts none), and a provisional link's discarded lessons produce none.

### In-app producers in slice 9

The notification `event` check widens with the six events below. Each row's `subject_id` is the submission, its `student_id` the submission's student, its `tutor_id` the issue's tutor and its `relationship_id` the submission's relationship, for the tutor colour; its key is its email's key from the [key table](#outbox-and-worker), or, for `issue.withdrawn`, which has no email, its own entry there. Recipients are decided in the producer's transaction under the relationships it holds `FOR SHARE` ([locks and the session re-check](accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check)): a tutor path (issuing, withdrawal, publication) locks the submissions' relationships in identifier order after the tutor's profile lock and before any group, lesson or issue, and a student's submission locks its relationship after the student's user row:

| Event | Recipient | Produced when | Key |
|---|---|---|---|
| `issue.created` | each student of the issue | the tutor issues work: one row per submission created | `(issue.created, submission)` |
| `issue.withdrawn` | each student who could read the submission just before the withdrawal | the tutor withdraws the issue | `(issue.withdrawn, submission)` |
| `submission.submitted` | the issue's tutor | a student submits or resubmits work with manual questions | `(submission.submitted, submission, submitted_at)` |
| `submission.checked` | the student, while they can read the submission | work without manual questions is submitted (review revision 0), or the tutor publishes a check | `(submission.checked, submission, review revision)` |
| `submission.returned` | the student, while they can read the submission | the tutor publishes a return | `(submission.returned, submission, review revision)` |
| `submission.review_updated` | the student, while they can read the submission | the tutor publishes a later revision of checked work whose comments, corrected file identifiers or feedback differ from the previous published revision's | `(submission.review_updated, submission, review revision)` |

- Autosaves, draft review saves, deadline and linked-lesson changes, closures, erasures, the reclaim's flag and points-only publications produce no row.
- A student who cannot read the submission at that moment gets no row, so a publication or an issue reaching a `reclaim_pending` link produces nothing for its student.

Historical student homework rows are filtered before pagination, unread counting and read-marker writes while the relationship is `reclaim_pending` or `discarded`, the issue is marked for erasure, or the submission no longer exists. The lifecycle filter deliberately does not require an active issue: `issue.withdrawn` stays visible, with neither title nor link. Reclaim keeps stored rows and their `read_at`; a keep restores them without another `notification.new`. Erasure deletes every homework event row naming the submission, for both recipients, before deleting the submission.

`GET /notifications` resolves each row's label and link at read time, under the rules every other route applies to the caller:

- **Export events** (specified 2026-11-23 by change U1b of the [interface rework plan](../plans/ui-rework.md), pending owner review; pending implementation by E1): the in-app rows and the email name no file, title or address; the `export.requested` email, in the account's language and timezone, says when the request was made, links to `/settings/data` only, never to the archive, and says to delete the export in My data and change the password at once if the account's owner did not request it; it is sent with high priority from one HTML and text template pair in `internal/services/notifications/templates`, with its wording in the catalogue in `internal/core`; its payload holds the export's identifier, the account and the request time, which the email shows (amended by E1b, pending owner review); it renders through the account email template pair; it is not dropped by the unconfirmed-recipient rule, which cannot apply because only a confirmed account can request. The texts of the two in-app events load with the notification panel's chunk ([states](../design/web/states.md#notifications)).
- Lesson titles and, from slice 9, assignment titles are tutor-written text: the same text-only rule applies to them as to names ([titles and descriptions](schedule.md#titles-and-descriptions)).
- **Homework links** (slice 9): `assignment` opens the student's assignment page `/homework/{submissionId}`, and `review` opens the tutor's review screen `/homework/review/{submissionId}`. The generated client, the exhaustive link switch and the six events' texts in the notification panel and page arrive together in the change that adds the events; the texts load with the panel and page in a lazily loaded `notificationEvents` feature section (named apart from the global `notifications` texts, whose section key a feature section cannot share) when adding them to the global language chunk would leave less than 1 KB of `pnpm check:bundle` headroom ([states](../design/web/states.md#notifications)).
- A link whose subject is outside the caller's current scope is `null`, and the row is shown without a link. On `invitation.accepted` and `relationship.ended` rows, a tutor's card label is the card's own display name while the card row exists and its name is not erased, also after it is discarded, and the fixed label above once the name is erased, never the account name; its link is `null` once the card is discarded.
- `label_deleted` is `true` only for a `join_request.created` row of an accepted request whose requesting account is `deleted`; the label of that row is `null`, and a deleted requester of a request that was not accepted is shown like any request outside the tutor's scope. The interface renders its own "Deleted account" text ([states](../design/web/states.md#notifications)).
- Labels are user-written names: the interface renders them as text, never as HTML or links ([T-14](../../docs/threat-model.md#threats-and-controls)), and the row's text never interpolates them ([states](../design/web/states.md#notifications)).

### Notification routes

- **`GET /notifications?limit=&cursor=`** answers `{items: [{id, event, created_at, read_at, label, label_deleted, label_kind, colour, link}], next_cursor, unread_count}` with one schema for both roles: `label` is a string or `null`, `label_deleted` a boolean ([labels and links](#labels-and-links)), `label_kind` (from slice 5) a fixed label code of the [label table](schedule.md#labels-per-role) or `null`, set only with a `null` label, `colour` a palette key or `null` (students only), and `link` is `{kind: student_card | join_requests | my_tutors | lesson | assignment | review | my_data, id}` or `null` (`lesson` from slice 5, `assignment` and `review`, each with a submission identifier, from slice 9, and `my_data` with the export's identifier from E1, pending owner review). Newest first, keyset paginated on `(created_at, id)`, 30 per page by default and at most 100 (the panel asks for 10), `Cache-Control: no-store`. `unread_count` counts at most 100 unread rows, and the interface shows "99+" above 99.
- **`POST /notifications/read`** takes exactly one of `{ids}` (1 to 100 of the caller's rows) or `{through}` (one of the caller's rows: that row and every row of the caller older than it by `(created_at, id)`), and answers `{unread_count}`. It is idempotent. An identifier that is not one of the caller's rows answers `404 not_found` like a random one, before `422 validation_failed` for both fields or neither. It is a write open to unconfirmed accounts, so it locks the account's row and checks the session again ([locks and the session re-check](accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check)), and, when it marked at least one row, it signals `entity.changed` with kind `notification` to the caller's own sockets. An empty `ids` list answers `422` (`invalid`), and one beside `through` counts as both fields. Accepted gap: a row whose `created_at` precedes the `through` row but whose producer commits after the list was shown is marked read without having been shown; the window is one producer transaction, and the row's `notification.new` still arrives, so the client refetches and lists it as read. Closing the gap would need a commit-ordered sequence, which PostgreSQL does not offer without serialising producers.

## Email

- **Templates** exist per event, in Russian and English, as HTML and plain text. They are rendered at send time in the recipient's language and timezone. The account emails (confirmation, reset, duplicate sign-up) share one HTML and one text template in `internal/services/notifications/templates`, rendered with `html/template` and `text/template`, and take their wording from the catalogue in `internal/core`; they contain no user-written text and no counts.
- **Plural rules and dates** arrive in slice 5 with the first emails that show a count or a lesson time, without a dependency: the CLDR cardinal rules for Russian (`one`, `few`, `many`, `other`) and English (`one`, `other`) are hand-written in `internal/core`, unit-tested over the numbers 0, 1, 2, 4, 5, 11, 12, 14, 21, 22, 25, 101 and 111, and used by the email catalogue. Emails show a lesson's start in the recipient's profile timezone and language (Russian month names in the genitive), with the city from the [timezone city table](schedule.md#recurrence-and-timezones) when the recipient's zone differs from the lesson's tutor-side zone, through the hand-written catalogue in `internal/core`.
- **Content:** subject and body include only what the recipient may see; emails to students never contain points or scores. A message digest names channels and counts, never message text. Lesson emails carry the title, the time and the audience label and never the description. Links point to OVC pages, never to provider or storage URLs.
- **Homework emails** (slice 9): `issue.created` (`homework`), `submission.submitted` (`submissions`), and `submission.checked`, `submission.returned` and `submission.review_updated` (`results`) are sent with normal priority from one HTML and text template pair, `homework.html` and `homework.txt` in `internal/services/notifications/templates`, in Russian and English, with their wording in the catalogue in `internal/core`. Each carries the assignment's title, the tutor's account name to a student or the card's own display name to the tutor (never the live account name), the deadline, when one is set, in the recipient's profile timezone and language as lesson times are shown, and one link to the OVC page: `/homework/{submissionId}` for a student and `/homework/review/{submissionId}` for the tutor. They never carry answers, comments, feedback, file names, points or scores. The payload holds the submission identifier and the key's `submitted_at` or review revision; the worker reads everything else at dispatch. The assignment's title is escaped and stripped as lesson titles are.
- **Escaping:** user-written text (names, titles) is HTML-escaped in HTML bodies and appears in plain text only as text, with runs of whitespace and line breaks collapsed; lesson titles also lose the invisible characters of the [title rule](schedule.md#titles-and-descriptions) in both bodies; in subjects and headers it follows the [title rule](schedule.md#titles-and-descriptions).
- **Relationship emails:** the invitation email is written in the language of the account that holds the card's email, otherwise in the tutor's; the tutor's name appears only in its body, as escaped plain text, never as a link or in the subject, and its links are only those the operator line below allows. The `relationship.ended` email to a tutor names the student by the tutor's own card display name, escaped, never by the current account name; for a card created from a join request that is the tutor-owned copy taken at accept time. The `relationship.ended` email is written in the recipient's language; to a student it names the tutor by the tutor's account name and links to My tutors, and to a tutor it links to the student card; its payload holds only the relationship identifier and `ended_at`, from which the worker reads everything else at dispatch. The `tutor.deleted` email is queued in the deletion's transaction for each student whose relationship the deletion ended and whose email is confirmed at that moment, is written in the student's language, names the tutor by the tutor's account name, escaped, and links only to My tutors; the worker reads the tutor and the recipient at dispatch. The three slice 3 emails share one new HTML and text template pair, `relationship.html` and `relationship.txt` in `internal/services/notifications/templates`, with their wording in the catalogue in `internal/core`; the user-written name stands in its own paragraph, with runs of whitespace and line breaks collapsed to single spaces. The invitation email carries, under its body, one fixed catalogue line in the recipient's language naming the operator and linking the privacy policy, the notice that article 18 part 3 of Federal Law 152-FZ requires for a person whose data came from a tutor ([ADR-0024](../../docs/decisions/0024-legal-documents-and-student-data.md)): the operator's name and contact email come from the typed settings `OVC_OPERATOR_NAME` (1 to 200 characters of valid UTF-8 after trimming, with no control, format, line or paragraph separator characters) and `OVC_OPERATOR_EMAIL` (one plain address of at most 254 bytes, with no display name, comment or list), read once by the worker and by the API with `OVC_TEST_CONTROL=1`, whose in-process worker sends email, required at their start-up, which refuses a missing or invalid value with no default and without echoing it, and added to the [configuration table](../architecture/detailed.md#configuration) and `backend/.env.example` with the code that reads them; they never come from user input and are escaped like any other text; the contact address is written as plain text, never as a `mailto:` or other anchor, in both parts; the policy link is `OVC_PUBLIC_URL` with the path `/privacy` and no query, fragment or per-recipient value. The email's only links, in its HTML and its text part alike, are the invitation link and that policy link. A send dropped silently (over the per-recipient cap, or for an address whose account is `blocked` or `deleted`) sends nothing, so it carries no notice; the invitation page links the policy for every card instead ([invitations](accounts.md#invitations)) (pending owner review). The `relationship.ended` email to a tutor and the lesson and homework emails to a tutor write the fixed text of `former_student` or `removed_student` in the recipient's language where the card's display name was erased before dispatch, never the account name; a `relationship.ended` or `submission.submitted` email whose card was discarded before dispatch is obsolete, so only lesson emails reach this case (pending owner review).
- **Headers:**
  - user-written text that reaches a header (names, titles) has CR and LF removed, loses the invisible characters of the [title rule](schedule.md#titles-and-descriptions), and is encoded with RFC 2047;
  - the `Message-ID` is derived from the outbox idempotency key, so a retried send keeps its identifier;
  - confirmation and reset emails also include the identifier of the token minted for the send, so a re-minted token is a distinct message; invitation emails also include the invitation identifier; an unsubscribe token has no identifier, so a retried optional email keeps its `Message-ID` although its token is minted again.
- **Emails with tokens:**
  - invitation emails carry the card's one open invitation link, whose token the worker decrypts from the stored ciphertext at dispatch ([invitations](accounts.md#invitations)); a row whose ciphertext the current key cannot open becomes `dead` with `permanent`; the payload holds the invitation identifier and the send count, and the `Message-ID` also includes the invitation identifier;
  - confirmation and reset emails have their token minted by the worker in a committed transaction just before sending, invalidating earlier tokens of the same purpose, so a retried send mints and commits a new token first;
  - no outbox payload ever contains a token.
- **Optional emails** (the groups `lesson_reminders` and `lesson_changes` from slice 5, `message_digest` from slice 6, and `homework`, `submissions` and `results` from slice 9) carry one-click unsubscribe headers and a visible link:
  - `List-Unsubscribe` points to `https://<OVC origin>/api/v1/unsubscribe?token=…`;
  - `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`;
  - both bodies carry a visible "Turn off these emails" link to `/unsubscribe?token=…`;
  - one token is minted per send at render time through the signer in `internal/core`, naming the recipient's own account and the email's preference group; it is never stored, never placed in a payload and never logged;
  - DKIM signing is the provider's, and must cover both headers; the local sink signs nothing, so the deployment checks of slice 10 confirm that the provider signs them.
- **Sender:** a fixed address on the OVC domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC. The SMTP connection uses a 30-second timeout.
- **Bounces and complaints** reported by the provider mark the address undeliverable. That stops optional emails until the user saves their email preferences again. How bounces arrive depends on the provider chosen ([open questions](../architecture/detailed.md#open-implementation-questions)); the undeliverable check at dispatch lands with the bounce intake in slice 10, because no mark can be set before it. The chosen provider must keep message logs for at most 30 days.
- **Tests** use the database mail sink, which the [test harness](../../docs/development.md#end-to-end-test-harness) can read; from slice 5 the sink also stores each email's headers as sent, so tests read unsubscribe tokens from the emails themselves.

## Dispatch-time checks

Each **email** is decided again when the worker sends it, not only when it is queued (historical in-app homework rows use the lifecycle filter above). The worker loads the recipients, preferences and subjects of each claimed row in its own queries, row by row (the owner accepted row-by-row reads on 2026-10-27, and a measurement at the slice 5 review decides whether batching per claimed batch is needed; pending owner review). It drops a row, marking it `done` with reason `obsolete`, when:

- the recipient is `blocked` or `deleted`, or the address is undeliverable for an optional email;
- the recipient is `unverified` and the email is neither an account email (confirmation, reset, duplicate sign-up notice) nor an invitation email; each email builder reports the state of the recipient's account (for an invitation, the account that holds the card's email, if any), and the dispatcher applies the account-state parts of this rule and the previous one (blocked, deleted and unverified recipients) to every kind; the undeliverable check for optional emails lands with the bounce intake in slice 10;
- the recipient's preference group is off; a missing preference row means on. From slice 5 the dispatcher reads the recipient's preference group for each row before it reserves a daily-cap slot and records `email.dropped` (DEBUG, `reason` `preference`);
- the recipient is a student over the daily cap of lesson emails: one cap of 20 per student per UTC day covers the student-addressed `lesson_reminders` and `lesson_changes` emails together; a tutor's emails about their own lessons are not capped, because the tutor's own write limit bounds them. The dispatcher reserves the slot only after every other check of this list has passed (eligibility, obsolescence, preference, the tutor's state), immediately before sending, atomically in a per-recipient, per-UTC-day counter, as the rate-limit windows do ([rate limit counter](data-model.md#rate-limit-counter)), and conditionally: the counter is incremented only while it is below 20, so an email dropped over the cap holds no slot; when the send fails, transiently or permanently, it releases the slot (`ReleaseRateLimitSlot`) of its own reservation, naming that reservation's `window_start`, so a send failing after UTC midnight never decrements the next day, and a retried send and an obsolete row use no slot; the hourly cleanup of rate-limit windows removes old counters. An email over the cap is dropped (`obsolete`, recorded as `email.dropped` with `reason` `daily_cap`), while its in-app row still arrives; the 24-hour reminder has no in-app row, so a 24-hour reminder over the cap is lost. Residual: an ambiguous or crashed send may count once more or once less: an SMTP timeout after the provider accepted the message releases the slot and the retry delivers the email again, and a crash between the reservation and the send uses a slot for an email that was not sent;
- the recipient is over a daily cap of homework emails (slice 9, [T-31](../../docs/threat-model.md#threats-and-controls)): one cap of 20 per student per UTC day covers the `homework` and `results` emails together, across all of the student's tutors, as the lesson cap does, in its own rate limit counter `homework_emails`, separate from the lesson cap's; one cap of 50 per tutor per UTC day, the counter `submission_emails`, covers the `submissions` emails. Each slot is reserved, released and cleaned up exactly as for the lesson cap, after every other check of this list; an email over its cap is dropped (`obsolete`, recorded as `email.dropped` with `reason` `daily_cap`), while its in-app row still arrives, with the same residual;
- the event is a student-addressed lesson email (a reminder, `lesson.moved`, `lesson.cancelled`, `lesson.series_changed`, and `group.archived` from the lesson effects of tutoring change, owner's decision of 2026-10-28, pending owner review) and the lesson's tutor is not `active`, for example blocked by the operator: the email is dropped (`obsolete`, recorded as `email.dropped` with `reason` `tutor_inactive`), the lesson is not cancelled, and emails queued after an unblock are sent as usual;
- the event is a student-addressed homework email (`issue.created`, `submission.checked`, `submission.returned`, `submission.review_updated`) and the submission's issuing tutor is not `active`, blocked by the operator or with their deletion started: the email is dropped (`obsolete`, recorded as `email.dropped` with `reason` `tutor_inactive`, group `homework` or `results`) after the obsolescence and audience checks and before the preference read and the `homework_emails` slot reservation, so it holds no slot, while its in-app row still arrives; an email dropped while the tutor is blocked is not sent after an unblock, and one queued before a block but dispatched after the unblock is sent. A tutor's deletion closes their students' not-started, draft and returned submissions, so an `issue.created` email for such work is already obsolete through the closed rule below and logs no `tutor_inactive` drop. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-15 and the slice 10 change approved on 2026-11-16 (slice 10 plan, D12), pending owner review;
- the recipient is no longer in the audience the event was about (for lesson, homework and group events). For the lesson events of slice 5 and the homework events of slice 9:
  - reminders and `lesson.moved`: the lesson's current audience, so a student whose relationship ended, became `reclaim_pending` or was discarded, or who left the group, gets nothing; the tutor stays a recipient of their own lesson's reminders while the tutor is `active`;
  - `lesson.cancelled`: the frozen roster, except while the lesson's one-to-one link is `reclaim_pending`, when the email is obsolete, as the link's lessons are hidden from the account then (lesson effects of tutoring change, pending owner review);
  - `lesson.series_changed`: the roster or the current audience of a listed lesson;
  - `group.archived`: the members at archiving, on the roster of a lesson it cancelled, whose relationship with the tutor is still `active` at dispatch;
  - the student-addressed homework emails of slice 9 (`issue.created`, `submission.checked`, `submission.returned`, `submission.review_updated`): the submission's student while `ReadableStudentSubmission` lets them read the submission at dispatch, so a withdrawn or erased issue, a `reclaim_pending` or discarded link or a purged submission makes the email obsolete;
  - `submission.submitted`: the issue's tutor while the card of the submission's relationship is in the tutor's scope;
- the subject is obsolete:

  | Event | Obsolete when |
  |---|---|
  | Lesson reminder | The lesson is not `scheduled`, its schedule revision differs from the one in the key, or the send moment is more than 15 minutes past the reminder moment |
  | `lesson.moved` | The lesson's schedule revision has advanced again; the newer change sends its own email |
  | Confirmation | The email is already confirmed, or a newer confirmation request exists |
  | Password reset | A newer reset request exists |
  | `issue.created` | The issue is withdrawn, or the submission is `closed` |
  | `submission.submitted` | The submission is no longer `submitted` at the `submitted_at` in the key: it was checked or returned, or submitted again, which queues its own email |
  | `recording.held_quota` | The recording is no longer `held_quota` |
  | Message digest | No listed channel still has a message within its bounds that the recipient, still an active member, has not read and that is neither deleted nor erased |
  | Invitation | The invitation is used, revoked (also by the 90-day erasure of the card's email, pending owner review) or expired, its send count has moved on, or the tutor is no longer `active` |
  | `relationship.ended` | The relationship is no longer `ended` at the `ended_at` the email was queued for: it was reactivated, or reactivated and ended again, which queues its own email |
  | `tutor.deleted` | The tutor is no longer `deleted` |
  | Any event | The subject row no longer exists (it was purged) |

`lesson.cancelled`, `lesson.series_changed` and `group.archived` are always sent while the recipient remains eligible and under the daily cap; a `group.archived` email is sent also after the group was unarchived, listing the lessons its archiving cancelled, because unarchiving restores none of them (owner's decision of 2026-10-28, pending owner review).

## Reminders

- **Schedule:** a scheduler job runs every minute.
- **Window:** for each reminder kind with offset `o` (24 hours or 1 hour), it selects `scheduled` lessons whose start lies in `(now + o − 15 minutes, now + o]` and whose remembered reminder revision for that kind differs from their schedule revision. It uses a partial index on lesson start over scheduled lessons, 50 lessons at a time.
- **Rows:** each lesson is handled in its own transaction, which locks the audience's relationships, the group, the series and the lesson in the [schedule's lock order](schedule.md#locks-and-jobs) and decides the recipients under those locks: the current audience and the tutor. It writes one in-app row (for the 1-hour reminder) and one outbox row per recipient, with idempotency key `(event, lesson, recipient, schedule revision)` and `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. In the same transaction it records the lesson's reminder revision for that kind.
- **Progress:** a run keeps taking batches until no lesson in the window is left or its 20-second time budget is used. It takes the one-hour kind first and the 24-hour kind with what is left of the same budget, so a burst of 24-hour reminders never delays a one-hour reminder, and a 24-hour reminder left over is taken by a later run within its 15-minute window (pending owner review). The revision marker means a large burst of lessons starting together is worked through completely, never reselected.
- **Late scheduling:** a lesson scheduled or moved inside a reminder window gets only the reminders still ahead of it; no other email stands in for a missed reminder, and `lesson.moved` tells the audience of a move.
- **Lateness:** a reminder whose window has passed is never selected, which bounds lateness to 15 minutes without separate skip records.
- **Moved lessons** get a new revision and therefore new keys; reminders queued for the old revision become obsolete at dispatch.

## Message digest

Slice 6. The bookkeeping lives in `digest_pending`, `digest_state` and the member rows' `digested_through_seq` ([data model](data-model.md#presence-and-digest-bookkeeping)), never on the user row; the [slice 6 plan](../plans/slice-6.md#3-decisions-the-plan-proposes) records the alternatives.

- **Aggregate presence:** the flag and digest read the account summary in `user_presence`, recomputed from all API instance leases, using the same injected business clock as lease writes. A local close cannot make a connected account on another API eligible; cleanup preserves historical real `last_seen_at`, never a future lease deadline. Multi-instance clock tests wait for the absolute-set acknowledgements of every API before any send or digest execution ([presence and typing](realtime-and-video.md#presence-and-typing)).
- **Flag:** a send inserts a `digest_pending` row, advancing a sequence-backed revision on conflict while retaining the earliest pending time, for each active member of the channel whose `online_until` has passed, in the send transaction under the channel lock, without locking a user row. Reading the member's channels up to their latest messages clears only the captured flag revision, taken before the unread check; PostgreSQL rechecks that revision after a concurrent update. A new message either invalidates the captured revision or recreates pending after the clear.
- **Sending:** the `messages.digest` job runs every minute on the injected clock. It takes members through the `(pending_since)` index whose flag is at least 10 minutes old, whose `online_until` is at least 10 minutes past and whose last digest is at least 30 minutes old, 200 per batch within a 20-second budget, each member in its own transaction. Business time controls eligibility; a separate injected wall clock and a cancellable execution context bound connection acquisition and SQL/lock waits. Capture the pending revision before enumerating channels. Lock the existing recipient user `FOR KEY SHARE` first, then channels `FOR SHARE` in identifier order, affected member rows in identifier order, digest state and pending. Recheck eligibility after serialization. Missing accounts or pending rows are skipped; a changed revision rolls back the whole transaction and defers the member. Never acquire additional channel/member locks after pending.
- **Selecting by sequence:** per channel the member is an active member of at that moment, the digest counts the messages whose `seq` is above both the member's `last_read_seq` and `digested_through_seq`, that were created after the member's `last_seen_at`, and that are neither deleted nor erased; it then sets `digested_through_seq` to the channel's `last_seq` read under the lock. A send holds the channel `FOR UPDATE` and upserts pending recipients in user-identifier order. Each insert/update obtains a non-reused sequence-backed revision, including after deletion and recreation. A send in a newly added channel either changes the captured revision before the digest checks it, or waits on pending and recreates the flag after deletion. A stale candidate cannot hide that message. Digest-state serialization and eligibility rechecking also prevent two overlapping worker runs from producing separate windows for the same work.
- **Building the digest:**
  - with no message counted, the job clears the flag and sends nothing;
  - otherwise, in the member's transaction, the job writes one outbox row keyed `(messages.digest, member, digest time)`, where the digest time is the job's `now`, which it records as `digest_state.last_digest_at` (at least 30 minutes after the previous one, so keys never repeat), with the per-channel sequence bounds in its payload, records the channels' `digested_through_seq`, and clears the flag.
- **Content:** each channel by the recipient's own label from the [label table](realtime-and-video.md#labels-per-role) and its count of messages within its bounds that are still unread and neither deleted nor erased, at most 20 channels and then "and {count} more"; never message text, previews, senders or file names. Dispatch aggregates all bounded channel counts in one query, returns at most twenty label rows with the total eligible-channel count, and does not perform a query per payload channel. For a student, messages at or below a retained provisional-through boundary are excluded at dispatch, so an outbox item queued before reclaim cannot expose past provisional messages after keep. This floor does not apply to the tutor. Labels are escaped and stripped as lesson titles are. At dispatch the worker re-checks for each listed channel that the recipient is still an active member, and drops the email (`obsolete`, recorded as `email.dropped` with `reason` `obsolete_digest`) when no listed channel has such a message left.
- **Optional and bounded:** the digest belongs to the `message_digest` group, so it carries the one-click unsubscribe headers and the visible link, with a token minted at render time; it counts against no daily cap, and the 30-minute rule bounds it to 48 a day. A digest to an account whose email is not confirmed is dropped like every optional email.
- **Counts:** unread counts include deleted and erased messages, which stay in the list as placeholders; digests count only messages that still have a body.

## Preferences and unsubscribe

Slice 4 stores preferences, mints and verifies tokens, and serves the routes and pages; the worker mints tokens from slice 5, when the first optional email carries them, and the test control route that minted them for tests in slice 4 is removed then, so tests read tokens from the emails in the sink.

- **Preference groups** per role, listed in this fixed order and all on by default:
  - tutors: `lesson_reminders`, `submissions`, `message_digest`;
  - students: `lesson_reminders`, `lesson_changes`, `homework`, `results`, `message_digest`.
- **Routes:** `GET /users/me/email-preferences` and `PUT /users/me/email-preferences` use `{groups: [{group, enabled}]}`, listing the caller's role's groups in that order, one schema for both roles. `PUT` takes a partial list and answers the full one; a group of the other role answers `422 validation_failed` with `not_allowed`, and a group named twice with `invalid`. A save writes one row per named group and locks the account's row and checks the session again first ([locks and the session re-check](accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check)). Both answer with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Saving preferences also clears an undeliverable mark once bounce intake exists (slice 10).
- **Unsubscribe token:**
  - `v1.<payload>.<mac>`, each part in unpadded base64url: the payload is the account identifier (16 bytes), the group (1 byte: 1 `lesson_reminders`, 2 `lesson_changes`, 3 `homework`, 4 `results`, 5 `submissions`, 6 `message_digest`; these codes never change while tokens that carry them can be valid) and the expiry (8 bytes, Unix seconds, big-endian); the MAC is an untruncated HMAC-SHA-256 over the payload under a key derived by HKDF-SHA-256 from `OVC_UNSUBSCRIBE_SIGNING_SECRET` with the info string `unsubscribe`, so the purpose is bound to the key;
  - minted at dispatch, through the signer in `internal/core`, with an expiry 90 days after the send; it is never stored, never logged and never placed in an outbox payload;
  - parsed strictly, and its MAC compared in constant time, before any query, so a forged token costs no database work: any byte of the payload or MAC outside the unpadded base64url alphabet (`A`–`Z`, `a`–`z`, `0`–`9`, `-`, `_`), a line break or a space included, refuses the token, and each part is decoded strictly, so non-zero trailing bits refuse it too and no token has a second spelling;
  - its payload is readable, not encrypted: it shows the account's opaque identifier to anyone holding the link;
  - it never signs the user in, sets no cookie and returns no token.
- **The key:** `OVC_UNSUBSCRIBE_SIGNING_SECRET` is a dedicated secret of at least 32 bytes, required by the API and the worker from the change that adds it and refused when it equals `OVC_JWT_SECRET` or `OVC_FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY`; rotating the JWT secret signs everyone out by design and must not also invalidate 90-day unsubscribe links ([configuration](../architecture/detailed.md#configuration)).
- **Two entry points:**
  - **Mail clients** performing RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe send `POST /api/v1/unsubscribe?token=…` with the body `List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`.
  - **People** opening the link in the email body land on the web page `/unsubscribe?token=…`, which follows the [token page rule](../design/web/navigation.md#token-pages). It calls `GET /api/v1/unsubscribe/preview?token=…`, which answers only `{group}` and changes nothing. The page shows a confirmation button that sends the same `POST`.
- **`POST /unsubscribe?token=`** accepts no body, or a body of at most 1 KiB that is `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` or `multipart/form-data` (media types parsed with their parameters, such as `charset` or `boundary`) holding exactly the field `List-Unsubscribe` with the value `One-Click`, and answers `204`. Another content type answers `415 unsupported_media_type`; a form or multipart body without that field, with another value or with any other field answers `422 validation_failed` (`body.List-Unsubscribe`). It upserts the one preference row without locking the user row; the foreign key takes `FOR KEY SHARE`, so a concurrent purge either finishes first, and the upsert's foreign-key violation answers `422 token_invalid`, or waits for the upsert; the upsert that waits behind a purge holding the user row, or behind the purge's deletion of an existing preference row, also ends in that violation and answers `422 token_invalid`; when a preference row already exists and the purge only holds the user row, the upsert changes no key column, waits for nothing, commits first and answers `204`, and the purge's cascade then deletes the row.
- **Refusals,** the same on the preview and on the `POST`: every invalid, expired, forged or malformed token, and a token of an account that no longer exists or is `deleted` or `blocked`, answers `422 token_invalid` and changes nothing.
- **Order of checks:** the token's format and MAC (no query); then, for a token with a valid MAC, a limit of 60 requests per hour per account and group counted across both routes (`429 rate_limited` with `Retry-After`), because mail providers send one-click `POST`s from shared addresses, so a per-address limit would refuse unrelated recipients; then the account and the change. An invalid token costs no query and is refused before any limit; a per-address limit on invalid tokens is added only if a measurement asks for it.
- **Behavior:** the `POST` is idempotent and repeatable. Nothing changes on any `GET`, so link scanners cannot unsubscribe anyone. An unsubscribe changes only the preference, never an undeliverable mark. Both routes send `Cache-Control: no-store` and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`; access records carry only route templates, and the reverse proxy planned for slice 10 must redact the `token` query parameter of `/unsubscribe` and of every path under `/api/v1/unsubscribe` (the confirmation and its preview) in its logs.
- **Required emails** ignore preferences.

## Outbox and worker

- **Outbox rows** are written in the same transaction as the change that causes them. Each row has:
  - lane (`email`, `provider_call`, `recording_copy`, `storage_delete`) and priority (`high` for reminders, confirmation, reset and invitation emails; `normal` otherwise);
  - kind: the event name for emails, or the effect name;
  - payload of identifiers only;
  - idempotency key, unique, inserted with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`;
  - status (`pending`, `in_progress`, `done`, `dead`);
  - attempts, next attempt time, lease token and lease expiry, last error code.
- **Idempotency keys:**

  | Event or effect | Key |
  |---|---|
  | Reminders, `lesson.moved` | `(event, lesson, recipient, schedule revision)` |
  | `lesson.cancelled` | `(event, lesson, recipient)` |
  | `lesson.series_changed` | `(event, operation identifier, recipient)`; the identifier is generated per request and kept in the payload with the identifiers of the affected lessons (at most 100) |
  | `group.archived` | `(event, group, archived_at, recipient)` |
  | `relationship.ended` | `(event, relationship, ended_at)` |
  | `issue.created`, `issue.withdrawn` | `(event, submission)`; one submission has one student, so the key names the recipient |
  | `submission.submitted` | `(event, submission, submitted_at)` |
  | `submission.checked`, `submission.returned`, `submission.review_updated` | `(event, submission, review revision)` |
  | `signup.duplicate_attempt` | `(event, account, UTC date)` |
  | Confirmation and reset | `(event, email token request)`, a row recorded when the request is accepted |
  | Invitations | `(invitation.created, invitation, send count)`; each send of the same open link increments the count, so a resend is a new key |
  | `tutor.deleted` | `(event, tutor, deleted_at, recipient)`; the payload holds the tutor, `deleted_at` in microseconds and the recipient |
  | Digests | `(messages.digest, member, digest time)`, the digest time being the job's `now` recorded as `last_digest_at` |
  | `export.requested` | `(event, export)` |
  | `export.ready`, `export.failed` | `(event, export, build)` |
  | Export intent cleanup | `storage_delete:export:<build id>:intent:0` and, once required by definite acknowledgement, `:intent:1` ([ADR-0025](../../docs/decisions/0025-export-upload-cleanup-adoption.md)) |
  | Export object deletion | `storage_delete:export:<build id>` |
  | Chat intent cleanup | `storage_delete:<attachment>:intent:0` and, once required by definite acknowledgement, `storage_delete:<attachment>:intent:1`; fixed identities survive in the journal |
  | Explicit upload cleanup retry | One immutable outbox identity per caller-supplied attempt UUID; replay never grants fresh attempts |
  | Recording copies | `(recording, attempt generation)` |
  | Everything else | `(event, subject, recipient)` |

- **The scheduler loop and one loop per lane**, each in its own goroutine:
  - **Dispatcher:**
    - **claiming:** each lane has a slot count equal to its concurrency. Its loop claims up to its free slots of due rows, high priority first, in a short transaction (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`), starts each claimed effect at once, and claims again as soon as a slot frees; when nothing is due it polls every second. A claim increments `attempts`, sets `in_progress` with a fresh lease token and a lease of 5 minutes (60 minutes for `recording_copy`, renewed while it streams), and commits. A row is claimed only when a slot is free, so a claimed row starts at once; a slot is released only after the row's completion or retry update, so one worker never claims a row twice. A claim that takes longer than 30 seconds fails like any other claim, in the continuous loops and in `Tick`. In the continuous loops a row's completion or retry is timed at the instant its effect finishes; in `Tick` it uses `now`. Lanes run side by side, so a slow email never stalls its own lane beyond its slot or another lane at all. The lanes registered in slice 4 are `email` (10 slots) and `storage_delete` (4);
    - **no `NOTIFY` wake-up:** a wake-up when rows are inserted is deferred until a measurement asks for it. It would need a second listening connection, in the worker, with its own lifecycle and loss handling, and a `pg_notify` in every transaction that inserts an outbox row would put each such commit on PostgreSQL's notification lock; polling every second keeps a reminder far inside NFR-4.5's two minutes;
    - **SMTP:** connections are reused across sends, and the provider's sustained send rate is recorded when the provider is chosen, because NFR-4.5 depends on it;
    - **performing:** each effect runs outside any transaction, with a timeout shorter than its lease (30 seconds for email and provider calls);
    - **completing:** marking a row `done`, or scheduling its retry, is a conditional update on its lease token, so a reclaimed row's late finisher changes nothing;
    - **retries:** use exponential backoff from 30 seconds up to 1 hour, with jitter from an injected random source;
    - **dead rows:** after 8 attempts, or when a lease expires on a row that already has 8 attempts, the row becomes `dead` and an `ERROR` event is emitted. A failure that can never succeed (an outbox kind with no email defined, or a message the mail adapter refuses as malformed) makes the row `dead` at once with the error code `permanent`; an email builder treats a payload it cannot parse the same way. Where an entity owns the effect it records the failure (a recording becomes `failed`); dead emails have no entity effect;
    - **lanes:** each has its own concurrency limit (email 10, provider calls 4, recording copies 1, storage deletes 4), so long copies never delay emails;
    - **the export lane** (specified 2026-11-23 by change U1b of the [interface rework plan](../plans/ui-rework.md), pending owner review; pending implementation by E1): not an outbox lane but a claim loop over `queued` exports with 2 slots across all users, polling every 5 seconds when none is due, started like the other lanes through the recovering goroutine helper; each build runs outside transactions within its 2-hour budget, as [exports](files.md#building) defines, and on shutdown a running build is cancelled at once, set `failed` with `error`, its cleanup requested and `export.failed` inserted for its owner, who may request again because a failed export does not count toward the 24-hour limit, so a restart never resumes a half-written archive (amended by E1b, pending owner review); long builds can delay later ones, a queue T-20 records;
    - **the scheduler loop** runs due jobs and reclaims expired leases every second;
    - **failures:** a failure of the worker's own infrastructure (claiming, completing) ends the run with an error; on shutdown no lane claims, and effects already started finish within their timeout;
    - **leases:** rows with an expired lease are reclaimed.
  - **One pass:** `Tick(now)` runs the due scheduler jobs and reclaims expired leases, then runs every lane's loop side by side until no row is due at `now` and none is in flight, so a second tick at the same instant has no further effect. A pass stops claiming after 4 minutes of wall time, below the 5-minute lease, so a row claimed late in a long pass never starts with an expired lease; this claim budget applies to `Tick` only, because the continuous loops claim only into free slots. A panic in an effect frees its slot and counts as a failed attempt. With `OVC_CLOCK=system` the worker process runs the continuous loops; with `OVC_CLOCK=test` only `POST /__test__/tick` runs `Tick`, at the shared clock's instant, and the clock and tick routes exist only in that mode. On shutdown the worker claims nothing new and lets effects already started finish within their timeout.
  - **Message identifiers:** an email's `Message-ID` is derived from its outbox idempotency key and the host of `OVC_PUBLIC_URL`, so a retried send keeps the same identifier.
  - **Scheduler:** runs jobs whose due time has passed, recording each run in a job-run table, so hourly and daily jobs catch up once after downtime. The jobs registered on the shared scheduler loop run one after another, so a job that uses its whole time budget delays the jobs after it in that pass by at most that budget; a job registered to run on its own loop (horizon extension) runs on a scheduler loop of its own, started through the recovering goroutine helper, so its budget delays no other job. `Tick` runs every due job, both kinds, one after another in registration order (pending owner review). A job that fails is recorded as `failed` and reported as `job.run` at ERROR with an error kind, and the pass continues with the other jobs and the lanes (an owner decision): only a failure of the worker's own infrastructure, such as recording a job run or claiming rows, ends the pass. A purge that cannot remove one account skips past it within the run, so the accounts behind it are still purged.
    - **Every minute:** reminders, digests (`messages.digest`, from slice 6, 200 members per batch within 20 seconds), the lesson status job (its bounds are in the [schedule](schedule.md#lesson-status-and-join-window)), and from slice 6 `chat.erase_channels` (within 20 seconds; an empty run is one partial-index probe, the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review, [channels](realtime-and-video.md#channels)).
    - **Every minute, daily upload accounting:** `uploads.daily_cleanup`, with an independent 20-second cancellable pass, at most 100 students and 1,000 aggregates, at most 100 rows per delete statement. Student locks precede aggregate locks and skip locked candidates; past days remain while an upload of that day is uploading ([daily upload accounting](files.md#durable-daily-upload-accounting)). It is registered in production composition as a separate scheduler job; `Tick` invokes the same job.
    - **Every minute, upload recovery:** `uploads.recover`, one 30-second monotonic pass, at most twenty batches of 200 candidates, each with a one-second budget capped by the remaining pass. Its retained keyset cursor advances after failed candidate transactions and wraps after keyspace exhaustion. Partial failures are recorded while later work proceeds; no storage call runs in the scan transaction ([durable cleanup](files.md#durable-chat-upload-cleanup)).
    - **Every minute, homework cleanup** (slice 9): `homework.cleanup`, within a 20-second budget, selects only marked rows, through the partial indexes on `issues.erase_pending_since` and `submissions.release_pending_since`, so an empty run is two index probes. It erases each issue marked for erasure with its submissions, answers, review revisions and their `issue_snapshot`, `answer` and `review_item` references and charges, decreasing the tutor's snapshot total by the issue's `snapshot_bytes`, and in the same batch deletes the in-app notification rows whose subject is an erased submission (the `issue.*` and `submission.*` events, for both roles) through the homework subject index of the [notification table](data-model.md#notification), so a later holder of the account never sees rows of the earlier holder's work; and for each submission with `release_pending_since` it removes the `answer` references created after the submission's latest `submitted_at` (all of them when it was never submitted), with their charges, and clears the mark. It works in batches of 500 rows, each in its own transaction that locks the charged tutor's profile first, then the rows in the [lock order](accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check), and queues each file's `storage_delete` with its last reference, as `chat.erase_channels` does for channels ([homework references and charges](files.md#homework-references-and-charges)); pending `answer` and `corrected` uploads scoped to an erased submission go through the intent-routed deletion of [upload flow](files.md#upload-flow) item 11. A run keeps taking batches until no marked row is left or its budget is spent, and the next run continues.
    - **Every minute, export recovery** (specified 2026-11-23 by change U1b of the [interface rework plan](../plans/ui-rework.md), pending owner review; pending implementation by E1): `exports.recover`, within a 20-second budget, fails every `building` export whose build started more than 2 hours and 15 minutes ago by the injected clock, at most 100 per run through the partial index on `build_started_at`, each in its own transaction that locks the export row and then its intent and requests cleanup ([ADR-0025](../../docs/decisions/0025-export-upload-cleanup-adoption.md)); export intents are otherwise recovered by `uploads.recover`.
    - **Every 30 minutes:** recording reconciliation.
    - **Hourly:**
      - recording and upload expiry; `invitations.expire` (500 per run; clears the ciphertext of expired invitations), `join_requests.expire` (batches of 200, at most 5 per run; a failing request is skipped, and a run that stops at its bound is resumed where it stopped by the next run) and `reclaim.expire` (batches of 100 accounts, at most 1,000 per run; drops reclaim items left unchosen for 7 days); `cards.erase_emails` (batches of 100 cards, at most 1,000 per run; erases an unaccepted card's email 90 days after it was entered, [retention of card data](accounts.md#tutorstudent-relationships), pending owner review);

      - `exports.expire` (specified 2026-11-23 by change U1b of the [interface rework plan](../plans/ui-rework.md), pending owner review; pending implementation by E1): batches of 100, at most 1,000 per run, sets `ready` exports past `expires_at` to `expired` and queues their objects' deletion, and deletes `expired` rows 3 days after `expires_at` and `failed` rows 3 days after `built_at` (or `requested_at`), each in a transaction that locks the export row first ([retention and invalidation](files.md#retention-and-invalidation));

### Intent-aware storage deletion

Chat uploads with a durable intent use immutable attachment/key/generation identity, or an explicit operator attempt UUID, rather than a snapshot of the MPU identifier. Effects read the current journal identifier under attachment-then-intent locks, perform storage outside transactions, and recheck identity and the late NULL-to-ID transition before finalization. Each effect has one 30-second monotonic budget. Generation 0 cannot overwrite generation 1 or erase its required flag; pending/bound ownership transferred by publication is protected from stale work.

Eight attempts and existing backoff apply per fixed cycle. Lease expiry consumes the same budget. The intent owns its result independently of outbox retention: dead or missing expired outbox rows do not authorize a new automatic cycle. Recovery reconciles safe results without inventing identities. At most two automatic generations are possible. A manual cycle requires an explicit idempotent local operator request, and active manual/automatic cycles exclude another admission; a required final generation waits durably. Uncertain completion remains uncertain after successful Delete, without an automatic retry loop. See [ADR-0019](../../docs/decisions/0019-durable-upload-cleanup.md).

Export archives ([ADR-0025](../../docs/decisions/0025-export-upload-cleanup-adoption.md), pending owner review; pending implementation by E1) use the kind `export_intent` with the payload `{build_id, storage_key, generation}` under the same rules, with the export row (found by its build identifier, if it still exists) in place of the attachment in the lock order and the recheck, and the kind `export_object` with the payload `{export_id, build_id, storage_key}` for a published archive, which deletes the object unconditionally, because its row was deleted, withdrawn or set `expired` with its storage location cleared in the transaction that queued it, and no intent exists for it.

### Meeting cleanup terminal receipts

Meeting cleanup uses the [lease-fenced transaction](data-model.md#lease-fenced-meeting-cleanup-completion) for its durable outcome. `CompleteMeetingRecovery` requires a typed result and evidence revision; generic `Complete` refuses this kind. Terminal `Fail` and expired eighth-lease reclamation save the cycle's exhausted receipt atomically with the dead outbox row. Earlier failures retain the existing backoff and eight-attempt budget. Other effect kinds retain their existing completion, retry, reclaim and retention behavior. The worker dispatches `meeting_cleanup` only through its typed meeting handler on `provider_call`; absent or mismatched handlers fail explicitly through the existing retry path. Other kinds still use generic handlers and completion. The typed effect runs under the existing 30-second timeout and panic recovery, then completion uses the worker clock and the original claim lease. Constructor-injected tests assemble four provider slots; production registration uses those same bounds as described below.

### Constructor-injected meeting recovery scanner

`meetings.RecoveryScanner.Run` can be injected as the `meeting.recovery` worker job at a one-minute interval. Each pass has one 20-second context covering local admission, connection acquisition, SQL and lock waits, queueing and receipt collection; an injected monotonic source also checks remaining work between statements. Business time comes from the worker's `now`. A cancellation-aware per-instance permit serializes access to the injected entropy reader. Separate instances coordinate through the persisted cursor revision.

One pass reads at most 100 existing eligible ledger rows after the durable cursor. Before attempting each row, it commits a cursor compare-and-swap; losing ownership stops candidate processing without retrying the claim or queueing the item. Item errors are accumulated and returned while subsequent candidates advance. A short final page wraps the cursor to zero; it never starts another traversal in the same pass. Rows acquiring eligibility behind the cursor wait for the next traversal. Cursor advancement does not certify successful queueing: a crash or failure after advancement leaves that obligation for the next traversal.

The scanner uses the existing atomic revision-checked automatic `QueueCycle`. Pending and spent cycles are not recreated; a definitive late acknowledgement may admit the existing single final-cycle allowance, and `started` or `uncertain` evidence without a create time may admit one reconciliation cycle once 1 hour has passed since its `dispatched_at` ([quiet-window settlement](realtime-and-video.md#quiet-window-settlement)). The repository statement enforces the same predicates. The provider is never called by the scanner. After candidate processing, at most 100 settled receipts older than seven days are deleted within the same deadline, even when candidate processing reported an item error or lost cursor ownership. Unresolved and pending-cycle evidence remains retained. The job's affected count is queued cycles; scanner diagnostics separately count removed receipts.

Only existing cleanup-requested or orphaned ledger rows are candidates for cycles. Before them, each pass closes at most 100 attached `creating` business rows older than one business minute through the partial `meetings_creating_idx` index, oldest first, each in its own transaction under the lesson's locks, and queues their first cycle. A failed row is reported in the aggregate and does not stop the others in the pass; a permanently failing row stays at the head of later passes and stays visible as a failed job. Public route activation is a separate change. Runtime registration is described below; local operator recovery follows the video specification. Constructor-injected tests alone do not establish production activation, and local process tests do not establish remote cleanup guarantees.

### Meeting ended hint effects

The kind `meeting_ended_hint` runs in the `provider_call` lane through the generic `Perform` handler, with the existing 30-second deadline, eight attempts and backoff. Its payload holds the lesson and meeting identifiers and the check number, nothing else. A check whose number differs from the meeting's counter, or whose meeting is no longer open, finishes as `obsolete`, so a retried effect queues no second recheck. See [callbacks](realtime-and-video.md#callbacks).

### Registered meeting recovery job

Production startup registers `meeting.recovery` once per minute with the existing bounded scanner and four `provider_call` slots per process. Each pass still selects at most 100 candidates, advances the persistent CAS cursor, and removes at most 100 eligible seven-day receipts inside one 20-second budget. Effects keep their 30-second deadline and finite eight-attempt cycle. API test-control Tick uses this same composition; the separate shared-test-clock worker does not run independently. Registration creates no new Create dispatch, callback, public route or automatic replenishment of exhausted cycles. The scanner and effect diagnostics remain the existing fixed events and safe fields.
