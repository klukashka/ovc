# Files specification

Behavior for FR-14 and every upload in OVC. Storage is S3-compatible object storage hosted in Russia ([ADR-0008](../../docs/decisions/0008-hosting-and-data-residency.md)). Integration tests use a local S3-compatible server; unit and contract tests may use the in-memory storage fake.

## Contexts and limits

| Context | Who uploads | Scope given at upload | Allowed kinds | Per-file limit | Per item |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `avatar` | any user | — | JPEG, PNG, WebP | 5 MB | 1 |
| `prompt_image` | tutors whose email is confirmed (slice 8) | — (own library) | JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF | 5 MB | 3 per prompt |
| `library` | tutors whose email is confirmed (slice 8) | — (own library) | images, PDF, office documents, audio, MP4 video | 100 MB; a WebP picture at most 8 MiB, because the metadata filter holds its first part ([pictures](#pictures)) | 1 per material |
| `chat` | active members of a writable channel whose email is confirmed (slice 6) | the channel | images, PDF, office documents, audio, plain text | 25 MB; a WebP picture at most 8 MiB | 10 per message |
| `answer` | the submission's student whose email is confirmed (slice 9) | `submission:<submission id>:<question id>` | the kinds in the question's `allowed_kinds` | 25 MB; a WebP picture at most 8 MiB | up to the question's `max_files` |
| `corrected` | the issue's tutor whose email is confirmed (slice 9) | `submission:<submission id>:<question id>` | images, PDF, office documents, audio | 25 MB; a WebP picture at most 8 MiB | 5 per answer |

- **Kinds** map to an explicit allowlist of media types:
  - images: JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF;
  - PDF;
  - office documents: DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, ODT, ODS, ODP;
  - audio: MP3, M4A, OGG, WAV;
  - video: MP4;
  - plain text.

  A `file` question's `allowed_kinds` name the kinds `images`, `pdf`, `office` and `audio` of this list; an `answer` upload whose detected kind is outside its question's `allowed_kinds` is refused with `415 unsupported_media_type` (slice 9). Video and plain text are never accepted in the `answer` and `corrected` contexts.
- **Type detection** reads the content, not the client's claim, with the standard library only (no sniffing dependency):
  - ordinary kinds by magic bytes from the first part: JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, PDF; MP3 by an ID3 tag or, without one, at least three consecutive valid MPEG audio frame headers; M4A by its `ftyp` brand (a major or compatible brand in the `chat`, `answer` and `corrected` contexts; in the `library` context the major brand alone, by the [MP4 rule](#mp4-video)); OGG by a Vorbis or Opus first page; WAV; and, for the `library` context only, MP4 video by the [MP4 rule](#mp4-video) (slice 8);
  - plain text as valid UTF-8 without NUL or control characters other than tab, line feed and carriage return, checked over the whole stream, served as `text/plain; charset=utf-8`;
  - office documents by [inspecting the container](#office-containers); a generic ZIP is refused.

  The extension, compared case-insensitively, must be one accepted for the detected kind; anything else is refused with `415 unsupported_media_type`. The accepted extensions (slice 6):

  | Detected kind | Extensions |
  |---|---|
  | JPEG | `.jpg`, `.jpeg` |
  | PNG | `.png` |
  | WebP | `.webp` |
  | GIF | `.gif` |
  | PDF | `.pdf` |
  | DOCX, XLSX, PPTX | `.docx`, `.xlsx`, `.pptx`, each only for its own main part |
  | ODT, ODS, ODP | `.odt`, `.ods`, `.odp`, each only for its own `mimetype` |
  | MP3 | `.mp3` |
  | M4A | `.m4a` |
  | OGG | `.ogg`, `.oga`, `.opus` |
  | WAV | `.wav` |
  | MP4 (the `library` context only, slice 8) | `.mp4` only |
  | Plain text | `.txt` only |

  Unit tests cover each kind, each disguise and each boundary, and include that a script or markup extension (`.html`, `.htm`, `.svg`, `.xml`, `.js`, `.mjs`, `.sh`, `.bat`, `.cmd`, `.ps1`, `.vbs`, `.hta`) carrying valid UTF-8 text is refused, as is a file without an extension.
- **No archives and no executables.** Malware scanning is not in the MVP and is recorded as a threat-model limitation.

### MP4 video

From slice 8, for the `library` context only. A file is MP4 video only when it starts with a box of type `ftyp` whose declared size is between 16 and 4,096 bytes and lies inside the first part. The major brand alone decides, whatever the compatible brands say:

- `isom`, `iso2`, `mp41`, `mp42`, `avc1` and `M4V` followed by a space are MP4 video;
- an M4A major brand stays M4A audio, with its own extension;
- the QuickTime brand `qt` followed by two spaces, and every other brand, is refused with `415 unsupported_media_type`.

Unit tests cover each brand, each disguise (an MP4 named `.m4a` or `.mov`, an M4A named `.mp4`), each precedence case and each size boundary. MP4 files are served only as `attachment` downloads ([downloads](#downloads)).

### Office containers

From slice 6. DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, ODT, ODS and ODP are stored first, while the attachment row is `uploading`, and then inspected over an `io.ReaderAt` that reads the stored object by ranges, before the row becomes `pending`; a refused container answers `415 unsupported_media_type`, its row is marked `deleted` and its object's deletion is queued:

- **Bounds:** one ranged read of the last 64 KiB plus 22 bytes finds the end-of-central-directory record and, when present, the zip64 locator and record; a container that declares more than 10,000 entries or a central directory larger than 4 MiB, or whose directory does not lie inside the object, is refused before anything is parsed. The directory is fetched in one bounded read and parsed by the standard library's `archive/zip` over it; duplicate entry names, compared case-insensitively, are refused. OOXML's `[Content_Types].xml` is read inflated up to 1 MiB; ODF's `mimetype` must be the first entry, stored uncompressed and at most 256 bytes, and `META-INF/manifest.xml` is read up to 1 MiB; every `.rels` entry is read inflated and parsed as a stream, all of them together at most 8 MiB, at most 2,000 `.rels` entries; a container that exceeds any bound is refused, never partly skipped. An ordinary presentation has two or three relationship files per slide, so these bounds refuse only abnormal containers.
- **Storage requests:** the tail read is kept in memory, and so is the directory when it lies inside the tail; otherwise the directory is one more bounded read. The directory must end exactly where the end-of-central-directory record (or the zip64 record) begins, must hold at least 46 bytes per declared entry, and is walked record by record before `archive/zip` sees it; the walk must find exactly the declared number of records and only local headers before the directory, and each entry's local header and compressed data must end before the next entry's local header (or the directory), so entries cannot overlap, and the library reads a view of the object that ends at the directory's end followed by a rebuilt end record, so it can never parse headers beyond the declared directory. Each inspected entry is one ranged request from its local header to the next entry's local header (or the directory), at most 1 MiB; an inspection reads at most 32 MiB from storage in all and runs within 30 seconds, and a container that needs more is refused.
- **Content rules** (the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review): refused are macros by part name (`vbaProject`), by content type and by relationship type, and a macro-enabled main content type; a main part whose content type disagrees with the extension (a `.docx` whose main part is a spreadsheet); ActiveX parts and their relationships; alternative format chunks (the `aFChunk` relationship, which imports HTML, RTF or another document into the main part); OLE embeddings (`embeddings/oleObject*.bin` and the OLE relationship types); every relationship with `TargetMode="External"` whose type is not the hyperlink type, for example an external `attachedTemplate`. The `package` relationship type of a native chart's embedded workbook is not an OLE type and is accepted. In ODF, refused are `Basic/` and `Scripts/` folders, manifest entries with script or OLE media types, and manifest entries that point outside the container. The OOXML main parts and ODF `content.xml` and `styles.xml` are not parsed, so their contents are accepted, a residual of [T-13](../../docs/threat-model.md#threats-and-controls): links and linked objects (`xlink:href`), OOXML field codes such as `DDE`, `DDEAUTO`, `INCLUDETEXT` and `INCLUDEPICTURE`, and ODF event listeners; the supported office applications warn before updating fields or links from outside the document, and a document link opens nothing by itself without the user's action.

### Pictures

From slice 6, for chat pictures, from slice 8 for every JPEG, PNG, WebP and GIF of the `library` and `prompt_image` contexts, because library pictures reach students as lesson materials, and from slice 9 for every picture of the `answer` and `corrected` contexts, so a student's phone photo loses its location before anyone downloads it:

- **Dimensions:** JPEG, PNG, WebP and GIF get `width` and `height` read from their headers at upload, without decoding, so the conversation reserves their space; a picture above 50 megapixels is stored as a file without a preview.
- **Metadata** is removed by a streaming allowlist filter that keeps only the structures a picture needs and drops everything else, without decoding (the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review); the stored size and checksum are those of the filtered bytes:
  - JPEG: SOI, DQT, DHT, DRI, the SOF markers, SOS with its entropy-coded data through EOI, APP0 JFIF rebuilt with a zero-size thumbnail (so no embedded thumbnail survives), APP2 `ICC_PROFILE` and APP14 Adobe, plus a rebuilt APP1 holding only the EXIF orientation when the original had one; every other segment (other APP1, APP2 MPF, APP13, comments) and everything after EOI is dropped;
  - PNG: the critical chunks and `tRNS`, `gAMA`, `cHRM`, `sRGB`, `iCCP`, `sBIT`, `pHYs`, `bKGD` and the animation chunks `acTL`, `fcTL`, `fdAT`; nothing after `IEND`;
  - WebP: `VP8` (the lossy chunk, whose four-character code ends in a space), `VP8L`, `VP8X`, `ALPH`, `ANIM`, `ANMF` and `ICCP`, with the RIFF size and the `VP8X` flags corrected. Because the RIFF size and the flags sit at the start, the first part is held in memory, filtered and rewritten before it is sent, so a WebP picture larger than one 8 MiB part is refused with `413 payload_too_large` and the field code `webp_too_large`; the inline refusal reads "WebP pictures can be at most 8 MB", the 8 MiB limit rounded for display;
  - GIF: the header, screen and image descriptors, colour tables, image data, graphic control extensions and the `NETSCAPE2.0` loop extension; comments, plain-text extensions, other application extensions and everything after the trailer are dropped.
- **Previews** are the original (filtered) picture constrained by CSS and loaded lazily.

### File names

From slice 6: the client's name from the part's `filename` parameter, with every path component before the last `/` or `\` removed, then at most 255 bytes of UTF-8; a longer name, a name that is not valid UTF-8 or that is empty after the path is removed, control characters and every Unicode format character (category `Cf`, which includes the bidirectional overrides) are refused with `422 validation_failed` (`body.file`: `invalid`), never truncated, so the extension that type detection compares is always the one the client sent; the name is stored as given and rendered as text in an element isolated with `dir="auto"`.

## Upload flow

The general pipeline below is built in slice 6 for the `chat` context; the `library` and `prompt_image` contexts adopt it in slice 8, with their durable cleanup decided in [ADR-0022](../../docs/decisions/0022-library-upload-cleanup-adoption.md), and the `answer` and `corrected` contexts in slice 9, with their durable cleanup decided in [ADR-0023](../../docs/decisions/0023-homework-upload-cleanup-adoption.md). Avatars keep the [buffered flow](#avatars). Slice 8 first makes the chat-specific admission, scope checks and pending sums context-driven with no change of behaviour, keeping the chat entry points as wrappers over the context-driven core.

1. **Request:** `POST /api/v1/uploads?context=<context>&scope=<scope>`.
   - The scope is required where the table names one: `channel:<channel id>` for chat, and `submission:<submission id>:<question id>` for answer and corrected uploads. The `library` and `prompt_image` contexts take no scope: the scope is the caller's own library, and a `scope` parameter answers `422 validation_failed` (`query.scope`, `not_allowed`).
   - The caller must be allowed to write to it, and the scope decides which tutor's quota is charged. A chat upload needs an active member of a writable channel whose email is confirmed: an unconfirmed account gets `403 email_not_verified` from the audience rule before the body is read, a channel outside the caller's scope `404`, and a read-only channel `409 channel_read_only`. A `library` or `prompt_image` upload needs a tutor whose email is confirmed (the owner's decision of 2026-11-10 in the [slice 8 plan](../plans/slice-8.md#4-owner-questions)): a student gets `404` for the role, and an unconfirmed tutor `403 email_not_verified` before the body is read, as for chat; it is charged to the uploading tutor.
   - **Homework scopes** (slice 9): `submission:<submission id>:<question id>` names a submission and a question of its issue's snapshot; any other format answers `422 validation_failed` at `query.scope` (`invalid`), and a missing scope `422` at `query.scope` (`required`). Both contexts are charged to the issue's tutor. An unconfirmed caller gets `403 email_not_verified` from the audience rule before the body is read, as for chat, so a student on a provisional link uploads file answers only once confirmed. Every check below that needs no body runs before the body is read, and every `404` has the body of a random identifier; only the allowed-kinds check (`415`) needs the body and runs after content detection:
     - **`answer`:** the caller must be the submission's student, the submission readable to them by `ReadableStudentSubmission`, the one readability predicate of [homework](homework.md#student-readability) (their own submission, of an `active` issue not marked for erasure, whose relationship is `active`, `provisional` or `ended`), and the question a `file` question of the snapshot; otherwise `404`, for a tutor too. A readable submission the student may not write, because its status is not `not_started`, `draft` or `returned` or its relationship is `ended`, answers `409 not_editable`. After content detection, while streaming ([streaming](#upload-flow)), the detected kind must be in the question's `allowed_kinds`, `415 unsupported_media_type` otherwise.
     - **`corrected`:** the caller must be the issue's tutor, the submission reviewable (`submitted` or `checked`, of an `active` issue not marked for erasure) and the question a `file` question of the snapshot; otherwise `404`, for a student too, so a tutor's upload to a `not_started`, `draft`, `returned` or `closed` submission answers `404` like another tutor's.
   - The scope is stored on the attachment and checked again at completion and at binding; for the homework contexts the second session repeats every check above under the locks of [homework references and charges](#homework-references-and-charges), so a submission submitted, closed, withdrawn or marked for erasure while its file streamed refuses the completion with the same refusal, and its object is deleted.
   - **Request bounds:** the body holds exactly one part, named `file`, and nothing else (any other part or field answers `422 validation_failed` at `body.file`); part headers total at most 8 KiB; the whole body may exceed the context limit by at most 64 KiB of multipart overhead, enforced while reading; a malformed body answers `400 bad_request`; the [file name](#file-names) comes from the part's `filename` parameter.
2. **First session**, before any body byte or storage request: for a signed-in tutor, recheck the authenticated session after the profile lock under the [shared tutor rule](accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check), using a fresh post-lock clock instant. Enforce existing account/session, scope, writability, cap and quota checks, then atomically create the `uploading` attachment and its independent durable cleanup intent, reserving global capacity last. Both use the same server-generated immutable random key. Only a confirmed commit allows storage Create. Create has one SDK attempt; record its returned MPU identifier exactly once in the journal and the still-uploading attachment before sending any part. An identical ID is idempotent; a different ID is refused. Cleanup may already have revoked publication: retain a late ID for cleanup but send no parts or Complete. If cleanup removed the journal before Create returned, never recreate it; attempt bounded abort of that returned ID. A crash or failed ID persistence can leave an incomplete MPU for the bucket lifecycle, not an authorized completed object. See [durable cleanup](#durable-chat-upload-cleanup).
3. **Streaming:**
   - the server parses the multipart body as a stream without spooling to disk, and writes it to storage as an S3 multipart upload with 8 MiB parts;
   - it counts bytes and computes SHA-256 as it goes, and aborts with `payload_too_large` the moment the limit is passed;
   - ordinary kinds are detected from the first part, plain text over the whole stream, pictures pass the [metadata filter](#pictures), and office documents are [inspected](#office-containers) after the object is stored;
   - no database session is held during the transfer;
   - **early refusals:** the refusals answered before the body is read (`403 email_not_verified` and a `Content-Length` over the limit) carry `Connection: close`, so the server does not drain an unread body before answering;
   - **deadlines:** the read deadline is the context's per-file limit at 128 KiB per second, at least 60 seconds and at most 10 minutes (25 MB: about 195 seconds), with the 35-second write deadline after the body ends; a body that delivers less than 16 KiB in any 30-second window is aborted as stalled; the floor, the rate and the stall window are constructor settings, so tests use a short floor; deadlines run on the system clock, as every transport deadline does; an upload still running when shutdown's 40-second drain ends is aborted and the client retries;
   - **memory** is at most 32 uploads × one 8 MiB part = 256 MiB per instance, separate from the avatar decoding budget, plus the bounded inspection reads (at most the 64 KiB tail, 4 MiB of directory and 1 MiB per entry read, one entry at a time per upload) and the picture filter's look-ahead of at most 64 KiB per upload; the held WebP part is the one 8 MiB part counted above; memory does not grow with file size.
4. **Complete and publication:** before the single Complete invocation, lock attachment then intent and confirm matching identity, uploading/streaming state and no cleanup request; atomically mark completion started. Only its confirmed commit authorizes Complete, with SDK retries disabled. Carry a definite success acknowledgement into settlement and persist it within the shared settlement budget. After inspection, recheck account/session (including the tutor session under the profile lock and a fresh post-lock clock instant), channel membership, writability and exact size-dependent caps, then atomically publish the attachment as `pending`, remove its intent and release global capacity. Pending/bound state with matching key and no intent means ownership transferred; uncertain commit reconciliation must preserve it. No later storage write is allowed after publication.
5. **Failure:** request cleanup of an unpublished upload in a transaction, preserving its journal even when purge removed the attachment. One detached monotonic 30-second budget covers acknowledgement, clock reads, pool acquisition, locks, settlement and discard; it is not renewed between phases, and the earlier process-drain deadline wins. Deadline or database failure returns uncertainty and releases the permit; no background goroutine retains the upload buffer or permit. Never unconditionally delete after an uncertain publication commit. The outbox performs abort/delete outside transactions under [durable cleanup](#durable-chat-upload-cleanup). Bucket lifecycle handles unknown incomplete MPUs where supported; it does not remove completed objects or fence an ambiguous Complete.
6. **Limits** (chat, from slice 8 the library contexts and from slice 9 the homework contexts as stated; the concurrency and hourly limits are in-memory counters per API instance through an injected clock and counter, the pending limit is counted from attachment rows and the daily limit from durable aggregates):
   - at most 4 concurrent uploads per user and 32 per API instance;
   - separately, at most 4,096 unresolved durable chat intents globally; full capacity answers structured `503 service_unavailable` before body reads or storage, without a promised recovery time or automatic client replay;
   - at most 20 chat uploads per user that are `uploading` or `pending` and not expired, counted from the rows;
   - at most 60 chat uploads started per user per hour; a start counts once the scope, writability and pending checks pass, before the daily cap and the quota are checked, so a start that those refuse still uses a slot;
   - at most 200 MB of chat attachment bytes per student per tutor per UTC day, on the injected business clock, counted from durable daily aggregates (the sizes of the student's successfully completed chat uploads to that tutor's channels, including physically deleted ones; the day is the UTC day of the attachment's `created_at`, read from the injected clock inside the creation transaction after acquiring the student user lock): an upload starts only while the day's total is below the cap, and its bytes count when it completes;
   - **the library contexts** (slice 8): the general 4 concurrent uploads per user and 32 per instance apply; per tutor, across `library` and `prompt_image` together, at most 20 uploads that are `uploading` or `pending` and not expired, counted from the rows, and at most 60 uploads started per hour, counted once the pending check passes and before the quota is checked; at most 1,024 unresolved durable intents of the library capacity class globally, separate from chat's 4,096, so slow library uploads never make chat uploads answer `503` ([ADR-0022](../../docs/decisions/0022-library-upload-cleanup-adoption.md)), and a full library class answers structured `503 service_unavailable` before body reads or storage, without a promised recovery time; the daily byte cap stays chat-only;
   - **the homework contexts** (slice 9): the general 4 concurrent uploads per user and 32 per instance apply; per user, across `answer` and `corrected` together, at most 20 uploads that are `uploading` or `pending` and not expired, counted from the rows, and at most 60 uploads started per hour, counted once the scope, writability and pending checks pass and before the byte caps and the quota are checked; at most 1,024 unresolved durable intents of the `homework` capacity class globally, shared by both contexts and separate from chat's 4,096 and the library's 1,024, so slow homework uploads never make chat or library uploads answer `503` ([ADR-0023](../../docs/decisions/0023-homework-upload-cleanup-adoption.md)); a full homework class answers structured `503 service_unavailable` before body reads or storage, without a promised recovery time;
   - **`answer` byte caps** (slice 9), per student and the charged tutor, so one student cannot fill or block a tutor's quota with answers: at most 100 MB of `uploading` and `pending` uploads that are not expired, counted from the rows (a `pending` row with its size, an `uploading` row with its `reserved_bytes`, this upload's own row included); and at most 200 MB of completed bytes per UTC day on the injected business clock, counted from the durable daily answer aggregate of [durable daily upload accounting](#durable-daily-upload-accounting): an upload starts only while the day's total is below the cap, and its bytes count when it completes. Both refuse with `429 rate_limited` and `Retry-After`, as the chat caps do. `corrected` uploads, like every tutor upload, have no byte cap besides the quota;
   - **deadlines of a 100 MB library file:** its read deadline reaches the 10-minute ceiling, which requires about 170 KB/s for its upload, and the download route's 10-minute streaming ceiling requires the same of a student downloading it on a phone; both are accepted residuals of the slice 8 plan;
   - beyond the per-user and per-instance limits above, `429 rate_limited` with `Retry-After`; a restart resets the in-memory counters (the concurrency and hourly counts) ([T-20](../../docs/threat-model.md#threats-and-controls)).
7. **Size-dependent checks** before the size is known: before streaming, the route refuses a `Content-Length` above the context limit plus the 64 KiB overhead, a quota with no room left (used + pending ≥ quota), a daily total already at its cap and, for an `answer` upload, pending bytes of the student to that tutor that would pass 100 MB with this upload's `reserved_bytes`; the exact checks with the final size run in the second session, where an upload that no longer fits is refused with `507 quota_exceeded` or `429 rate_limited` and its object deleted. A request without `Content-Length` is streamed under the same per-file limit.
8. **Response:** `{id, filename, media_type, size_bytes}`. The storage key is never returned.
9. **Pending state:** the attachment is `pending`, owned by the uploader and invisible to others, until a create or update request binds it to an item: a message, an answer, a review, a material, a prompt or an avatar. From slice 8 `POST /library/materials` binds one `library` upload to a new material, and a template save binds `prompt_image` uploads named by its prompts ([template references](homework.md#template-references)); each binds only the caller's own `pending` upload of its context whose 24-hour expiry has not passed, and any other identifier answers `404 not_found` like a random one. From slice 9 an autosave (`PUT /submissions/{id}/answers`) binds the student's own `answer` uploads and a review draft save (`PUT /submissions/{id}/review`) the tutor's own `corrected` uploads, each only an unexpired `pending` upload scoped to that same submission and question; an attachment the same answer, or the same draft, already references is accepted as it is, and any other identifier answers `404 not_found` like a random one ([homework references and charges](#homework-references-and-charges)).
10. **Binding** checks that:
    - the uploader is the caller;
    - the context and scope match the item;
    - the item's count limit holds;
    - the quota still holds.

    A message send binds only the caller's own `pending` chat uploads scoped to the same channel whose 24-hour expiry has not passed, at most ten, in the send transaction; any other identifier answers `404 not_found` like a random one; more than ten identifiers are refused by the request schema with `422 validation_failed` before any identifier is resolved, so that refusal reveals nothing about them; an attachment bound to one message is never bound again. A channel that is discarded, pending erasure or gone is never bindable.
11. **Purge of chat, library and homework uploads** (the library contexts from slice 8, for the tutor purge, the 7-day cleanup and the immediate purge of an unconfirmed tutor; from slice 9 the `answer` and `corrected` uploads of a purged student or tutor, and those scoped to a submission that no longer exists, removed by an erasure or a purge): retain the existing reference/charge batches and unbound-upload deletion. Before removing uploading attachments, lock the complete attachment set in identifier order, then its intents in that order; request intent cleanup in the same transaction. The independent journal survives account, channel and attachment deletion. A generic storage-delete producer must route an upload with an intent through that intent, never create a competing unconditional deletion. Pending/bound attachments whose intent was removed retain ordinary reference/deletion ownership.
12. **Expiry:** the hourly `uploads.expire` job revokes pending uploads older than 24 hours, uploading rows older than the longest upload deadline plus ten minutes, and pending rows whose scope is no longer bindable; each claim re-checks the row's state and age on the row it locks (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` with the condition repeated outside the candidate subquery), and the discard skips a claimed row that its lock finds `bound` or `deleted`, so a bind that commits at the 24-hour boundary keeps its file, its references and its charges (slice 8 review follow-up, pending owner review); from slice 8 a `library` or `prompt_image` upload is no longer bindable once its tutor is deleted or blocked; from slice 9 an `answer` upload is no longer bindable once its submission is gone, no longer readable to its student or no longer writable to them, and a `corrected` upload once its submission is gone or no longer reviewable, or its tutor is deleted or blocked. Uploading intent cleanup is requested transactionally; age revokes publication but never proves that remote Complete ended. The separate recovery scan handles missing attachments and unfinished intent bookkeeping.

### Durable daily upload accounting

Approved 2026-11-01 in the [implementation plan](../plans/slice-6-durable-daily-upload-accounting.md). A daily aggregate contains only student ID, tutor ID, UTC upload day and completed bytes. Creation and completion serialize on the existing student user lock. Successful publication increments the aggregate in the same transaction as the uploading-to-pending transition; failed validation, quota refusal, rollback and repeated settlement do not increment it. Tutor uploads remain exempt. From slice 9 a second aggregate of the same design, `daily_answer_upload_bytes` ([data model](data-model.md#daily-chat-upload-accounting)), counts the completed bytes of student `answer` uploads per student, charged tutor and UTC day, under the same rules: creation and completion serialize on the student user lock, publication increments it in the uploading-to-pending transaction, and nothing subtracts from it, a withdrawal, an erasure, an ending's deferred release and a replaced file answer included. It starts empty, because no `answer` upload existed before slice 9, so it needs neither a backfill nor an activation cutover, and the chat activation barrier below does not apply to it; `uploads.daily_cleanup` removes its past days under the same eligibility and within the same bounds, and the tutor purge removes its rows in batches, as for chat. Deleting or expiring attachments, physically removing objects or attachment rows, releasing quota charges, erasing channels and ending relationships never subtract daily bytes. Block, soft deletion and restoration preserve accounting; final account purge removes obsolete identity as described in the [data model](data-model.md#daily-chat-upload-accounting).

Old upload writers stop before migration. Migration backfills retained completed student chat rows by their original UTC day, including completed deleted rows; already removed rows cannot be reconstructed. After backfill it records the next UTC midnight as the activation instant, once. Until then student chat upload admission returns structured `503 service_unavailable`, `Connection: close` and `Retry-After` rounded up to seconds, bounded to one day, before body reads or storage work. The initial interruption is at most one UTC day. Tutor uploads and authorized downloads remain available. New code checks activation again before Complete and publication: surviving uploads whose creation day precedes activation are cleanup-only even after activation. They retain the existing durable cleanup protocol; time is not a fence for remote Complete.

The singleton activation is immutable: updates, deletes and truncation are refused; schema constraints reject duplicate or malformed state. Re-running the migration runner does not reapply an applied migration or move activation. Missing or unreadable state fails closed with `503` and a one-minute retry hint; this hint is not a recovery guarantee. A schema rollback stops writers first and removes both the aggregate and activation: it is not a supported way to resume old writers while claiming the new daily guarantee. Re-adoption repeats the backfill and next-day cutover. Neither rollback nor migration reconstructs bytes whose attachment rows have already gone.

`uploads.daily_cleanup` runs separately once per minute. Its own cancellable 20-second context covers connection acquisition, candidate selection, statements and the transaction. It locks at most 100 candidate students `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, in UUID order, before any aggregate lock, takes no tutor/profile/channel lock, and deletes at most 1,000 aggregate rows in batches of at most 100. Only past UTC days with no remaining uploading attachment for that student/tutor/day are eligible; eligibility is checked again after student serialization. Already pending/bound files need no old daily row. Creation reads business time after its student lock, preventing an upload queued across midnight from recreating an expired bucket. A locked student does not prevent other candidates from progressing. No storage request runs in the cleanup transaction, and this deadline does not change `uploads.expire`.

The service records `upload.accounting.cutover` at INFO for a refused cutover upload and `upload.accounting.unavailable` at ERROR with a safe error kind for unavailable accounting state. The worker owns the cleanup's existing `job.run` outcome, count and safe failure kind. These events contain no storage identity, filename, content or credential.

### Durable chat upload cleanup

Approved 2026-11-01 in [ADR-0019](../../docs/decisions/0019-durable-upload-cleanup.md), and adopted unchanged for the `library` and `prompt_image` contexts from slice 8 by [ADR-0022](../../docs/decisions/0022-library-upload-cleanup-adoption.md), with their own capacity class of 1,024 unresolved intents, and for the `answer` and `corrected` contexts from slice 9 by [ADR-0023](../../docs/decisions/0023-homework-upload-cleanup-adoption.md), with the `homework` capacity class of 1,024 unresolved intents shared by both; every rule below applies to them, with "chat" read as the upload's context. The journal's [data model](data-model.md#upload-cleanup-intent) contains opaque technical identity and state only, no account/channel identifier, filename, file content or session credential. It has no foreign key to purged business rows. Identity is immutable; MPU ID permits only NULL-to-ID, even after revocation. Completion-started, confirmed and cleanup-requested transitions are monotonic.

Initial cleanup is generation 0. A definite producer acknowledgement after initial cleanup was scheduled records one required final generation 1; no third automatic generation exists. Each has a fixed outbox identity and the existing eight-attempt policy, independent of later outbox retention. A worker validates immutable attachment/key/generation (or explicit operator UUID) and reads the current journal MPU ID; its payload never freezes an earlier NULL. Abort and Delete occur outside transactions. The final attachment-then-intent recheck observes a newly recorded MPU ID and, if it changed from NULL, requires one additional abort/delete sequence within the same 30-second effect budget. A changed non-NULL ID is a protocol violation.

Removal and capacity release are atomic and permitted only after cleanup when no Complete was authorized and the latest recorded MPU was handled, or when completion was definitely confirmed and deletion was selected after that confirmation. A pending/bound object whose ownership transferred is never deleted by stale intent work. A successful Delete alone leaves an unconfirmed started Complete `uncertain`; absent objects, Abort, time, lease expiry and shutdown do not justify forgetting it. A late generation-0 result cannot overwrite generation 1. Dead work is retained as failed; scheduler scans never grant fresh attempts when its outbox row expires.

`uploads.recover` runs once per minute, with one 30-second monotonic pass budget including selection, clock/pool/SQL waits, at most twenty batches of 200. Each candidate has at most one second capped by the remaining pass, locks attachment before intent and rolls back only itself on failure. Its indexed keyset cursor advances after the transaction ends even on failure, survives between passes in constant process memory, and wraps at keyspace end. Later candidates can progress past locked ones; repeated restarts may delay progress. Partial failures are reported without dropping durable work. No S3 call occurs in a scheduler transaction.

[Operator commands](accounts.md#administration-command) can inspect and explicitly grant another bounded cycle using a caller-supplied UUID. Receipts outlive outbox retention and are retained until safe intent removal. They have no total count cap; only explicit privileged retries grow them. Indexed UUID and active-cycle lookups avoid scanning history under the intent lock; history uses keyset pages of at most 100. Pending/in-progress cycles exclude new cycles; required final generation 1 waits durably behind a manual cycle. Replaying a UUID never resets attempts. No force-forget or automatic resurrection exists.

Unresolved remote completion or persistent failures can retain intents indefinitely, consume capacity and require operator/provider intervention. Automatic physical deletion is not guaranteed in those cases. These accepted residuals replace the previous unconditional chat cleanup promise; avatars retain their separate residual. Migration/adoption stops old writers first, conservatively backfills legacy unresolved uploads and fails visibly if capacity would be exceeded. Local tests do not certify another provider; see [storage evidence](../research/s3-multipart-cleanup-2026-11-01.md).

## Avatars

Slice 2 implements only the `avatar` context, and it departs from the general flow above in these ways:

- **Buffered, not streamed to storage:** the part is read into memory up to 5 MB plus one byte (more answers `payload_too_large`), because an avatar fits one S3 request; the request body is limited to 5 MB plus 64 KiB and has a 60-second read deadline.
- **Type:** JPEG, PNG or WebP by magic bytes, with an agreeing extension (`.jpg`/`.jpeg`, `.png`, `.webp`); `image.DecodeConfig` must report the same format and at most 4096 × 4096 pixels before anything is decoded.
- **Metadata removal:** the image is decoded and encoded again, which drops EXIF, XMP and text chunks: JPEG stays JPEG (quality 90), PNG and WebP become PNG. The stored file is named `avatar.jpg` or `avatar.png`, so no client file name is kept, and an encoded result above 10 MB is refused as `payload_too_large` the moment the encoder passes that size. 16-bit and CMYK images are refused as `unsupported_media_type`. Decoding memory has a budget of 256 MiB per instance: each decode reserves width × height × 8 bytes for PNG and WebP (the decoded image and the encoder's working copy) or × 16 bytes for JPEG (progressive coefficients for three components plus the image), and WebP reserves the larger of 8 bytes per pixel and 5 bytes per pixel plus 132 MiB, because a lossless image may declare up to 2600 Huffman table groups (about 131 MiB) whatever its size, so the largest JPEG fills the budget alone and only one WebP decodes at a time; a user decodes at most one image at a time, and an upload that does not fit answers `503 service_unavailable` with `Retry-After: 5`. Outside that budget, each of the at most 32 concurrent uploads holds its body (up to 5 MB) and, while encoding, its result (up to 10 MB).
- **Order:** the attachment row is created first, in a transaction that locks the user row and checks the pending limit again, and only then is the object stored; a failed store marks the row deleted and queues its deletion, and after a successful store the row is locked again: if a purge removed it meanwhile, a `storage_delete` row is queued for the object in that transaction and the upload fails, so no object exists without a row.
- **Limits per user:** at most 3 pending avatar uploads that have not passed their 24-hour expiry (an expired one stops counting before the hourly expiry job removes it), 20 avatar uploads per hour (`429 rate_limited` with `Retry-After`), and the general 4 concurrent uploads per user and 32 per instance.
- **Binding:** `PATCH /users/me` with `avatar_id` binds the caller's own pending avatar upload that has not passed its 24-hour expiry; the previous avatar loses its reference, becomes `deleted` and gets a `storage_delete` outbox row in the same transaction; `null` removes the avatar the same way. The reference is the only record of the avatar.
- **Serving:** `GET /api/v1/avatars/{id}` reads the object through the API (the CSP stays `'self'`) and answers only for an attachment with a live avatar reference whose owner is active or unverified, with `Content-Type` from detection, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Disposition: inline` without a file name, `Cache-Control: private, max-age=86400`, `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; sandbox` and `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-origin`; everything else is `404`. The body is streamed from storage with `Content-Length` set from the stored size, never held in memory whole; an instance serves at most 32 avatars at once and answers `503 service_unavailable` with `Retry-After: 1` beyond that; the attachment lookup and the storage read share a 30-second deadline, and the response write is bounded by the request's write deadline; the last byte is sent only after the object is confirmed to end exactly at its recorded size, so an object shorter or longer than recorded always reaches the client short of its `Content-Length` before the connection is aborted, and is reported as `avatars.get.failed` with kind `size_mismatch`.
- **Deletion:** a `storage_delete` outbox row carries the attachment identifier and its storage key; the worker's lane deletes the object and then the attachment row, and skips the row if the attachment exists and is not `deleted`; an account purge deletes the rows first, so the key in the payload is what lets the object follow; the hourly `uploads.expire` job marks pending uploads older than 24 hours as `deleted` and queues their deletion, in batches of 200.

## References

- An attachment is shared through **references**: rows that say "this item uses this attachment", for a message, an answer, a review item, a material, a template, an issue snapshot, a lesson, a series (from slice 8, for the files of a series' materials) or an avatar.
- **Duplicating a template** or issuing a snapshot adds references; it never copies bytes. So does attaching a material to a lesson or a series, and copying a series' materials into a new occurrence.
- **Downloads** are authorized through any reference whose item the caller can see. Review-item references point to a specific review revision, which students can see only once published ([answer visibility](homework.md#answer-visibility)).
- **An attachment is deleted from storage**, through an outbox `storage_delete` row, when its last reference is removed.

### Homework references and charges

From slice 9. Every rule of [quotas](#quotas) applies; this section names the homework items:

- **Kinds:** an `issue_snapshot` reference names the issue; an `answer` reference names the submission and the question (`question_id`); a `review_item` reference names the review revision and the question. All three charge the issue's tutor. The same-tutor rule is checked by the homework service before inserting, because the reference is polymorphic: a snapshot file is one the template references at issuing (a prompt image or a material's file), an `answer` file was uploaded in the `answer` context scoped to that submission and question, and a `review_item` file was uploaded in the `corrected` context scoped to that submission and question, or is copied from the published revision a new draft starts from.
- **Issuing** adds one `issue_snapshot` reference per distinct attachment the snapshot shows. The tutor already holds a charge for each through the template, so issuing creates no charge and checks no quota; the snapshot keeps its references, and with them the charge, after the template or the material is deleted.
- **Autosave** binds the listed `answer` uploads with `answer` references, creating a charge when the tutor holds none, and removes the references of files no longer listed; a file losing its last reference loses its charge and gets its `storage_delete` in the same transaction. A submission's file answers hold at most 250 MB in all (`422 validation_failed`, `body.answers`, `too_many`), and a binding that would pass the tutor's quota answers `507 quota_exceeded`, which the student sees as "your tutor's storage is full". The save decides from the request, before locking, whether it binds or releases a file; only then does it take the tutor's profile lock, re-checking the decision under the locks, retrying once on a changed set and then answering `409 conflict`.
- **Review drafts:** a new draft copies the latest published revision with new `review_item` references to its corrected files, which the tutor is already charged for; binding a `corrected` upload adds a `review_item` reference and a charge when the tutor holds none (`507 quota_exceeded` past the quota); a file removed from the draft loses that draft's reference. Published revisions keep their references until the purge or the erasure.
- **Withdrawal** releases the references and charges of its submissions' file answers, which are all drafts because nothing was submitted, queueing each file's `storage_delete` with its last reference; the snapshot keeps its references until the purge, so the tutor's addresses on the withdrawn issue keep working.
- **Ending a relationship**, and every relationship a tutor deletion ends, releases no charge in its own transaction: it closes the open work and marks each closed submission whose answers hold `answer` references created after its latest `submitted_at` (all of them when it was never submitted) with `release_pending_since`. The `homework.cleanup` job ([scheduler](notifications.md#outbox-and-worker)) later removes exactly those references, with their charges and `storage_delete` rows; files the student submitted before stay referenced, and a released file leaves `answers.files` for both roles, so closed work never lists a file that can no longer be downloaded.
- **Erasure:** the same job erases an issue marked for erasure with its submissions, answers, review revisions and every `issue_snapshot`, `answer` and `review_item` reference and charge, at most 500 deleted rows of each kind per transaction. Children drain in resumable stages, selecting exact composite reference keys; the issue marker remains until completion. Each answer-reference stage updates file-byte totals and the reference-derived file projection in the same transaction, including between stages.
- **Final user purge** (owner decision, 2026-11-13): generic upload purge retains bound `answer` files and clears their uploader through `ON DELETE SET NULL` when deleting the user. Their references and charges remain until `homework.cleanup` removes them and queues last-reference storage deletion under D30. New uploads require an uploader, and binding without one is refused. Pending and uploading files remain selected by generic purge and pass through its existing intent-aware deletion, preserving unresolved cleanup ownership.
- **Purges:** the tutor purge removes review-item references and charges, answer references and charges, review revisions, answers, submissions, issue-snapshot references and issues in batches before the library batches; the student purge deletes the student's submissions with their answers, review revisions and references in batches, each locking the charged tutors' profiles first ([purge](data-model.md#purge)).
- **Locks:** each path takes the charge locks of [locks and the session re-check](accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check) in the homework order: a student's autosave locks the student's user row with the session re-check, then the tutor's profile only when it binds or releases a file, then the relationship `FOR SHARE`, the issue `FOR SHARE` and the submission; review saves and publications lock the tutor's profile with the session re-check, the relationship `FOR SHARE`, the issue `FOR SHARE`, the submission and the draft revision; issuing and withdrawal lock the tutor's profile first; the cleanup job and the purge batches lock the charged tutor's profile first; the attachments and their intents come last, as the upload rules require.
- **Residual** ([T-20](../../docs/threat-model.md#threats-and-controls)): the answer files of submitted work stay bound and charged to the tutor until the purge, and a tutor has no route to remove them.

## Downloads

- **Private files:** `GET /api/v1/attachments/{id}` authorizes the caller through the attachment's references; an unauthorized caller gets `not_found`. For a chat attachment, the caller must be an active member of a channel that holds a referencing message that is not deleted.
- **On success** it returns `{url, expires_at}`, which the client puts in `src` or `href`. Media elements cannot send an `Authorization` header, so the API never redirects. From slice 6 (the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review) `url` is an OVC download address served by the API itself, `/api/v1/files/{id}?t=<token>`, so the content security policy stays `'self'`, OVC sets every response header, and a deleted message stops its files at once. Message responses carry each attachment's address minted per response, so a page of history needs no request per picture; the client fetches the page again before `expires_at` and once when a picture fails to load. From slice 8 files referenced by materials, templates and lessons, and from slice 9 those referenced by issue snapshots, answers and review revisions, use the same addresses, bound to their referencing item ([references other than messages](#references-other-than-messages)), and recordings keep their own playback route.
- **Minting:** `GET /api/v1/attachments/{id}?disposition=` mints an address for the attachment's one referencing message (a chat attachment is bound to exactly one), with `disposition` `attachment` by default or `inline`, which only a picture with recorded dimensions (one that has a preview) accepts; any other value, a repeated `disposition`, or `inline` for anything else answers `422 validation_failed` at `disposition`, after the scope check. Message responses mint `inline` for pictures with recorded dimensions and `attachment` for every other kind, a picture above 50 megapixels included.
- **The download token** `t` is strict unpadded base64url (any other alphabet, padding or length is refused) of a fixed layout: a version byte `1`, the attachment identifier (16 bytes), the referencing message's identifier (16 bytes), the expiry in Unix seconds (8 bytes, big-endian), the disposition (1 byte: `inline` or `attachment`), and an untruncated HMAC-SHA-256 over all the preceding bytes, keyed with a key derived by HKDF-SHA-256 from `OVC_JWT_SECRET` with the info string `download-url`; no new secret is needed, and rotating the secret invalidates every address. The expiry is the next 5-minute boundary after now plus 5 minutes, so an address is valid for 5 to 10 minutes and identical for every response within a 5-minute window, which keeps image elements stable across refetches; a token whose expiry lies more than 10 minutes and 30 seconds ahead is refused, and so is `inline` for anything but a picture with recorded dimensions. The token is a credential in a URL allowed by [ADR-0009](../../docs/decisions/0009-owner-approved-exceptions-and-visibility.md) item 1: it is never logged, its query never reaches an access record, and the reverse proxy of slice 10 drops query strings of `/api/v1/files/*` from its records.
- **The referencing message in the database** (slice 6): a chat attachment is bound only to a message of its own scope channel, and deleting or erasing a message removes its attachment references in the same transaction; both download checks also read the message itself, so neither depends on those invariants alone: `GET /attachments/{id}` requires a reference to a message whose channel is the attachment's scope channel and that is neither deleted nor erased, and the caller's active membership of that channel, and `GET /files/{id}` requires the reference to the token's message, that message in the attachment's scope channel and neither deleted nor erased, and a scope channel that is neither discarded nor pending erasure.
- **`GET /api/v1/files/{id}?t=`** checks the token's MAC in constant time and its expiry, checks that the path's identifier equals the token's attachment, and re-checks that the attachment is `bound` and still referenced by the message the token names, whose channel is neither discarded nor pending erasure; every refusal is the same `404`. It streams the object, never holding it in memory, at most 64 downloads at once per instance (`503 service_unavailable` with `Retry-After: 1` beyond), with a 30-second opening deadline covering attachment lookup and storage opening, followed by one absolute streaming deadline of the file's size at 128 KiB per second, at least 60 seconds and at most 10 minutes, measured from successful storage opening (approved 2026-11-01). The same streaming deadline cancels upstream reads, including the final size probe, and bounds the HTTP writer; closing stops its timer and releases the slot once. A response that delivers less than 16 KiB to its client in any 30 seconds spent writing is aborted as stalled, mirroring the upload stall rule: time spent waiting on storage does not count, writes go to the client in pieces of at most 4 KiB so that a slow but steady client always shows progress, and the abort sets the write deadline to the present, so the slot of a client that stopped reading is released at once. A client that keeps reading just above that minimum is not stalled, so each client address (an IPv4 address, or the /64 of an IPv6 address, resolved through the trusted proxies as for the sign-in limits) may hold at most 8 downloads that have run longer than 5 seconds: while an address holds 8 such downloads, every new download from it answers `503 service_unavailable` with `Retry-After: 1` before it takes an instance slot, and a download already admitted that reaches 5 seconds while its address holds 8 is aborted the same way as a stalled one and logged, releasing its slot; quick downloads such as a page's pictures never count while the address is below its limit, so one address can hold at most 8 slots for longer than 5 seconds. Each address may also have at most 24 downloads in flight, long or quick, and a new download beyond that answers the same `503` before it takes an instance slot, so one address cannot keep every slot busy by reopening downloads before they reach 5 seconds, while a page loading up to 24 pictures at once is unaffected. The counts live in each API process, like the 64 slots. The stall window and minimum, the two per-address counts and the 5 seconds are constructor settings, so tests use short values (slice 8 review follow-up, owner decision of 2026-11-11, pending owner review). It sends these headers:
  - `Content-Type` from detection (plain text as `text/plain; charset=utf-8`);
  - `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`;
  - `Content-Disposition` from the token: `attachment` with the file name in RFC 6266 form (an ASCII fallback with CR, LF, quotes and backslashes removed, plus `filename*` in UTF-8), or `inline` for a picture shown as a preview;
  - `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; sandbox; frame-ancestors 'none'`, `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-origin` and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`;
  - `Cache-Control: private, no-store`, so no browser keeps a file after its message is deleted or its channel erased; a cache lifetime is adopted only if the slice review measures a need;
  - `Content-Length` from the stored size, with the last byte sent only after the object is confirmed to end at that size, as for avatars.

  It ignores `Range` and always answers the whole file: in slice 6 only pictures are shown inline, and audio and every other kind is a download.

### References other than messages

From slice 8 (the owner's decision of 2026-11-10 in the [slice 8 plan](../plans/slice-8.md#4-owner-questions), which amended [ADR-0009](../../docs/decisions/0009-owner-approved-exceptions-and-visibility.md) item 1), files referenced by an item other than a message are downloaded through the same OVC addresses, bound to the referencing item:

- **Token version 2** has the fixed layout: a version byte `2`, the attachment identifier (16 bytes), a reference kind byte, the referencing item's identifier (16 bytes), the expiry in Unix seconds (8 bytes, big-endian), the disposition (1 byte), and an untruncated HMAC-SHA-256 over all the preceding bytes, under the same HKDF key, the same expiry rule (5 to 10 minutes) and the same refusals as version 1. The kind bytes are fixed: `1` `material`, `2` `template`, `3` `lesson`, and from slice 9 `4` `issue_snapshot`, `5` `answer` and `6` `review_item`, authorized under the owner's decision of 2026-11-11 in the [slice 9 plan](../plans/slice-9.md#4-owner-questions), which amended [ADR-0009](../../docs/decisions/0009-owner-approved-exceptions-and-visibility.md) item 1 and the credentials-in-URLs rule of `AGENTS.md` to name issue snapshots, answers and review revisions as referencing items, keeping every bound of that rule. The parser picks the layout by the version byte and requires that version's exact length; version 2 refuses `message` and every unknown kind byte, and version 1 only ever serves message references.
- **Staging:** the token layout, the `ref` parser and the kind enum land first, and each kind's authorization, with its audience and denial rows, lands in the change that creates its table: `material` and `template` with the library, `lesson` with lesson materials, `issue_snapshot` with the `issues` table, `answer` with the `answers` table and `review_item` with the `review_revisions` table; a kind without its authorization is refused like an unknown one.
- **Minting:** `GET /api/v1/attachments/{id}?ref=<kind>:<item id>&disposition=` mints only when a live reference row (the attachment, the kind, the item) exists and the caller can see that item: the tutor for their own materials that are not deleted, their own templates that are not deleted, and their own lessons; a student for a lesson they can see, decided by `VisibleLessonReferences`, a copy of the [visibility rule](schedule.md#visibility) with the same predicate as `GetStudentLessonView`, compared with the other copies by `TestTheStudentVisibilityCopiesAgree` (change B5, pending owner review). From slice 9, for the homework kinds, where "readable" means readable to the student by `ReadableStudentSubmission`, the readability predicate of [homework](homework.md#student-readability), compared with its other copies by `TestTheStudentSubmissionReadabilityCopiesAgree`:
  - for every homework kind, the issue's tutor never mints for an issue marked for erasure, which answers `404` like a random identifier, as every tutor route of that issue does ([tutor reads](homework.md#tutor-reads));
  - `issue_snapshot:<issue id>`: the issue's tutor, also while the issue is `withdrawn`; a student holding a readable submission of that issue;
  - `answer:<submission id>`: the issue's tutor, also while the issue is `withdrawn`; the submission's student while the submission is readable;
  - `review_item:<revision id>`: the issue's tutor, for every revision, drafts included; the student only when the revision is the latest published revision of a readable submission, never a draft or an earlier published revision.

  Its refusal order, every `404` with the same body as a random identifier:
  1. whether the caller can see some item that holds a live reference to the attachment, `404` otherwise;
  2. a missing `ref` for an attachment that is not a chat attachment, or any `ref` on a chat attachment, `404`;
  3. the format of `ref`: exactly one value, a known kind and a UUID, `422 validation_failed` at `ref` otherwise;
  4. whether `ref` names an item the caller can see that holds a live reference to the attachment, `404` otherwise;
  5. `disposition`, `422 validation_failed` at `disposition`.

  Chat attachments are still minted without `ref`.
- **Disposition:** a picture with recorded dimensions may be `inline`, and `inline` for any other kind answers `422` at `disposition`; every other kind is an `attachment` download, so PDFs, audio and video open outside the page and never play inline in OVC's origin.
- **Serving:** `GET /api/v1/files/{id}` accepts token versions 1 and 2 and, for version 2, re-checks that the attachment is `bound`, that the named reference row (attachment, kind, item) still exists and, for a material, that the material is not deleted; from slice 9, for `issue_snapshot`, `answer` and `review_item`, also that the issue is not marked for erasure, and for `answer` that the submission's relationship is neither `reclaim_pending` nor `discarded`; a withdrawn issue does not stop serving, so the tutor's addresses on it keep working. Every refusal is the same `404`, and the headers, deadlines and limits are those above. The token is a bearer credential, as for chat, and names an item, not a caller: an address minted before a student loses sight of a lesson, or before a student loses access to an issue snapshot or a review revision (a withdrawal, a reclaim that hides the link's work, or a newer published revision), keeps working until its expiry, at most 10 minutes (an accepted residual, recorded in [T-27](../../docs/threat-model.md#threats-and-controls)).
- **Responses that list materials or prompt images** (the library listing, the template, its student view and the lesson detail) carry addresses minted per response, as message pages do: `inline` for pictures with recorded dimensions and `attachment` for every other file, each naming its own referencing item (`material`, `template` or `lesson`); they send `Cache-Control: no-store`. From slice 9 the same holds for every homework response that carries addresses: the tutor's `GET /issues/{id}` (`issue_snapshot`), both schemas of `GET /submissions/{id}` and the answers of autosave, submitting, review saving and publishing (`issue_snapshot` for the snapshot's files, `answer` for file answers, `review_item` for corrected files, the student's naming only the latest published revision); every homework route sends `Cache-Control: no-store`.
- **Avatars** are public by design: `GET /api/v1/avatars/{id}` serves them without authentication, so the invitation page can show the tutor.
- **Previews** use the original image, constrained in size by the client. Generated thumbnails are a later optimisation.

## Quotas

- **Every tutor has two quotas:** 5 GB of files and 50 GB of recordings by default. The operator changes them with `ovc-admin tutors set-quota`.
- **The file quota counts each attachment once per tutor**, when it is referenced from:
  - the tutor's library, templates, prompts, issue snapshots, lessons and series;
  - the tutor's direct and group channels, whoever sent the file;
  - student answer files and corrected files in the tutor's issues.
- **Charging is incremental:** a charge row per tutor and attachment is created with the first reference and removed with the last one. Usage changes by the attachment's size only then, so no reference change rescans the tutor's files.
- **Chat charges** (slice 6): binding an attachment to a message creates its reference and, when the channel's tutor has no charge for it, a charge row and the size added to `file_usage_bytes`, in the send transaction; removing the last reference that charges the tutor removes the charge and subtracts the size in the same transaction. Every path that creates or removes a charge takes the charge locks of the [lock order](accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check): the charged tutors' profiles up front, then the channel, then the message. Binding refuses with `507 quota_exceeded` when the usage would pass the quota, which is the exact bound.
- **Homework charges** (slice 9): issuing, autosave, review drafts, withdrawal, the ending's deferred release, erasure and the purges charge and release as [homework references and charges](#homework-references-and-charges) states, under the same exact bound at binding (`507 quota_exceeded`).
- **Library charges** (slice 8): creating a material, saving or duplicating a template, attaching materials to a lesson or a series, and copying a series' materials into an occurrence add references; each creates a charge only when the tutor holds none for the attachment, which happens only for a newly bound upload, because a material's file is charged from the material's creation. Removing a reference removes the charge only with the tutor's last reference to the attachment. Every such path takes the tutor's profile lock first, then the locks of the item it changes in the order of [locks and the session re-check](accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check), and binding refuses with `507 quota_exceeded` when the usage would pass the quota.
- **Pending uploads count, but are not charged:** before streaming, admission refuses when the tutor's charged usage plus existing non-expired `uploading` and `pending` uploads charged to that tutor, of every context (chat uploads in that tutor's channels, from slice 8 the tutor's own `library` and `prompt_image` uploads, and from slice 9 the `answer` and `corrected` uploads scoped to submissions of the tutor's issues), already meets or exceeds quota (used + pending ≥ quota), read through the partial index on `attachments (quota_tutor_id)` of `uploading` and `pending` rows; the not-yet-inspected new upload is not added at admission. Both admission and completion read pending usage from database rows; a `pending` row counts with its size, an `uploading` row with its `reserved_bytes` (the request's `Content-Length` capped at the context limit, or the context limit without one); the completion check excludes the completing row itself and uses its final size. These checks refuse early; binding under the charge locks is the exact bound, which two racing completions cannot pass.
- **Avatars** count against no quota.
- **Over the file quota:** an upload or binding is refused with `quota_exceeded`, and a student sees "your tutor's storage is full".
- **Recording quota:** recording usage changes when a recording becomes available, expires or is deleted ([recordings](realtime-and-video.md#recordings)).
- **Usage** is shown in the tutor's settings and updated in the same transaction as each charge change; from slice 6 `GET /tutor/settings` answers `{file_quota_bytes, file_usage_bytes}` and Teaching settings show a usage bar for files only.

## Deletion

- **Deleting a message** removes its attachment references in the same transaction, which releases their charges and queues each object's `storage_delete` ([messages](realtime-and-video.md#messages)); an erased channel's messages lose theirs through the erasure job.
- **Account purge** removes every reference the account's items held, which deletes files that are no longer referenced ([account deletion](accounts.md#account-deletion)); a tutor's purge also deletes the attachments that students uploaded to the tutor's channels, which are charged to the tutor, with physical removal subject to the durable cleanup and finite-retry residuals above.
- **Homework files** (slice 9) leave as [homework references and charges](#homework-references-and-charges) states: a withdrawal releases its draft file answers at once, an ending's never-submitted file answers and a marked issue's files are released by the `homework.cleanup` job, pending `answer` and `corrected` uploads whose submission is gone go through the intent-routed deletion of [upload flow](#upload-flow) item 11, and snapshot files and submitted answer files stay until the purge.
- **Library materials** referenced by templates, issued snapshots, lessons or series stay in storage until those references are gone ([library](homework.md#library)); deleting a template, removing a prompt image or a material block from a template, detaching a material from a lesson or a series, and deleting a material remove their own references in the same transaction, which releases the charge and queues the object's `storage_delete` with the last one.
- **A tutor purge** removes the library and the lesson and series materials in batches with their references and charges ([purge](data-model.md#purge)).

A null attachment uploader is valid only for `answer` context in `bound` or `deleted` state. Migration `00042_attachment_uploader_retention.sql` enforces this with a check constraint; other orphan uploads cause deletion to fail transactionally. Its downgrade restores `NOT NULL` before removing the constraint and refuses while retained null uploaders exist.
