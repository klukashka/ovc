# Where the end-to-end suite and the merge gate spend their time

Recorded on 2026-11-11 as input to slice 9's first change, which speeds up and consolidates the end-to-end suite. The full local check run, as the merge gate runs it, peaked at 581 s on 2026-11-10 against the 10-minute budget in `AGENTS.md` (NFR-6.2), and slice 9 adds more suites. This record is a read-only analysis of existing gate logs and test code: no suite, gate or server was run for it, so every saving below is an estimate to be confirmed by measurement.

## Sources and method

- **Gates.** The 21 merge gates with the full check list that passed between 2026-11-10 04:40 and 2026-11-11 13:58 UTC, taken from `merge-gate.log` in the gate's lock folder (`~/Library/Caches/ovc-e2e/`). Each gate's command logs (`<index>.log` in its `logs.*` folder) were matched to the gate by time. The gates tested trees based on `c31de3c` through `6959442`; the suite grew from 1515 to 1788 test runs over the period.
- **Command and lane times.** The gate prints its timing summary to its caller, not into the log folder, so command times here are derived from the log files: a lane creates each command's log just before starting the command, so a command's time is the creation time of the next log of its lane minus its own, and the last command of a lane ends at its log's last write. For the 581 s gate (`e8069fb`) this method gives 581 s, the figure the gate reported. Lanes follow `full_checks` in `tools/merge-gate.sh`: `pre`, then lanes A, B and C together, then `e2e` alone.
- **Test times.** Per-test durations come from Playwright's list reporter in the `pnpm e2e` log (index 26). Unless stated otherwise, per-spec and per-test figures are the mean of the two latest passed gates with 1788 test runs (`2dad4ee`, `6959442`); variance figures use the five latest gates with at least 1755 runs.
- **Configuration.** `frontend/playwright.config.ts` at `a1b5df9`: Playwright 1.63.0, `fullyParallel: true`, `retries: 0`, no `workers` setting (so Playwright's default, which the logs print as "6 workers" on this 12-core machine), three projects (Chromium, Firefox, WebKit), with the three browserless specs only in Chromium and `@coarse-pointer` tests not in Firefox.
- Only timing and summary lines of the logs were read.

## Answers

### 1. The critical path: end-to-end is 72 % of the gate

The gate runs `e2e` only after lanes A, B and C have all ended (by design, so the end-to-end deployments' connections and CPU do not compete with `go test -race`), so its wall time is lane A plus the end-to-end run. The rest (the `pre` tool-binary check and gaps between phases) is under a second.

| Over the 21 passed gates | Minimum | Median | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate wall time | 395 s | 436 s | 581 s |
| Lane A (`go test -race ./...` alone) | 105 s | 131 s | 164 s |
| Lane B (Gitleaks, Go lint and vet, govulncheck, sqlc, merge-gate self-test) | 58 s | 64 s | 87 s |
| Lane C (docs, OSV, frontend install, typecheck, lint, unit tests, build, audits, hook checks) | 61 s | 69 s | 104 s |
| `pnpm e2e` | 282 s | 324 s | 417 s |

The six latest gates (1650 to 1788 runs) took 485 to 581 s, median 521 s, with lane A at 131 to 164 s and `e2e` at 347 to 417 s. `e2e` is 68 to 74 % of each gate's wall time (median 72 %) and lane A the rest. Lanes B and C finish 22 to about 100 s before lane A, so they are never on the critical path; lint and the scans cost nothing on it today.

Inside lane A, one package sets the length: `internal/api` took 97 s, 142 s and 136 s in the three gates read (`c31de3c`, `e8069fb`, `6959442`), against 53 to 84 s for the next package (`internal/adapters/meeting`). Its 793 test functions already call `t.Parallel()` widely, so the run is likely CPU-bound rather than serial.

**Growth.** The median `e2e` time rose from 307 s at 1515 runs to about 357 s at 1788 runs: about 0.18 s of wall time per added test run, or about 0.55 s per new test in three engines. At the median gate of the latest six (521 s) the budget leaves about 79 s, room for roughly 140 new three-engine tests; at the 581 s peak it leaves 19 s, about 35 tests.

The time is spread, not concentrated in a few slow tests:

- **No test in Chromium takes 5 s or more**; the slowest takes 4.2 s.
- **Almost every slow Firefox or WebKit test is slow only sometimes.** Across the five latest gates, only two tests took at least 4 s in every gate in some engine: `groups.spec.ts` "the group after the 200th is refused with the limit message" (4.0 to 4.8 s in every engine, because it seeds 199 groups through the API, in batches of 20 concurrent calls) and `messages.spec.ts` "a tutor and a student exchange messages live…" (3.6 to 4.2 s in Chromium and at least 4.6 s in Firefox in every gate, with WebKit spikes up to 24.5 s; a long two-page journey).
- **Spikes.** A Firefox or WebKit run of at least 3 s for a test whose fastest run in the five gates was under 1.5 s is counted as a spike. Firefox has about 44 spikes per gate, adding about 282 s of summed time (29 % of its time); WebKit about 18 spikes, about 145 s (21 %). The tests with the largest mean summed time (for example `session.spec.ts` "the sixth failed sign-in…": 0.8 s in Chromium, 15.4 s in WebKit; `students.spec.ts` "Show more for a card removed meanwhile…": 0.5 s in Chromium, 10.2 s in Firefox) are spikes, not slow tests.

### 3. The engines slow down as each project runs

The projects run one after another: all six workers run Chromium tests, then Firefox, then WebKit (in the `6959442` gate, test indices 1 to about 625 are Chromium, about 535 to 1250 Firefox and about 1205 to 1788 WebKit, with short overlaps at the switches). Comparing each Firefox or WebKit test with the same test in Chromium, by quarter of that engine's run:

| Gate (base) | Firefox / Chromium, quarters 1 → 4 | WebKit / Chromium, quarters 1 → 4 |
|---|---|---|
| `6959442` | 1.57, 2.06, 2.82, 4.27 | 1.36, 1.40, 2.08, 2.10 |
| `2dad4ee` | 1.60, 2.16, 2.50, 4.08 | 1.37, 1.42, 2.42, 2.77 |
| `c2bb228` | 1.73, 2.12, 2.24, 4.51 | 1.33, 1.47, 2.42, 2.53 |
| `e8069fb` | 1.76, 2.41, 2.94, 4.24 | 1.25, 1.59, 2.17, 2.67 |
| `9c063a8` | 1.66, 2.03, 2.52, 5.01 | 1.33, 1.40, 1.98, 2.15 |

The same tests cost Firefox 1.6 times Chromium early in its run and 4 to 5 times at the end; WebKit goes from about 1.35 to about 2.4. This matches the [end-to-end profile of 2026-10-29](check-budget-2026-10-29-e2e-profile.md#item-b-firefoxs-overhead-profiled-at-ba042d8): Playwright's Firefox build minimises memory on every tenth context close, and the stall grows with the number of contexts the browser has opened. With about 580 Firefox runs over 6 workers, that is roughly 58 tenth-closes per gate; the 44 spikes counted above are consistent with that, since the profile's stalls ranged from 0.3 to 7 s and many stay under the 3 s spike threshold. The WebKit spikes have no known mechanism. Each worker keeps one browser and one deployment for its whole life, so three causes are confounded and the logs cannot separate them: the browser's age, the growth of the worker's own database, and, in the last Firefox quarter, the WebKit workers starting beside it. Chromium's tests cannot show the same effect in this comparison, because they are its baseline.

If Firefox and WebKit stayed at their first-quarter ratios for their whole runs, Firefox would sum to about 590 s instead of about 935 s and WebKit to about 480 s instead of 620 to 720 s: about 490 to 590 s of summed time, or 80 to 100 s of wall time at 6 workers. That is an upper bound for removing the slowdown.

### 4. Seeding and sign-in: already through the API, a small share

- **Accounts** are created through the test-control route `/__test__/accounts` (`seedAccount` in `frontend/e2e/accounts.ts`; `seedVerifiedUser` is called 309 times in `frontend/e2e/`), and sessions through `/__test__/session` (`signInThroughTheApi`, called 293 times, which sets the refresh cookie and opens no page). Worker-scoped shared actors (`tutor`, `mariaAndAnna`, `pavelAndAGroup`, `studentsOfPavel` with 31 students, `mariaWithJoinRequests` with 31 requests) are seeded once per worker.
- **Sign-in through the form** remains only where the form is under test: `signInThroughTheForm` in `session.spec.ts` (8 call sites) and password-field fills in 13 other places across 8 specs (`signup`, `accessibility`, `home`, `join-requests`, `email-settings`, `invitations`, `reclaim`, `today`).
- **Cost.** The 25 browserless `session-transport` tests each seed two accounts and run an API scenario, and took 1.9 s summed in Chromium, so one seeding call costs at most about 38 ms. Even at two seeding calls per test run, seeding would be at most about 7 % of the 1.1 s mean test run. In Chromium a test that signs in through the API and opens one page takes about 0.19 s ("a signed-in tutor opening sign-in is sent to Today"), and the form sign-in tests take 0.2 to 0.8 s. Replacing the few remaining form sign-ins would save seconds, and they are the tests of the form itself. The per-test cost is the browser: a new context and page, the app's boot and its requests, which is also where Firefox and WebKit pay their overhead.

The seeding cost was not measured directly; a step reporter like the one in the 2026-10-29 profile would give the exact share.

### 5. Duplicated journeys

- **Matrices from loops.** 22 `test()` call sites generate more than one title (a language, theme, width or role loop), for 243 runs and 162 s summed, 8 % of the total. The largest are `today.spec.ts:346` (16 s), `messages.spec.ts:407` channel pagination in eight combinations (15 s), `schedule.spec.ts:200` (14 s), `desktop-layout.spec.ts:247` routes at widths (12 s), `lesson.spec.ts:400` (12 s) and the four `lesson-form-layout.spec.ts` loops (35 s together).
- **The same behaviour on several pages.** The lesson join flow is tested from the lesson page (`lesson-room.spec.ts`, "the lesson room through a new tab", 48 runs, 52 s), from Home ("Join on Home", 21 runs, 21 s) and from Today ("Join on Today", 6 runs, 14 s). The titles show different states rather than repeats (a held join, a refusal, an account change, an ending, a group removal), so merging them would drop distinct states. Sign-up and confirmation appear in 18 specs, but outside `signup.spec.ts` they are pilot steps or setup through the API.
- **Earlier evidence.** The [lesson spec consolidation record](check-budget-2026-10-29-e2e-lesson-consolidation.md) found that no pair of tests in `lesson.spec.ts` shared both arrangement and action, and that each candidate merge would have broken the three-phase rule in `AGENTS.md`; the owner closed that change. The titles read for this record show the same pattern: the specs test different states of the same pages.

The duplication that costs time is the three-engine multiplication: every browser test runs in three engines, and Firefox and WebKit together cost about 4.5 times Chromium's summed time for the same tests.

### 6. Worker count

Six workers (Playwright's default of half the 12 logical cores) is the measured optimum. The [2026-10-29 profile](check-budget-2026-10-29-e2e-profile.md#item-a-workers-and-pools-measured-at-ba042d8) found 8 and 9 workers no faster than 6 within the run-to-run spread, because summed test time grew with the worker count while the machine was CPU-saturated. The [slice 7 known limitations](../reviews/slice-7-known-limitations.md) record that limiting WebKit to three workers made the run slower (289 s against 220 s) and did not remove the WebKit timeouts, and that the owner decided on 2026-11-08 not to limit WebKit workers. The gate logs give no reason to revisit either: six workers run each engine in turn, and the slowdown in answer 3 grows with each browser's age, not with the number of workers.

### 7. Real timeouts and long condition waits

No test is slow because it waits on real time:

- Countdown and lesson-window tests use `page.clock` (31 `clock` calls in 9 specs) or the shared fake clock (`control.advance` and `control.tick`, 61 calls in 18 specs, helpers excluded). For example "the resend button waits 60 seconds after sign-up" runs the 60 s on `page.clock` and takes 0.8 s in Chromium; its 5.5 s in Firefox and 9.4 s in WebKit are spikes.
- The only explicit wait limits in specs are a 5 s `waitForFunction` in `smoke.spec.ts` and a 5 s `waitForLoadState` in `realtime.ts`; both end as soon as their condition holds.
- The two consistently slow tests (answer 2) are slow because of seeding volume and journey length, not waiting.

Real timeouts do cost time in failing gates, where each failing test waits out its timeouts (Playwright's default test timeout is 30 s, and `retries` is 0). On 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11 (UTC), `merge-gate.log` records 19 failed full gates; 18 left a full set of command logs and took about 181 minutes in all, against about 161 minutes for the 21 that passed. Fifteen of the 18 reached the end-to-end run and had failing tests. Twelve of those took 4.9 to 6.5 minutes in Playwright, like a passing run, but three took 17.6, 23.5 and 26.9 minutes, with 8, 14 and 19 failures, and one more took 9.3 minutes with 6. Some failures were real defects, not flaky tests; the logs' summary lines do not tell them apart.

## Speed-up options, ranked by recommendation

The order weighs saving against risk and coverage: options that keep coverage come first, so option 2, the largest lever, ranks below option 1. Savings are wall time of a passing gate unless stated. Each needs three runs with and three without the change on an otherwise idle machine before it is accepted, as earlier budget records required.

| Rank | Option | Estimated saving | Risk | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Give Firefox and WebKit fresh browsers more often.** For example, split each engine's project into several projects by spec group, so each worker serves fewer tests before a new worker starts with a new browser and a new deployment; or a fixture that relaunches the worker's browser every N tests. | 40 to 90 s (upper bound 80 to 100 s, answer 3) | Low to medium: each extra worker start costs a deployment (at most 1.7 s in the 2026-10-29 profile) and a browser launch; concurrency, and so the connection peak, stays at 6 workers. The share of the slowdown that comes from database growth or overlap with WebKit would not go away; measure Firefox alone first. | Kept: same tests, same engines |
| 2 | **Run Firefox, and possibly WebKit, on a tagged subset**, for example the pilot scenarios and the tests of engine-specific behaviour (focus, layout, clipboard, uploads, the coarse pointer), with Chromium keeping the whole suite. | Firefox on a fifth of its tests: about 125 s; adding the same for WebKit: about 90 s more | Medium: engine-specific defects in untagged pages reach `main` | **Reduced**: drops cross-engine coverage for untagged tests. Needs an explicit owner decision and an update of the supported-browser commitment in `AGENTS.md` and the requirements; never silent |
| 3 | **Move browser-independent assertions to Go integration tests**, as [proposal 0004](../../docs/proposals/0004-end-to-end-suite-cost.md) suggests: scope refusals, `not_found` answers, limits and data-shape checks that a page only displays. A test run costs about 3.1 s summed over its three engines, so each test moved saves about 0.5 s of wall time. | About 30 s per 10 % of tests moved | Low to medium: each move needs a mapping from the removed assertions to the Go test that now holds them | Kept when each assertion is mapped; the page still needs one end-to-end test for what it shows |
| 4 | **Shorten lane A**: profile `internal/api`'s 97 to 142 s, the longest package, and split or speed up its slowest tests. Lane A drops to the next limit, about 85 to 105 s (the next package, or lanes B and C). | 0 to 60 s, unknown until profiled: the package may be CPU-bound | Low | Kept |
| 5 | **Stop failing end-to-end runs early** with Playwright's `maxFailures` (for example 10) in the gate's run. | None for a passing gate; failing gates drop from up to 27 minutes to about the length of a passing run | Low: a failing run lists only the first failures, so a second run may show more | Kept for passing gates; a failing gate reports fewer failures. Owner decision, since it changes what a failed gate shows |
| 6 | **Bulk seeding for large fixtures**: one test-control call for the 199 groups of the group-limit test, and similar large seeds (`studentsOfPavel`, `mariaWithJoinRequests`, 31 each, once per worker). | Under 2 s for the group-limit test, which already seeds in batches of 20; under 5 s in all | Low; touches the test-control surface (`OVC_TEST_CONTROL=1`), so it meets the security trigger | Kept |
| 7 | **Share the arrangement of matrix tests** through worker-scoped fixtures where tests seed identical data per language, theme or width. Merging the tests themselves would break the three-phase rule. | Under 10 s (the loops total 162 s summed, 27 s of wall time) | Low | Kept |
| 8 | **Overlap the end-to-end run with lane A**, starting it as soon as lanes B and C end. | Lane A minus the later of lanes B and C, about 22 to 100 s on paper, less once `e2e` slows beside `go test -race` | High: the gate keeps `e2e` alone on purpose (about 70 connections, a CPU-saturated machine), and the slice 7 known limitations tie WebKit timeouts to machine load | Kept, but expect flaky failures; not recommended without a new design |
| — | More workers, or a WebKit worker cap | None (answer 6) | — | — |

Options 1 and 3 together keep coverage and could take 70 to 120 s off the median gate, which would give room for slice 9's suites. Option 2 is the largest lever but changes what the project promises about browsers, so it belongs to the owner. Option 4 is outside the end-to-end suite but on the same critical path.

## What this record does not cover

- No run was made, so the seeding share, the cause of the browser slowdown and every saving are inferred from logs and code, not measured.
- Command times are derived from log file times; they agree with the gate's reported wall time for the one gate checked against it, but are not the gate's own records.
- The gates ran with other sessions' work on the machine; load averages were not recorded in the logs, so part of the run-to-run spread (282 to 367 s at 1515 runs) is machine load.
