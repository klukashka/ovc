## 1. Problem, outcome and scope

**Problem.** After slice 9 the MVP's homework loop works locally, but four things stand between it and the pilot of [ADR-0013](../../docs/decisions/0013-pilot-deployment.md).

- **The check budget is exceeded.** The last three passing merge gates of slice 9 took 700.3, 639.9 and 716.1 s, a median of 700.3 s, above the 540 s at which the check-budget rule of [ADR-0018](../../docs/decisions/0018-targeted-checks-and-merge-gate.md) then made a consolidation due before further feature work (replaced on 2026-11-17 by the idle-machine rule, see D5). The end-to-end run is the larger part of each gate and grew from 1,859 to 2,102 runs during slice 9. Load from other work on the laptop and sleep on battery made several gates slow or flaky, and the gate does not record its power source or its load at the end.
- **Carried items are open.** The [slice 9 slice review](../reviews/slice-9-slice-review.md#carried-into-slice-10) and its [known limitations](../reviews/slice-9-known-limitations.md) carry 18 rows and three owner decisions into slice 10, and the roadmap entry of slice 10 holds carry-overs from slices 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8 and from the design slice.
- **The product is unfinished.** Today and Home still assemble their panels from several reads, `GET /home` is unbuilt, the first-run checklist cannot be dismissed, the Students list has no open assignments column, Home cannot say that checked feedback is new, and every page below 1024 px uses the interim one-column frame (FR-15, NFR-5.2). Web push (ADR-0015) is unbuilt and has no functional requirement.
- **OVC has no host.** It is local only, with no remote, no CI and no server. Pilot deployment, production serving, public web hygiene and the deployment's measurements need a server the owner does not have yet.

**Outcome.** End to end locally through the real API, worker, PostgreSQL, the local S3-compatible server and the production frontend build:

- the end-to-end suite is consolidated with every behaviour still covered, the merge gate prints its power source and load at its start and end, and the after-fix gate carries evidence that its change addresses the named failure;
- the carried fixes of slice 9 have landed: the tutor-state rule for homework emails, the flaky Firefox notes test, the `popstate` residual, the shared notices' focus, the assignment page's refusal focus tests, the narrower live refetch of homework frames, Upcoming deadlines expiring on an open page, the notification rows' text-load state and the pilot 2 actors' own settings;
- Today and Home each load through one `GET /home`, the checklist can be dismissed, the Students list shows open assignments, Home marks checked feedback the student has not yet seen, students keep reading their results for 30 days after a tutor's deletion, and a tutor issuing `after_check` work with no manual questions is told that its answers show at submission;
- every page has a dedicated phone layout inside a phone navigation frame, and an accessibility pass has checked WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes;
- web push is built and tested behind a setting that keeps it off, with its functional requirement and an installation guide;
- the performance risks carried from slices 8 and 9 are measured locally by query plans and counts and fixed where unbounded, the failures under load are diagnosed, the undeliverable mark works with a fake event source, and all four pilot scenarios pass in their MVP form.

## 3. Decisions the plan proposes

Each names the alternative where one was weighed. The contract changes (S2, S4 and the contract part of each later plan revision) land them in the specifications; `docs/` changes there are marked pending owner review.

### Shape and execution

- **D1. Five sub-slices, each with its own slice review (Q1 A).** 10.1 to 10.5 run in order; a sub-slice starts when the previous one's planned changes have merged, and its slice review may run while the next sub-slice's contract change is reviewed. Each sub-slice closes with a slice review whose scope is its own list of changes in section 7 (including any consolidation D5 inserted, its plan revision and the follow-ups of the previous sub-slice's review that merged after that review started), stated in the review record with each change's commit, so a change that merges while the previous review runs is reviewed exactly once, by its own sub-slice's review, with one reviewer per changed layer as `AGENTS.md` sets, and its known limitations go to `knowledge/reviews/slice-10-known-limitations.md`, one file for the slice with a sub-slice column. The alternative, one slice review at the end, would review a diff larger than slice 9's, which already needed subdivided reviewers. Because `AGENTS.md`, ADR-0011 and ovc-verify define the slice tier per roadmap slice, T1 states there that each sub-slice of slice 10 counts as a roadmap slice for the slice tier (pending owner review), and the security reviewer of 10.5's slice review also covers, per trust-boundary family, the cumulative diff since `c218028`, so no trust-boundary interaction across sub-slices goes unreviewed (K17).
- **D2. The server-bound work moves to a new "Pilot deployment" slice (Q2 A).** It starts when the owner has hosting and holds every item in section 1's "Not in scope" table that needs a server, together with the `deploy/` folder, because a `deploy/` folder makes `pnpm lint` require the 14-day release age of [ADR-0017](../../docs/decisions/0017-dependency-release-age.md), and the restore and its dependency review belong just before the first deployment (K1). Slice 10 keeps the parts that run locally: the undeliverable mark with a fake event source (H4), the log queue (H5) and process leaks (H6).
- **D3. Later sub-slices get plan revisions (Q8 A).** Before 10.3, 10.4 and 10.5 start, a revision of this plan adds their decisions, contracts, owner questions and full acceptance criteria, gets the planning reviews of section 2 and the owner's approval, and merges as a Markdown-only change. The change rows and minimum criteria here bind those revisions: a revision may split a row into several changes or add one, and says so in its changelog; it never drops a carried item without an owner decision.
- **D4. Execution.** At most three changes at once, each in its own session and worktree; one coordinator keeps the reviews, merge gates, merges, heavy-run scheduling and owner questions. - **D5. The check-budget rule applies throughout.** Every change's task plan states the end-to-end and lane A time it expects to add and extends an existing spec's arrangement where one fits; every merge report gives the gate's duration and its power and load lines (D7). Since revision 4 (2026-11-17) every full-run gate starts only on an idle machine, and when the median of the last three passing full-run gates on mains power exceeds 540 s, the consolidation question (C1, C2 and so on, each with its own task plan) goes to the owner at the earliest sub-slice slice review that is not yet resolved, as ADR-0018's check-budget rule sets since the owner's decision of 2026-11-17; a passing gate of 600 s or more still goes to the owner before that change merges.

### The check budget (10.1, changes C0a and C0b)

- **D6. C0 comes first.** No change of the slice merges before C0a and C0b, except T1, which changes only policy text. Other 10.1 changes may be developed meanwhile but merge after C0b, so C0b's paired measurements compare against a `main` that does not move under them.
- **D7. C0a: the gate prints its power source and load.** `tools/merge-gate.sh gate` prints, at its start and at its end, one line each with the power source (`mains`, `battery` or `unknown`) and the one-, five- and fifteen-minute load averages, on macOS from `pmset -g batt` and `sysctl -n vm.loadavg`, on Linux from `/sys/class/power_supply/*/online` and `/proc/loadavg`, with `unknown` when the source cannot be read. The end line also carries `battery-during-run` and `sleep` fields (`yes`, `no` or `unknown`), on macOS from `pmset -g log` between the start and end times, otherwise `unknown`. The lines go to the gate's log and its summary, and `tools/check-merge-gate.sh` asserts them. ADR-0018's check-budget rule counts a gate as on battery when either line says `battery` or `battery-during-run` is `yes`, keeping "unknown counts as mains"; and because a battery gate leaves the median, every merge report lists the durations of the battery gates beside the median, and when two of the last three passing gates ran on battery, the coordinator reports this to the owner before the next feature change merges (pending owner review, T-41).
- **D8. C0b: baseline, then consolidate (Q4 A).** C0b first measures three end-to-end runs of `main` at its base, on mains power under `caffeinate -dis`, each started once the one-minute load average is below 6 with no gate, other end-to-end run or full backend run beside it, as the [C1 measurement](../analysis/e2e-small-consolidation-2026-11-12.md#measurements) did. It then consolidates, choosing in its task plan among the options of the [end-to-end timing analysis](../analysis/e2e-speed-2026-11-11.md#speed-up-options-ranked-by-recommendation) that keep coverage: moving browser-independent assertions to Go integration tests or the Chromium-only contract suite with each assertion mapped, merging duplicated journeys, worker-scoped arrangements of matrix tests and bulk seeding of large fixtures (which touches T-36). It passes when, in three alternating pairs under the same conditions, the branch's mean end-to-end wall time is at most 80 % of `main`'s. Because moving assertions into Go tests lengthens lane A, its record also gives `go test -race ./internal/api/...` and lane A times on `main` and the branch, and the gate time projected from both. From its merge onward the ADR-0018 rule watches the gate median (D5). The owner approved C0b's task plan on 2026-11-16: implement the options that keep coverage and measure, applying D10 with the measured pairs if the target is missed, although the plan projected about 8 %; and, beyond the options listed above, advance the client save delays of the end-to-end tests with Playwright's `page.clock`, a fake clock, rather than waiting for them in real time.
- **D9. No coverage is dropped silently.** C0b records in `knowledge/analysis/e2e-consolidation-2026-11-<day>.md` every test it merges, moves or removes, with the test and assertions that now hold each behaviour, and the `--list` difference against `main`; it skips nothing, deletes or loosens no assertion, changes no `pilot-` title and raises no timeout, retry or budget. A Firefox or WebKit subset stays excluded, as the owner decided twice in slice 9. Every moved or merged test that `docs/threat-model.md` or a specification cites (for example the content-security-policy and console smoke in every engine, the storage hygiene test, the `next` and invitation redirect tests, the test-control absence test) keeps its engine coverage, and C0b updates those citations in its own diff. Bulk seeding, if chosen, sits under `/__test__/` behind `OVC_TEST_CONTROL=1` with the same loopback `Host` and JSON-only checks, and the absence test covers it (T-36).
- **D10. When C0b misses its target, or the median stays high.** If no option set that keeps coverage reaches 80 % in the paired runs, C0b does not merge as is: the coordinator asks the owner by quiz whether to merge the saving reached, to add options, or to revisit the subset question, with the measured pairs in the question. If C0b merges and the median of the first three passing gates after it still exceeds 540 s, the coordinator asks the owner by quiz before starting another consolidation, offering named options (lane A's `internal/api` profiling, option 4 of the timing analysis; further end-to-end moves; carrying on under the 600 s limit), so the rule cannot block the carried fixes indefinitely without an owner decision; since revision 4 a high median no longer blocks feature work and instead goes to the owner at a slice review (D5).

### The after-fix evidence (10.1, change T1)

- **D11. After-fix gates carry evidence, without a reviewer (Q6 A).** A gate counts as an after-fix gate only when, in addition to the 2026-11-15 definition, the readiness report quotes the failed assertion or check output of the previous gate, names the diff hunk that addresses it, and shows the failed test or check failing before the fix and passing after it with the same command and exit statuses; for an intermittent failure, the same command run a stated number of times on the unfixed and the fixed tree, with the failure reproduced on the unfixed one. Without that evidence the gate is a rerun and follows the rerun rule. T1 also lands D1's sub-slice rule. The slice review re-verifies each after-fix row. T1 lands the rule in `AGENTS.md`, ADR-0018, `docs/development.md` and ovc-verify (pending owner review) and maps it to T-41. The alternative, a fresh scoped reviewer before every after-fix gate, would have cost about ten reviews in slice 9.

Shared notices move focus after commit.** `FocusedNotice` in `frontend/src/components/ui/LiveNotice.tsx` moves focus in an effect after commit instead of a `requestAnimationFrame` callback, for every page that uses it (the lesson, group, account settings, messages, student card, library, issue, review and My tutors pages), each keeping a test that asserts the focused element (F-a). The assignment page's refusal focus moves get tests.** The fake transport (`frontend/src/lib/api/fakeTransport.ts`) has no submissions routes, so F-a first adds fake `GET /submissions/{id}` and `PUT /submissions/{id}/answers` handlers (a fake, not a mock), with a refusal queue for `validation_failed` and `507`, and adds their scenarios to the shared transport contract (`frontend/src/lib/api/transportContract.ts`), which runs against the fake in `fakeTransport.test.ts` and against the real backend in `e2e/session-transport.spec.ts`, as `AGENTS.md` requires of fakes. ### Results and home pages (10.2)

- **D21. `GET /home` serves both roles in one request.** It replaces Today's and Home's separate reads, keeping each panel's content and visibility rules.
  - The student's part is already specified in [the API](../specs/api.md): the next lesson, work to do, the last five checked submissions with excerpts and the corrected-file flag, the next seven days' lessons and the active tutors, with the `tutor` filter.
  - S2 specifies the tutor's part from what Today reads now: today's lessons in the tutor's day, the Needs checking count and its first five items, Upcoming deadlines within three days, the unread channels with their count, the pending join requests with their count and the tutor code, and the checklist's step states with the dismissal.
  - Every list is bounded with a total count, so the response has a fixed upper size; join-request actions, Join controls and "Show more" keep their own routes.
  - The route stays `user, unverified ok`. An unconfirmed caller sees only what its current reads already show it (T-44), and the response is `no-store`. The student's `tutor` filter answers `404` for a relationship outside the caller's active ones, also a discarded or `reclaim_pending` one (T-5). The tutor's unread channels part carries labels and counts, never a message preview.
  - The student's part is a student schema: its structs live in the student schema package under the conventions test, and the answer-key contract test asserts its exact key set (T-9).
- **D22. One failure state, panels kept.** A failed first load of `GET /home` shows the page's error state inside its `Page`; a failed background or live refetch keeps every panel's loaded data and its focus, as the design slice follow-up rule sets for each panel now. A live frame that touched any panel refetches `/home` once, coalesced: frames that arrive while a refetch is in flight cause at most one further refetch, so a burst of frames costs at most two reads (K8).
- **D23. The checklist can be dismissed.** `PATCH /tutor/settings` is registered with one accepted field, `{"onboarding_dismissed": true}`; `false` is refused with `422`, because dismissal is final. `GET /tutor/settings` and the tutor's `/home` carry `onboarding_dismissed`. The dismissal is stored as `tutor_profiles.onboarding_dismissed_at`, removed with the profile at the tutor's purge. Today's checklist shows "Dismiss" and, on dismissal, moves focus to the next panel's heading, as S2 specifies in `pages.md`. The recording fields stay unregistered (K13). As an `unverified ok` write it takes only the tutor's profile lock and re-checks the session under it, accepting an `active` or `unverified` account, as `api.md` and `accounts.md` specify (corrected by change B2 with the owner's decision of 2026-11-18), and counts against a new per-tutor limit, `tutor_settings_writes_hour`, which S2 adds to `data-model.md` and `api.md`.
- **D24. Open assignments on the Students list.** Each row of `GET /students` carries `open_assignments`, the number of that student's submissions in the calling tutor's own issues that are `not_started`, `draft`, `returned` or `submitted`, computed for the page in one grouped query over at most 100 rows. Another tutor's issues never count, even for a shared student (T-5).
- **D25. The seen marker (Q10 A), keyed to what the student can see.** A [points-only publication](../specs/homework.md#points-only-publications) must stay invisible to the student, so the marker never compares against review revisions. It uses the submission's student-visible `version`, which never advances on a points-only publication.
  - `submissions.feedback_version` records the `version` that the latest publication visible to the student (not points-only) produced. `submissions.feedback_seen_version` records the `version` at which the student last saw the published review. Both are nullable integers.
  - `POST /submissions/{id}/feedback-seen` with `{"version": …}` is student-only and scoped to the caller's own readable submissions, under the same predicate as `GET /submissions/{id}`. It answers `404` outside that scope. It answers `409 conflict` when `version` is not the submission's current student `version`, the same answer a stale autosave gets, and it reveals nothing that a `GET` does not.
  - Otherwise it sets `feedback_seen_version` to `feedback_version` and answers `204`; repeating it is idempotent. Its refusal order is `401` (the session under the account's row lock), `429`, `404`, then `409`.
  - The migration backfills `feedback_version` for reviews published before it from the submission's current `version`, so earlier checked work does not all show New; `feedback_new` is false while `feedback_version` is null.
  - The write advances no `version`, changes no column the tutor sees or orders by, and sends no frame or notification. As an `unverified ok` write it re-checks the session under the account's row lock and counts against a new per-student limit, `feedback_marks_hour`, which S2 adds to `data-model.md` and `api.md` (T-44).
  - The assignment page sends the `POST` once when it shows a published review. A `GET` never writes (K14).
  - `/home` and `/assignments?status=checked` items carry `feedback_new`: true only when `review` is non-null, `feedback_version` is non-null, and `feedback_version` is greater than `feedback_seen_version` (or the latter is null). A points-only publication changes neither field.
  - The answer-key contract test and the points-only invisibility test cover `feedback_new` and the `POST`. Both columns go with the submission at every purge and erasure stage, and no tutor schema field holds them (T-30, T-34).
- **D26. Results after a tutor's deletion.** Slice 9 kept students' read access to their own submissions of a deleted tutor until the tutor's purge, 30 days later. B4 inventories that against FR-11.1 and `pages.md` ("Account closed — results available until {date}" under Past tutors, the results reachable read-only, nothing after the purge), completes what is missing, and proves the boundary on the injected clock. Reads stop at `account_closed_until` whatever the purge's progress, so a lagging purge never keeps results readable past the date the student was shown: a read succeeds at 30 days less a minute and answers `not_found` from that instant, before and after the purge job runs (T-34).

### Web push (10.4, detailed in its plan revision)

- **D30. Built, tested and off.** Web push follows ADR-0015 completely: the setting defaults to off in every deployment, the API and worker read it once, the frontend hides the subscribe control while it is off, and the subscription routes are not registered while it is off, so no subscription row can exist. No production deployment turns it on before the decision record on minors and the legal assessment are recorded.
- **D31. The functional requirement (Q5 A).** The plan holds its text; S4 lands it as FR-13.5 in `docs/requirements/functional.md`, pending owner review:

  > **FR-13.5** (from slice 10; off in every production deployment until the decision record on minors and the legal assessment are recorded, ADR-0015) A user turns on push notifications on a device only by an explicit action in their settings, and turns them off there again. Push carries only lesson reminders and lesson changes, each shown with a fixed text per kind in the user's language that names no person, time, subject or content, and opens the related OVC page when tapped. Push never replaces an email or an in-app notification. Signing out, or anything else that ends the session that subscribed, stops push to that device.

- **D32. No new cryptography dependency by default.** The message encryption of RFC 8291 and the VAPID signature of RFC 8292 are built on the Go standard library (`crypto/ecdh`, `crypto/ecdsa`, `crypto/hkdf`, `crypto/aes`), proven against RFC 8291's test vectors and RFC 8292's example; the ephemeral key and salt are injected only through the constructor for those tests, never by configuration; a dependency is added only if the 10.4 revision shows the standard library cannot reasonably do it, recorded in the stack table (T-39).
- **D33. Testing the fixed host list.** The worker's sender takes its push-service host list and its dialer through its constructor; production wiring passes only the hosts fixed in code, and integration tests pass a loopback fake push service. No setting, environment variable or test-control route extends the list (K5). The production constructor cannot receive a dialer that allows loopback; the production dialer's refusal of loopback, private, link-local and metadata addresses is tested through its own control hook, so the permissive test dialer is not the only one exercised. No test reaches a vendor push service. S4 decides whether `p256dh` and `auth` are encrypted at rest, which ADR-0015 leaves to this slice, and rewords T-36's sentence on the fake push service to match.

## 5. Conflicts and silences (none resolved silently)

- **K1. A local `deploy/` folder against ADR-0017.** Creating `deploy/` makes `pnpm lint` fail while the release age is 3 days, so locally prepared deployment files would force the restore early. - **K2. The decision of 2026-11-08 against local work.** That decision deferred the stress readiness check, the diagnosis of WebKit failures under load and the performance measurements until OVC is deployed. ## 9. Risks

- **The consolidation misses 20 %, or the median stays high.** Two consolidations of slice 9 saved little (its C1 3 s, its C2 nothing merged), and a 20 % end-to-end saving is about 60 to 100 s against gates of 640 to 716 s. D10 sends both cases to the owner with the measurements instead of merging a weak change, loosening coverage or blocking the carried fixes indefinitely.
- **The budget is crossed again.** Phone layouts, the home pages and web push add end-to-end tests. D5 runs every gate on an idle machine and puts a consolidation to the owner at the earliest unresolved sub-slice review whenever the median passes 540 s; D29 keeps phone tests in the existing projects.
- **Machine load and battery.** The power and load lines make slow gates explicable, not rarer. Heavy runs stay one at a time, measurements run on mains under `caffeinate`, and the owner keeps other projects' load off the machine during gates where possible.
- **`/home` grows expensive.** One read for every panel, refetched on frames. D21's bounds, D22's coalescing and H1's burst measurement contain it; a per-panel split stays possible if H1 shows a problem.
- **Push cryptography.** A home-grown RFC 8291 implementation can be subtly wrong. D32 requires RFC 8291's test vectors and RFC 8292's example; an interoperability check against a browser would need a vendor push service, real network and data abroad, so the 10.4 revision decides whether it runs once by the owner by hand or not at all.
- **Phone layouts expand.** Every page in two roles, three engines and both themes. The design step bounds the scope before implementation, and the 10.3 revision may split page groups further.
- **Diagnosis without end.** H3 is bounded by run counts in its record, and a family not reproduced in the stated runs is recorded as such rather than chased indefinitely.
- **Plan revisions add overhead.** Three revisions, each with planning reviews. They replace detail that would otherwise be guessed now and rewritten later.
