## 1. Problem, outcome and scope

**Problem.** After slice 9 the MVP's homework loop works locally, but four things stand between it and the pilot of [ADR-0013](../../docs/decisions/0013-pilot-deployment.md).

- **The check budget is exceeded.** The last three passing merge gates of slice 9 took 700.3, 639.9 and 716.1 s, a median of 700.3 s, above the 540 s at which the check-budget rule of [ADR-0018](../../docs/decisions/0018-targeted-checks-and-merge-gate.md) then made a consolidation due before further feature work (replaced on 2026-11-17 by the idle-machine rule, see D5). The end-to-end run is the larger part of each gate and grew from 1,859 to 2,102 runs during slice 9. Load from other work on the laptop and sleep on battery made several gates slow or flaky, and the gate does not record its power source or its load at the end.
- **Carried items are open.** The [slice 9 slice review](../reviews/slice-9-slice-review.md#carried-into-slice-10) and its [known limitations](../reviews/slice-9-known-limitations.md) carry 18 rows and three owner decisions into slice 10, and the roadmap entry of slice 10 holds carry-overs from slices 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8 and from the design slice.
- **The product is unfinished.** Today and Home still assemble their panels from several reads, `GET /home` is unbuilt, the first-run checklist cannot be dismissed, the Students list has no open assignments column, Home cannot say that checked feedback is new, and every page below 1024 px uses the interim one-column frame (FR-15, NFR-5.2). Web push (ADR-0015) is unbuilt and has no functional requirement.
- **OVC has no host.** It is local only, with no remote, no CI and no server. Pilot deployment, production serving, public web hygiene and the deployment's measurements need a server the owner does not have yet.

**Outcome.** End to end locally through the real API, worker, PostgreSQL, the local S3-compatible server and the production frontend build:

- the end-to-end suite is consolidated with every behaviour still covered, the merge gate prints its power source and load at its start and end, and the after-fix gate carries evidence that its change addresses the named failure;
- the carried fixes of slice 9 have landed: the tutor-state rule for homework emails, the flaky Firefox notes test, the `popstate` residual, the shared notices' focus, the assignment page's refusal focus tests, the narrower live refetch of homework frames, Upcoming deadlines expiring on an open page, the notification rows' text-load state and the pilot 2 actors' own settings;
- Today and Home each load through one `GET /home`, the checklist can be dismissed, the Students list shows open assignments, Home marks checked feedback the student has not yet seen, students keep reading their results for 30 days after a tutor's deletion, and a tutor issuing `after_check` work with no manual questions is told that its answers show at submission;
- every page has a dedicated phone layout inside a phone navigation frame, and an accessibility pass has checked WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes;
- web push is built and tested behind a setting that keeps it off, with its functional requirement and an installation guide;
- the performance risks carried from slices 8 and 9 are measured locally by query plans and counts and fixed where unbounded, the failures under load are diagnosed, the undeliverable mark works with a fake event source, and all four pilot scenarios pass in their MVP form.

### The check budget (10.1, changes C0a and C0b)

C0a: the gate prints its power source and load.** `tools/merge-gate.sh gate` prints, at its start and at its end, one line each with the power source (`mains`, `battery` or `unknown`) and the one-, five- and fifteen-minute load averages, on macOS from `pmset -g batt` and `sysctl -n vm.loadavg`, on Linux from `/sys/class/power_supply/*/online` and `/proc/loadavg`, with `unknown` when the source cannot be read. ### The after-fix evidence (10.1, change T1)

- **D11. After-fix gates carry evidence, without a reviewer (Q6 A).** A gate counts as an after-fix gate only when, in addition to the 2026-11-15 definition, the readiness report quotes the failed assertion or check output of the previous gate, names the diff hunk that addresses it, and shows the failed test or check failing before the fix and passing after it with the same command and exit statuses; for an intermittent failure, the same command run a stated number of times on the unfixed and the fixed tree, with the failure reproduced on the unfixed one. Without that evidence the gate is a rerun and follows the rerun rule. T1 also lands D1's sub-slice rule. The slice review re-verifies each after-fix row. T1 lands the rule in `AGENTS.md`, ADR-0018, `docs/development.md` and ovc-verify (pending owner review) and maps it to T-41. The alternative, a fresh scoped reviewer before every after-fix gate, would have cost about ten reviews in slice 9.

### Results and home pages (10.2)

- **D21. `GET /home` serves both roles in one request.** It replaces Today's and Home's separate reads, keeping each panel's content and visibility rules.
  - The student's part is already specified in [the API](../specs/api.md): the next lesson, work to do, the last five checked submissions with excerpts and the corrected-file flag, the next seven days' lessons and the active tutors, with the `tutor` filter.
  - S2 specifies the tutor's part from what Today reads now: today's lessons in the tutor's day, the Needs checking count and its first five items, Upcoming deadlines within three days, the unread channels with their count, the pending join requests with their count and the tutor code, and the checklist's step states with the dismissal.
  - Every list is bounded with a total count, so the response has a fixed upper size; join-request actions, Join controls and "Show more" keep their own routes.
  - The route stays `user, unverified ok`. An unconfirmed caller sees only what its current reads already show it (T-44), and the response is `no-store`. The student's `tutor` filter answers `404` for a relationship outside the caller's active ones, also a discarded or `reclaim_pending` one (T-5). The tutor's unread channels part carries labels and counts, never a message preview.
  - The student's part is a student schema: its structs live in the student schema package under the conventions test, and the answer-key contract test asserts its exact key set (T-9).
- **D22. One failure state, panels kept.** A failed first load of `GET /home` shows the page's error state inside its `Page`; a failed background or live refetch keeps every panel's loaded data and its focus, as the design slice follow-up rule sets for each panel now. A live frame that touched any panel refetches `/home` once, coalesced: frames that arrive while a refetch is in flight cause at most one further refetch, so a burst of frames costs at most two reads (K8).
- **D23. The checklist can be dismissed.** `PATCH /tutor/settings` is registered with one accepted field, `{"onboarding_dismissed": true}`; `false` is refused with `422`, because dismissal is final. `GET /tutor/settings` and the tutor's `/home` carry `onboarding_dismissed`. The dismissal is stored as `tutor_profiles.onboarding_dismissed_at`, removed with the profile at the tutor's purge. Today's checklist shows "Dismiss" and, on dismissal, moves focus to the next panel's heading, as S2 specifies in `pages.md`. The recording fields stay unregistered (K13). As an `unverified ok` write it takes only the tutor's profile lock and re-checks the session under it, accepting an `active` or `unverified` account, as `api.md` and `accounts.md` specify (corrected by change B2 with the owner's decision of 2026-11-18), and counts against a new per-tutor limit, `tutor_settings_writes_hour`, which S2 adds to `data-model.md` and `api.md`.
- **D24. Open assignments on the Students list.** Each row of `GET /students` carries `open_assignments`, the number of that student's submissions in the calling tutor's own issues that are `not_started`, `draft`, `returned` or `submitted`, computed for the page in one grouped query over at most 100 rows. Another tutor's issues never count, even for a shared student (T-5).
- **D25. The seen marker (Q10 A), keyed to what the student can see.** A [points-only publication](../specs/homework.md#points-only-publications) must stay invisible to the student, so the marker never compares against review revisions. It uses the submission's student-visible `version`, which never advances on a points-only publication.
  - `submissions.feedback_version` records the `version` that the latest publication visible to the student (not points-only) produced. `submissions.feedback_seen_version` records the `version` at which the student last saw the published review. Both are nullable integers.
  - `POST /submissions/{id}/feedback-seen` with `{"version": …}` is student-only and scoped to the caller's own readable submissions, under the same predicate as `GET /submissions/{id}`. It answers `404` outside that scope. It answers `409 conflict` when `version` is not the submission's current student `version`, the same answer a stale autosave gets, and it reveals nothing that a `GET` does not.

Whether a browser-level end-to-end subscription test is possible without a vendor push service is open for the 10.4 revision; it may not add a test-control route that extends the list.
- **K6. NFR-2 "on the reference deployment".** NFR-2.1's latencies name the reference deployment, and NFR-2.2 and NFR-2.5 need a realistic host and network. They are measured in the pilot deployment slice; slice 10 measures plans, counts, bundle sizes and memory (D34, D35).
- **K7. "All four pilot scenarios in their MVP form".** Scenario 1's recording parts and scenario 2 step 15 are after the MVP, so the MVP form leaves them out; D40 says so in the scenarios file.
- **K8. One `/home` read against the narrower live refetch.** `GET /home` gathers every panel into one read, so a frame that touches one panel refetches all of them, which works against the goal of D18. D22 bounds the cost by fixed list sizes and coalescing; S2 also lists, per frame kind, whether it invalidates `/home` at all, and H1 measures `/home` under a burst of frames.
- **K9. A target is not a budget.** ADR-0018 requires a change to a check budget to land as its own change. C0b's 80 % is a measurement target for one change, not a standing budget, and C0a changes no limit; neither changes a check budget.
- **K10. A local run before an after-fix gate.** D11 requires the failed test to fail before the fix and pass after it with the same command before the gate; this goes beyond the wording of the owner's answer to Q6, which named only the passing run, and the owner approves it with this plan. An end-to-end or full backend run is a heavy run, so the coordinator schedules it like the others (D4).
- **K11. Phone layouts against the check budget.** A phone device project would add a fourth configuration to every gate. D29 keeps phone tests inside the existing projects at 360 px; if the 10.3 revision finds a page whose phone behaviour cannot be proven that way, it asks the owner.
- **K12. What slice 9 already delivers for results after a tutor's deletion.** Slice 9's D35 kept the read access; how much of the Past tutors row and its date the interface already shows is not recorded. B4 inventories it first (D26).
- **K13. `PATCH /tutor/settings` in the MVP.** `api.md` says the route is not registered in the MVP because recording retention comes after it and the onboarding dismissal with slice 10. S2 registers it with only the dismissal field and keeps that sentence's recording part.
- **K14. A seen marker against "a `GET` never writes".** Marking feedback as seen when the assignment page loads would make `GET /submissions/{id}` write. D25 uses an explicit `POST` sent by the page instead.
- **K15. Sub-slices and the roadmap's definition of done.** The roadmap's conditions apply to each sub-slice: requirements end to end locally, its pilot scenario segments, its checks, its documents, its merges through the gate and its own slice review, recorded under the slice as "Sub-slice 10.n closed at `<commit>`". Slice 10 closes when 10.5 closes.
- **K17. Sub-slice slice reviews against the slice tier.** `AGENTS.md`, ADR-0011 and ovc-verify run the slice tier over the diff since the previous roadmap slice closed. D1 and T1 make each sub-slice count as a roadmap slice for that tier, with its scope defined by its list of changes rather than by a commit range, because a sub-slice's contract change may merge while the previous review runs, pending owner review, and 10.5's security reviewer also covers the cumulative diff since `c218028` per trust-boundary family.
- **K18. The tutor-state rule against an accepted residual.** T-18 records, from 2026-11-12, that results emails queued before a tutor's deletion may still be sent and that no inactive-tutor rule is introduced. The owner's decision of 2026-11-15 to fix this in slice 10 supersedes it; E1 rewrites that residual.
- **K19. Frames beyond `{kind, id}`.** `realtime-and-video.md` says an `entity.changed` frame carries its identifier and nothing else. D18 adds identifiers per recipient, so F-b changes that sentence, `homework.md`'s frames paragraph and frame key sets, the strict frame parser and the realtime contract test together.
- **K20. NFR-5.2's 360 px against WCAG's 320 px.** NFR-5.1 asks for WCAG 2.2 AA, whose reflow criterion (1.4.10) asks for content without two-dimensional scrolling at 320 CSS px, while NFR-5.2 and D29 name 360 px as the narrowest width. By Q13 A, layouts and tests stay at 360 px and A1 records and fixes reflow at 320 px per page (D44); NFR-5.2 is not changed.
- **K21. Phone texts against the entry chunk.** F1 left 0.7 KB of the entry chunk's budget in `pnpm check:bundle` (180.3 KB of 181.0 KB on 2026-11-19). The bottom bar's short labels and other frame texts belong to the global language chunk, which is in the entry chunk. G0 states each new text's chunk and bytes, and G1 to G5 keep `pnpm check:bundle` passing without raising a budget; the frame's labels are needed on every page and cannot leave the global chunk, so if they do not fit, the alternative is raising the entry budget of `frontend/scripts/bundle-budget.ts` (NFR-2.4), which would be its own change with the owner's decision, not part of a 10.3 change.
- **K16. An undeliverable mark with no production source yet.** Until the pilot deployment slice adds the Data Streams consumer, nothing in a deployment sets the mark. That is acceptable because nothing is deployed before that slice; H4 adds no `ovc-admin` command or HTTP route that sets it.

## 9. Risks

- **The consolidation misses 20 %, or the median stays high.** Two consolidations of slice 9 saved little (its C1 3 s, its C2 nothing merged), and a 20 % end-to-end saving is about 60 to 100 s against gates of 640 to 716 s. D10 sends both cases to the owner with the measurements instead of merging a weak change, loosening coverage or blocking the carried fixes indefinitely.
- **The budget is crossed again.** Phone layouts, the home pages and web push add end-to-end tests. D5 runs every gate on an idle machine and puts a consolidation to the owner at the earliest unresolved sub-slice review whenever the median passes 540 s; D29 keeps phone tests in the existing projects.
- **Machine load and battery.** The power and load lines make slow gates explicable, not rarer. Heavy runs stay one at a time, measurements run on mains under `caffeinate`, and the owner keeps other projects' load off the machine during gates where possible.
- **`/home` grows expensive.** One read for every panel, refetched on frames. D21's bounds, D22's coalescing and H1's burst measurement contain it; a per-panel split stays possible if H1 shows a problem.
- **Push cryptography.** A home-grown RFC 8291 implementation can be subtly wrong. D32 requires RFC 8291's test vectors and RFC 8292's example; an interoperability check against a browser would need a vendor push service, real network and data abroad, so the 10.4 revision decides whether it runs once by the owner by hand or not at all.
- **Phone layouts expand.** Every page in two roles, three engines and both themes. The design step bounds the scope before implementation, and revision 5 splits the work into five page groups (D42); a group that grows past one reviewable change is split again in its task plan, which says so.
- **Phone tests cost gate time.** Phone tests in three engines add runs to a gate whose median was 591.8 s at the 10.2 record, before traces were turned off. D43's target of about 25 s of wall time, measured from each gate's own output, the sweeps that replace today's per-page 390 px tests, and D5's rules contain it; a change above the target goes to the owner before it merges.
- **The entry chunk is nearly full.** 0.7 KB was left on 2026-11-19 (K21); G0 measures it again and counts the bytes of every new text; page texts that do not fit go to a feature section, while frame labels, needed on every page, could only fit through the owner's separate budget change.
- **Phone behaviour that the existing projects cannot show.** Touch-only behaviour is not emulated in Firefox and only partly in the desktop projects; the `@coarse-pointer` tag covers Chromium and WebKit, and anything beyond it goes to the owner (K11, D29).
- **Manual checks of the audit.** VoiceOver and Safari on iOS need a person and a device; the audit records them as `unverified` when not run, and the 10.3 slice review states what stayed unverified.
- **Flaky browser tests during 10.3's gates.** The assignment, reclaim and WebKit closure flakes of the known limitations may fail 10.3's gates; X4 runs early so their next failure names its cause, and the rerun rule of ADR-0018 applies unchanged.
- **Diagnosis without end.** H3 is bounded by run counts in its record, and a family not reproduced in the stated runs is recorded as such rather than chased indefinitely.
- **Plan revisions add overhead.** Three revisions, each with planning reviews. They replace detail that would otherwise be guessed now and rewritten later.
