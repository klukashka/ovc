# Interface decisions and order of work

Status: the owner's decisions of 2026-11-06, taken after a review of the running interface at commit `57bf16d` with demonstration data. The owner approved the order of work below the same day, including D0, which gives these decisions a home in the roadmap, D6, which gives the Join button an owner, and steps L and C, which came from the owner's remarks after using the interface and seeing layout sketches; decisions 7 and 8 record what those sketches showed. This file is a plan, and plans are not decisions: change D0 lands the decisions in `docs/roadmap.md`, marked as pending owner review, and each later change updates the specifications it alters.

## Evidence and its limits

The review covered the real tutor and student pages at 1440 px and 390 px in Chromium with the light theme, and compared the Today and Home pages with the [prototype](../design/web/prototype/README.md). It did not examine the dark theme, Firefox or Safari. The library, builder, review and student homework pages do not exist yet, so only their specifications and the prototype were read. No real user has seen the interface; the findings are one reviewer's judgement and the owner's remarks.

Findings at that commit:

- **Today and Home:** Today shows join requests, the tutor code and a server status block, and no lessons, although lessons are scheduled that day. Home shows only the tutor list and the server status block.
- **Phones:** every move goes through the overlay sidebar, the page title appears twice in a row, and the schedule toolbar takes nearly half the first screen.
- **Messages:** the list and the conversation split 1120 px in half, the page is not as tall as the window, and channel rows and the history sit in frames rather than between hairlines. "Attach files" is the widest control, and Send does not look like the main action.
- **Schedule:** the week always opens at 08:00 in a window of fixed height, so lessons at 20:30 and 22:00 are out of view. Short lessons are cut mid-word. "Scheduled" repeats on every ordinary lesson.
- **New lesson dialog:** it lists every student as a radio button, takes duration as a number and clips the date in Chromium.
- **Student card and group page:** edit forms stay open among the data, notes sit at the bottom under the name field, and the card shows the email twice. The group page shows the colour's name as a line of its own.
- **Linear layout:** the shared `Page` component limits a page to one 720 px column by default and stacks its content, so at 1440 px about a third of the content width stays empty while data and forms run downward. This is the common cause behind the linear feel, which the owner named as a problem.
- **Colour choice:** each swatch carries a poetic name such as "Moss" or "Ink", and the group page repeats it as a line of its own. Some palette colours are hard to tell apart in the narrow colour edge of a lesson: by CIE76 distance the closest pairs are `slate` with `ink` (12.7 light, 11.6 dark) and `clay` with `rust` (15.0 light, 11.3 dark), and `moss` with `teal` looks close too (21.3 light, 21.0 dark). `slate` also equals the `info` state colour in the light theme.
- **Details:** storage is shown in bytes, the timezone is a raw list of zone identifiers, and the Today and Settings icons look alike.

## Decisions

1. **Computer first stays for both roles.** Tutor and student pages must work correctly on a computer first, and the adapted phone interface comes later, in slice 10, as [ADR-0010](../../docs/decisions/0010-quiet-visual-language.md) says. The review proposed phone first for students because the pilot runs with tutors of school students ([open implementation questions](../architecture/detailed.md#open-implementation-questions), [proposal 0003](../../docs/proposals/0003-students-aged-14-and-over.md); `AGENTS.md` and `docs/product.md` still say adult students until that proposal lands). The owner declined. No bottom bar is added before slice 10, and until then the interim one-column frame applies.
2. **Today and Home are built in parts, starting now,** rather than all in slice 10.
3. **The interface work is a separate design slice, run in parallel with slice 7,** and it finishes before slice 8, which adds materials to the same pages. It does not touch the lesson page or the video room.
4. **Three pages are reworked:** Messages at full window height, the New lesson dialog with the week view, and the student card with the group page.
5. **The lesson page is a proposal to slice 7 only:** the join block comes first and is the most prominent element, and the date and audience appear in one place instead of in both columns. Slice 7 decides how to apply it.
6. **The quiet visual language stays,** with two exceptions: the title size of decision 7, a change to the type scale in `tokens.md`, and the palette of decision 9. The typefaces and the look do not change.
7. **Every page gets a desktop layout, not one column running down.** These rules become the standard for all signed-in pages on a computer; below 1024 px pages keep folding into one column, and public pages with short forms keep their reading width:
   - a one-line header: the title on the left, the main action on the right, and a menu for rare actions; a 28 px title, with the larger one kept for Today and Home;
   - a main column and the 300 px side column of `tokens.md`, with the main content visible without scrolling at 1440 × 900;
   - lists as tables, with columns, filters in one row above them and actions at the end of each row;
   - editing in a dialog or in place rather than through forms that are always open, and notes that save themselves;
   - long forms in a grid, with short fields in one row and rare ones collapsed; settings as rows with the label on the left and the value on the right;
   - dangerous actions in a menu, with the confirmation their page specification defines.
8. **Colour is chosen by swatch alone.** Round swatches with no visible names, and a free colour already chosen. Screen readers and the tooltip use plain colour words such as "blue" and "green". The group page no longer names its colour.
9. **Similar palette colours are separated.** The closest colours move to more distinct shades, as muted as the rest. Step C measures every pair in both themes, and the owner sees samples in both themes before it lands.

## Order of work

The design slice is a roadmap slice: its slice plan gets one standard reviewer before D1 starts, plus a security-profile reviewer because D2 and D6 are expected to meet the trigger, each step is one standard-tier change with its own task plan, change checks, reviewer and merge gate, and the slice review runs when its last step has merged. Heavy runs go one at a time across all worktrees, because the end-to-end run lock is shared.

Each task plan states the security trigger with its reasons and the full acceptance criteria of its step; the results below name each step's main check only. D1, L, C, D3, D4 and D5 are expected not to meet the trigger, because they change presentation and client behavior over existing routes and authorization; L's plan argues this explicitly for the account actions in Settings (change password, sign out everywhere, delete account), and C's plan redoes the classification if it renames a stored colour key. D2 shows data from several relationships on one page, and D6 drives join links and the meeting provider, so both start as meeting the trigger: their task plans and their diffs get a security-profile reviewer unless the plan shows otherwise.

## Open items for the step plans

- **First-run checklist.** FR-15.2 requires dismissal. The stored field exists (`onboarding_dismissed_at` in [the data model](../specs/data-model.md)), and [the API specification](../specs/api.md) leaves its write route, `PATCH /tutor/settings`, unregistered until slice 10. The D2 plan either brings that route forward, updating the API specification, or leaves dismissal for slice 10 and asks the owner. The "create an assignment" step stays hidden until the library and builder exist.
- **`GET /home`.** The specifications place it in slice 10. D2 uses existing routes first and states whether it still needs that route.
- **Status word.** Removing "Scheduled" changes the shared state vocabulary; the D4 plan lists every page affected. The lesson page follows once slice 7 has finished with it.
- **Side column at 1024 px.** At 1024 px the content is 664 px wide, so a 300 px side column and its gap leave a main column of 300 px. L's plan checks this width and, if the side column does not fit, asks the owner where it goes below 1280 px.
- **Palette against states.** Lesson blocks carry the audience colour and the status together, so C's plan also measures the palette against `info`, `warning`, `danger`, `live` and `accent`.
- **Confirmations.** Join-request decisions and archiving a group with nothing to lose have reasoned no-dialog rules in `pages.md`; the D2 and D5 plans keep or change them explicitly.
- **Order of L.** L comes before D2 to D5, because they build on its components. Removing the 720 px default touches every page, so its plan names the affected end-to-end specs.
- **School students on phones.** The computer-first decision stands without usage measurements. Slice 8 and 9 plans keep the assignment page usable in one column, and pilot usage data can reopen this decision.
