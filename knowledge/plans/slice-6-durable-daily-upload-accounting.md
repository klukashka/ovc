# Preserve daily upload accounting after file cleanup

Status: approved on 2026-11-01; independent security planning review passed after clarification; owner approved implementation on 2026-11-01 within this plan. Inspected baseline: `5962f0466d56fbe33a541afb331fe679d73c969c`. Resolves the cumulative deletion/security review's major S6-SEC-02.

## Problem and outcome

`SumDailyChatBytes` sums existing attachment rows. A student can upload, bind, delete the message and wait for the storage worker to delete the attachment row; its bytes then disappear from the same UTC day's total. This contradicts the specified 200 MB per student/tutor/day limit, which includes deleted completed uploads. Ordinary storage quota is correctly released, but that must not refund the daily transfer allowance. The existing daily-cap test leaves deleted rows in the database and does not exercise physical cleanup. The finding is confirmed by code tracing; a real HTTP/worker negative control is still required.

Keep the existing daily cap, upload-start day, successful-completion accounting, hourly/concurrency limits and precise quota semantics. Persist completed byte totals independently of attachment, reference, storage object and upload-intent lifetime. No filename, object key, message body or token belongs in that accounting state.

Tier: standard; security trigger met by resource admission, durable accounting, deletion and migration (T-13/T-18/T-19/T-20/T-21/T-29/T-34/T-41). Obtain a fresh full-security planning review and owner approval, then independent standard/security readiness reviews and a separate exact-tree gate.

## Proposed implementation

Add an indexed daily aggregate keyed by student account, tutor account and UTC upload day, with a non-negative completed-byte total. The student identifier references the already-locked student user with final-delete cascade; the tutor identifier has no foreign key, avoiding an implicit late tutor-user or tutor-profile KEY SHARE lock. Final tutor purge explicitly removes its aggregate rows after stabilizing the tutor's channels and preventing new membership/upload admission. Tests must prove that a racing upload cannot recreate a row after either final purge; block, soft deletion and restoration must preserve rows. Document these deliberate FK and orphan-prevention semantics in the data model. Increment it in the same transaction that changes a validated upload from uploading to pending. The existing upload-state transition prevents double charging after retries or an uncertain acknowledgement; rollback charges nothing. Read this total for admission and exact completion checks. Deleting or expiring an attachment, releasing a charge, purging a channel or changing relationship state never subtracts from it. Final deletion of an account may remove its aggregates because the same account identity can no longer upload; mere block, soft deletion or restoration must not reset them.

Reuse the existing student-user serialization for student upload checks and completion, preserving the documented user/profile/channel/attachment/intent order. Do not add an explicit or implicit late tutor-user/profile lock, including through foreign-key checks, or hold a ledger lock across storage I/O. The typed repository boundary owns the SQL; production services never issue direct queries. Tutor uploads retain their existing exemption from this student-only daily cap.

Bound historical accounting through a separately scheduled once-per-minute cleanup job. Its own cancellable context covers candidate selection, pool waits and transactions with a 20-second pass deadline; at most 100 student candidates and 1,000 aggregate deletions per pass, in batches of at most 100 rows. Candidate admission locks student users in UUID order with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` before aggregate locks. It takes no tutor/profile/channel lock. A past-day aggregate is removable only when no uploading row for that student/tutor/day remains, under the same student serialization used by upload creation and completion. Obtain upload creation's day from a fresh transaction-local injected-clock read after acquiring that existing serialization lock, so a request queued across midnight cannot create an older bucket after its cleanup. Keep business-day attribution to the resulting row's `created_at`. Pending/bound attachments no longer require retaining their already-expired daily aggregate. A blocked student must not prevent unrelated cleanup progress. Register the job in real production composition and prove its activation through the real worker/test-control path; do not infer wiring from a direct service test. This independent deadline does not alter `ExpirePending` or presume that method already has a monotonic deadline.

## Migration and unrecoverable historical bytes

Previously deleted attachment rows cannot be reconstructed honestly. The proposed safe cutover stops old writers, backfills retained completed student chat rows by their original UTC day, and installs a singleton activation instant at the next UTC-day boundary after migration. Until that instant, new student chat uploads receive the existing structured service-unavailable refusal with a bounded Retry-After; tutor uploads and existing authorized downloads remain available. This deliberately prevents an unknown remainder-of-day allowance from being treated as zero usage. Maximum initial student-upload interruption is one UTC day; this is explicit approval scope. Completion of pre-cutover uploads must not bypass the barrier; old writers are stopped before migration and surviving durable intents follow the already approved cleanup protocol. Surviving pre-cutover intents are cleanup-only: completion must refuse an upload whose creation day predates activation even after activation has passed. Persist the activation instant exactly once; migration re-entry must not move it. Missing, multiple or malformed activation state fails closed before student streaming/publication. The activation instant is read against the injected business clock, so real integration tests cover pre/post-boundary behavior without sleeping or modifying the owner's environment.

Do not pretend backfill reconstructs already deleted bytes, alter timestamps to make old rows new, or silently weaken the first-day cap. The implementation must define safe migration rerun/idempotence and final-account purge behavior. If a simpler cutover can prove the same invariant without an availability penalty, present it for approval rather than changing this decision silently.

## Files and acceptance evidence

Expected files include a new backend migration and schema rollback, attachment/daily-accounting queries and generated sqlc output, typed repositories, files upload/maintenance services, real API/worker and migration regression tests, production composition and worker contracts for the explicitly bounded accounting-cleanup job, and `knowledge/specs/files.md`, `data-model.md`, the existing lock/cleanup contracts, a pending-owner-review threat-model update and a verification record. The existing tutor-session recheck change must be integrated first. No new dependency, HTTP route or test interception exception is proposed. Diagnostics retain existing safe outcomes; counters and cleanup failures must not expose credentials or storage identity.

Acceptance requires:

- A real student HTTP upload/send/delete followed by real worker object and row cleanup still consumes its daily bytes. Repeating reaches the same 200 MB cap and the next upload is refused even though storage quota is free. Retain the failing baseline negative control.
- Expiry, message deletion, channel erasure and relationship removal do not refund the day; another student or tutor has an independent allowance, and the next UTC day permits a new allowance.
- Concurrent successful completions cannot exceed the cap; failed validation, quota refusal and rolled-back completion add no bytes. Duplicate settlement cannot charge twice. Existing durable unknown-commit behavior still protects published ownership.
- An upload crossing midnight is attributed to its actual creation day. A creation waiting across midnight uses the fresh post-lock instant. Historical cleanup racing creation/completion preserves accounting and progresses around blocked candidates.
- Migration backfill, immutable activation state, fail-closed invalid state, the pre-activation refusal, refusal of pre-cutover publication even after activation, and post-activation success are tested with real PostgreSQL and the injected clock. Block/unblock and soft deletion/restore preserve accounting; final purge removes obsolete identifiers without losing live-account accounting or introducing a lock cycle.
- Full backend race/static/generation checks, affected production upload/message browser journeys, docs and scanners pass. Independent reviewers explicitly close S6-SEC-02 and inspect migration availability, lock order and privacy retention. The separate gate/finish preserves the 260-second ceiling and all previous failed-run evidence.

This plan does not accept the finding as a residual limitation or close slice 6. Native memory, large-data maintenance and final cumulative measurements still follow the approved closure plan.
