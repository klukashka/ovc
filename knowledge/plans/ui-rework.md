## 1. Problem, outcome and scope

**Problem.** On 2026-11-23 the owner asked for four things: OVC must hold all the logic of Weekify («Викифай»), a tutor CRM, and let a Weekify user move in; every user must be able to download their data as files; Messages must follow the familiar messenger pattern; homework must become intuitive, with a file or a photo as a task or as an answer. The [Weekify research](../research/weekify-2026-11-23.md) and the [gap map](../analysis/weekify-gap-map-2026-11-23.md) show what OVC lacks; the [UI guidelines research](../research/ui-guidelines-2026-11-23.md) lists 23 interface gaps (G1 to G23 there) and a 17-question reviewer checklist. [Proposal 0007](../../docs/proposals/0007-tutor-crm-scope.md) carries the scope changes.

**Outcome of this stage.** On computers (from 1024 px):

- Messages look and behave like a familiar messenger: bubbles, replies, read ticks in direct chats, a pinned message, search in a conversation, picture albums, reactions, a jump-to-latest control and a visible message menu;
- a tutor sets a task in one page, with files attached while writing it, as a test checked automatically or a free answer the tutor checks, and issues it in the same flow; a student answers with text and files or pictures;
- every user downloads their data as one ZIP of XLSX tables, an `.ics` calendar, their files and a JSON copy;
- the desktop gaps of the UI guidelines research are fixed across both roles, and every reworked page passes the research's reviewer checklist.

**In scope:** the four outcomes above, their requirement, specification, threat model and legal-record changes, a desktop prototype the owner approves before feature code, and the rule amendment that gives the stage its own slice review.

**Out of scope:**

- the phone design of reworked pages (owner, 2026-11-23: "concentrate on the computer version, the phone version is postponed", D2); reworked pages keep working on phones as D2 sets, and NFR-5.1 applies at every width;
- finances, students without an account, online booking and the task tracker: each is a later stage of slice 10 with its own plan (proposal 0007, D1);
- the MAX or VK bot (left out for now by the owner on 2026-11-23, proposal 0007) and the Weekify import (deferred by the owner, proposal 0007);
- voice messages, a Telegram bridge, a subscribed calendar feed, camera capture and the touch message menu (phone, with D2).

- **D7. Messenger contracts (M2 to M5), specified in U1b after the owner accepts proposal 0007's messenger part.** Every new frame goes only to the channel's active members, through the same predicate as message frames; a suspended `reclaim_pending` row and a removed member receive nothing.
  - **Replies (M2):** a send may carry `reply_to_message_id`. The server resolves it before any `409 channel_read_only` refusal and answers the same `404` at that field for a random identifier, a message of another channel and a message the sender cannot read (T-5). The quote shows the quoted message's sender label from the reader's per-role [label table](../specs/realtime-and-video.md#labels-per-role) ("Deleted user" included) and the first 120 code points of its current body, or the placeholder the chat specification gives that message ("Message deleted", "Message removed"), read at render time; a client holding a quote refreshes it on the quoted message's `message.updated` and `message.deleted` frames, so a quote never outlives an edit, a delete or a purge. Jumping to the quoted message loads history around its `seq`. Editing does not change `reply_to_message_id`.
  - **Pinned message (M2):** the tutor pins one message per channel and unpins it; a pinned message that is deleted or erased stops being pinned in the same transaction; a frame tells active members of the change; a read-only channel keeps its pin and allows only unpinning.
  - **Read ticks in direct chats only (M3):** amends the chat specification's rule that a read-marker move reaches no other member ([messages](../specs/realtime-and-video.md#messages), "no read receipts"), the [API specification](../specs/api.md)'s statement that a read-marker move changes nothing other members see, and FR-12.4, which gains the read ticks. In a direct channel with both members active, each member's channel detail and history response carry the other member's `last_read_seq`, and a read-marker move signals `read.updated` to the other member's sockets as well, coalesced to at most one frame a second. When the channel becomes read-only or the student's row becomes `reclaim_pending`, the exposed marker freezes at its value at that moment and later reads are not exposed; when the relationship is restored, or a reclaim keeps the link (which resets the student's marker, [channels](../specs/realtime-and-video.md#channels)), the exposed marker stays frozen until that member's next read after the restore, so reads made while the channel was read-only are never disclosed (T-44). Group channels change nothing, so students never learn each other's reading.
  - **Search in a conversation (M4):** `POST /channels/{id}/messages/search` with `{q, cursor}` in the body, so search terms stay out of URLs and logs; `q` of 2 to 100 code points; a case-insensitive substring match with `%`, `_` and `\` escaped, over the bodies of messages the caller can read, deleted and erased bodies excluded, newest first, keyset paginated like history; at most 30 searches a minute per user, answering `429` with `Retry-After`. M4 measures the plan and the time at 50,000 messages in one channel and adds a trigram index through a migration only if the measurement asks for it, recording the `pg_trgm` extension in the stack table if it adds it.
  - **Reactions (M5):** a member reacts to a message with one of six fixed reactions (an enum in one place), at most one per member and message, replaced or removed by the member; reacting in a read-only channel answers `409 channel_read_only`. Messages carry the count per reaction and the caller's own reaction; a tutor's reads also carry who reacted; a student's reads and every frame a student receives carry no other member's identifier. Reactions count as message writes in the 30-a-minute limit; a deleted message drops its reactions. The residual that in a group with one other student a count reveals who reacted is recorded in T-6. The owner may strike M5 at approval (Q2) without touching M1 to M4.
- **D8. The simple task path (H1, H2) uses existing contracts.**
  - "New task" is a page at `/homework/new-task` for a tutor whose email is confirmed, as library uploads and issuing require; an unconfirmed tutor sees the shared "Confirm your email" notice in its place.
  - It creates the template and issues it through existing routes. Each attached file is uploaded as a library material (the `library` context, with its kinds, limits and quota) into a library folder "Task attachments" in the tutor's language, found among the root folders by that exact name, or best effort by its NFKC and case-folded form, and created if missing; a `taken` answer from a parallel creation, or an unknown outcome of the creation, is followed by one re-listing that picks the existing folder, and when none matches, or the folder cap is reached, the material goes to the library's root (amended 2026-11-24 by H1, pending owner review); a renamed folder leads to a new one, which is acceptable because it is only a place for files.
  - The template holds an instruction block, one material block per file and one list of questions in which the tutor adds any of the six question types and mixes them freely (owner's approval of the prototype, 2026-11-23): single choice, multiple choice, short text and short number, checked automatically, and long text and file (files or pictures), checked by the tutor. Each question shows who checks it, and the list shows the count of each kind and a result hint: with only automatic questions the score is ready at submission; with only manual ones the work waits in the review queue; with both, the automatic part is scored at submission and the work waits in the review queue with it ([assignment blocks](../specs/homework.md#assignment-blocks)). A task with no questions is content only and is issued unscored; it has no key, so its policy defaults to "after checking" and no reveal warning shows. The instruction text is required. Each question needs its prompt and the fields its type requires (options with the correct ones, accepted answers, a value and tolerance), checked inline before issuing, so the template is ready under the completeness rules ([validation](../specs/homework.md#validation)); the page fills the required structural fields itself: a `multiple_choice` question's scoring `all_or_nothing`, a `long_text` question's maximum of 5,000 characters, and a `file` question's four kinds and up to 5 files, changeable in the builder.
  - Questions take the points the tutor gives them: 1 by default for automatic questions and 10 for manual ones, changeable in the builder.
  - It issues with the chosen audience and deadline. A task with at least one manual question defaults to the "after checking" answer policy; a task whose questions are all automatic defaults to "never", because such work is checked at submission, so "after checking" or "after submission" would reveal its key at once ([answer visibility](../specs/homework.md#answer-visibility)); the default follows the list until the tutor chooses a policy. The tutor may change the policy in a disclosure, which shows the same reveal warning as the Issue page whenever the chosen policy reveals answers at submission.
  - Failures: an upload refused before the template is saved leaves the materials uploaded so far in the folder, and the page lists them with Retry and Remove; a failure after the template is saved leaves a draft template in the library, and the page says so with a link to it; nothing is half-issued, because issuing is one request.
  - "Open in the builder" opens the saved template. H2 gives the student's work page a drop zone and a file picker for `file` questions on computers, keeps the questions as separate groups and marks each as checked automatically or by the tutor, with no points; the mark comes from the question type the student schema already carries, with no new student field and the answer-key contract test's allowed key sets unchanged ([separate schemas](../specs/homework.md#separate-schemas)). Answer visibility, scores and the review screen do not change (FR-9, FR-10).
- **D9. The export (E1, E2), specified in U1b after the owner accepts proposal 0007's export part.**
  - **Requesting:** for an account whose email is confirmed, as uploads require; `POST /exports` with the caller's current password in the body, checked and throttled like a password change (T-1); `202` with the export. A mandatory email "An export of your data was requested", which cannot be turned off, goes to the account's confirmed address. The owner chose both on 2026-11-23 (Q4).
  - **Routes:** `GET /exports/current` returns the caller's export with its state (`queued`, `building`, `ready`, `failed`, `expired`) and progress; `DELETE /exports/current` cancels or deletes it; a ready export carries a download address minted on request as a 5-minute signed storage URL, as [ADR-0009](../../docs/decisions/0009-owner-approved-exceptions-and-visibility.md) allows for authorized downloads, signed with `Content-Type: application/zip` and `Content-Disposition: attachment`, never logged, with `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` on the page; the storage origin's other response headers are recorded against T-35's headers in U1b.
  - **Limits:** each user holds at most one export; a new request deletes the previous one; at most one export started per 24 hours (Q3), where a failed or cancelled export does not count; a built export is kept 3 days and then deleted with its storage object by the worker. The ZIP's size is bounded by the files the export holds plus 200 MB for tables and the JSON copy; export objects are not charged to any quota; each user holds one, but a file shared by many readers, such as a group chat's picture, is copied into each reader's export, so the uncharged total is bounded by one export per user, one start per 24 hours and 3 days' retention rather than by the quotas, which T-20 and T-48 record. The worker's export lane builds at most two exports at a time across all users, which T-20 records as a queue that long builds can delay, and a job that runs longer than 2 hours fails with a notification naming the reason; E1 measures the build time of the largest export a tutor's quota allows and records it, and if it is near the bound, the export splits files into a second archive in a later change.
  - **Invalidation (T-34, T-6, T-44):** these effects delete, by the paths of "Storing the archive", the exports in any state that holds a key and may hold the data they make unreadable, in the same effects: a user's deletion, block or purge (their own export, and the exports of every tutor and student they had a relationship or a group with), a relationship ended or discarded and a channel erased (the exports of its tutor and student), a link becoming `reclaim_pending` (the exports of its tutor and student), a member removed from a group (that member's export and the tutor's), an issue marked for erasure (the exports of its tutor and its students) and a tutor's deletion (the exports of their students). Smaller changes, a message edited or deleted, an issue withdrawn and an attachment reference removed, do not invalidate exports, because they are frequent; the 3-day retention bounds them, and T-48 records that residual. An invalidated export does not count toward the 24-hour limit. Exports of a deleted tutor's students also expire at that tutor's `account_closed_until`, when the students' reads end ([student readability](../specs/homework.md#student-readability)).
  - **Storing the archive (T-34, T-48, T-20):** export uploads adopt the durable upload cleanup of [ADR-0019](../../docs/decisions/0019-durable-upload-cleanup.md) through a new adoption record, written in U1b with the next free number (ADR-0025 at `f6b202d`, since ADR-0024 records the legal documents); it amends ADR-0019, ADR-0022 and ADR-0023, adds a fourth capacity class of 256 unresolved intents, and states what replaces the attachment in each place ADR-0019 uses it: the intent's identity, the lock order (the export row, then the intent), recovery's batches and cursor, operator receipts, the outbox worker's validation and reconciliation, and the migration's capacity row. The plan fixes these requirements for that record:
    - every build gets a new random storage key and reserves its intent at build start, not at request; a full class at build start leaves the export `queued` and retries on a later tick, and `POST /exports` answers `503 service_unavailable` when the class is already full at request;
    - parts and Complete run outside database transactions; exactly one Complete is authorized by a committed completion-started marker;
    - before publication the job locks the export row and then the intent and confirms the build identifier, the `building` state and that no cleanup was requested; only then does it publish `ready` and remove the intent in that transaction; once cleanup was requested, by a deletion, an invalidation or a failure, the export never becomes `ready` and no ready notification is sent;
    - every outcome other than a definite success (an error, a timeout, a lost acknowledgement, a failed part, the 2-hour bound) requests cleanup through the journal; a definite late acknowledgement leads to generation 1 deleting the object; a lost acknowledgement leaves the intent uncertain with its capacity held, the residual ADR-0019 already accepts;
    - deleting a `queued` export, which has no key or intent yet, deletes its row with no storage work, and invalidation leaves it alone because its build will read current data; the move from `queued` to `building` (the key and the intent reservation) happens under the export row's lock, so it cannot race a deletion; deleting or invalidating a `building` or `failed` export requests cleanup through its intent; deleting a published export (`ready`, `expired`), whose intent is gone, deletes its row and queues its object's deletion through the storage-delete outbox in the same transaction, as ADR-0019 does for published objects, so no new intent and no capacity is needed;
    - a recovery pass, like `uploads.expire` for uploads, fails every `building` export whose build started more than 2 hours and 15 minutes ago and requests its cleanup, so a crashed worker does not leave it stuck.

    The residuals of ADR-0019 (an unresolved remote completion or a persistent storage failure keeps an intent and may need the operator; an upload created before its identifier was recorded is removed only by the bucket lifecycle) are recorded in T-48. A file already downloaded is outside OVC; T-48 records that residual too.
  - **Content:** built by a worker job from the same repositories and the same scope rules as the API, never from a broader query: a tutor's own relationships (never a `reclaim_pending` account's identity or answer files), groups, lessons and series, issued work, submissions with scores, the latest published review revisions and corrected files, library index, private notes, chats and files; a student's tutors, lessons (a former member's group lessons with the neutral "Group lesson" label), homework through the student schemas (the exact key set of the student routes; no key before the policy allows, no points or scores and no field revealing a points-only publication, ADR-0009, T-30), feedback, chats and files; never a card's email or a tutor's notes. The export carries no signed or minted download address: every `url` and `expires_at` of the schemas is replaced by the file's path inside the archive (T-27). Group chats hold only what the member can read in OVC, with senders labelled by the reader's per-role label table; discarded and erase-pending channels and issues marked for erasure are left out.
  - **Formats:** XLSX workbooks whose every cell is an inline string, number or date, never a formula or a hyperlink, so text starting with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` stays text, and whose sheet names are fixed by OVC, never built from user input; an iCalendar file with one event per lesson in UTC, every text value escaped and folded as RFC 5545 requires and no property built from user text other than `SUMMARY` and `DESCRIPTION`; a JSON copy with `format_version`; files under `files/<kind>/<id>-<name>`, where the name is normalised to NFC, reduced to safe characters without `..`, separators, control characters or a leading dot, capped at 100 bytes and unique within the archive. Whether the XLSX writer is a small standard-library module or a library is decided in U1b under the dependency rule of `AGENTS.md` (K6), and a library enters the stack table with its reason.
  - **Resources:** the job streams files from storage into the ZIP without holding them in memory, writes the ZIP through a multipart upload, and stays within NFR-2.3's memory bound for one large file, measured once in E1.
  - **Notification:** an in-app notification of a new kind when the export is ready or failed; its texts load with the notifications panel's chunk, so the entry chunk's budget is not touched (D12).
  - **Observability:** `export.requested`, `export.built`, `export.failed` and `export.invalidated` with the user, export identifiers, counts, sizes, the cause and the failure kind; never file names, titles, contents or the download address.

## 9. Risks

- **Scope.** Twenty changes plus the review, five of them for ADR-0024; the messenger contracts alone are four. M5 can be struck; P1 and P2 can split per page if a review grows.
- **Read ticks and privacy.** Students learn when the tutor read their message and the reverse, which they could not before; limited to direct channels with active members, and subject to the owner's acceptance of proposal 0007.
- **Export cost and safety.** A large tutor's export with many files; streaming, the daily limit, the lane's concurrency, the time bound and the short retention bound the cost, and T-48 bounds the exposure. A wrong scope rule would leak across relationships, so the export reads through the API's repositories, and E1's tests mirror the routes' authorization tests. Invalidation touches many effect paths; U1b lists them in `accounts.md` and E1 tests each family.
- **Search performance.** An unindexed substring search may be slow on long chats; M4 measures before adding an index.
- **Phone regressions.** Desktop-first rework of pages 10.3 built for phones may change them; D2 keeps every listed test passing unless the owner approves a named replacement.
- **End-to-end budget.** New Playwright tests add to the gate; each change reports its check cost as the slice 10 plan's check budget requires and consolidates rather than drops coverage.
- **The prototype review.** Owner changes after U1a may move work between changes; such a change of this plan goes back to the owner before it continues.
