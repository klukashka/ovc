## 1. Problem, outcome and scope

**Problem.** OVC has no live channel. A tutor learns about a join request, an accepted invitation or a student who stopped studying only by reloading; there are no in-app notifications, no unread badge, no email preferences and no unsubscribe; a revoked session, a blocked or deleted account keeps whatever an open tab already shows, and nothing would close a socket if one existed (FR-1.4, FR-1.7 and FR-16.1 say live connections end). The worker drains lanes one after another in batches that wait for their slowest effect, which the slice 2 review measured as a ninefold throughput loss with one slow email in ten, before lesson reminders (slice 5) depend on prompt delivery (NFR-4.5). The single frontend bundle, which grew 16% in slice 3, has to be split by route before slice 5 (slice 3 review).

**Outcome.** End to end through the real API, worker, PostgreSQL and the production frontend build:

- every signed-in tab holds one WebSocket at `/api/v1/ws`, authenticated by its first frame and kept authenticated by fresh in-band tokens, that receives `notification.new` and `entity.changed` frames and recovers missed data through REST after any reconnect;
- signing out, signing out everywhere, refresh-token reuse, a password change or reset, a reclaim, a deletion, an operator block, the 7-day cleanup and a purge close the affected sockets with `4401` within 5 seconds, also when the revocation commits while the socket is still authenticating;
- a frame reaches only the accounts the committing transaction named, decided from relationship rows held under locks, so a removed provisional student, a former party of an ended relationship and the account behind a dropped reclaim link receive nothing more about it (Q1), and no frame tells a student that a tutor blocked them;
- the slice 3 events produce in-app rows: `invitation.accepted` and `join_request.created` for the tutor, `join_request.accepted` and `join_request.declined` for the student, `relationship.ended` for the other party;
- a user sees an unread badge on Notifications, a panel with the latest 10, the Notifications page, and marks rows read; new rows arrive live and are announced; focus survives live refetches;
- a user turns optional email groups on and off in Settings, and anyone holding a valid unsubscribe link turns one group off with one click or through the confirmation page, without signing in;
- the worker's lanes run side by side, each refilling its slots as effects finish, so a slow email no longer stalls its lane or another lane;
- the frontend loads each route's code on demand, and the student's first page stays inside its bundle budget.

**In scope:**

- FR-13.1; FR-13.3 (settings, the unsubscribe token, routes and page); the closing of live connections in FR-1.4, FR-1.7 and FR-16.1;
- the WebSocket gateway (first-frame and in-band authentication, registry, bounds, liveness, revocation, shutdown), the PostgreSQL `NOTIFY` broadcaster, its listener and heartbeat, and the revocation triggers;
- frames `ready`, `notification.new`, `entity.changed`, `pong`, `error` from the server and `auth`, `ping` from the client;
- the in-app notification table, routes, producers for the slice 3 events, retention and purge;
- the email preference table and routes, `OVC_UNSUBSCRIBE_SIGNING_SECRET`, `GET /unsubscribe/preview`, `POST /unsubscribe`;
- the worker's per-lane refill loops;
- route-level splitting of the frontend bundle (from the slice 3 review);
- the frontend realtime client in `lib/realtime`, live refetch on the existing tutor and student pages, the notifications bell, panel and page, Settings → Notifications, the `/unsubscribe` token page;
- carried over from the slice 2 review: the frontend session's `start` checks the epoch before it ends the session as unavailable;
- carried over from slice 3: in-app rows for accepted invitations, join requests and ended relationships, and live connections narrowed (Q1) when a provisional student is removed, a relationship ends or a reclaim drops a link.

## 3. Decisions the plan proposes

Each names the alternative and the reason. S1 lands them in the specifications.

- **D1. Signals are written inside the committing transaction.** Producers issue `SELECT pg_notify($1, $2)` with bound parameters through a repository, `Signals` in `internal/repositories`, in their own transaction; revocation signals come from database triggers (D3). PostgreSQL delivers a notification only when that transaction commits, in commit order, and never for a rolled-back one. Every API instance, including the one that produced the signal, delivers from its listening connection. *Alternative:* the specified "`NOTIFY` after commit" with the publishing instance delivering to its own sockets directly. *Reason:* a crash or a failure between commit and publish would lose a revocation signal, which is a security effect; one delivery path for all instances is simpler to test; the cost is one local round trip of a few milliseconds and PostgreSQL's notification lock at commit (section 9).
- **D2. Four signal kinds, recipients decided by the producer.** `recheck {users}` asks every instance to re-check the sockets of those accounts; `notification {user, id}` delivers `notification.new`; `entity {users, kind, id}` delivers `entity.changed`; `heartbeat {instance}` keeps the listener's liveness (D8). Payloads are versioned JSON on the channel `ovc_realtime`, at most 7,900 bytes, with at most 100 recipients per payload; a producer with more recipients sends several. *Alternative:* the specified listener that "loads the referenced rows in batches" to find recipients. *Reason:* the producer already holds the rows and the scope rules under its locks; resolving recipients again at delivery would read state from a later moment, and a recipient list fits the payload limit.
- **D3. Revocation is signalled by triggers on the rows that revoke.** Three row-level `AFTER` triggers call one PL/pgSQL function each, which calls `pg_notify('ovc_realtime', …)` with a `recheck` payload built by `json_build_object` from the account's identifier only, in the same format the listener parses (`TestTriggerPayloadsParseWithTheListenersParser`):
  - `AFTER UPDATE OF revoked_at ON sessions FOR EACH ROW WHEN (OLD.revoked_at IS NULL AND NEW.revoked_at IS NOT NULL)`, with the session's `user_id`;
  - `AFTER UPDATE OF state ON users FOR EACH ROW WHEN (NEW.state IN ('blocked', 'deleted') AND OLD.state IS DISTINCT FROM NEW.state)`;
  - `AFTER DELETE ON users FOR EACH ROW`.

  Every path that revokes, today and later, in the API, the worker or `ovc-admin`, therefore signals in its own transaction, also sign-out's single autocommit statement. PostgreSQL collapses identical notifications within a transaction, so revoking every session of an account sends one signal. Every notifying transaction takes PostgreSQL's notification lock at commit, so sign-outs, purge batches (one signal per purged account), producers and the heartbeats serialise on it (section 9 measures it). The function bodies are dollar-quoted, multi-statement PL/pgSQL, which goose v3.28.0 splits at its semicolons unless they are wrapped in `-- +goose StatementBegin` and `-- +goose StatementEnd`; AGENTS.md did not allow those markers, so D42 adds the exception and the migration holds the goose markers, the statement markers around the three function bodies, and SQL without comments. *Alternative:* the revoking repository statements notify in the same SQL statement, with a source scan of the revoking queries proving that none is missed; it needs no new marker. *Reason:* the trigger covers paths that do not exist yet and needs no scan of SQL text.
- **D4. Narrowing, not closing, on lost scope** (D37).
- **D5. Frames carry identifiers only, and signals carry no authority.** `notification.new {id}`, `entity.changed {kind, id}`, `ready {}`; the client refetches through REST, which enforces scope. A forged or replayed `recheck` can only make an instance check sockets, which closes nothing that is still valid; a forged `notification` or `entity` signal carries only identifiers whose content REST re-authorises; the listener validates every payload strictly against its typed form and drops anything else. Only database roles can `NOTIFY`. *Alternative:* frames carrying the notification row. *Reason:* the realtime principle already says frames are hints; identifiers leak nothing if a frame is delivered just after a scope change.
- **D6. `entity.changed` gains the kinds `join_request` and `notification`, and a student gets a frame only when the student's own view changes.**
  - `relationship` frames go to the tutor on every committed change of the card, and to the linked student account only when the student-visible projection of the relationship changes: the `MyTutor` fields that come from the relationship row (`state`, `colour`, `ended_at`, `ended_by`, `account_closed_until`), including the change that removes the relationship from the student's view (removal, a dropped reclaim link). Tutor-only edits (notes, the card name, the card email) send the student nothing.
  - `join_request` frames go to the tutor only for requests inside the tutor's scope at commit (never held, suppressed, closed by an unblock or of an operator-blocked account), and to the student only when the student's `SentJoinRequest` changes through a decision: accept, and decline, which sends the same frame with or without a block. Creation, release, suppression, un-suppression, the closing by an unblock, a repeated decline that adds a block, expiry (which the student's list derives from times) and an operator block send the student nothing distinguishable; the student's own code entry sends one identical frame to the student's other sockets whether the request was created, existing, held or suppressed.
  - `notification` frames go to the recipient's own sockets after a read change, so other tabs update the badge.
  - Every frame also reaches the acting account's other sockets, within the same projection rules.
  *Alternative:* a frame to both parties on every row change. *Reason:* the timing and presence of frames would tell a student that a tutor blocked them or suppressed their request (T-33), and would reveal that a tutor edited private notes.
- **D7. Socket limits.** At most 20 authenticated sockets per account per instance: the 21st `auth` closes with `4429` and the client backs off up to 60 seconds. At most 500 unauthenticated sockets per instance and 20 per client address (the trusted client address of `OVC_TRUSTED_PROXY_CIDRS`, as for every per-IP limit): the next upgrade answers `503`. *Alternative:* no per-account or per-address cap, or closing the oldest socket. *Reason:* bounds one account's and one address's share of an instance (T-20); closing the oldest makes tabs evict each other. Residual: a stolen access token can fill its account's 20 slots until the token lapses (D31) or the session is revoked.
- **D8. Listener liveness fails closed.** The listening connection uses TCP keepalives and, where the platform supports it (Linux in production), `tcp_user_timeout`, both confirmed against the pinned `pgx` in S3. Each instance sends itself a `heartbeat` signal every 2 seconds on its pool; the listener is lost when it has received none of its own heartbeats for 5 seconds, when the connection errors, or when `LISTEN` fails. A lost listener closes every local socket with `1012` and closes new connections after `auth` with `1013` until `LISTEN` is re-established; the listener reconnects with backoff from 1 to 30 seconds and increments its generation (5.2). A sweep every 60 seconds re-checks every local socket's session and account in one batched query, closing with `4401` those whose session expired or was revoked, as defence in depth. *Alternative:* keep sockets and rely on the sweep. *Reason:* fail closed; a connection that dies silently gives no error, and the sweep alone would stretch the revocation bound to a minute.
- **D9. No in-process broadcaster.** `OVC_BROADCASTER` and its `memory` value leave the configuration documents (no code reads them today); tests use the real PostgreSQL listener against the test database. *Reason:* with D1 publishing is a database statement, so an in-process fake would replace the behaviour under test.
- **D10. In-app rows store identifiers, never names.** The `notifications` table has `id`, `user_id` (recipient), `tutor_id`, `student_id` (nullable: the student the event concerns), `event`, `subject_id` (the relationship or join request), `relationship_id` (nullable, for the tutor colour), `idempotency_key` (unique), `created_at`, `read_at`, and no foreign keys (D11). `GET /notifications` resolves each row's label at read time under the rules every other route applies: for a tutor, the card's own display name, or the request's account name with "Deleted account" while that account is deleted; for a student, the tutor's account name and the relationship colour. Labels are rendered as text, never as HTML or links (T-14). *Alternative:* the specified "short structured payload (identifiers and titles)". *Reason:* a title snapshot of a student-chosen name would outlive the student's purge in the tutor's rows (T-34) and carry a possibly squatting account's chosen name (T-44).
- **D11. Notification rows have no foreign keys; the purge deletes them explicitly.** The invariant: once the purge of an account (the 30-day purge, the 7-day cleanup of an unconfirmed account or the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account its owner deletes) commits, no notification row has that account in `user_id`, `tutor_id` or `student_id`. It holds because the purge deletes those rows through indexes in its batches, and every producer of a row that names an account holds a lock the purge of that account also takes (the account's user row, its tutor profile, or the relationship or join request row the purge discards or deletes) and re-checks the state under it, so a producer waiting behind a purge finds its subject gone and inserts nothing. *Alternative:* `ON DELETE CASCADE` keys to `users`. *Reason:* inserting a row with a key to the recipient takes `FOR KEY SHARE` on the recipient's user row late in the transaction, which the lock order forbids (user rows are locked up front) and which tutor deletion must never take on a student's row.
- **D12. Producers and recipients of the slice 3 events.** In the producer's transaction, `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, and `notification` signalled only when a row was inserted:

  | Event | Recipient | Produced when | Key |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `invitation.accepted` | the tutor | an invitation links its card: new account (provisional, Q4), signed-in acceptance, merge, reactivation | `(invitation.accepted, invitation, tutor)` |
  | `join_request.created` | the tutor | a request becomes visible: created visible, released at confirmation or by the owner's keep, un-suppressed, reopened in place after an unblock | `(join_request.created, request, tutor)` |
  | `join_request.accepted` | the student | the tutor accepts | `(join_request.accepted, request, student)` |
  | `join_request.declined` | the student | the tutor declines, with or without a block (one identical row) | `(join_request.declined, request, student)` |
  | `relationship.ended` | the other party | either party ends; nobody when a deletion ends it | `(relationship.ended, relationship, ended_at)` |

  A held or suppressed request produces nothing for the tutor (T-33). *Alternative:* the event table's combined row "`invitation.accepted`, `join_request.accepted` → tutor, student". *Reason:* the table does not say which recipient gets which event; the tutor issued the invitation and the student made the request.
- **D13. `GET /notifications` has one schema for both roles.** `{items: [{id, event, created_at, read_at, label, colour, link}], next_cursor, unread_count}`, where `label` is a string or `null`, `colour` a palette key or `null` (students only), and `link` is `{kind: student_card | join_requests | my_tutors, id}` or `null` when the subject is outside the caller's current scope. Newest first, keyset on `(created_at, id)`, default 30 and maximum 100 (the panel asks for 10), `no-store`. `unread_count` counts at most 100 rows; the client shows "99+" above 99. *Alternative:* separate tutor and student schemas. *Reason:* one path cannot carry two response schemas (as in slice 3's D23), and the content is the same shape: a label the server chose for the caller's role.
- **D14. `POST /notifications/read`** takes `{ids}` (1 to 100 of the caller's rows) or `{through}` (one of the caller's rows: that row and every row of the caller older than it by `(created_at, id)`), exactly one; answers `{unread_count}`; idempotent; an identifier that is not one of the caller's rows answers `404 not_found` like a random one; it is a write open to unconfirmed accounts, so it goes through the session re-check under the user row lock; it signals `entity {kind: notification}` to the caller's sockets. Accepted gap, recorded in the slice's known limitations: a row whose `created_at` precedes the `through` row but whose producer commits after the list was shown is marked read without having been shown; the window is one producer transaction's duration, and the row's `notification.new` still arrives, so the client refetches and the page lists it as read. *Reason:* "Mark all as read" must not mark rows that arrived after the list was shown; closing the gap entirely would need a commit-ordered sequence, which PostgreSQL does not provide without serialising producers.
- **D15. Email preferences are a list.** `GET` and `PUT /users/me/email-preferences` use `{groups: [{group, enabled}]}` listing the role's groups in a fixed order: tutors `lesson_reminders`, `submissions`, `message_digest`; students `lesson_reminders`, `lesson_changes`, `homework`, `results`, `message_digest`. `PUT` takes a partial list; a group of the other role or named twice answers `422 validation_failed` (`not_allowed` or `invalid`). The table column is `email_group`, because `group` is a reserved word. *Reason:* the event table sends each group only to that role; one schema for both roles as in D13.
- **D16. Unsubscribe token.** `v1.<payload>.<mac>` in unpadded base64url: the payload is the account identifier (16 bytes), the group (1 byte) and the expiry (8 bytes, Unix seconds, big-endian); the MAC is an untruncated HMAC-SHA-256 under a key derived by HKDF-SHA-256 from `OVC_UNSUBSCRIBE_SIGNING_SECRET` with the info string `unsubscribe`, so the purpose is bound to the key; minted at dispatch with expiry 90 days after the send; parsed strictly and the MAC compared in constant time before any query, so a forged token costs no database work. The payload is readable, not encrypted: it shows the account's opaque identifier to anyone holding the link (T-18 residual). *Alternative:* a JWT through the existing `golang-jwt` dependency. *Reason:* a fixed binary layout has no algorithm choice to confuse and keeps the link short; the refresh cookie already uses the same construction.
- **D17. Optional-email headers and the dispatch-time preference check land with the first optional email in slice 5.** Slice 4 stores preferences, mints and verifies tokens, and serves the routes and pages; `internal/core` exposes the minting the worker will use. *Reason:* no optional email exists in slice 4, and a test-only email kind would be a placeholder; notifications.md already defers the undeliverable check the same way.
- **D18. The unsubscribe routes.**
  - `POST /unsubscribe?token=` accepts no body, or a body of at most 1 KiB that is `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` or `multipart/form-data` (media types parsed with their parameters, such as `charset` or `boundary`) holding exactly the field `List-Unsubscribe` with the value `One-Click`; it answers `204`. Another content type answers `415`; a form or multipart body without that field, with another value or with any other field answers `422 validation_failed` (`body.List-Unsubscribe`).
  - Every invalid, expired, forged or malformed token, and a token of an account that no longer exists or is `deleted` or `blocked`, answers `422 token_invalid` and changes nothing, on the preview as on the `POST`.
  - The order is: token format and MAC (no query), then the per-client-address limit of 60 requests per hour counted across both routes (`429 rate_limited` with `Retry-After`), then the account and the change.
  - Both send `Cache-Control: no-store`.
  *Reason:* the API requires JSON on every route with a body, but mail clients send forms (K18); a forged-token flood costs no database work, and valid-token traffic is bounded per address.
- **D19. A dedicated secret, required by the API and the worker from the change that adds it.** At least 32 bytes; the settings refuse a value equal to `OVC_JWT_SECRET` or to `OVC_FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY`. *Alternative:* derive the key from `OVC_JWT_SECRET`. *Reason:* rotating the JWT secret signs everyone out by design and must not also invalidate 90-day unsubscribe links; requiring it in the worker now (which mints from slice 5) means one owner step instead of two.
- **D20. Persisted presence waits for slice 6.** `users.online_until` and `last_seen_at` and their batched updates arrive with presence frames and the message digest, their only consumers. `POST /__test__/presence` answers in slice 4 from the API instance's registry (`{online, sockets}`). *Reason:* a write per connected user per minute with no reader.
- **D21. Frame types without reflection.** Generating OpenAPI components for the frames would need `reflect` in hand-written Go, which depguard forbids. Instead the frame structs stay in `internal/schemas`, the frontend's frame types and their strict parser are hand-written in `frontend/src/lib/realtime/frames.ts`, and a contract test runs the parser against the frames the real gateway emits: it drives every server frame type and close code through the real backend and parses each frame strictly, so a field the gateway adds, renames or removes fails the test. On the Go side, `TestEveryFrameTypeHasAGatewayTest` fails when a frame type or close code of the domain enums has no gateway test that produces it. *Alternative:* a documented operation whose responses reference the frame schemas. *Reason:* an operation that exists only to publish schemas would be a route nobody calls; the contract test checks the real wire format.
- **D22. One reconnecting client per tab, owned by the session epoch.** `lib/realtime` connects when the session is signed in, sends `auth` with the in-memory access token (refreshed first when it expires within 30 seconds), and is closed and discarded when the epoch changes. Whenever the session obtains a new access token (the existing proactive refresh, or any other refresh), the client sends it in-band as a new `auth` frame on the open socket (D31). Reconnect uses exponential backoff from 1 to 30 seconds with full jitter (60 seconds after `4429`); after every `ready` that follows a reconnect (not an in-band re-authentication) it invalidates the queries of section 5.10, so visible data is refetched. On `4401` it refreshes once: a new token reconnects; a refused refresh ends the session with the reason the refresh reports (D30). The client sends `ping` every 30 seconds and reconnects when no `pong` arrives within 10 seconds. The socket factory is injected; the fake used by component tests is checked by the same contract test as D21.
- **D23. The shared offline banner waits for slice 6.** pages.md's "No connection — reconnecting…" and the disabled actions arrive with chat, whose composer first needs them; slice 4's pages keep working through REST while the socket reconnects.
- **D24. Three test control routes**, all existing only with `OVC_TEST_CONTROL=1`: `POST /__test__/presence {user}` (D20) lets tests wait for a socket close; `POST /__test__/realtime/close {user, code}` closes the user's sockets on this instance with `4429` or `1012` through the real gateway, so the client's handling of codes the browser cannot provoke deterministically is tested end to end; `POST /__test__/unsubscribe-token {user, group}` mints a token through the real signer until slice 5 emails carry links. *Alternative:* intercepting the socket in Playwright. *Reason:* AGENTS.md forbids replacing behaviour behind the code's back; the routes drive the real implementation.
- **D25. Unverified accounts may connect.** The WebSocket route is `user, unverified ok`, like the notification routes; rows are produced for them; navigation still hides the bell from an unconfirmed student, who sees the rows once confirmed.
- **D26. The WebSocket route keeps the API conventions until the upgrade.**
  - `GET /api/v1/ws` is registered outside huma, but passes the same outer handlers as every route: recovery, request identifier, security headers and the access record (route template only).
  - Before `websocket.Accept`, the gateway requires exactly one `Origin` header equal to the normalised `OVC_PUBLIC_URL` in scheme, host and port; otherwise it answers `403 forbidden` with the API error body. Today's `requireOrigin` in `internal/api/auth.go` is huma middleware and reads only the first `Origin` value; S4 extracts the comparison into one plain `net/http` function over all `Origin` values, used by the gateway and by the refresh, sign-in and same-browser confirmation routes, so those routes also refuse a request with two `Origin` headers. S1 states the single-header rule in accounts.md and api.md and in T-15 and T-24. `coder/websocket` is then called with `OriginPatterns` set to the OVC host only and never with `InsecureSkipVerify`, as a second check. The library's own rules are weaker (it accepts a missing `Origin` and an `Origin` whose host equals the request's `Host`, matches patterns on the host ignoring the scheme, and refuses with a plain-text `403`), which S4 confirms against the pinned version and records in the stack table.
  - A request without an upgrade answers `400 bad_request`; a full unauthenticated budget (D7) `503 service_unavailable` with `Retry-After: 5`; each with the error body and `no-store`.
  - Read and write deadlines are cleared after the upgrade; on shutdown the gateway closes every socket with `1001` before the server's 40-second drain.
  - The audience test lists the route with its audience; the denial table records that it takes no identifier; the architecture test covers `internal/realtime`.
- **D27. Inbound frame rules.** Text frames only (binary closes with `1003`); a frame over 16 KiB closes with `1009`, which `coder/websocket` sends on its read limit; a malformed frame, an unknown type or a reserved slice 6 type answers an `error` frame (`bad_request`); more than 20 frames in a second answers `rate_limited`, and five violations within 10 seconds close with `4429`; any frame other than `auth` before authentication, and an `auth` for another session or account, close with `4401`. A `ref` must match `^[A-Za-z0-9._-]{1,64}$` (the request identifier's character set), or the frame is malformed; `ref` values are echoed but never logged.
- **D28. Per-lane loops.** In continuous mode (`Run`) each lane has its own goroutine and a slot count equal to its concurrency; it claims up to its free slots of due rows (high priority first), starts each effect at once, claims again as soon as a slot frees, and polls every second when nothing is due. Lanes run side by side; the scheduler loop runs due jobs and reclaims expired leases every second. A claim happens only when a slot is free, so a claimed row starts at once and the 4-minute claim budget applies to `Tick` only. `Tick(now)` runs the jobs and the reclaim, then runs every lane's loop side by side until no row is due at `now` and none is in flight, so two ticks at the same instant still have no further effect. A failure of the worker's own infrastructure (claiming, completing) ends the run with an error, as today. On shutdown no lane claims, and started effects finish within their timeout.
- **D29. Notification retention.** The hourly `notifications.cleanup` job deletes rows older than 90 days through the `created_at` index, in batches of 1,000, at most 10 per run, resuming from the oldest.
- **D30. The sign-out reason after an unfixable `4401`.** F1 changes the session API so that a refresh reports its result instead of a boolean: renewed, or ended with a reason. A refresh refused with `403 account_blocked` ends the session as `blocked`; any other refusal (a revoked or deleted session cannot be told apart, and must not be) ends it with a new reason `sessionEnded`, whose message is "You were signed out on this device" / "Вы вышли из аккаунта на этом устройстве".
- **D31. A socket lives no longer than its access token.** The gateway records the access token's `exp` at `auth`. A later `auth` frame carrying a valid token for the same session and the same account keeps the later of the recorded and the new expiry and is answered with `ready`; one for another session or account closes with `4401`. When the recorded expiry plus a grace of 30 seconds passes without a fresh token, the socket closes with `4401`. *Alternative:* an authenticated socket lives as long as its session (the earlier silence). *Reason:* a stolen access token should open nothing for longer than its own 15 minutes (T-29, T-37), and the client already refreshes before expiry.
- **D32. Lookups per inbound frame are bounded.** Every inbound frame after `auth` re-checks the session and account, but one socket performs at most one lookup per second: frames within that second reuse the result. Revocation still takes effect at once through `recheck` (5.2). *Reason:* with 20 frames per second per socket and 20 sockets per account, an unbounded check would let one account cause 400 lookups per second (K38).
- **D33. Re-checks run off the listener goroutine.** The listener only marks the account's sockets suspended and hands the account to a re-check worker; at most one re-check per account runs at a time, and signals that arrive meanwhile mark it to run once more after the current one (coalescing). The listener never waits for a query.
- **D34. Route-level bundle splitting lands in slice 4** (F0), from the slice 3 review's carried-over item: each route's page module loads on demand; the access rule, the frames and the token pages' address replacement stay in the entry chunk, so the token leaves the address before any lazily loaded code runs. Loading only the active language stays in slice 8.
- **D35. Focus on live refetch.** pages.md's shared behavior gains: when a live refetch removes the focused row of a list, focus moves to the row that took its place, else to the previous row, else to the list's heading; when it removes the subject of an open dialog, the dialog closes and the page's notice that says so ("This student is no longer on your list", "This request was already answered" and similar) takes focus; a refetch that keeps the focused element leaves focus where it is.
- **D36. Lock rule for frame recipients.** A transaction that names a student as a frame recipient reads the relationship row with at least `FOR SHARE`, which conflicts with the `FOR UPDATE` and `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` locks that ending, removal and a dropped reclaim link take; ending, removal and dropping already hold the row. So a producer waiting behind a removal sees the row discarded and names no former student.

## 5. Contract details

### 5.1 Frames

Envelope: `{"type": <enum>, "ref"?: string (D27), "payload"?: object}`; types and close codes are enums in `internal/domain`, payloads are structs in `internal/schemas`, mirrored by hand in `frontend/src/lib/realtime/frames.ts` and checked by the contract test (D21). Unknown properties are refused.

| Direction | Type | Payload | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| client → server | `auth` | `{access_token}` | first frame, within 5 seconds of the upgrade (injected duration, shortened in tests); later, a fresh token for the same session (D31); never logged |
| client → server | `ping` | — | re-checks the session and account (D32); answered with `pong` echoing `ref` |
| server → client | `ready` | `{}` | after a successful `auth`, first or in-band |
| server → client | `notification.new` | `{id}` | a row was inserted for this account |
| server → client | `entity.changed` | `{kind: relationship \| join_request \| notification, id}` | refetch the entity (5.10) |
| server → client | `pong` | — | echoes `ref` |
| server → client | `error` | `{code, message, request_id, ref}` | `request_id` is the frame identifier; the socket stays open |

Close codes: `1000` (client), `1001` (server shutdown), `1003` (binary frame), `1009` (frame over 16 KiB), `1012` (listener lost or a re-check failed: reconnect), `1013` (listener not yet back: reconnect later), `4401` (authentication failed, the token lapsed, or the session or account no longer allows the socket), `4429` (slow consumer, repeated frame violations, too many sockets). Close reasons are fixed short strings, never data.

### 5.2 Authentication and revocation

- **Upgrade:** the exact-origin check of D26 before `Accept`; no credential in the URL; no cookie is read.
- **`auth`:** the access token is verified as on REST (signature, expiry, claims). The socket is then registered under its account and session, **suspended** and tagged with the listener's current generation, before the session lookup; the lookup checks that the session is neither revoked nor expired and that the account is `active` or `unverified`. `ready` is sent, and the socket starts receiving, only if the lookup passed, the listener's generation is unchanged, and no `recheck` for the account arrived after registration that was not followed by a passing lookup: a `recheck` that arrives during authentication triggers one more lookup after the first, and if that lookup passes and the generation is still unchanged, the socket gets `ready` (another session's sign-out therefore does not close an authenticating socket). Otherwise the socket closes with `4401` (a failed lookup) or `1013` (a changed generation). A revocation committed while the socket authenticates is therefore never missed: its `recheck` finds the registered socket.
- **In-band `auth`** (D31): the same checks for a fresh token of the same session and account; the recorded expiry becomes the later of the two.
- **Every inbound frame** after `auth` repeats the session and account lookup, at most once per second per socket (D32); failure closes with `4401`.
- **`recheck` signals** (D3) arrive for: sign-out (`POST /auth/logout`), sign-out everywhere, refresh-token reuse, the same-browser confirmation (other sessions), a reclaim (every session, including the squatter's), a reset, a password change (other sessions), deletion, an operator block (FR-16.1, through `ovc-admin` in its own process), the 7-day cleanup and the purge. On a `recheck`, the listener marks that account's local sockets suspended at once and hands the account to the re-check worker (D33); while suspended, frames for those sockets are queued, counting against the socket's outbound bound (5.4), so overflow closes with `4429`; one query (2-second timeout) re-reads their sessions and the account; failing sockets close with `4401`, the rest deliver their queued frames in order and resume. A failed query closes the account's sockets with `1012`, and each reconnect authenticates afresh.
- **Token lapse:** at the recorded token expiry plus 30 seconds without a fresh `auth`, the socket closes with `4401` (D31).
- **Narrowing** (Q1): ending, removal, a dropped reclaim link and tutor or student deletion change no socket; their transactions name the recipients of `entity.changed` from rows they hold locked (D36), so the student of a removed or dropped link gets one last `relationship` frame (to refetch My tutors, which no longer lists the tutor) and nothing after it.
- **Bound:** a `recheck` is delivered at commit, so a revoked session's sockets close within 5 seconds while the listener is alive; a listener dead for 5 seconds without an error is detected by the missing heartbeat, and every socket is then closed (D8).

### 5.3 Authorization of frames

Inbound frames in slice 4 (`auth`, `ping`) carry no identifier, so nothing is resolved in a client-chosen scope; clients never subscribe. Outbound recipients are decided in the producer's transaction from rows it holds locked (D36), under the projection rules of D6:

| Frame | Recipients |
|---|---|
| `notification.new` | the recipient of the inserted row only |
| `entity.changed relationship` | the tutor on every committed change of the card; the linked student account only when the student-visible projection changes; never another tutor of the same student |
| `entity.changed join_request` | the tutor only while the request is visible to the tutor; the student only on accept and decline (identical with or without a block), and on the student's own code entry (identical for every outcome) |
| `entity.changed notification` | the recipient's own sockets |
| `recheck`, `heartbeat` | not frames; close or keep sockets, or prove the listener alive |

The acting account's other sockets also receive each frame its action produced, within the same rules. Denial tests connect a second tutor who shares the student and prove that no frame about the first tutor's relationship or requests reaches it, and prove that a student's frames are identical whether or not the tutor blocked them.

### 5.4 Broadcaster delivery and backpressure

- **Payload:** `{"v": 1, "k": "recheck" | "notification" | "entity" | "heartbeat", "u": [user ids], "e": kind, "i": id}`, at most 7,900 bytes and 100 recipients (D2); the trigger-built `recheck` payloads use the same form; the listener parses every payload into a typed value and drops anything else with `realtime.signal.rejected`.
- **Order:** PostgreSQL delivers notifications in commit order; the listener handles them one at a time, never waits for a query (D33) and enqueues frames without blocking; each socket's queue is FIFO, so one socket sees frames in commit order.
- **Fan-out:** each frame is serialised once and the same bytes are enqueued for every recipient socket on the instance.
- **Backpressure:** each socket has an outbound queue of 256 frames or 1 MiB, including frames held while the socket is suspended; an enqueue that would exceed either closes the socket with `4429` instead of waiting; the writer goroutine writes with a 10-second deadline and closes with `4429` when it passes. A slow socket therefore never delays the listener or another socket (ADR-0009 item 3).
- **Liveness:** the server pings every 25 seconds and closes a socket without a pong for 60 seconds; the listener's heartbeat and keepalives (D8); every goroutine starts through `core.Go`.
- **Limits:** D7 and D27.
- **Reconnect and refetch:** the database is the source of truth; a client that reconnects after any close refetches on `ready` (D22, NFR-4.3).

### 5.5 Notification and preference storage

- **Migration (next free number when S5 merges; goose markers only):** `notifications` as in D10, check on `event` in the five slice 4 events, unique `idempotency_key`, indexes `(user_id, created_at DESC, id DESC)`, partial `(user_id) WHERE read_at IS NULL`, `(created_at)`, `(tutor_id)`, partial `(student_id) WHERE student_id IS NOT NULL`; no foreign keys (D11).
- **Migration (S3):** the three revocation triggers of D3 with their `WHEN` conditions, and their three PL/pgSQL functions, each body wrapped in `-- +goose StatementBegin` and `-- +goose StatementEnd` (D42); the conventions tests check that these markers appear only around function bodies, each alone on its line.
- **Migration (S6):** `email_preferences (user_id → users ON DELETE CASCADE, email_group, enabled, updated_at)`, primary key `(user_id, email_group)`, check on the six groups. A missing row means enabled; a save writes a row per named group.
- **Purge:** every purge path (the 30-day purge, the 7-day cleanup and the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account its owner deletes) deletes the account's notification rows and the rows naming it (D11) inside its existing batches, and the user row's deletion removes the preferences.

### 5.7 The unsubscribe token

Lifetime 90 days from the send; scope one account and one group; it never signs anyone in, sets no cookie and returns no token; nothing changes on `GET`; it appears only in the query string of the two routes and in the `/unsubscribe?token=` page address, which follows the token-page rule (read once, `history.replaceState`, kept in component state only, never in `next`); access records carry only route templates; the reverse proxy planned for slice 10 must redact the `token` query parameter of `/unsubscribe` and `/api/v1/unsubscribe` in its logs (T-27); every response sends `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and the two routes `Cache-Control: no-store`; the token is never logged, never put in an outbox payload (slice 5 mints it at render time) and never placed in another URL.

### 5.8 Per-lane worker loops

As D28. The lanes registered in slice 4 are `email` (10 slots) and `storage_delete` (4). Lease, retry, dead-row, panic and completion rules are unchanged; a slot is released after the completion or retry update, so a row is never claimed twice by one worker. With `OVC_CLOCK=test` only `Tick` runs, as today.

### 5.9 Lock order and transactions

- Producer signals and notification inserts take no row locks of their own: `pg_notify` is a statement in the transaction, and notification rows have no foreign keys (D11). They are issued after the transaction's existing locks. PostgreSQL's notification lock is taken at commit, after every row lock, so it cannot close a cycle with them.
- A transaction that names a student as a frame recipient holds the relationship row at least `FOR SHARE` (D36); the paths that name students already lock the relationship in the existing order (relationships after user rows and profiles), so the rule adds no new ordering.
- The revocation triggers run inside the statements that revoke or change state, on rows those statements already lock.
- Existing transactions gain statements only: linking, confirmation, the reclaim choice, removal, ending, request creation, release, un-suppression, accept, decline, expiry, unblock, deletion and purge each add their notification rows and signals at the end, without changing their lock order.
- `POST /notifications/read` and `PUT /users/me/email-preferences` lock the acting account's row through `lockActingAccount`, re-check the session, then update only that account's rows.
- `POST /unsubscribe` upserts one preference row without locking the user row; the foreign key takes `FOR KEY SHARE`, so a concurrent purge either finishes first (the upsert fails with a foreign-key violation, answered `422 token_invalid`) or waits for the upsert.
- Tutor deletion keeps its rule of never locking a student's user row; it inserts no notification rows and sends its `relationship` frames in chunks of 100 recipients.

## 6. Observability and threat model

### 6.1 Observability

New and changed events (INFO unless noted), never with tokens, frame payloads, `ref` values, names or emails; the gateway binds `connection_id` for the socket's lifetime and a frame identifier per inbound frame, and `user_id` and `role` once authenticated:

- **Gateway:** `ws.upgrade.refused` (`reason` `origin`, `not_upgrade`, `busy`, `address_busy`), `ws.connection.opened` (DEBUG), `ws.connection.authenticated`, `ws.connection.reauthenticated` (DEBUG), `ws.auth.refused` (`reason` `timeout`, `invalid_token`, `session`, `account_state`, `too_many_sockets`, `listener_down`, `revoked_during_auth`, `protocol`, `other_session`), `ws.token.lapsed`, `ws.frame.rejected` (`reason` `malformed`, `unknown_type`, `rate`, `before_auth`, `binary`), `ws.connection.closed` (`code`, `reason`, `duration_ms`, frame counts), `ws.backpressure.closed` (WARNING); `ping` and `pong` never log at INFO.
- **Broadcaster:** `realtime.listener.connected`, `realtime.listener.lost` (ERROR, with a kind, `heartbeat` when the heartbeat stopped), `realtime.signal.rejected` (WARNING), `realtime.recheck.failed` (ERROR, with a kind), `realtime.sweep` (DEBUG, sockets checked and closed), `realtime.delivery` (DEBUG, kind, recipients, sockets, duration).
- **Notifications:** `notification.created` (`event`, the recipient as `target_user_id`, `notification_id`), `notifications.read` (DEBUG), `email_preferences.updated` (changed group names), `unsubscribe.applied` (`target_user_id`, `group`), `unsubscribe.refused` (`reason` `malformed`, `signature`, `expired`, `account_state`, `throttled`); `notifications.cleanup` reports through `job.run`.
- **Worker:** `outbox.lane.started` and `outbox.lane.stopped` (DEBUG), `outbox.claim.failed` (ERROR, with a kind).

S1 adds them to observability.md; each change asserts its events and that captured records hold no access token, unsubscribe token, `ref`, name or email (`TestRealtimeRecordsCarryNoTokenOrPersonalData`, `TestUnsubscribeTokensNeverReachAccessRecords`).

### 6.2 Threat model

S1 amends each, pending owner review:

- **T-2:** sign-out and refresh-token reuse close the session's sockets.
- **T-5:** frame recipients decided under locks within relationship scope; notification rows, their labels and links scoped to the recipient; the denial tests.
- **T-14:** notification labels are user-written names rendered as text only, never as HTML or links.
- **T-17, T-32:** the token format, dedicated derived key, MAC before any query, 90-day expiry, one group, `GET` changes nothing, `POST` idempotent and limited per address, no sign-in, deleted and blocked accounts refused; residual: anyone holding a forwarded email can turn one optional group off for that account.
- **T-18:** realtime and notification records hold identifiers only; rows store identifiers, labels resolved at read; residual: the unsubscribe token shows the account's opaque identifier to whoever holds the link.
- **T-19:** `OVC_UNSUBSCRIBE_SIGNING_SECRET` as a `Secret`, never logged, required by the API and the worker, refused when equal to another key.
- **T-20:** authentication timeout, unauthenticated sockets per instance and per address, sockets per account, frame size and rate, bounded lookups per socket, outbound queue (including suspended frames) and write deadline, ping timeout, payload limit, the unsubscribe limit, notification retention and the bounded unread count; residuals: `NOTIFY` serialises committing notifiers, a full notification queue makes notifying commits fail, and a stolen access token can fill its account's socket slots until it lapses.
- **T-21:** the slice 4 residual becomes narrowing (Q1).
- **T-22:** an operator block closes the account's sockets.

## 9. Risks, performance and the check budget

**Risks.**

- **`NOTIFY` serialises committing notifiers** on PostgreSQL's notification lock; at MVP scale this is milliseconds, measured below. If it bites, the fallback is batching signals per transaction, which D2 already does.
- **A full notification queue fails notifying commits.** A listener that stops consuming lets PostgreSQL's notification queue (8 GB by default) fill, and every transaction that notifies then fails at commit, including sign-outs and every producer of rows: a stalled listener becomes an outage of writes. The listener never waits on a socket or a query (5.4, D33), the heartbeat detects a stalled or dead listener and reconnects it, and the slice review measures queue usage under load.
- **Origin behaviour of the library** differs from OVC's exact rule; the gateway checks first (D26), and S4 confirms the library's behaviour against the pinned version.
- **Delivery to sockets that are about to close:** suspension during a re-check and during authentication (5.2) prevents it on revocation; scope changes rely on identifier-only frames (D5) and recipients read under locks (D36).
- **Browser differences:** `connect-src 'self'` must admit same-origin `wss:` in all three engines, and the development and preview proxies must forward upgrades; the end-to-end smoke fails on any CSP violation.
- **Flaky live tests:** every Playwright assertion waits on a visible condition with a deadline; the server-side close codes are driven by test control routes, never by timing.
- **Lane concurrency bugs** (double claims, lost slots): the race detector, the slot tests and the unchanged lease-token completion.
- **Deadlocks:** no new row locks beyond `FOR SHARE` on rows each path already locks in order (5.9); `TestTutorDeletionSplitsItsSignalsAndLocksNoStudentRow`, `TestFrameRecipientsAreReadUnderTheRelationshipLock` and the extended slice 3 race tests guard the order.
- **The owner's `backend/.env`** needs the new secret after S6; checks never depend on it.
- **The dependency dates** put F0 and so the frontend work after 2026-11-04 (D39).
- **Bundle growth:** the realtime client, panel and pages add an estimated 6 to 10 KB gzip; F0's split and its check keep the student's first load inside the 250 KB budget.
