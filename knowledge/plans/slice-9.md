## 1. Problem, outcome and scope

**Problem.** A tutor can build a ready assignment in the library, but cannot give it to anyone. FR-8 (issuing), FR-9 (completing) and FR-10 (checking) are unbuilt: no issue, submission, answer or review table exists, the upload route refuses the `answer` and `corrected` contexts, the download token refuses the reserved kinds `issue_snapshot`, `answer` and `review_item`, the Issue actions and the Issued and Needs checking tabs are hidden, and students have no Homework section. Pilot scenario 2 stops after step 5. At the same time the full local check run peaked at 581 s of its 10-minute budget during slice 8 (median 436 s over 21 gates), and the entry with its largest language chunk keeps 1.4 KB of headroom under the 180 KB limit of `pnpm check:bundle` ([slice 8 known limitations](../reviews/slice-8-known-limitations.md)), so slice 9 cannot add three browsers' worth of end-to-end tests and pages without first making room.

**Outcome.** End to end through the real API, worker, PostgreSQL, the local S3-compatible server and the production frontend build:

- the end-to-end suite is faster and consolidated before any slice 9 feature lands, and new interface texts load with their pages;
- a tutor issues a ready template to one student or one group, with an optional deadline, an optional linked lesson and an answer visibility policy; the issue holds an immutable snapshot with fresh option identifiers, and later template edits or deletions never change it (FR-6.3, FR-8.2);
- each student gets their own submission, an email and an in-app row, answers in any order with autosave, uploads file answers from a phone camera, and submits after a confirmation listing unanswered questions; automatic questions are scored and frozen on submission, late work is marked, and fully automatic work becomes checked at once;
- students never receive points, totals, overrides, scores or percentages, and receive correct answers and explanations only when the policy allows, proven by an allowlist contract test over every student surface;
- the tutor works through the review queue oldest first, scores manual questions, overrides automatic scores with a reason, comments, attaches corrected files, writes feedback, saves drafts, marks work checked or returns it; the student revises returned work and resubmits;
- the tutor changes a deadline or the linked lesson and withdraws work nobody has submitted;
- ending a relationship closes unsubmitted work, dropping a provisional link erases its work, and the purges remove homework with its files from the change that creates each table;
- Today shows work needing checking and upcoming deadlines, Home shows work to do and recently checked work, the student card has its Homework tab and open assignments, the group page lists its issued work, and the lesson page lists its linked work.

### Issues and snapshots

- **D5. One service, `internal/services/homework`,** owns issues, submissions, answers, scoring, reviews and the student projections of issued work; it is already in the [module map](../architecture/detailed.md#backend-backend) and covered by the `internal/services` row of the package-layer table. It reads templates through a narrow interface that `internal/services/library` implements (the template's tutor schema and its references), so the library keeps its own repository. Pure rules (scoring, percentage, the submission state machine, the reveal rule) live in `internal/domain` with unit tests (D24, D25).
- **D6. Issuing.** `POST /issues {template_id, audience: {relationship_id} | {group_id}, lesson_id, deadline_at, answer_policy}` with an `Idempotency-Key`, stored per tutor as `issue.create_key`; tutors only, with a confirmed email (`403 email_not_verified` otherwise). The template must be the tutor's, not deleted and `ready`; a template that is not ready answers `409 not_editable` (K4). The audience is one `active` relationship, or one `provisional` relationship directly (Q4 A), never through a group; or one non-archived group with at least one member. An invited, ended or `reclaim_pending` relationship answers `409 relationship_not_active`, an archived or empty group `409 audience_not_active`. A `lesson_id` names a lesson of the same tutor and audience in any status (`404` otherwise); a `deadline_at` must be later than now (`422 validation_failed`, `body.deadline_at`, `invalid`). It answers `201` with the tutor's issue.
- **D7. Locks of issuing.** It takes the tutor's profile lock with the session re-check, then the audience's relationships `FOR SHARE` in identifier order, re-reading the group's members and each relationship's state under those locks, as the [schedule's per-path lock rules](../specs/schedule.md#locks-and-jobs) do for lesson audiences, so a concurrent ending either waits for the issue or is seen and excluded; then the group, the linked lesson, the issue and its submissions.
- **D8. The snapshot** copies the template's title and blocks into `issue.snapshot` with every option identifier replaced by a fresh random UUID (block identifiers are kept as question identifiers, unique within the snapshot), each material block's current title and attachment identifier copied in, and one `issue_snapshot` reference per distinct attachment the snapshot shows (prompt images and material files). The tutor already holds a charge for each of these files through the template, so issuing creates no charge and needs no quota check; the snapshot keeps its references, and with them the charge, after the template or the material is deleted. `max_points`, `has_manual_questions` and `snapshot_bytes` are stored beside it.
- **D9. Submissions** are created in the issue's transaction: one for the relationship's student, or one per current member of the group, each carrying `relationship_id`, the student's relationship with the tutor, used for scope, the tutor colour and the ending rules. Students who join the group later get nothing.
- **D10. Bounds** (T-20): at most 100 issues created per tutor per hour (`issues_hour`); all other issue writes count toward 600 homework writes per tutor per hour (`homework_writes_hour`); the snapshot JSON of all of a tutor's issues together holds at most 512 MiB, kept as a running total on the tutor profile under the profile lock, increased by issuing and decreased under the same lock by D33's erasure and the purge, never by a withdrawal (`422 validation_failed`, `body`, `too_many`); at most 20 issues link to one lesson (`422`, `body.lesson_id`, `too_many`). Slots are reserved as library writes reserve them. The caps are constructor settings.
- **D11. Changing an issue.** `PATCH /issues/{id} {deadline_at?, lesson_id?, version}` takes the profile lock, the submissions' relationships `FOR SHARE` (D30), a newly linked lesson, then the issue row, and conditions on `version`; `deadline_at` may be null or later than now (K17); a new deadline recomputes every submission's `late` from its `first_submitted_at` and the submissions' copied `deadline_at` in the same transaction; the policy never changes. A stale `version` answers `409 conflict`.
- **D12. Withdrawing.** `POST /issues/{id}/withdraw` takes the profile lock, the submissions' relationships `FOR SHARE` (D30) and the issue row; with any submission whose `first_submitted_at` is set it answers `409 issue_has_submissions`; otherwise the issue becomes `withdrawn`, its submissions leave every list and their routes answer `404`, the references and charges of their draft file answers are released (from B4, when answers exist), the snapshot keeps its references until the purge (K14), and each student gets `issue.withdrawn`. Repeating it answers the current state. Every autosave and submission takes the issue row `FOR SHARE` and checks its status, so a withdrawal never races them.
- **D13. Tutor reads.** `GET /issues?status=active|withdrawn&group=&student=&needs_checking=&deadline_before=&limit=&cursor=` answers the tutor's issues keyset paginated on `(issued_at DESC, id)`, or, with `deadline_before`, on `(deadline_at, id)` among active issues with a deadline before it (Today's upcoming deadlines, K20); each item carries progress counts (students, submitted, checked, returned, late, needs checking) from one aggregate query per page; with `student` (a relationship identifier) each item also carries that student's `submission: {id, status, late, final_points, issued_before_reclaim}`, which the student card's Homework tab and Overview read; with `group` it lists the group's issues, which the group page reads. `GET /issues/{id}` answers the tutor schema: the snapshot with its keys, points and download addresses, and the submissions with their status, late flag, scores, `issued_before_reclaim` and whether a review draft exists. Every tutor surface names a student by the card's own display name, never the live account name (T-44).

### Completing

- **D14. Readability.** One predicate, `ReadableStudentSubmission`, decides whether a student reads a submission: their own submission, of an issue that is `active` and not marked for erasure, whose relationship is `active`, `provisional` or `ended`, never `reclaim_pending` or `discarded`; work issued while a link was provisional and kept after a reclaim stays readable with the cue of D49. `/assignments`, `GET /submissions/{id}`, the lesson detail's linked work, the three download kinds' minting and serving (D37), the in-app rows' labels and links and the email dispatch check (D39) all apply it, and `TestTheStudentSubmissionReadabilityCopiesAgree` proves the copies agree, as `TestTheStudentVisibilityCopiesAgree` does for lessons (T-6, T-44). The predicate lands in B2, with the first student surfaces. Events that remove access (a withdrawal, an erasure) decide their student recipients by this predicate as it held just before the change, under the change's own locks, so the student still gets `issue.withdrawn` and the frame; a `reclaim_pending` or discarded student never does. A purged student receives no final submission frame: the seven-day cleanup marks provisional issues in the final purge transaction, after student batches that leave current provisional work intact (owner amendment, 2026-11-12). A student writes only when the submission is readable, its relationship is `active` or `provisional`, and its status allows it.
- **D15. Student reads.** `GET /assignments?status=todo|submitted|checked|closed&tutor=&limit=&cursor=` (`student, unverified ok`, because a provisional student reaches their work, Q4 A) answers the caller's readable submissions across tutors: `todo` (not started, draft, returned) ordered by `(deadline_at NULLS LAST, issued_at, id)`, `submitted` by `(submitted_at DESC, id)`, `checked` by `(checked_at DESC, id)`, `closed` by `(issued_at DESC, id)`. An item is `{submission_id, title, tutor: {relationship_id, name, colour}, status, late, issued_before_reclaim, deadline_at, issued_at, submitted_at, checked_at, feedback_excerpt, has_corrected_files}`, where the last two come only from the latest published review revision and follow the excerpt rule of `GET /home` (K3). The answer also carries `counts: {todo, closed}`, each capped at 100, for the student's Homework count and the Closed tab (K21). No item carries points.
- **D16. The student schema.** `GET /submissions/{id}` for the owning student answers `{id, title, tutor, status, late, issued_before_reclaim, deadline_at, version, blocks, answers, editable_question_ids, review, revealed}`: `blocks` in the student content schema of the [builder preview](../specs/homework.md#builder-preview) built from the snapshot, with `issue_snapshot` download addresses; `answers` as `{question_id, value, files}` with `answer` download addresses; `review` the latest published revision's comments, feedback and corrected files (`review_item` addresses) or `null`; `revealed` present only when the reveal rule allows (D24), holding per question the correct option identifiers, accepted answers, value and tolerance, and explanations, and no per-answer verdict (K22). `version` is the submission's student-visible version: it advances on autosave, submission, every status change and a publication that changes student-visible content, never on a points-only publication, a draft review save, or a deadline or linked-lesson change, which the frame makes the page refetch instead, so autosave never meets a spurious `conflict`. The tutor, and only the tutor, gets the tutor schema from the same route: the snapshot with keys, answers with normalised forms, automatic points and correctness, every review revision with points and overrides, and the scores.
- **D17. Autosave.** `PUT /submissions/{id}/answers {version, save_id, answers: [{question_id, value}]}` with `value` `{option_ids}`, `{text}` or `{attachment_ids}` by question type (`short_number` keeps its raw text in `text`). Order: readability (`404`), the replay of `save_id` (the current state), the status (`409 not_editable` when not `not_started`, `draft` or `returned`), the `version` (`409 conflict`), frozen answers (`409 answer_frozen`), structural limits (`422` with field paths: option identifiers of the question without repeats, one for single choice; a short text up to 500 characters; a long text up to the question's `max_length`; a number text up to 64 characters; at most the question's `max_files` attachments), file bindings (`404`, D18), the file bytes cap (`422`), the quota (`507`). The first save moves `not_started` to `draft`. Rates: 2 per second per submission (`answer_saves_second`) and 3,600 per student per hour (`answer_saves_hour`). The client saves one second after the last change, with one request in flight.
- **D18. File answers.** An `attachment_ids` value may hold files the answer already references, or the caller's own unexpired `pending` uploads of the `answer` context scoped to this submission and question (`404` for anything else). The save binds them with `answer` references (`ref_id` the submission, `question_id` the question), charged to the issue's tutor, and releases the references of files no longer listed. A submission's file answers hold at most 250 MB in all (`422 validation_failed`, `body.answers`, `too_many`). A save decides from the request, before locking, whether it binds or releases a file; only then does it take the tutor's profile lock (charge locks), re-checking the decision under the locks, retrying once on a changed set and then answering `409 conflict`.
- **D19. Submitting.** `POST /submissions/{id}/submit {version}`: readability (`404`), then the current state for a repeat of a submission already `submitted` or `checked` whatever the version, so a retried submit after a lost answer succeeds; `409 not_editable` for `closed`; then `409 conflict` for a stale version. It scores every automatic question still unscored with text normalisation and number parsing version 1, storing the raw value, the normalised or parsed form, the version, `auto_points` and `auto_correct`, freezes those answers, sets `first_submitted_at` once and `submitted_at` each time, sets `late` when the first submission came after the deadline, and computes `auto_points` and `final_points`. Without manual questions the submission becomes `checked` at once with `checked_at` and `submission.checked` for review revision 0; otherwise it becomes `submitted` and the tutor gets `submission.submitted`. Resubmitting returned work changes only the manual answers.
- **D20. Who may write.** A removed group member keeps their work and may still submit while the relationship is active. A closed submission is readable but not writable. File answer uploads need a confirmed email, as chat uploads do, so an unconfirmed student on a provisional link can answer every question but a file question.

### Visibility and scoring

- **D21. Separate schemas.** Student-facing structs in `internal/schemas` have no field for points, totals, overrides, scores, percentages, `max_points`, correctness, accepted answers, values, tolerances, explanations, the scoring mode or `has_manual_questions`; the revealed data is a separate struct added only by D24. A conventions test parses `internal/schemas` with `go/parser` (no `reflect`) and fails when a struct whose name marks it student-facing declares any of these field names or JSON keys; the forbidden names are exact Go and JSON identifiers listed in the test; the only exemptions are the named structs of the revealed tree (its container and its per-question item), whose exact field sets (the container: the per-question items; the item: `question_id`, `correct_option_ids`, `accepted_answers`, `correct_value`, `tolerance`, `explanation`) the test pins, still with no points, correctness or scoring mode; the one allowed referrer is the student submission struct's `revealed` field (a pointer, omitted when absent), and the test fails when an exempted struct is embedded in or referenced from any other struct. The student answer's own `value` is not a forbidden name.
- **D22. The answer-key contract test** is an allowlist, as the builder preview's test is: for a seeded issue holding every block type, with distinctive sentinel points and maximum (compared as JSON number values) and distinctive sentinel accepted answers, values, tolerances and explanations (compared as substrings), it drives submissions through every state (`not_started`, `draft`, `submitted`, `returned`, `checked`, `closed`) under each policy, including a points-only revision, and for every student surface (the `/assignments` answer, the student `GET /submissions/{id}`, the autosave and submit answers, the error bodies of student routes, the `201` answer of an `answer` upload, the lesson detail's linked work, the `/attachments` mint answer, every homework notification row, every `entity.changed` frame a student receives and the HTML and text body of every student homework email from the sink) asserts the exact key set of every object, decoded into `map[string]json.RawMessage` without `any` or `reflect`, with allowed key sets written as literals; it asserts that no sentinel appears anywhere in a student surface, and that `revealed` appears exactly when D24 allows. It grows with each change that adds a surface and is complete in B5.
- **D23. Points-only publications stay invisible.** A later publication on `checked` work that keeps it `checked` and whose comments, corrected file identifiers and feedback equal the previous published revision's (K19) sends the student no frame, no row and no email, advances no student-visible field, and leaves the student's `GET /submissions/{id}` equal apart from freshly minted addresses (T-30). Residual, recorded in T-30 pending owner review: a corrected file's address names the revision that references it, so a student comparing addresses can tell that some revision was published, never what changed.
- **D24. Reveal rule** in `internal/domain`, unit-tested over every policy and status: `after_submission` reveals for `submitted` and `checked`, `after_check` for `checked`, `never` never; with `after_submission` returning is unavailable (`409 return_not_allowed`).
- **D25. Scoring rules** in `internal/domain`, unit-tested: single choice, multiple choice all or nothing and partial (`floor(points × max(0, correct_selected − incorrect_selected) / correct_options)` in integers), short text after normalisation against every accepted answer, short number with `|answer − value| ≤ tolerance` in `math/big` rationals, unanswered as 0, the final score (override, else manual points, else automatic) and the percentage rounded half up, absent when the maximum is 0; and the submission state machine, which refuses every transition outside the lifecycle diagram.

### Checking

- **D26. Review drafts.** `PUT /submissions/{id}/review {revision, version, save_id, feedback, items: [{question_id, points, override_points, override_reason, comment, corrected_attachment_ids}]}` saves the one draft revision. When no draft exists, `revision` must be the latest published number plus one and the server creates the draft as a copy of the latest published revision, with new `review_item` references to its corrected files; afterwards it conditions on `version` (`409 conflict`), replays `save_id`, and answers the tutor schema. Bounds: `points` from 0 to the question's points on manual questions only, `override_points` in the same range with a reason of 1 to 500 code points on automatic questions only, a comment and the feedback up to 10,000 code points (restricted Markdown for the feedback, plain text for comments), at most 5 corrected files per file answer, at most 100 revisions per submission (`too_many`). Rates: 2 per second per submission and 3,600 per tutor per hour (`review_saves_*`). Reviewable states are `submitted` and `checked` (`409 not_editable` otherwise, `returned` included, K23).
- **D27. Corrected files** may be files the draft already references (copied from the published revision or added earlier), or the tutor's own unexpired `pending` uploads of the `corrected` context scoped to this submission and question (`404` for anything else), bound with `review_item` references (`ref_id` the revision, `question_id` the question), charged to the tutor; a file removed from the draft loses that draft's reference.
- **D28. Publishing.** `POST /submissions/{id}/check {revision}` requires points for every manual question and makes the submission `checked` (`checked_at` set once), and `POST /submissions/{id}/return {revision}` requires a comment or feedback, at least one manual question, an `active` or `provisional` relationship (Q4 A) and a policy other than `after_submission` (`409 return_not_allowed`), and makes it `returned`. Both publish the named draft (the same published number again answers the current state; any other number `409 conflict`), store `final_points`, set `published_revision_id`, and notify the student: `submission.checked` or `submission.returned`, or, for a later revision of checked work, `submission.review_updated` only under D23's visible-change rule. Checked work without manual questions may get a published review (overrides, comments) as revision 1 after revision 0 (K18).
- **D29. The review queue.** `GET /review-queue?issue=&group=&student=&limit=&cursor=` answers the tutor's `submitted` submissions of issues with manual questions, keyset on `(first_submitted_at, id)` through the partial index, each `{submission_id, issue_id, title, student: {relationship_id, display_name}, submitted_at, first_submitted_at, late, has_draft, issued_before_reclaim}`, plus `count`, the number of such submissions capped at 100, which the Homework count and Today read (K13).

### Relationship, account and purge effects

- **D30. Lock order.** The general order gains, after lessons: issues, submissions, review revisions, then the attachments and intents of the upload rules. Tutor writes take the tutor's profile lock first with the session re-check (D7 for issuing). A student's autosave and submission lock the student's user row with the session re-check, then the tutor's profile only when D18 decided the request binds or releases a file, then the relationship `FOR SHARE`, the issue `FOR SHARE` and the submission. Reviews take the profile lock, the relationship `FOR SHARE`, the issue `FOR SHARE`, the submission and the draft revision. Every homework path that names students as frame or notification recipients (issuing, a deadline or lesson change, withdrawal, publication, closure, marking for erasure) locks those submissions' relationships `FOR SHARE` in identifier order, after the profile lock and before any group, lesson or issue, and decides the recipients by D14 under those locks, as the frame-recipient rule of [locks and the session re-check](../specs/accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check) requires; a reclaim, which locks the relationship, therefore either waits for the change or is seen by it. The hot-row cost of the profile lock is a risk (section 9). A race test per pair (withdraw and submit, ending and a file-binding autosave, issuing and ending, a return and ending) waits on explicit row locks, never on sleeps.
- **D31. Ending a relationship** closes, in the ending's transaction and after its existing locks, every `not_started`, `draft` and `returned` submission of the relationship (`closed`); `submitted` work stays checkable but not returnable; checked work stays readable. The ending releases no charge itself, as chat's discard paths do not: it sets `release_pending_since` on each closed submission whose answers hold file references never submitted: the `answer` references created after the submission's latest `submitted_at` (all of them when it was never submitted), compared through the reference's `created_at`; the files it submitted before stay referenced, so the tutor's review and the student's view keep them, and the cleanup job of D33 releases them later under the charge lock order (K8); a released file leaves `answers.files` for both roles, so closed work never lists a file that can no longer be downloaded. Tutor deletion applies the same to every relationship it ends. Reactivation reopens nothing.
- **D32. A dropped provisional link's work** is marked for erasure by setting `issues.erase_pending_since` on the link's one-to-one issues, in the transactions that apply the removal effects (removal, stopping studying on a provisional link, a dropped reclaim link, `reclaim.expire`, the 7-day cleanup, the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account, student deletion's removal effects on its provisional and reclaim-pending links) and in tutor deletion's discard of provisional and reclaim-pending links, each after its lesson locks and before nothing else. The student purge's `student_purged` discard does not mark: it removes the student's submissions itself (D34) and leaves the tutor's issues in the tutor's history. Marked issues are unreadable at once (D14).
- **D33. The cleanup job** `homework.cleanup` runs every minute within 20 seconds and selects only marked rows through partial indexes, so an empty run is two index probes: it erases marked issues with their submissions, answers, review revisions and references, and releases the never-submitted file references of submissions marked by D31, at most 500 deleted rows of each kind per transaction, draining children in resumable stages and retaining markers until completion; each batch locks only the charged profile, issues, submissions, revisions and attachments in that order, rechecks the marker and existence under lock, and queues each file's `storage_delete` with its last reference, as `chat.erase_channels` does for channels.
- **D34. Purges,** each in the change that creates its table, so no purge path fails while the slice is half built. The tutor purge's batch kinds gain, before the library batches (issues restrict templates and lessons): review-item references and charges, answer references and charges, review revisions, answers, submissions, issue-snapshot references and issues, 500 rows per batch inside the round-robin budget, on every tutor purge path. The student purge deletes the student's submissions with their answers, review revisions and references in batches through `submission.student_id`, each batch locking the charged tutors' profiles first, before the transaction that deletes the user row. `submissions.published_revision_id` references the revision `ON DELETE SET NULL`, so deleting revisions before their submission never meets the circular key. Pending `answer` and `corrected` uploads of a purged account, or scoped to a submission that no longer exists, go through the intent-routed deletion of the upload flow.
- **D35. After a tutor's deletion** students keep reading their own submissions until the purge, through the ended relationship (K11): unsubmitted work is closed by D31, checked work stays checked, and submitted work stays `submitted` and read-only, because nobody can check it any more; slice 10's results page builds on this.

### Uploads and downloads

- **D36. The `answer` and `corrected` contexts adopt the general upload pipeline** with the durable cleanup journal in their own `homework` capacity class of 1,024 unresolved intents (Q2 A). Scope `submission:<submission id>:<question id>`: for `answer` the caller is the submission's student with a confirmed email, the submission is writable to them (D14) and the question is a `file` question it may still change, and the detected kind is in the question's `allowed_kinds`; for `corrected` the caller is the issue's tutor, the submission is reviewable and the question is a `file` question. Both are charged to the issue's tutor; the scope is stored and checked again at completion and at binding. Limits: 25 MB per file, a WebP picture at most 8 MiB, every picture through the metadata filter (student phone photos lose their location), the general 4 concurrent uploads per user and 32 per instance, and per user across both contexts at most 20 uploads that are `uploading` or `pending` and not expired and 60 starts per hour. For `answer` uploads also, per student and tutor: at most 100 MB `uploading` or `pending` and not expired, counted from the rows, and at most 200 MB of completed bytes per UTC day on the injected business clock, counted from a durable daily aggregate of the same design as chat's ([durable daily upload accounting](../specs/files.md#durable-daily-upload-accounting)), so one student cannot fill or block a tutor's quota with answers (K9). A scope outside the caller's reach answers `404`. Residual, recorded in T-20 pending owner review: bound answer files of submitted work stay charged until the purge, and a tutor has no route to remove them.
- **D37. Download addresses for the three reserved kinds** (Q1 A), on token version 2 with the kind bytes slice 8 fixed (`4` `issue_snapshot`, `5` `answer`, `6` `review_item`), minted only for a live reference row the caller can see: `issue_snapshot:<issue>` for the issue's tutor and for a student holding a submission of that issue that D14 lets them read; `answer:<submission>` for the issue's tutor and for the submission's student while D14 lets them read it; `review_item:<revision>` for the issue's tutor, and for the student only when the revision is the latest published revision of a submission D14 lets them read. Serving re-checks, for each kind, that the attachment is `bound`, the named reference row exists and the issue is not marked for erasure, and for `answer` also that the submission's relationship is neither `reclaim_pending` nor `discarded`; the tutor's addresses keep working on a withdrawn issue, which `GET /issues?status=withdrawn` still lists. The token names an item, not a caller, so an `issue_snapshot` or `review_item` address a student minted before losing access keeps working until its expiry, at most 10 minutes, the residual already accepted for lesson materials and recorded in T-27 pending owner review. The refusal order is that of [references other than messages](../specs/files.md#references-other-than-messages). Pictures with recorded dimensions may be `inline`; every other kind is an `attachment` download. Responses that carry addresses send `Cache-Control: no-store`. Each kind's authorization lands with its table: `issue_snapshot` in B2, `answer` in B4, `review_item` in B5.

## 9. Risks

- **C0 may not reach D2's target** with the options that keep coverage; its task plan says so with measurements and the owner decides Q5 again before slice 9's features start. (Outcome: the target was missed after C0 had merged; the owner kept Q5's answer on 2026-11-12, see D2.)
- **The check budget.** Two new specs, four pilot segments, three browsers and D22's matrix in lane A add minutes. D4's 540 s rule, which stopped feature work for a consolidation, is suspended for the rest of the slice (D2, D4), and the last measured gate, 567.6 s, left about 32 s to NFR-6.2's 600 s. A gate at or above 600 s still breaks NFR-6.2 and the budget rule of `AGENTS.md` (make tests faster or consolidate them); the coordinating agent takes it to the owner as a multiple-choice question before the next merge.
- **The bundle.** The 1.4 KB headroom cannot take any new global text section; D41 and D44 keep new texts in feature sections, and each frontend-touching change reports the entry size.
- **Answer keys through shared code.** A tutor struct reused in a student path would leak keys; separate structs, D21's conventions test and D22's allowlist test cover it, and every reviewer checks the student paths.
- **Lock order and a hot row.** File-binding autosaves of all of a tutor's students take the tutor's profile lock; D30 fixes the order, each pair gets a race test, and the contention is measured with the deployment.
- **Snapshot size.** Copying up to 1 MiB of block JSON per issue costs a transaction; D10 bounds it with a running total; measurements wait for the deployment, as the owner decided on 2026-11-08.
- **Autosave subtleties.** A save answering after newer typing must never overwrite it, as in the builder; F2's tests cover it.
- **iOS camera pictures.** HEIC from the camera is outside the allowlist; D45's `accept` list makes iOS convert to JPEG; WebKit's emulation cannot prove it, so a real-device check stays `unverified` until the pilot.
- **Erasure and purge order.** Issues restrict templates and lessons, and revisions and submissions reference each other; D34 orders the batches, and each creating change carries its purge test.
- **Q1 amends `AGENTS.md`**, which S1 carries with a security-profile reviewer and the owner's review.
- **Migrations in parallel branches** collide on numbers; section 7's merge order handles it.
- **Flaky tests** stop gates; a failed assertion is never rerun without the owner's decision.
