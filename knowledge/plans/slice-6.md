## 1. Problem, outcome and scope

**Problem.** OVC has relationships, groups, lessons, live updates and in-app notifications, but no way for a tutor and a student to talk or exchange a file. FR-12 (chat) and FR-14 (files) are unbuilt: the upload route accepts only avatars, no attachment is ever charged to a tutor's quota, no file can be downloaded through its references, and the Teaching settings show no storage usage. The message digest, the only remaining optional email of the MVP before homework, does not exist, so pilot scenario 3 cannot reach its digest and unsubscribe steps. The slice 4 plan left the chat frames, persisted presence, the `channel` kind of `entity.changed` and the shared offline banner to this slice, and the slice 3 plan left the read-only chat after an ending or an archiving, "Deleted user" senders, the provisional link's direct channel (T-44), the erasure of a provisional period's messages on a dropped reclaim link, and the rest of the Teaching settings page. The merge gate already takes 187 to 252 seconds against a temporary threshold of 260 seconds, so the slice must add its browser tests without crossing it.

**Outcome.** End to end through the real API, worker, PostgreSQL, the local S3-compatible server and the production frontend build:

- every relationship has a direct channel from the moment it becomes `provisional` or `active`, and every group a group channel; the channel list shows each with its last activity, an unread count capped at "99+" and its writability, and the sidebar's Messages item shows how many channels have unread messages;
- members send plain-text messages with up to ten attachments; other connected members see them within a second, pictures show a preview, and history stays paginated;
- an author edits a message within 24 hours and deletes it at any time; the tutor deletes any member's message, also in read-only channels, and authors delete their own there too (D47), freeing the storage of their attachments;
- the chat of an ended relationship or an archived group is read-only, and becomes writable again on reactivation or unarchiving; a removed group member loses the group channel with its history; a provisional student reaches only the direct channel, and a discarded provisional link's messages are erased as Q6 decides;
- members see who is online and who is typing, students only for their tutor (D51); presence is persisted, so every process can tell who is online;
- a member who has been offline for ten minutes with unread messages gets at most one digest email every 30 minutes, naming channels and counts, never message text, with one-click unsubscribe;
- uploads stream to storage in parts within per-context size limits and a content-detected type allowlist, are charged once per tutor and attachment, are refused beyond the tutor's quota (pending uploads counted), and are downloadable only by users who can see an item that references them;
- the tutor sees their file quota and usage in Teaching settings;
- the shared offline banner appears in the signed-in frame while the realtime connection is lost, and the chat writes are disabled with their reason until it returns (D75);
- account deletion shows the account's messages as from "Deleted user" until the purge, and the purge erases or deletes them, with their files, in bounded batches.

**In scope:**

## 3. Decisions the plan proposes

Each names the alternative and the reason. S1 lands them in the specifications; `docs/` changes are marked pending owner review. Where a decision depends on an owner question, it says so. Revision 2 amends D3, D5, D9, D11, D12, D13, D14, D15, D16, D17, D19, D20, D21, D23, D25, D27, D29, D31, D35, D36, D39, D45 and D46 in place and adds D53 to D77.

### Channels and membership

- **D1. Channels are created with their relationship or group.** A direct channel is created in the transaction that first makes a relationship `provisional` or `active` (invitation sign-up, signed-in acceptance, accepting a join request), never for an `invited` card; a group channel in the transaction that creates the group. Reactivation reuses the relationship's channel. *Reason:* realtime-and-video.md's channel table; one channel per relationship keeps the history across ending and reactivation.
- **D2. The migration backfills existing data.** S3's migration creates a direct channel for every relationship that is `provisional`, `active`, `ended` (an ended one read-only since its `ended_at`) or `reclaim_pending` (with the tutor's member row and the student's row suspended, under D3), a group channel for every group (an archived one read-only since its `archived_at`), and the member rows the rules below give, so data created before slice 6 behaves like data created after it. *Alternative:* creating channels lazily on first use. *Reason:* lazy creation would add a write to read paths and a race between two first uses (K26).
- **D3. Membership rows mirror relationships and groups, in the same transaction.** `channel_member` holds the tutor and the student of a direct channel while the relationship is `provisional`, `active` or `ended`, and the tutor and the current members of a group channel. Every path that changes those states updates the rows in its own transaction, after locking the channel `FOR UPDATE` (D5): linking, confirmation, the reclaim and the owner's choice, removal, stopping studying, ending, reactivation, group creation, adding and removing members, student and tutor deletion, the 7-day cleanup, the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account, `reclaim.expire` and the purge. While a link is `reclaim_pending`, the tutor's member row stays and the student's row is suspended (`suspended_since` set; every access path reads members through the one active-member predicate of D81, which skips a suspended row), so the account reaches nothing of the channel; the channel stays writable for the tutor, whose messages wait for the student. Keeping the link clears the suspension and resets the student's read marker as D80 says, so the owner sees the provisional period's messages as unread; dropping it discards the link and erases the channel (D48) (K32). An ended relationship keeps both rows (read access); a discarded one loses both, and its whole channel is erased (D48, D62). A newly added or re-added group member's row starts at the channel's latest message (D76). A table-driven test drives every path and compares the rows with the rule. *Alternative:* deriving membership from relationship and group rows at query time. *Reason:* the specification stores member rows, which also hold the read markers; the slice 5 review found five drifting copies of a derived visibility rule, and one stored source avoids that.
- **D4. Read-only is a channel column, set in the same transaction.** `read_only_since` is set when the relationship ends (to `ended_at`), when the group is archived (to `archived_at`), and cleared on reactivation and unarchiving. Tutor deletion ends relationships and archives groups, so its channels become read-only through the same statements. Every author write (send, edit, upload to the channel's scope) answers `409 channel_read_only` in a read-only channel; deletes stay allowed (D47). Reading and moving the read marker stay allowed.
- **D5. Lock order and the channel row as the serialisation point.** The [lock order](../specs/accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check) gains channels in identifier order after groups and before series, and messages in identifier order after channels: user rows, tutor profiles, relationships, invitations, join requests, groups, channels, messages, series, lessons. Every path that changes a channel's membership or writability locks the channel `FOR UPDATE` after its existing relationship and group locks. Every chat producer (send, edit, delete, read marker, typing) locks the channel `FOR SHARE` or, for a send and for a charge release, `FOR UPDATE` (D9, D53), and reads the members under that lock; frame recipients are those members. This satisfies slice 4's rule that recipients are decided from rows held under locks that conflict with the paths that remove them, without locking up to 31 relationship rows per message; presence recipients are the one stated exception (D20). *Alternative:* each message locking every recipient's relationship `FOR SHARE`, as slice 4's rule reads literally. *Reason:* one lock per message instead of 31, and the same guarantee, because every path that removes a recipient also locks the channel (K1). Charge-releasing paths add tutor profiles up front (D53).
- **D6. Recipients are decided by the producer; no membership cache** (with Q4 A). Chat signals carry the members read under the channel lock, at most 100 per signal (a group channel has at most 31), as every other producer's do (D61). The listener resolves nothing, and the roadmap's per-instance cache is not built (K1).
- **D7. Labels per role.** One table fixes every name a chat response, the digest and the frame-triggered refetches show:

  | Field | Tutor | Student |
  |---|---|---|
  | Direct channel label | the card's own display name, also while `provisional` (never the account name a possibly squatting account chose, T-44), with the cue "Email not confirmed" while the link is provisional (D67) | the tutor's account name and the relationship colour |
  | Group channel label | the group's name and colour | the group's name, with the tutor's account name and the relationship colour as its tutor label |
  | Sender of a message | the card's display name for a student of the tutor; "You" for the tutor's own | the tutor's account name; another student's account name, on that student's messages only (Q9 B); "You" for the student's own |
  | Group members in the conversation header | card display names | none: the header shows the group's name and tutor label only (Q9 B) |
  | Typing indicator | the card's display name | the tutor's account name; for another student "Someone is typing…", from a `typing` frame whose `user_id` is `null` (Q9 B) |
  | Presence | every member's | the tutor's only; never another student's (Q9 B) |
  | Sender whose account is `deleted` | `label_kind: deleted_user` ("Deleted user"), body kept until the purge | the same |
  | Sender purged | `label_kind: deleted_user`, body erased (`message_removed`) | the same |

  Fixed labels travel as `label_kind` codes beside a `null` label, as slice 5 decided for lessons. *Reason:* card display names are the tutor's own data and never reach students; a squatter's chosen name never reaches the tutor (T-44).
- **D8. `entity.changed channel`.** Sent to every member, under the channel lock, when what that member sees of the channel changes: becoming read-only or writable, a member added or removed (to the remaining members and to the one who joined or left), the start of an erasure (D62), and the channel's label (a group rename or recolour, an account name change of a member, as D7 shows it). The member who left gets one last frame and then nothing about the channel. Message changes use the message frames, not this kind. A student's frames never reveal another tutor's channels.

### Messages

- **D9. A per-channel sequence orders messages.** Each message gets `seq`, the channel's `last_seq + 1`, assigned under the channel's `FOR UPDATE` lock in the send transaction; history is keyset on `seq` (newest first, 50 per page by default, at most 100), and `GET /channels/{id}/messages?after_seq=` returns up to 100 newer messages oldest first for catching up after a frame. A member's read marker is `last_read_seq`; the unread count is `last_seq − last_read_seq` capped at 99, with no aggregate over messages; deleted and erased messages count, because they stay in the list as placeholders (D64). *Alternative:* the specified keyset on `(created_at, id)` with the last read message identifier. *Reason:* `created_at` is read before commit, so a message committed later with an earlier time would be counted as read without being shown, and unread counts would need an aggregate query; sends within one channel are rare enough to serialise (K15).
- **D10. The body.** 1 to 4,000 Unicode code points of plain text, stored as entered after refusing NUL and the control characters other than line feed and tab (`422 validation_failed`); a message with at least one attachment may have an empty body; whitespace-only bodies count as empty. Rendered as text with line breaks kept by CSS, in an element isolated with `dir="auto"`; links follow D67; nothing is rendered as HTML (K11).
- **D11. Sending** (with Q3 A). `POST /channels/{id}/messages` with `{body, attachment_ids}` and an `Idempotency-Key` header under D65; the answer is `201` with the message. The web client sends through this route; the WebSocket's client frames are `typing.start` and `typing.stop` only, and `message.send` and `message.read` are removed from the reserved list; pilot scenario 3 step 7 is reworded to send through the composer and REST (K8, K16).
- **D12. Editing and deleting.** `PATCH /messages/{id}` with `{body, version}` replaces the body within 24 hours of `created_at` (`409 edit_window_passed` after), sets `edited_at` and increments `version` (`409 conflict` on a stale one); attachments are not edited; editing needs a writable channel. `DELETE /messages/{id}` sets `deleted_at`, erases the body, and removes the message's attachment references in the same transaction, under the charge locks of D53, which removes the quota charge with the last reference and queues each object's `storage_delete`; it is idempotent. Who may delete in which channel, read-only ones included, follows D47. *Reason:* a deleted message keeps no copy of its text or files (data minimisation, T-34) (K25).
- **D13. The read marker.** `POST /channels/{id}/read` with `{seq}` moves the caller's marker forward only (a lower or equal value changes nothing and answers the current state), clamped to the channel's `last_seq`, answers `{unread_count, unread_channels}`, and signals `read.updated {channel_id}` to the caller's own other sockets only; no other member learns it. It takes no tutor profile lock (D15). Sending a message moves the sender's own marker to it, so earlier messages count as read: replying is taken as having seen the conversation. *Alternative:* read receipts to the other members. *Reason:* FR-12.4 asks for unread counts, not receipts (K27).
- **D14. Rate limits in memory, injected.** At most 30 message writes (sends, edits and deletes together) per minute per user across channels; at most 200 MB of chat attachment bytes per student per tutor per UTC day, checked before streaming (an upload starts only while the day's total is below the cap) and counted when the upload completes (D56); at most 60 chat uploads started per user per hour; beyond any of them, `429 rate_limited` with `Retry-After`. The pending-upload cap of D55 is counted from database rows, not in memory. These rate counters are counted in memory per API instance, as the specification says, through an injected clock and counter so tests run them deterministically; a restart resets the counters, recorded as a T-20 residual. *Reason:* the specification fixes in-memory counting; one API instance in the MVP (K29).
- **D15. Writes by students re-check the session; writes by tutors take the profile lock.** Every chat write by a student (send, edit, delete, read marker, upload creation and completion) locks the student's user row first and re-checks the session, under the [session re-check rule](../specs/accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check); every chat write by a tutor except a read-marker move takes the tutor's profile lock first and answers `401 unauthenticated`, changing nothing, when the tutor is no longer `active`, as every tutor write does. A tutor's read-marker move re-checks only that the session is valid, as a read does, because it changes nothing anyone else sees. Charges follow D53 (K13, K31).
- **D16. Deletion and purge of messages.**
  - From an account's deletion until its purge its messages show "Deleted user" with their bodies (D7).
  - A student's purge erases the bodies of the student's messages (`erased_at`, shown as "Message removed"), sets `sender_id` to null and removes their attachment references, in batches of 500 messages per transaction inside the purge's round-robin budget, each batch locking the charged tutors' profiles and the channels as D53 says, before the transaction that deletes the user row; that final transaction locks the account's channels `FOR UPDATE` and then deletes its member rows and its `digest_pending` and `digest_state` rows (D17).
  - A tutor's purge deletes the attachment references and quota charges of the tutor's channels, queueing a `storage_delete` for every attachment that loses its last reference, whoever uploaded it (students' uploads included, D73), then messages, member rows and channels, each 500 rows per batch in key order, before group members, because channels reference the relationships and groups; the final transaction finds no charge left, so `attachments.quota_tutor_id`'s `ON DELETE RESTRICT` never fails the purge (K18).
  - A discarded provisional link's channel is erased whole (D48), through D62.
- **D17. Presence and the digest flag leave the user row.** Persisted presence keeps `user_presence (user_id, online_until, last_seen_at)` as the account summary and adds `user_presence_instances (user_id, instance_id, online_until, last_seen_at)` with primary key `(user_id, instance_id)`, cascading user foreign keys on both tables and an expiry index on instance `online_until`. Each process generates a named UUID through the injected random source once, never accepts it from clients, exposes it to browsers or reuses it after restart. Every lease mutation locks existing users `FOR KEY SHARE` in identifier order, initializes and locks summaries in identifier order, then changes leases and recomputes summaries. Missing purged accounts are skipped. The summary serializes cross-instance operations on the same account, without an unbounded process-wide mutex. Short operator and purge transactions holding `FOR UPDATE` may delay presence, an owner-approved exception without changing their locks; `FOR SHARE` and `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` do not block it. `TestPresenceWaitsOnlyForConflictingUserRowLocks` and `TestAPresenceBatchSurvivesAConcurrentPurge` prove these boundaries. The digest flag lives in `digest_pending (user_id, pending_since, revision)` and the digest bookkeeping in `digest_state (user_id, last_digest_at)` and in the member rows' `digested_through_seq` (D63), keyed by the account without a foreign key, like notification rows: the only producer of `digest_pending` is a send, which holds the channel lock, and the purge locks the account's channels before it deletes the account's member rows and digest rows (D16), so no row names a purged account once the purge commits. `users.online_until`, `last_seen_at`, `digest_pending_since` and `last_digest_at` are not added. A send inserts `digest_pending` rows with a fresh sequence-backed revision on conflict, retaining the earliest pending time for the members whose `online_until` has passed. *Alternative:* the specified user columns. *Reason:* setting a column on 30 recipients' user rows inside a send would lock user rows after relationships and channels, against the lock order, and deadlock with a student's deletion, which locks the user row first and then waits for the relationship the send holds; tutor deletion must never lock a student's user row; and the gateway's per-minute presence batches would contend with every transaction that holds a user row (K2).

### Presence, typing and frames

- **D18. Frames.** Server frames, payloads as structs in `internal/schemas`, mirrored in `frontend/src/lib/realtime/frames.ts` by the change that emits them (D68) and checked by the realtime contract test:

  | Frame | Payload | Recipients |
  |---|---|---|
  | `message.new` | `{channel_id, message_id, seq}` | the channel's members under the lock, the sender's other sockets included |
  | `message.updated`, `message.deleted` | `{channel_id, message_id}` | likewise |
  | `read.updated` | `{channel_id}` | the reader's own other sockets |
  | `typing` | `{channel_id, user_id}`, with `user_id: null` in the frames students receive about another student (D51) | the channel's other connected members |
  | `presence` | `{user_id, online}` | users who share a writable channel with the user (D20) |

  Message frames carry identifiers only; the client refetches through REST. `typing` and `presence` carry an account identifier and, for presence, a boolean: they are ephemeral, never persisted (typing) or only as times (presence), and their recipients are authorized like every other frame; on every `ready` the client refetches presence through REST. *Reason:* the principle that frames never carry authoritative state holds for stored data; typing has no stored state to refetch (K7).
- **D19. Persisted presence.** An API extends only its own lease about once per minute to the greater of its existing deadline and business time plus 90 seconds; activity instants are monotonic. Its last socket closing deletes only that lease. The summary keeps the greatest retained lease deadline, or on close the greatest closing instant and retained deadlines; `last_seen_at` preserves greatest real activity across leases and the existing summary, never a future deadline. Account-wide online means summary `online_until` is in the future. A crashed instance contributes only until its lease expires; no expiry frame is promised and REST supplies current state. `chat.expire_presence` runs once a minute, deletes at most 500 expired leases per transaction within a 20-second monotonic deadline including SQL and locks, selects identifiers through the expiry index without locking leases before users and summaries, rechecks expiry under the locks and preserves renewals, purges and historical summaries. Cleanup never creates future online time. D57 defines readiness and failed-release handling; the [completion plan](slice-6-presence-completion.md) defines executable acceptance criteria.
- **D20. Presence frames.** Sent when an account's first socket on the instance becomes `ready` and when its last one closes, debounced per instance and account to at most one change every 5 seconds on the injected scheduler before recipient work (D58), not a cluster-wide rate guarantee, to the accounts that share a writable channel with it. Frames use committed aggregate presence: another API's unexpired lease prevents offline. Deferred emissions lock the summary again, read the current aggregate and call `pg_notify` in that same transaction, ordering signal commits. An already committed offline frame can arrive after a later online commit, followed by the online frame in listener commit order. The recipients are read without locks in a short transaction, a stated exception to slice 4's rule that recipients are decided under locks (T-29): presence reveals only whether someone is connected, and a recipient removed concurrently can receive at most one such frame. A student's presence reaches only the tutors the student shares a writable channel with, never another student (D51); a tutor's presence reaches the students of their writable channels. A provisional account's presence therefore reaches its tutor only. A tutor with many students shares a writable channel with each of them, so one change of that tutor's presence is chunked into signals of at most 100 recipients (a tutor with 500 students sends five); D61 counts these in its rate estimate.
- **D21. Typing.** `typing.start {channel_id}` and `typing.stop {channel_id}` from a current member of a writable channel. The gateway first coalesces in memory, per socket, account and channel, to at most one relayed frame every 2 seconds on the injected clock (D58), and drops the frames in between before any database work; a frame that passes is checked in a short transaction that locks the channel `FOR SHARE`, reads the members and writes the `typing` signal with `pg_notify` inside that same transaction, so the signal's recipients are the members under the lock and it is delivered when that transaction commits (D61); anything else answers an `error` frame (`not_found` or `channel_read_only`) and the socket stays open. The producer splits the recipients it read under the lock into those who receive the typing account and the students who receive `user_id: null` because the typing account is another student (D51), and the signal carries both lists (D61), so the listener decides nothing and queries nothing. Typing is never persisted; the client hides an indicator 5 seconds after the last frame, and the dots stop with reduced motion. Typing frames count toward the inbound limit of 20 frames per second. Delivery follows D61.
- **D22. The test control presence route stays registry-based.** `POST /__test__/presence` answers local `{online, sockets}`, counting ready sockets. A pending uncommitted release returns a structured failure. Successful local offline acknowledges only release of this API's lease; global presence is asserted separately through REST or the database. No new route or client-supplied instance identifier is added (T-36).

### Uploads, quotas and downloads

- **D23. The general upload pipeline, `chat` context first.** `POST /uploads?context=chat&scope=channel:<id>` creates the attachment row first (D54), then streams one `file` part (D66) without spooling to disk to an S3 multipart upload in 8 MiB parts under a 32-byte random key, counting bytes and computing SHA-256 as it goes, and aborts with `413 payload_too_large` the moment the 25 MB limit is passed; the storage adapter gains multipart upload, abort and ranged reads, contract-tested against the local server and the memory fake. Database sessions: one short session before streaming (session re-check for students, the scope, the pending and hourly caps, the daily cap and the quota pre-check of D55 and D56, creating the `uploading` row) and one after it (session re-check, the scope and membership again, the exact quota and daily-cap check, `pending`), none during the transfer. At most 4 concurrent uploads per user and 32 per instance (`429 rate_limited`). The response is `{id, filename, media_type, size_bytes}`; the storage key is never returned. Pending uploads are invisible to others, expire after 24 hours through the existing `uploads.expire` job, and are bound by a send.
- **D24. Type detection without a dependency.** Ordinary kinds by magic bytes from the first part (JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, PDF, MP3 with an ID3 tag or, without one, at least three consecutive valid MPEG audio frame headers, M4A by its `ftyp` brand, OGG with a Vorbis or Opus first page, WAV), plain text as valid UTF-8 without NUL or control characters other than tab, line feed and carriage return, checked over the whole stream; the extension must agree with the detected kind (`415 unsupported_media_type` otherwise), through the per-kind extension allowlist of the files specification, added when the contract was specified (`.txt` the only plain-text extension, `.oga` and `.opus` accepted for OGG, a file without an extension refused). Unit tests cover each kind, each disguise and each boundary. *Reason:* the allowlist is short and intricate; a sniffing library would need a stack-table row (AGENTS.md).
- **D25. Office containers are inspected after storing, through bounded ranged reads.** DOCX, XLSX, PPTX, ODT, ODS and ODP are stored first as above, while the row is `uploading`, then inspected over an `io.ReaderAt` that reads the stored object by ranges, before the row becomes `pending`:
  - one ranged read of the last 64 KiB plus 22 bytes finds the end-of-central-directory record and, when present, the zip64 locator and record; a container that declares more than 10,000 entries or a central directory larger than 4 MiB is refused before anything is parsed, and so is one whose directory does not lie inside the object;
  - the directory is fetched in one bounded read and parsed by the standard library's `archive/zip` over it; duplicate entry names, compared case-insensitively, are refused;
  - `[Content_Types].xml` (OOXML) is read inflated up to 1 MiB, ODF's `mimetype` must be the first entry, stored uncompressed and at most 256 bytes, and `META-INF/manifest.xml` is read up to 1 MiB; every `.rels` entry is read inflated and parsed as a stream, so one relationship file may use the whole 8 MiB that all of them together may hold, and the container may hold at most 2,000 `.rels` entries, bounded by the 10,000-entry limit as well; a container whose entries exceed any of these bounds is refused, never partly skipped. An ordinary presentation has two or three relationship files per slide (a 25-slide deck with notes has about 65, a 100-slide deck about 250, each a few KiB), so these bounds refuse only abnormal containers; `TestAHundredSlideDeckWithNotesIsAccepted` keeps it so;
  - **storage requests:** the tail read of the first step is kept in memory, and so is the directory when it lies inside that tail (most documents); otherwise the directory is one more bounded read; each inspected entry is read with one ranged request covering its local header and its compressed data, with a read-ahead of at most 1 MiB, so an inspection costs a handful of requests instead of one per small read;
  - the content rules of D77 apply: macros are refused by content type and relationship type, not only by part name (a `vbaProject` part or relationship, a macro-enabled main content type), and the main part's content type must agree with the extension (a `.docx` whose main part is a spreadsheet is refused).

  A refused container is answered `415 unsupported_media_type`, its row is marked `deleted` and its object's deletion is queued (D54). *Alternative:* the specified 1 MiB tail ring buffer. *Reason:* the tail holds the central directory, which names entries but not the relationship files' contents, so external relationships cannot be detected from it; bounded ranged reads keep memory and work bounded without spooling (K3).
- **D26. File names.** The client's name with every path component before the last `/` or `\` removed, then at most 255 bytes of UTF-8; control characters and every Unicode format character (`Cf`, which includes the bidirectional overrides that make `gpj.exe` read as `exe.jpg`) are refused with `422 validation_failed`; the name is stored as given and rendered as text in an element isolated with `dir="auto"`. `Content-Disposition` follows RFC 6266: an ASCII fallback with CR, LF, quotes and backslashes removed, plus `filename*` in UTF-8.
- **D27. The upload read deadline.** An upload route's read deadline is the context's per-file limit at 128 KiB per second, at least a floor of 60 seconds and at most 10 minutes (25 MB: about 195 seconds), with the 35-second write deadline after the body ends; the floor and the rate are constructor settings, so tests use a short floor (D58). A body that delivers less than 16 KiB in any 30-second window is aborted as stalled, so a trickling client cannot hold a slot for the whole deadline. Either way the multipart upload is aborted. Shutdown still drains for 40 seconds: an upload still running then is aborted and the client retries (K9). Deadlines run on the system clock, as every transport deadline does. *Alternative:* the 30-second default. *Reason:* the open implementation question names a per-MiB rate with a ceiling for slice 6; 30 seconds would refuse a 25 MB file below about 7 Mbit/s.
- **D28. Memory per instance.** Upload memory is at most 32 uploads × one 8 MiB part = 256 MiB, separate from the avatar decoding budget, plus the bounded inspection reads of D25 (at most the 64 KiB tail, 4 MiB of directory and 1 MiB per entry read, one entry at a time per upload), and does not grow with file size (NFR-2.3). The picture filter of D50 adds a look-ahead of at most 64 KiB per upload; for WebP, whose RIFF size and `VP8X` flags sit at the start and change once chunks are dropped, the first part is held in memory, filtered and rewritten before it is sent: WebP metadata chunks normally precede or follow the image data within a file far smaller than 8 MiB, and a WebP picture larger than one part (8 MiB) is refused with `413 payload_too_large` and the field code `webp_too_large`, the same status as the other size limits, so the held part stays the one 8 MiB part counted above; S1 states the 8 MiB WebP limit in files.md, and the inline refusal reads "WebP pictures can be at most 8 MB".
- **D29. Quota charges.** Binding an attachment to a message creates its reference and, when the channel's tutor has no charge for the attachment, a charge row and the size added to `file_usage_bytes`, in the send transaction under the charge locks of D53; removing the last reference that charges the tutor removes the charge and subtracts the size in the same transaction, under the same locks. Binding refuses with `507 quota_exceeded` when the usage would pass the quota. An attachment bound to one message is not bound again (one reference per chat attachment). *Reason:* [files](../specs/files.md#quotas).
- **D30. Binding.** A send binds only the caller's own `pending` uploads of context `chat` whose scope is the same channel and whose 24-hour expiry has not passed, at most ten, in the send transaction; more than ten identifiers are a schema refusal, `422 validation_failed` at `body.attachment_ids` with code `invalid`, before anything is resolved, so it reveals nothing about them; within that bound any other identifier answers `404 not_found` like a random one (the schema bound replaced the service's eleventh-attachment check on 2026-10-30 by the owner's decision, pending owner review).
- **D31. Downloads** (Q2 C). `GET /attachments/{id}` authorizes the caller through the attachment's references (a member of a channel that holds a referencing message that is not deleted) and answers `{url, expires_at}`, with `url` an OVC address `/api/v1/files/{id}?t=<token>` in the format of D59, valid until a 5-minute boundary (D59). `GET /files/{id}` checks the token in constant time and its expiry, checks that the path's identifier equals the token's attachment, and re-checks that the attachment is `bound` and still referenced by the message the token names, whose channel is not pending erasure (a deleted message or a discarded channel stops its files at once), and streams the object with the headers of D70, never holding it in memory; at most 64 downloads at once per instance (`503` with `Retry-After: 1`); every other refusal is `404`. Access records name the route template only, never the query. Message responses carry each attachment's `{id, filename, media_type, size_bytes, width, height, url, expires_at}` minted per response, so a page of history needs no request per picture; the client fetches the page again before `expires_at` and when a picture fails to load. The content security policy stays `'self'`, and the open question on `nosniff` is resolved by D70.
- **D32. Pictures and previews.** JPEG, PNG, WebP and GIF attachments get `width` and `height` read from their headers at upload (no decoding), so the conversation reserves their space and keeps its scroll position; a picture above 50 megapixels is stored as a file without a preview. Previews are the original picture constrained by CSS, loaded lazily. Picture metadata is removed by the allowlist filter (D50).
- **D33. Tutor settings.** `GET /tutor/settings` answers `{file_quota_bytes, file_usage_bytes}` for the calling tutor (`tutor, unverified ok`), `no-store`; `PATCH /tutor/settings` is not registered in the MVP, because recording retention comes after the MVP and the onboarding dismissal with slice 10 (K20). Teaching settings show a usage bar for files only.

### Decisions added in revision 2

- **D53. Charge locks.** Every path that creates or removes a quota charge locks the charged tutors' profiles up front, after the user rows it locks and before any relationship, in identifier order, reading which tutors are charged first without locks and re-checking under the locks (a changed set retries once, then answers `409 conflict` for a request or defers the item to the next run for a job); then the channel `FOR UPDATE`, then the message. The paths and their locks:
  - **binding** (a send with attachments): the sender's user row when a student (D15), the channel's tutor's profile, the channel;
  - **an author's delete:** the author's user row when a student, the channel's tutor's profile when the message has attachments, the channel, the message;
  - **a tutor's delete** (Q5): the tutor's profile, already first under D15, the channel, the message;
  - **the deferred erasure job** (D62): the channel's tutor's profile, the channel, its messages;
  - **a student's purge batches:** the student's user row, already locked by the purge, then the profiles of the tutors charged for the batch's attachments, then their channels;
  - **a tutor's purge batches:** the tutor's user row and profile, as today;
  - `uploads.expire` releases no charge, because pending uploads are not charged.

  The discard paths (removal, stopping studying on a provisional link, a dropped reclaim link, `reclaim.expire`, the 7-day cleanup, the immediate purge of an unconfirmed account, tutor deletion) and a student's ending release no charge in their own transactions, because the erasure is deferred (D62); they add only the channel lock of D3 after the locks they already take, so none of them gains a profile lock. Read-marker moves take no profile lock (D15). `TestChargeReleasesMembershipChangesAndEndingsNeverDeadlock` races each charge path against a membership change, an ending, a removal and a purge batch (`pg_locks`, both orders). *Reason:* `file_usage_bytes` is a column of the tutor profile, so every charge change writes that row; locking it late, after a channel or a relationship, would invert the lock order against tutor writes, which lock the profile first.
- **D54. The attachment row comes first.** The first session creates the attachment as `uploading`, with its random storage key, uploader, context, scope and the client's file name, after the session re-check and the caps, and only then is the object streamed; the second session locks the user row again, re-checks the session, the scope's membership and writability and the exact quota and daily cap, and sets the row `pending` with its detected type, size and checksum. Any failure after the row exists (a refusal, a stall, a disconnect, a refused container, a revoked session, a refusal at completion) marks the row `deleted` and queues a `storage_delete` with its key in one transaction, after aborting the multipart upload; `uploads.expire` cleans `uploading` rows only by age: one older than the longest deadline of D27 plus 10 minutes is marked `deleted`, its multipart upload aborted by its stored identifier (D82) and its deletion queued; it also marks every `pending` row that is no longer bindable (its channel missing, discarded or pending erasure, or left by its uploader, D83) as `deleted` with its deletion queued, so later runs never find it again. The second session, whenever it finds its row no longer `uploading` (already deleted by the age rule or a refusal) or gone, queues the `storage_delete` of the object it has just completed, so a crash between the row and the object leaves no object without a row and no row without its cleanup. Office inspection runs while the row is `uploading`. This follows the avatar flow and the [session re-check rule](../specs/accounts.md#locks-and-the-session-re-check) (K39).
- **D55. Pending uploads count, and are capped.** The quota pre-check and the completion check count, from database rows only, the tutor's charged usage plus the chat uploads scoped to that tutor's channels that are `uploading` or `pending` and not expired, plus the new upload: used + pending + new ≤ quota. A `pending` row counts with its size; an `uploading` row, whose size is not known yet, counts with its `reserved_bytes`, set when the row is created to the request's `Content-Length` capped at the context limit, or to the context limit without one. The completion check excludes the completing row itself and uses its final size. A user has at most 20 chat uploads that are `uploading` or `pending` and not expired, counted from the rows; the hourly count of 60 is the in-memory counter of D14. Pending uploads are not charges: these checks refuse early, and the exact bound is binding under the charge locks of D53 (D29), which a race between two completions cannot pass. Both caps are recorded in T-20.
- **D56. Size-dependent checks before the size is known.** Before streaming, the route refuses a request whose `Content-Length` exceeds the context limit plus the multipart overhead allowed by D66, a quota with no room left (used + pending ≥ quota), and a daily total already at its cap; the exact checks with the final size run at completion (D54), where an upload that no longer fits is refused with `507 quota_exceeded` or `429 rate_limited` and its object deleted. A request without `Content-Length` is streamed under the same per-file limit.
- **D57. Presence readiness, release and clock acknowledgement.** Before initial `ready`, reserve a local account entry, persist the instance lease and summary, then recheck closure, account/session revocation and listener generation, with no gateway mutex across database work. Failure closes reconnectably without ready. Per-account desired state coalesces with generations into at most 4096 total authenticating, active and pending-release accounts, at most four writers and a five-second cancellation-aware end-to-end deadline including admission. Capacity refuses new accounts before ready, never evicting accepted pending releases. A failed release retains one pending entry, retries on the injected one-minute cycle and does not count the closed socket live; uncertain commits remain pending. Free an entry only after confirmed release or deletion and completion or definitive cancellation of older writes. Shutdown drains within its deadline, cancels scheduling and reports incomplete releases. Both Set and Advance on `POST /__test__/clock`, including Set of the same instant, flush only this API's live leases before success. Failure returns a structured error and may leave the clock changed, requiring explicit local recovery. For multiple APIs the controller sets the same absolute instant on all APIs and awaits every response before assertions, sends or worker/digest execution; none runs during the intermediate interval. This is local acknowledgement, not a distributed clock barrier (T-36).
- **D58. Injected time sources.** Business times (presence, the digest, the daily cap's UTC day, the 24-hour edit window) come from the injected `core.Clock`. The gateway has no scheduler seam today: `backend/internal/realtime/socket.go` arms its timers with `time.AfterFunc` and takes their durations from its constructor. S5 therefore adds one, a small `Scheduler` interface in `internal/realtime` (`AfterFunc` and a ticker) with a system implementation used in production and a manual one used in tests, and moves the gateway's existing timers onto it without changing their durations; the presence extension loop, the 5-second presence debounce and the 2-second typing coalescing run on it. The upload read deadline's floor and rate and the stall window are constructor settings of the upload route; the frontend's offline grace and typing expiry run on the frontend's existing injected scheduler. Tests drive each through its seam, never with sleeps.
- **D59. The download address.** `t` is a token in strict unpadded base64url (any other alphabet, padding or length is refused) of a fixed layout: a version byte `1`, the attachment identifier (16 bytes), the referencing message's identifier (16 bytes), the expiry in Unix seconds (8 bytes, big-endian), the disposition (1 byte: `inline` or `attachment`), and an untruncated HMAC-SHA-256 over all the preceding bytes, keyed with a key derived by HKDF-SHA-256 from `OVC_JWT_SECRET` with the info string `download-url`; no new secret is needed, and rotating the secret invalidates every address. The expiry is the next 5-minute boundary after now plus 5 minutes, so an address is valid for 5 to 10 minutes and identical for every response within a 5-minute window, which keeps image elements stable across refetches without a browser cache (D70); a token whose expiry lies more than 10 minutes and 30 seconds ahead is refused, and so is `inline` for anything but a picture with recorded dimensions; the files specification adds `GET /attachments/{id}?disposition=` to mint either kind. The route never redirects, its query never reaches a record, and the slice 10 reverse proxy must drop query strings of `/api/v1/files/*` from its records. ADR-0009 item 1 is amended to "at most 10 minutes" for these addresses (K44).
- **D60. A cheap unread read for the sidebar.** `GET /channels/unread` answers `{unread_channels}` (at most 100) from one indexed query over the caller's member rows and their channels' `last_seq`; the sidebar fetches it at start, refetches it on `message.new`, `read.updated` and `entity.changed channel`, coalesced to at most one request a second, and never refetches `GET /channels` for the badge; an open channel list refetches its first page the same way. The NFR-2.2 measurement includes these refetches.
- **D61. Signal transport.** Chat signals go through the broadcaster, written by the `Signals` repository in the producing transaction: a new signal kind `chat` in the existing payload version 1, `{"v": 1, "k": "chat", "u": [members], "e": "message.new" | "message.updated" | "message.deleted" | "read.updated", "c": channel, "i": message | null, "s": seq | null}`, parsed strictly by the listener (an unknown `e` is rejected as today). Typing and presence go through the same broadcaster, so the rule that every instance delivers from its own listening connection holds: a `typing` signal (`{"v": 1, "k": "typing", "u": [recipients shown the account], "n": [students shown null], "c": channel, "a": account}`, refused by the listener's strict parser when an account appears in both lists) is written inside D21's short transaction, under the channel lock; a `presence` signal (`{"v": 1, "k": "presence", "u": [recipients], "a": account, "o": online}`) is written with `pg_notify` in the transaction holding the summary lock and reading the current aggregate and D20's lock-free recipient scope, chunked at 100 recipients. Estimated peak at NFR-3.1's 2,000 connected users: presence at most one change per instance and account every 5 seconds, a tutor's change costing one signal per 100 of their students (five for a tutor with 500), and typing at most one per account and channel every 2 seconds; with 500 tutors and 5,000 students, reconnect churn of a tenth of the users within a minute and a tenth of them typing, about 100 to 250 notifications a second in a busy minute, each taking PostgreSQL's notification lock at commit. S5's readiness report includes a quick rate check: exactly 200 connected test accounts: 20 tutors and 180 students, nine per tutor, every account reconnecting and typing for one minute, with notifications per second, send commit latency, writer queue and shutdown outcomes, on an otherwise idle machine, for both one and two instances; the NFR-2.2 measurement records the notification rate, the commit latency of sends and sign-outs beside it, and the listener's queue. If the measurement shows contention, the fallback is local delivery of typing on a single instance, which needs a specification amendment and an owner question at the slice review.
- **D62. Deferred erasure of a channel.** Every discard path (D48) erases this way. A discarding transaction does not erase in place: it deletes the channel's member rows, sets `erase_pending_since` on the channel under its `FOR UPDATE` lock, marks the channel's `pending` chat uploads `deleted` with their `storage_delete` queued (an `uploading` row is left to its own request: its second session finds the membership gone, refuses, marks the row `deleted` and queues the object's `storage_delete` itself, and `uploads.expire` covers a request that never reaches its second session, D54), and sends the last `entity.changed channel`; from that commit on, no member reaches the channel, `GET /files` refuses its files (D31), and no new upload or binding can name it. Removing a member from a group likewise marks that member's `pending` uploads to the group's channel `deleted` with their `storage_delete` queued, leaving `uploading` rows to their requests in the same way. The job then erases the stored messages; until its next run, which is at most a minute later while a channel is pending, the bodies and files exist in the database and storage but are reachable by no one. The `chat.erase_channels` job, which runs every minute (an empty run is one partial-index probe; the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review), erases each pending channel in batches of 500 messages per transaction under the locks of D53 (the tutor's profile, the channel), releasing charges and queueing `storage_delete`, and clears the flag when none is left. A pending channel is invisible to everyone. *Reason:* a provisional link can carry up to seven days of messages (at most about 300,000 at the rate limit), and tutor deletion, which discards every provisional link of the tutor in one transaction, must stay bounded.
  The cadence is every minute from S3 on, where the job only clears `erase_pending_since`; S4 adds the 20-second budget together with the message batches, because only those batches take time.
- **D63. The digest selects by sequence.** Each member row gains `digested_through_seq`. A digest counts, per channel, the messages whose `seq` is above both the member's `last_read_seq` and `digested_through_seq` and that were created after the member's `last_seen_at`, and that are neither deleted nor erased; it then sets `digested_through_seq` to the channel's `last_seq` read under the channel's `FOR SHARE` lock. A send holds the channel `FOR UPDATE`, so it either committed before the digest's transaction read the channel (and is counted) or waits for it to commit and then inserts a new flag; the flag that the digest clears can therefore never hide a message that it did not count. *Reason:* a window by creation time has the same commit-order gap as D9, and clearing the flag could race a concurrent send. Presence and last-seen are read from the account summary across all API leases, never from one instance's registry; D57's multi-instance clock acknowledgements precede sends and digest execution.
- **D64. Deleted messages in counts.** Unread counts include deleted and erased messages, which stay in the list as placeholders and are read like any other; digests count only messages that still have a body. A digest whose listed messages are all deleted by dispatch is obsolete (D36).
- **D65. Message idempotency.** The `Idempotency-Key` of a send is 1 to 64 characters of `^[A-Za-z0-9._-]{1,64}$`, stored as `message.client_key`, unique per sender and channel while the sender exists. A repeated key is authorized again before it is answered: the caller must still be a member of the channel (otherwise `404`), and the answer is the first message as the caller now sees it (an edited or deleted message is returned in its current state); the replay creates nothing and consumes no rate slot. The key is never logged.
- **D66. Upload request bounds.** A chat upload body holds exactly one part, named `file`, and nothing else (any other part or field answers `422 validation_failed`, `body.file`); part headers total at most 8 KiB; the whole body may exceed the context limit by at most 64 KiB of multipart overhead, enforced while reading; the file name comes from the part's `filename` parameter (D26). A malformed body answers `400 bad_request`.
- **D67. Links and the unconfirmed cue.** In message bodies, `http` and `https` addresses become links built from the parsed URL with `rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow"` and `target="_blank"`, showing the text as typed; an address with user information (`user@` or `user:password@` before the host) or containing a Unicode format character stays plain text, because both can disguise the real host; no other scheme becomes a link. While a direct channel's link is provisional, the tutor's channel list and conversation header show "Email not confirmed", so a squatter's links are read with that cue (T-44 residual: an unconfirmed account can send links to its tutor).
- **D68. Frame parsing lands with the frame.** Each change that makes the gateway emit a frame or an `entity.changed` kind adds it to `frontend/src/lib/realtime/frames.ts`, its strict parser and the realtime contract test in the same change: `channel` in S3, `message.new`, `message.updated`, `message.deleted` and `read.updated` in S4, `typing` and `presence` in S5. So no deployed bundle drops its socket on a frame its parser does not know (the slice 4 known limitation). The live-refetch handling of those frames lands in F2 and F3, which depend on the emitting changes.
- **D69. `channel_id` on the lesson detail.** `GET /lessons/{id}` carries the caller's channel for the lesson's audience only while the caller is a member of it: `null` for a student who holds the lesson only by its frozen roster (a former group member, an ended one-to-one relationship keeps its direct channel and carries it), `null` while the link is `reclaim_pending`, and `null` for a discarded link. A test covers each case.
- **D70. Download headers and byte ranges.** `GET /files/{id}` answers with `Content-Type` from detection (plain text as `text/plain; charset=utf-8`), `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Disposition` from the token (D26), `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; sandbox; frame-ancestors 'none'`, `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-origin`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `Cache-Control: private, no-store` (so no browser keeps a file after its message is deleted or its channel erased; T-18, T-34; a cache lifetime is adopted only if the slice review measures a need, with the residual recorded then) and `Content-Length` from the stored size, with the last byte sent only after the object is confirmed to end at that size, as for avatars. It ignores `Range` and always answers the whole file: in slice 6 only pictures are shown inline, and audio, video and every other kind is a download (D32). The response's write deadline is the size at the rate of D27 with the same floor and ceiling; residual (T-20): a slow reader holds one of the 64 download slots until that deadline.
- **D71. Rate-limit coverage.** The 30-per-minute limit of D14 counts sends, edits and deletes; a replayed send (D65) and a refused request count nothing; the read marker and typing have their own bounds (once a second from the client; D21).
- **D72. Typing and presence work before the database.** Typing frames are coalesced in memory before any transaction (D21), and presence changes are debounced in memory before any recipient query (D20), so neither can turn the inbound frame rate into a database load.
- **D73. The tutor purge deletes students' uploads in the tutor's channels.** Attachments that students uploaded to a tutor's channels are charged to the tutor; the tutor's purge removes their references with the messages and queues their `storage_delete`, so no student-uploaded object outlives the channel.
- **D74. Frontend behaviour under test.** The typing dots stop with reduced motion (component); below 768 px the list and the conversation are separate screens, with Back to the list (folded into the exchange test at 375 px); the sidebar's Messages count and the hidden sidebar's dot (component); the conversation header's link (folded into the exchange test); focus when the open channel is removed live (component, D38); channel search (component).
- **D75. Only chat writes are disabled while offline** (Q11 A). The banner shows on every signed-in page; Send, Edit, Delete and attaching in Messages carry the disabled state of D39 while it shows; every other action stays enabled and works through REST; pages.md's shared offline rule is amended, pending owner review.
- **D76. A new or re-added group member sees the whole history** (Q12 A), with the member row's `last_read_seq` and `digested_through_seq` set to the channel's `last_seq` when the row is created, so nothing counts as unread and no digest lists the past.
- **D77. Office content beyond macros** (Q13 A). Refused: macros by part, content type and relationship type; ActiveX parts and their relationships; OLE embeddings (`embeddings/oleObject*.bin` and OLE relationship types); every relationship with `TargetMode="External"` whose type is not the hyperlink type, for example an external `attachedTemplate`, which a test names as an expected refusal. The `package` relationship type, which a native chart uses for its embedded workbook, is not an OLE type and is accepted, and `TestOfficeContentRulesFollowTheDecision` includes an accepted document with a native chart. In ODF: `Basic/` and `Scripts/` folders, manifest entries with script or OLE media types, and manifest entries that point outside the container. `content.xml` and `styles.xml` are not parsed, so links and linked objects inside them (`xlink:href`) are accepted and recorded as a T-13 residual; a document link opens nothing by itself in the supported office applications without the user's action.
- **D79. `GET /channels/{id}`** (member, `user, unverified ok`, `no-store`) answers one channel with the fields of a `GET /channels` item (D35) plus the tutor's header link (`relationship_id` or `group_id` for the tutor, absent for students), under D7 and Q9 B: no member list for students. The Messages page uses it for a deep link to `/messages/{channelId}` and refetches it after `entity.changed channel` for that channel; a channel outside the caller's membership, or pending erasure, answers `404`. It enters the denial and audience tables (D43) and lands in S3 (K48).
- **D80. What keeping a reclaimed link restores** (the owner's decision of 2026-10-29, pending owner review). The reclaim transaction, which already holds the account's row and suspends its member row (D3), also sets the direct channel's `provisional_through_seq` to its `last_seq` under the channel's `FOR UPDATE` lock: every message up to it was written during the provisional period, because the channel was created when the link became provisional. On keep, the owner's choice locks the channel and sets the restored row's `last_read_seq` to just before the first message up to `provisional_through_seq` whose sender is not the account (or leaves it at `provisional_through_seq` when there is none), so those messages and everything after them count as unread, and sets `digested_through_seq` to the channel's `last_seq`, so no digest lists the past. Every message whose sender is the account and whose `seq` is at most `provisional_through_seq` is shown with the fixed cue "Sent before the account was reclaimed" (`sent_before_reclaim: true` on the message, for both roles), derived at read time from the channel's column and the message's sender and sequence. *Alternative:* a per-message flag set at keep. *Reason:* the derived cue needs no update of up to about 300,000 rows inside the owner's choice, cannot drift from the messages, and the sequence boundary is exact where reclaim timestamps would have the commit-order gap of D9; it is a fixed label and a boolean, never the squatter's chosen name, avatar or any other account data. A link confirmed from the account's own browser is not a reclaim, sets no boundary and shows no cue.
- **D81. One active-member predicate.** "An active member of a channel" (a member row that is not suspended, of a channel that is neither discarded nor pending erasure) is one repository query fragment, used by every access path: the chat routes and `GET /channels/{id}`, unread counts and `GET /channels/unread`, the read marker (D13), the idempotency replay (D65), author deletes (D47), both upload sessions (D23, D54), downloads (D31), frame and signal recipients (D5, D8, D18), presence recipients and `online` (D20, D35, D79), typing recipients (D21), the `digest_pending` insert (D17) and the digest (D36). Deletion and purge lookups (D16, D62, the discard paths and the purges) do not use it: they find every member row of the account or channel, suspended ones included, so none survives a purge or an erasure. `TestASuspendedMemberReachesNothingAndIsShownToNoOne` drives every listed path against a `reclaim_pending` channel.
- **D82. Multipart uploads are always aborted.** The first session records the storage multipart upload's identifier on the attachment row (`multipart_upload_id`) as soon as the upload is created, so every cleanup, the age rule of `uploads.expire` included, aborts it by identifier after a crash; S2 adds to the local storage server's start-up configuration the bucket lifecycle rule that aborts incomplete multipart uploads after one day ([files](../specs/files.md#upload-flow)) where the server supports it, and records the rule as a deployment obligation for slice 10; tests assert that no incomplete upload remains.
- **D83. A discarded channel stays recognisable.** The discarding transaction sets the channel's `discarded_at`, which is never cleared, beside `erase_pending_since`, which the erasure job clears when it is done; `uploads.expire`, the second session, binding and downloads treat a channel with `discarded_at` set, with `erase_pending_since` set, or that no longer exists (after the tutor's purge) as unbindable and unreachable.

### 6.4 Threat model

S1 amends each, pending owner review:

- **T-5, T-6, T-21, T-29:** channels, messages, frames and downloads resolved through stored membership under the channel lock (D3, D5); a second tutor sharing a student, a removed member and a student of another group reach nothing; a removed member's sockets get one last `entity.changed channel` and nothing more; presence recipients are read without locks, a stated exception that can deliver one presence frame to a concurrently removed member (D20); residual: a download address minted before a removal keeps working until its expiry, at most 10 minutes (D59), unless its message is deleted.
- **T-13:** streaming limits, magic-byte detection, bounded container inspection by ranged reads and the content rules of D77 (D24, D25), with the residual that links and linked objects inside ODF `content.xml` and `styles.xml` are not inspected, request bounds (D66), file-name rules (D26), concurrency, pending and hourly caps.
- **T-14:** message bodies, file names and labels as text only, links only for parsed `http` and `https` addresses without user information or format characters (D10, D26, D67).
- **T-17, T-32:** the digest's unsubscribe token, minted at render time, one group.
- **T-18:** chat and file records hold identifiers only; the digest holds channel labels and counts only; picture metadata removed by the allowlist filter (D50); students see other students only on their messages, with no member list and no presence of other students (D51); residual: in a group with only two students, anonymous typing still tells one student that the other is typing.
- **T-20:** message write and attachment rate limits, pending and hourly upload caps, pending uploads in the quota check (D55), upload concurrency, deadlines and the stall rule (D27), download concurrency, container bounds, digest frequency, typing and presence coalescing before the database (D72); residuals: a slow upload holds one of 32 slots for up to its deadline, a slow download reader one of 64 slots, and in-memory counters reset on restart.
- **T-27, T-35, the leaked-link actor and boundary 3:** the download address under Q2 (C: OVC-signed, versioned, bound to its attachment and message, 5 to 10 minutes, never logged, `no-referrer`, query strings dropped by the slice 10 proxy; the file's headers set by OVC, D59, D70).
- **T-31:** the digest's labels escaped and stripped like lesson titles, no message text.
- **T-34:** messages erased or deleted by the purges (D16, D73), presence, digest and member rows purged under the channel locks, discarded provisional channels erased whole (D48, D62).
- **T-36:** the clock route's presence step (D57); proposal 0004's technique 3 under Q1 A (E7).
- **T-37:** the content security policy stays `'self'` (Q2 C); its wording names the OVC download route instead of a storage host.
- **T-44:** an unconfirmed provisional account reaches only its direct channel (D3, D34), presence with its tutor only (D20), no attachments (D49), its channel erased whole when discarded (D48); the tutor sees the card's name, never the account's chosen name, and the "Email not confirmed" cue beside its messages (D7, D67); residual: it can send links to its tutor. While a link is `reclaim_pending` the account's member row is suspended (D3, D81): it reaches nothing and is shown to no one, while the tutor keeps the channel and may write; keeping the link resumes the row with the provisional period's messages that the account did not send counted as unread, and marks every message the account sent during the provisional period with the fixed cue "Sent before the account was reclaimed", shown to the owner and the tutor alike and revealing nothing about who sent it (D80); dropping the link erases the whole channel (D48, D62).

## 9. Risks, performance and the budgets

**Risks.**

- **Membership drift:** the mirror of D3 touches about fifteen existing paths in the tutoring service; the table-driven test of S3 is the guard, and the slice review's backend reviewer re-derives the path list from the code.
- **Lock order:** chat writes add the channel between groups and series, and charge paths add tutor profiles up front (D53); the `pg_locks` tests of S3, S4 and S5 and the slice 3 to 5 race tests guard it.
- **The send path's cost:** a student's send locks the user row, the channel and, with attachments, the tutor's profile; NFR-2.2 is measured below, and a profile lock held by a long tutor write delays that tutor's students' attachment sends.
- **Notification lock:** typing and presence add `pg_notify` traffic to every commit that notifies (D61); measured below, with a named fallback.
- **The offline banner (F1):** every page renders inside the frame that shows it, and the disabled state lives in shared components; the risks are a page whose own busy or disabled state the new state overrides, a focus move when controls become disabled, a banner that flashes on fast reconnects, and existing tests that act on a control while the realtime client reconnects. Mitigations: the 5-second grace, `aria-disabled` without `disabled` so focus stays, Q11 A limiting the disabled controls to chat, and the repeated runs of the realtime suites in F1's acceptance criteria.
- **Container inspection:** ranged reads cost a few extra storage requests per office document; bounded by D25.
- **Mixed content and storage hosts:** avoided, because downloads are served by the API on the OVC origin (Q2 C).
- **Downloads through the API** (Q2 C) add the pilot's file traffic to the one VM; at the pilot's scale this is far below its network capacity, and NFR-2.3's streaming keeps memory flat.
- **Minors** (the owner's decision of 2026-10-25 that students may be minors; no decision record yet): chat adds no field about age and collects no new personal data beyond messages and files; picture metadata (Q8) and students' visibility to each other (Q9) are the points a decision on minors may revisit.
- **The owner's `backend/.env`** needs nothing new: the download key derives from `OVC_JWT_SECRET` (D59).
- **A dependency change outside the slice** (the `golang.org/x/net` upgrade whose advisory ignores expire on 2026-10-31) may land meanwhile as its own change; it moves `main`, so later merges squash.

**Performance measurements at the slice review,** serially on an idle machine, recorded in `knowledge/analysis/`:

- NFR-2.2: 2,000 connected sockets, 30-member group channels, 20 messages per second across the instance, with typing from a tenth of the users and the presence churn of reconnects: time from send to every member's refetched message, p95 (target under 1 s), including the coalesced `GET /channels/unread` and first-page refetches of D60, the notification rate, the commit latency of sends and sign-outs and the listener's queue (D61);
- NFR-2.3: 32 concurrent 25 MB uploads and 64 concurrent downloads: process memory flat with file size; an office document at the inspection bounds;
- `GET /channels` and `GET /channels/unread` for a tutor with 500 channels and a student with five tutors and three groups, and a history page in a channel of 100,000 messages: p95;
- the digest job with 5,000 flagged members, and the erasure job with a 300,000-message channel: run time within their budgets;
- the presence batch with 2,000 accounts: duration and its lock waits;
- a tutor purge with 100,000 messages and 5,000 attachments, and a student purge with 50,000 messages across five tutors: batches and duration;
- repeated picture loads under `Cache-Control: private, no-store` (D70): 100 conversation switches over a channel with 200 pictures, with the bytes downloaded, the download slots in use and the time to show each conversation's pictures, which decides whether a cache lifetime is needed;
- frontend: the Messages chunk, a conversation scrolled through 1,000 messages, 100 conversation switches with flat listeners, DOM nodes and query-cache entries;
- the merge gate, cold, three times on an idle machine.
